# 7. 7. Growth Scenarios to 2035

This chapter estimates alloy demand through 2035. The team used a calibrated tier 1 model for established applications and a separate tier 2 register for emerging applications (Product Strategy 2035 Team, 2026a).

## 7.1 7.1 Growth Model and Scenario Assumptions

Tier 1 models demand as D = A × I × S. A represents industry activity, I represents alloy mass per unit, and S represents the share of units using a high-performance alloy. Each factor is a ratio of its 2035 value to its 2025 value. The index sets 2025 at 100. The model uses measured end uses, including the 11,499 t of cobalt used in aviation out of 20.0 kt used in superalloys in 2025. The IEA outlook for nickel outside energy technologies sets the broader limit for the estimates (Cobalt Institute, 2026; International Energy Agency, 2026b).

The team used a backtest to see how well past trends predicted recent outcomes. A trend fitted through 2019 underestimated gas power investment from 2020 to 2025. It had a mean absolute percentage error of 36.5 percent and a bias of minus 36.0 percent. The trend for EU vehicle output overestimated output by 23.0 percent (Product Strategy 2035 Team, 2026a).

The model corrects half of each growth error, up to 1 percentage point per year. The remaining error widens the range in the Monte Carlo simulation. The team used 10,000 draws and seed 20261108 to estimate P10, P50, and P90, the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles.

The supply analysis estimates that IEA cobalt mine supply falls 30 percent short of 2035 demand. The gap reaches 36 percent if the Congo quota stays at its 2026 level. Superalloys require only 13 to 20 percent of the cobalt, so the shortfall is more likely to raise costs than to cap superalloy production. The estimated 21 percent refined nickel gap is an upper bound. Copper mines cover 62 percent of projected demand before scrap is counted (Product Strategy 2035 Team, 2026a; U.S. Geological Survey, 2026e).

Engine and turbine growth is concentrated in the United States and Western Europe. The United States accounts for a fifth of superalloy cobalt. China has 40 of the 81 reactors under construction, so much reactor tube demand is tied to China. Asia accounts for more than 80 percent of expected chemical industry growth (BASF, 2026; U.S. Geological Survey, 2026e; World Nuclear Association, 2026b).
