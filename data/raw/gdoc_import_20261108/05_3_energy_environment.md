## 5.3 5.3 Energy and Environmental Technology

Energy technology shows two different trends. Gas turbine makers are struggling to fill orders. Many announced hydrogen projects have yet to generate orders.

### 5.3.1 5.3.1 Demand, Geography and Market Size

Orders for gas turbines have grown as data centers need power and operators work to keep electricity grids stable. Siemens Energy is sold out until fiscal 2028. It plans to raise annual gas turbine capacity from about 17 GW in fiscal 2024 to more than 30 GW (Siemens Energy, 2025). Investment in gas-fired power rose from USD 64 billion in 2019 to USD 112 billion in 2025. Data centers could use about 945 TWh of electricity in 2030, more than twice their 2024 use (International Energy Agency, 2025a, 2026j).

Low-emissions hydrogen production was about 1 Mt in 2025, compared with total hydrogen demand of more than 100 Mt. The announced 2030 project pipeline fell to 27 Mt (International Energy Agency, 2026d, 2026e).

The IEA database lists 4.3 GW of operating electrolysis capacity and 19.3 GW at final investment decision or under construction. Alkaline electrolyzers use nickel and account for 60 percent of capacity under construction (International Energy Agency, 2026g). Green hydrogen orders at thyssenkrupp nucera, a leading European alkaline electrolyzer maker, fell from EUR 356 million to EUR 26 million in fiscal 2024/25 (thyssenkrupp nucera, 2025).

Nuclear power offers steadier demand that takes longer to develop. Of 81 reactors under construction, 40 are in China. An October 2025 U.S. framework covers at least USD 80 billion of AP1000 reactors (Westinghouse Electric, 2025; World Nuclear Association, 2026b).

The IEA estimates that hydrogen technologies used 2.3 kt of nickel in 2025. Its 2035 estimates reach 5.2 kt under stated policies and 32 kt in the high-demand case. Public sources do not report the tonnage of superalloys used in gas turbines (International Energy Agency, 2026b).

### 5.3.2 5.3.2 Applications and Opportunities

Table 5 compares when three energy opportunities could generate demand. Gas turbine parts are needed now. Fuel cell demand for specific metals is less clear. Bloom Energy plans to double its annual solid oxide output to 2 GW by the end of 2026, but it does not identify its interconnect material (Bloom Energy, 2026).

In nuclear power, Alleima is reopening its Sandviken tube mill to increase steam generator tube capacity by about 60 percent. It melts and remelts its own alloys there, so the expansion creates little open demand for bar or hollows from other producers (Alleima, 2026a).

Table 5: High performance metal applications in energy technology, outlook to 2035

| Outlook | Applications | Materials and product forms |
|---|---|---|
| Grows strongly | Gas turbine blades, vanes, combustors and repair | Superalloy remelt stock, combustor sheet, welding wire |
| Grows from 2030 | Nuclear steam generator tube | Alloy 690 tube, mostly from integrated mills |
| Option | Solid oxide cell interconnects, alkaline electrolyser electrodes | Coated ferritic strip, nickel strip and mesh |

*Sources: team assessment (International Energy Agency, 2026g; Siemens Energy, 2025; thyssenkrupp nucera, 2025; World Nuclear Association, 2026b).*

Implications for suppliers:

* Qualify superalloy remelt stock and combustor sheet with turbine casting houses while capacity is sold out to 2028.
* Treat electrolysers, fuel cells and nuclear tube as options released only against signed volumes, and in nuclear tube only where a tube maker buys outside hollows.
