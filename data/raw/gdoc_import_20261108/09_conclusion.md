# 9. 9. Conclusion

The study asked how supply concentration, metal conversion, and industry needs shape opportunities through 2035. The findings show that converting metals into qualified products creates much of the value. Nickel is in surplus, while quotas and licenses restrict cobalt, tungsten, and molybdenum. Countries that make qualified bar, strip, and tube earn trade surpluses on those products. Cobalt shortages are more likely to raise superalloy costs than to cap production.

The tier 1 results reported here put the 2035 P50 index at 156 for energy technology and 147 for aerospace. Automotive demand stays close to its starting level. The highest-ranked opportunities supported by measured evidence are turbine remelt stock, engine billet, and precision FeNi foil in the United States, Europe, and East Asia. Products that require customer approval follow. The tier 2 register tracks solid oxide strip, fusion magnet tape, additive powder, and small reactor tubing against milestones.

The estimates have limits. Official statistics stop at the mill product stage, and most indices rely on activity measures and weights estimated by the team because alloy tonnage by industry is unavailable. The results support three actions through 2035. Producers should secure their inputs, qualify early with engine and turbine makers, and invest in capacity after customers sign orders.
