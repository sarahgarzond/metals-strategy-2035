# 3. 3. Introduction to the Analytical Framework and Methodology

The study follows seven steps in the metal value chain: material, product form, application, industry, geography, market size, and growth. Table 1 shows how the chapters cover these steps. The study compares figures from the same stage. For example, it separates contained metal from gross weight and actual output from production capacity. Mine output, alloy melt, mill products, and industry revenue are reported separately.

The main sources are structured data from the USGS, UN Comtrade, Eurostat PRODCOM, the World Bank, OECD, UNIDO, and the IEA. The study also uses industry associations, official outlooks, and profiles of 61 companies. Commercial market reports serve as a cross-check. Where a second source exists, the study checks the figures that support its main findings. The source register flags figures that come from a single source and records each value's unit, stage, data type, source, and formula.

Tier 1 estimates demand for established applications. It multiplies industry activity by the metal content per unit and by the share of units that use a high-performance alloy. Examples of activity measures include engine deliveries, gigawatts of gas turbines, and joint replacements. The model starts with measured 2025 values, including cobalt use in aviation.

To check the model, the team fitted trends to data through 2019 and compared the projections with actual outcomes from 2020 to 2025. The team also checked earlier official outlooks where available. A Monte Carlo simulation estimates the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles of 2035 demand. A separate supply analysis tests how export quotas and licensing changes could affect the results.

Tier 2 records emerging applications that current data cannot confirm. Examples include hydrogen-fueled turbines, supercritical carbon dioxide recuperators, molten salt and small modular reactors, fusion magnet substrate tape, and additively manufactured engine parts. The team identified candidates from patents, pilot plants, standards under development, and announced projects. For each candidate, it assessed technology readiness, strength of evidence, required alloys and product forms, and the earliest date it could reach commercial volume.

The register identifies an event that could move each candidate into tier 1, such as a final investment decision or a first serial order. Chapter 8 uses the same eight criteria to score both tiers and identifies the evidence behind each score. Producers can use the results to decide which opportunities to monitor and which have enough evidence to consider funding.
