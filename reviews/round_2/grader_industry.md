## 1. Research depth (explicit judgement)

What is built from structured data and filings (good):
* Chapter 4 metal volumes come from USGS MCS 2026 commodity chapters plus the study groups (INSG, Cobalt Institute, ICSG, IMOA, ICDA, IAI, IMnI). Prices from the World Bank Pink Sheet. This is the right hierarchy.
* Chapter 6 uses Comtrade reporter data for HS 7502, 7505, 7506, 7507, 8105, 8311, 8411, and the German HS 7506 unit value (USD 578 m / 17,775 t = USD 32,517 per t) reconciles with data/clean/comtrade_7506_2019-2024.csv and is cross-checked with Eurostat within 1.8%. That is the single best piece of analysis in the report.
* Company figures (Acerinox HPA EUR 1,683 m and 83 kt melt, ATI USD 1,762.9 m jet engine revenue, Siemens Energy 17 to 30 GW, Safran EUR 150 m press, Carpenter medical USD 278.4 m) are taken from filings and reconcile with research/companies/.

What is weak or borrowed:
* The only market size for high performance alloys (chapter 1, USD 13.6 bn and USD 10.2 bn) comes from two paid market reports (The Insight Partners, IMARC). There is no primary triangulation, although one is possible from INSG first use (non ferrous alloys and superalloys share of nickel use), Cobalt Institute superalloy demand (20 kt Co), Comtrade weights for HS 7505 to 7507, US Census NAICS 331491 shipments, the BLS PPI for nickel alloy mill shapes, and peer melt tonnes (Acerinox HPA 83 kt melt and 47 kt finished, Aperam Alloys and Specialties 61 kt at EUR 17,475 per t, Imphy about 36 kt). All of these sit in research/ already.
* Comtrade covers ten reporters only (Germany, USA, China, Japan, Korea, UK, Italy, Indonesia, France, India per the table note, and the clean file holds eight with world totals). France, Sweden, Austria, Switzerland, Canada are missing, so "Germany is the world's largest exporter of nickel plate, sheet, strip and foil" (6.1) is a ranking within the sample, not a world ranking. Mirror data (partner imports from world) would settle it.
* PRODCOM appears twice in the text (titanium and one cross-check), UNIDO INDSTAT never, OECD STAN once (German E&E value added). A3 lists fifteen PRODCOM codes and fourteen HS codes, most of which are never used in the body (7219, 7220, 7223, 8109, 7409, 7606).
* reviews/research_audit.md shows research/materials/apparent_consumption.md as FAIL (no Comtrade, no Pink Sheet, no USGS, 7 sources). That is exactly the note that should have produced Material > Product form tonnage for Germany and the EU, and its absence is why the data chain stops at "index".
* The titanium Comtrade file (comtrade_8108) is empty, so the report has no titanium trade evidence even though it calls aerospace titanium a high availability risk.
* The 61 company profiles are much richer than the report. Aubert & Duval (owned by Airbus, Safran, Tikehau), Proterial, Nippon Yakin, Fushun, Western Superconducting, voestalpine HPM (EBIT margin 2.1%), PCC/Special Metals (USD 10.8 bn), Aperam/Universal Stainless, Howmet, Nornickel and Höganäs are researched but not used, which is why the competitor picture looks thin.

Verdict: the metal facts are database driven, the market size and the scenarios are not. Grade 2.7.

## 3. Gaps that keep the report from going to a board

1. No identity check of the client. The report cannot say whether the company melts (VIM, VAR, ESR), forges, rolls or only finishes, yet Tier 1 is a vacuum melted superalloy capacity bet. A board needs a one page "capability gate" before any recommendation.
2. No peer benchmark table. research/ already holds melt tonnes, revenue, EBITDA margin and ASP per tonne for Acerinox HPA (83 kt melt, 47 kt finished, EUR 1,683 m, 8% EBITDA, H1 2026 sales down 18% and EBIT negative), Aperam A&S (61 kt, EUR 17,475 per t, 10.2% EBITDA, Imphy capacity doubled for LNG Invar, new VIM), voestalpine HPM (2.1% EBIT), ATI, Carpenter, PCC, Haynes, Nippon Yakin, Fushun (gross margin 0.65%), Western Superconducting. The fact that Europe's leading nickel alloy peer had negative EBIT in H1 2026 is missing and changes the tone of chapter 8.
3. Competitor landscape is incomplete. Missing or barely mentioned: PCC (Special Metals, Wyman Gordon, SMC, Cannon-Muskegon), Aubert & Duval, Proterial (Invar foil for FMM, 718 billet), Nippon Yakin (Invar for LNG membrane, Ni alloy plate), Böhler, VAC, Kanthal (resistance alloys), Chinese entrants (Fushun, CISRI Gaona, Baowu Special Metallurgy, Western Superconducting, Jiangsu Toland) and India (MIDHANI).
4. Missing metals for superalloys: niobium (5% in 718, the largest volume superalloy, about 80% from CBMM in Brazil), rhenium and hafnium (single crystal blades), tantalum. Section 4.7.5 only covers iron.
5. Qualification is reduced to ISO 15156 and ISO 13485. Insiders expect AMS/AMS 2628 class ultrasonic specs, OEM source approvals, AS9100/EN 9100, NADCAP (heat treating, NDT, materials testing lab), ASME Section II and code cases, ASME III NCA-3800, RCC-M, PED, AD 2000 W-sheets and VdTÜV Werkstoffblätter (decisive in the German chemical market), NORSOK M-650, API 6ACRA, and their lead times.
6. Pricing mechanism is one sentence ("pass through under surcharges"). Missing: monthly alloy surcharge formula on prior month LME, Fastmarkets cobalt and Platts MoO3, the lag effect and inventory gains or losses in falling markets, LIFO at ATI and Carpenter, fixed price aerospace LTAs with raw material indices, and a mill product price level per form (bar, plate, strip, wire in EUR per kg).
7. Raw material qualification depth. Which class 1 nickel (Vale Clydach pellets, Glencore Nikkelverk cathode, Sumitomo, Jinchuan, Indonesian LME brands), what share was Russian (Nornickel, researched), which cobalt sources are aerospace approved (Glencore Nikkelverk, Vale Port Colborne, Sherritt, Umicore), and revert economics.
8. Trade policy is absent: US tariffs on EU goods and the aircraft parts carve out, Section 232 on steel derivatives, EU CBAM (covers chapter 72 grades such as FeNi36 and Alloy 800, not chapter 75 nickel alloys), the new EU steel import regime, DFARS specialty metals (Germany is a qualifying country, which is an advantage in US defence).
9. Distribution channel is missing, although stockists (thyssenkrupp Materials Services, Rolled Alloys, NeoNickel, Sandmeyer, Corrotherm) handle a large share of European nickel alloy flat and bar volume, and 6.3 itself recommends "stock and distribution".
10. Spotlight facts in several sectors lack "likely suppliers" as the standard demands. Safran, Siemens Energy, Bloom, TechnipFMC and the FDA talus implant all lack the mill names.
11. Missing high value niches: LNG carrier Invar membrane (GTT NO96, record LNG carrier orders, Aperam Imphy and Nippon Yakin as incumbents), nitinol and MP35N for medical wire (Fort Wayne Metals, ATI, Confluent), CRA OCTG (G3, 028, super 13Cr), clad plate (voestalpine Grobblech, JFE, JSW), chlor alkali Nickel 200 (thyssenkrupp nucera's larger business), PM disc superalloys.
12. No figure at all in the main text. A board deck needs at least the supply concentration chart, the Comtrade unit value chart, the scenario fan and the opportunity bubble chart (model.py already draws these).

## 5. What would make me forward this to my board

* One page answer first: three product and customer bets with tonnes, EUR, margin per tonne, capex and qualification years, plus what to stop.
* A sized market by alloy family and form (bar and billet, flat, wire, tube, powder, remelt stock) in kt and EUR for 2025 and 2035, built from Comtrade, INSG, Cobalt Institute and peer filings.
* A competitor map with melt routes and capacity additions (ATI sixth melt path, Alleima fourth VAR, Aperam Imphy VIM, Outokumpu Avesta 13 kt, Daido Chita, Chinese additions) set against demand, showing whether the shortage lasts beyond 2028.
* A qualification and approval table, because approvals are the moat the report keeps talking about without naming.
* The raw material and surcharge section, because that is where nickel alloy producers actually win or lose a year's earnings.
