## Can a producer decide on Monday?

- What to invest in: mostly. Superalloy bar, billet, remelt stock and repair wire for engine and turbine chains rank first, and the reasons are clear. What is missing is the size of the cheque per tonne of capacity and the size of the prize in tonnes or EUR.
- Where to sell: yes. The report names engine and turbine chains in Western Europe and the US, EPC contractors for India and Southeast Asia, gas projects in the Americas and Africa, and implant makers in Europe and the US. The trade corridors in 6.3 back this up.
- What to stop: partly. The four items are diesel only parts, European base chemical new build, speculative electrolyser capacity and fuel cladding zirconium. They are named, but nothing says how much capacity or revenue they typically tie up.

## Five questions my board would ask that the report does not answer

1. How big is each Tier 1 prize in tonnes and EUR? The report gives EU nickel mill product consumption (72 kt) but no size for aero engine superalloy bar and billet, turbine remelt stock, repair wire or FeNi foil, even as a labelled team estimate (for example from the 20 kt of superalloy cobalt, or from Comtrade 7505 values for the US and Europe).
2. What does a qualified tonne of new superalloy capacity cost, and how long does it take before the first revenue? The appendix lists ATI's fifth VIM (about 9 kt), Aperam Imphy (EUR 160m), Carpenter Athens and Daido (JPY 36bn), but the report never turns these into a EUR per tonne range or a time to first approval.
3. What happens to European exporters under US tariffs? The US is a priority region, and German bar and wire exports go mainly to the US and UK. Section 6.4 says Section 232 "favours local supply" but does not say whether HS 7505 to 7507 are covered, or what that does to landed cost.
4. What if supply, not demand, is the binding scenario? If Congo tightens cobalt quotas again or China stops tungsten licences, what happens to the Tier 1 ranking and to the cost of superalloys? Chapter 7 holds prices and availability constant, so the supply half of the central question has no 2035 answer.
5. How robust is the ranking? Four Tier 1 entries sit at 4.00 to 4.40, with FeNi and repair wire exactly on the threshold. Would a different weighting (for example 20 percent on entry barriers), or a downside case like 2020 in aerospace, change the tiers?

## Smaller points

- Table 2 gives copper as "USD 9,947/t, LME record in 2026", while 4.7.2 says records above USD 14,500 in 2026. Label the first as the 2025 average.
- 5.2 Implications say "Sub Saharan Africa", while the evidence (A3) says Africa overall. Align the wording.
- 7.2 is headed "by Region" but has no regional numbers. A simple table of where each Tier 1 driver sits (share of engine deliveries, turbine capacity and implant procedures by region) would fill the link.
- Chapter 2 states where the value lies but not what to stop. One sentence on the stop list would let a board read chapter 2 plus chapter 9 in 15 minutes and have the full answer.
