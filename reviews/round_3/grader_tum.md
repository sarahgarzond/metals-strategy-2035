## Justification

**Research (1.7).** USGS MCS 2026, INSG, Cobalt Institute, IMOA, World Bank Pink Sheet, Comtrade, PRODCOM, Cefic, SEMI, OECD and filings drive the numbers, and the editions are current. Spotlights are strong in aerospace (Safran 30,000 t press at Gennevilliers, 2029), energy (Siemens Energy sold out to FY2028) and electronics (NVIDIA Kyber, DNP Kurosaki). Deductions: the first numbers of the report (ch. 1, p. 3) come from McKinsey and two paid market reports (The Insight Partners, IMARC), which the source rules allow only for triangulation. The copper price in Table 2 rests on a blog (The Oregon Group). CMOC is cited as author of Mining Weekly and mining.com articles. Several analytical claims carry no citation: "heavy duty rotor discs are mostly steel" (5.3.1), "surcharges pass most nickel and molybdenum cost changes through" (5.5.2), "Every producer meets ISO 15156" (5.2.2). Automotive (5.1) still lacks a full spotlight with alloy grade, form and supplier.

**Model (2.3).** The Growth link is transparent and reproducible (formula, Table 12, Table A1). Three weaknesses hold it back. (a) The Market size link is mostly activity indicators: chapter 5 subsections are titled "Demand, Geography and Market Size" but none states a size of high performance metal demand for its industry, and chapter 6 measures only EU nickel mill products. (b) Table 10 (p. 23) is internally implausible: plate, sheet and strip at 57.8 kt and EUR 697 m is about EUR 12,000 per tonne, and tubes at 9.2 kt and EUR 42 m is about EUR 4,600 per tonne, both below the value of the contained nickel, while section 4.1 and 6.2 argue that mill products sell at about twice the metal price (USD 32,500 per tonne). Either the codes include something else, the confidential cells distort the result, or there is a unit error, and the text does not notice. (c) The growth rates in Table 12 are asserted, not derived: aerospace 4.5 percent against a fleet growth of 3.4 percent is "adjusted for material intensity" without saying by how much. The 1 to 5 scores in Table 14 have no scoring scale, and the growth score of Precision FeNi strip (4) contradicts its own base index of 110 in Table 13, which is the lowest of all product families. Since FeNi lands exactly on the 4.00 Tier 1 threshold, this inconsistency changes a tier.

**Storyline (2.0).** Chapter 2 states the question, thesis ("value is created by conversion and qualification rather than by metal volume") and roadmap, and the conclusion answers the question in the same words. Metal sections hand over cleanly (cobalt to chromium to molybdenum). Deductions: chapter 1 is a generic opener ("For centuries, metals...") that delays the answer by a page and repeats ch. 2. Chapter 4 does not end with a metal by industry bridge, so the reader cannot see which metal feeds which of the seven industries until chapter 7. Chapter 6 introduces the trade surplus as "the market's central economic fact" but the recommendations in chapter 8 never use trade (no corridor, no import dependence enters Table 14). The conclusion calls "aerospace engines and gas turbines" two industries, while the report's own industries are Aerospace and Energy and Environmental Technology.

**Actionability (2.3).** Priorities are specific (alloy family, form, region), there is a stop list (8.2) and kill criteria (A8). For a generic producer, owners can only be functions, but the roadmap names none (sales, quality and approvals, operations, procurement). Sizing is thin: the only investment benchmark for the superalloy engine programme is Outokumpu's high nickel flat line at Avesta, which is the wrong product (flat, not VIM/VAR bar), while ATI's fifth VIM furnace in Monroe and Aperam's Imphy VIM programme sit unused in Table A9. Gates are good, timing is coarse (three horizons).

**Writing (2.3).** Academic register is mostly right and paragraphs largely follow topic sentence, evidence, explanation, transition, especially in chapter 4. Deductions: about 25 sentences exceed 48 words and chain three or four facts with "and" or "while" (for example 6.2 first paragraph, 62 words, 4.7.2, 4.7.4, 8.1 second paragraph), which breaks the evidence then explanation rhythm. Several 5.x.2 subsections open with a one sentence paragraph before the table (5.3.2, 5.4.2, 5.5.2), which is not an essay paragraph. Stray comma "production from 2027, (BorgWarner, 2026)" (5.1.2). Some table source lines read "Source:" instead of the italic "Sources:" required by the design. The reference list counts against this criterion: ATI 2026a and 2026b are the same 10-K, BASF 2026a and 2026b share one URL, Boeing 2026a and 2026b are the same CMO, "United Nations (2026)" and "United Nations Statistics Division (2026a to d)" are the same database under two authors, the 1.27 cm hanging indent is applied on p. 31 and p. 42 but missing on pp. 32 to 41, and the justified layout spreads short entries across the line ("Airbus.      (2026a,      July)").

**Visuals (2.3).** Tables are consistent and captioned, Table 14 now fits one page. But the main text has only three figures in 30 pages, none in chapters 5 or 8, and no visual for the chain itself in chapter 3. Table 14 uses unexplained abbreviations (Mkt, Gro, Sup, Comp, Barr, Diff). The title page still names Münvest twice, which the team should decide on given the no client rule.

## The ten changes that would most improve the grade (ranked by impact)

1. **Fix Table 10 and reconcile it with the conversion thesis** (sections/06_trade.md, 6.1 first paragraph and Table 10; p. 23). Problem: implied unit values of EUR 12,000 and EUR 4,600 per tonne are below contained metal value and contradict the USD 32,500 per tonne argument in 4.1 and 6.2. Fix: recheck the PRODCOM extraction (units, confidential cells, whether imports and exports use the same quantity basis), add a unit value column, and either correct the figures or explain in one sentence why apparent consumption values are understated. Impact: Model 2.3 to 2.0, protects credibility with industry experts.
2. **Give every industry a Market size sentence on the chain** (sections/05_1 to 05_7, x.1 subsection, last paragraph). Problem: the headings promise market size but deliver activity indicators. Fix: close each x.1 with one paragraph that names the best available measured size for that industry's metal demand (for example Cobalt Institute 11,499 t of cobalt in aviation, PRODCOM bar code for aerospace and energy, Comtrade HS 8411 for engines) or a `TODO(data)` if none exists, labelled as stage specific. Impact: Model and Storyline each about +0.3, about 0.1 overall.
