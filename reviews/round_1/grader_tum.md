## Research depth and source quality: 2.7

The nickel profile is the standard the rest of the report does not meet. Section 4.1 keeps mine output, primary metal, stainless melt and mill product on separate balances, and the arithmetic is shown. World contained mine production is 3.71 million tonnes in 2024 and an estimated 3.90 million tonnes in 2025, with Indonesia at 2.31 and 2.60 million tonnes. INSG primary production, use and the stated surpluses (116 thousand tonnes in 2024 and 299 thousand tonnes in 2025) are dated and sourced. World stainless melt of 64.157 million tonnes in 2025, and EU melt of 5.659 million tonnes, are then set against EU nickel flat apparent consumption. For PRODCOM 24452200 in 2024, sold production 80.9 million kilograms plus imports 14.4 million kilograms minus exports 37.5 million kilograms equals 57.8 million kilograms. The German flat balance closes the same way (30.6 + 17.9 − 18.1 = 30.4). German HS 7506 exports of 17,775 tonnes sit next to the PRODCOM export leg of 18,087,152 kilograms, and the text assigns each source a job. Acerinox High Performance Alloys (83 thousand tonnes melted, 47 thousand tonnes finished, EUR 1,683 million) and the Outokumpu Avesta addition (about 13 thousand tonnes) are treated as supply ceilings. That is how a metal chapter should be written. Chromium and molybdenum continue the habit with a 2025 chromite figure, a 260,000 tonne molybdenum mine figure, a US contained-metal balance and an oxide price.

That standard covers one metal. Cobalt (164 words), titanium (142) and tungsten (150) are caveats. Zirconium, copper, aluminium and manganese describe stages and then stop. Five `TODO(data)` lines sit in the body of sections 4.2, 4.5, 4.6 and 4.7. A submitted project study does not contain working notes. The scope in Chapter 2 promises ten metals. Six of them do not yet answer production, availability or price.

The same break appears between chapters. Section 4.2 says no current global cobalt balance is claimed. Section 5.4 then states aviation cobalt use of 11,499 tonnes in 2025, a 73% share for the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and a nickel surplus for 2026 and 2027. Those facts belong in the metal chapter. Leaving them only in the industry chapter breaks the metal-first sequence.

Chapter 6 names the HS codes and the bilateral files, then repeats Table 10 from section 4.1 and declines to report any other flow. OECD ICIO intermediate use of all non-ferrous metals (USD 220 billion into electrical equipment and USD 109 billion into motor vehicles in 2022) is correctly caveated and does not measure a corridor. Sourcing dependence, which is the Trade dimension in Chapter 3, is not shown.

Spotlight facts are uneven. Automotive, oil and gas, aerospace and medical name buyers and, in places, a year and a product. Energy, chemical processing, and electronics name alloys and then say "stack makers", "fabricators" and "stampers". Those three sections also omit the numbered table the team's own sector rule requires. Prices, which Supply is supposed to cover, appear for molybdenum oxide and almost nowhere else.

Paid reports are not used as the sole source of a number. That restraint is right. It does not compensate for metals and corridors that were extracted and then left out of the text.

## Model transparency and reproducibility: 3.0

The formula is clear and the arithmetic reproduces. For an industry and a case, the 2035 index is 100 times (1 plus the annual rate) to the power 10, rounded to the nearest integer. The 21 cells in Table 11 match that rule, including the two worked examples in Appendix A2 (energy base 134.4 to 134, aerospace upside 155.3 to 155). Metal prices are held constant. No company total is printed, because shipment shares were not supplied. Appendix A3 warns that the medical device path uses a bio-health proxy and that cars with a plug can still contain engines. The refusal to publish an aggregate market value is the correct reading of the team's own methodology.

Transparency is not a market model. The rates are described as author-selected sensitivities. The reader cannot see why aerospace base growth is 3% a year, energy upside 6%, or automotive upside 0%. Section 7.2 is titled results by industry, product and region, and then says products and regions stay qualitative. The data chain's market-size step is empty for every application except the nickel mill balances, which are 2024 facts and are not run forward to 2035. Appendix A2 shows two cells, not the sheet that generates Table 11.

Two horizons sit side by side without a bridge. The scenario index runs ten years from 2025. Table 8 grows the medical device market from 2023 to 2035 at 5.3% a year, and implant counts on a 2013 to 2023 rate, while the medical row in Table 11 grows at 2% a year from 2025. Table 6 grows aviation cobalt with the fleet at 3.4% a year to about 16,100 tonnes, with no substitution haircut, in the same section that treats ceramic matrix composites as the long-term threat to superalloy demand. The automotive upside in Table 11 is flat, so the October 2026 committee vote on the 2035 car rule never enters the model.

## Storyline from data to strategy: 2.3

This is the strongest criterion after the nickel research, and it is what holds the official grade at 2.7. The answer is stated before the evidence is piled up. A mine total is not a plate order. Stainless melt is tens of millions of tonnes of steel. European nickel flat apparent consumption is about 58 thousand tonnes of mill product. Münvest lives in the last of those layers. Chapter 8 then ranks aerospace qualification, chemical replacement plate and welding products, and precision electrical strip and wire, and it holds energy and powders to funded orders. The conclusion uses the same ranking. Supply, demand and outlook do meet, for nickel and for the three priorities.

The chain fails where the text claims a sequence and then skips a step. Chapter 4 does not feed cobalt, titanium or tungsten into aerospace and medical. Chapter 6 does not change Chapter 8: the EU is a net exporter of nickel flat products and a net importer of nickel bar, rod and wire, and the recommendation never says whether the first sales push is bar into that deficit or flat product into a region that already exports. The eight assessment criteria named in Chapter 3 are not applied in Chapter 8. The text says it will not collapse them into one score. That is a reason to skip a weighted total. It is not a reason to skip the comparison.

The demand chapters also slip the rule the scope chapter states correctly. VDM Metals is an application benchmark, and VDM and Haynes are one Acerinox business. Sections 5.3, 5.5 and 5.6 say "benchmark". Sections 5.1, 5.2 and 5.7 say "the company sells" and cite VDM for the applications, the sour-gas approvals and the medical forms. Section 5.4 says the company sells nickel superalloy bar and cobalt alloy sheet, and the sources on that paragraph are Airbus, NASA and the US Geological Survey. A competitor catalogue and a set of industry statistics are not evidence of Münvest's orders. Until that attribution is repaired, the path from Chapter 5 to Chapter 8 rests on the wrong subject.

## Actionability and strategic insight: 2.7

The sector closings are more useful than Chapter 8. Glow plugs come off the growth list. Hydrogen spending waits for financed stack makers. Chemical premiums are reserved for duties where stainless or a lining fails. China implant bar is treated as a price problem after an 82% central-purchasing cut. Safran is named as the first European engine account while LEAP output is high. Buyers are named where the work was done: Garrett Motion, BorgWarner, Niterra, Eberspächer, Autoliv, SLB, TechnipFMC, BUTTING, GE Aerospace, Safran, Rolls-Royce, Stryker, Zimmer Biomet, Smith and Nephew, Johnson and Johnson. The 0 to 3, 3 to 12 and 12 to 24 month gates, and the instruction to use the company's own hurdle rate, are the right shape for a mill that has not opened its order book.

The initiatives are not sized, costed or owned beyond "the commercial lead", "the operations team" and "application engineers". No tonne, no qualification budget, no working-capital days, no account that is dropped. Competitive intensity is one of the eight criteria, and Chapter 8 does not mention Acerinox High Performance Alloys, ATI, Carpenter Technology, Alleima or Special Metals. Those names appear in Chapters 4 and 5, including ATI at about 68% of sales in aerospace and defence and Carpenter at 65%, with specialty-alloy operating margins far above standard products. Recommending nickel superalloy bar to Safran without saying who already holds the approval is a list of attractive markets. It is not a strategy. The first three months are an internal data collection project, which A4 already requires before investment. A recommendation chapter that sends the reader back to collect the data has not yet chosen.

## Writing and house style: 3.0

Chapters 1, 2, 3, 4.1, 6, 8 and 9 are linked, concrete and inside the intended sentence length. Sentence length was checked across `sections/*.md`. Almost every sentence clears the 12 word floor. The failure is elsewhere.

Sections 5.1 and 5.2 are a stack of one-sentence paragraphs. They do not open by taking up the previous sentence, and they do not hand the reader to the next one. Sections 5.3, 5.5 and 5.6 switch into the imperative ("Target", "Prioritise", "Require") while the other sectors speak about the company. The report reads as two drafts bound together.

House-style marks that a lint pass would catch: "Nickel-iron", "hot-section", "3D-printed", "corrosion-resistant", "High-volume" and "Cobalt-chromium" (alloy names and compounds the style sheet wants unhyphenated where the official name allows it). Table 7 contains a semicolon. The medical source line writes the range as "2013-2023". Citation practice is split. Chapters 4, 6, 7 and 9 use source-log markers. Chapter 5 uses author-date, often with "and" between several works inside one parenthesis. Sections 4.5 to 4.7 cite "USGS, n.d.-a" through "n.d.-e" around prose that states no figure. Table source lines are italic in some tables and roman in others. Estimates are labelled "our estimate", "our assessment" and "team calculation" in different chapters. The required label is one phrase, used every time.

`TODO(data)` in the body is a writing failure as well as a research failure. So is Table 1's source line, "Project framework", which is not a source.

## Visuals and design: 3.3

The graded text contains eleven tables and no figures. There is no message-title chart of the stage ladder (mine, primary, stainless melt, 58 thousand tonnes of flat product), no chart of the flat surplus against the bar deficit, and no fan of the three 2035 cases. Files under `report/fig/` are not referenced from `sections/*.md`. Tables 2, 6, 8 and 9 have an italic source line. Tables 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10 and 11 do not. Sections 5.3, 5.5 and 5.6 have no numbered table at all, so the seven sectors are not comparable on the page. The 30 page cap cannot be confirmed from markdown. At this word count the draft is probably inside it, because the missing metals and the missing corridors were not written. That is not a design success.

## What earns the grade it received

The stage separation in section 4.1, the closed apparent-consumption identity, and the decision to treat competitor melt as a ceiling. The decision not to invent an addressable-market total. Table 11, which a reader can rebuild from the stated formula. Named buyers and a few genuine stops in four sector closings. Appendix A4, which tells the client what the public record cannot decide.

## Ten changes, ranked by impact on the grade

### 1. Write sections 4.2 to 4.7 to the standard of section 4.1

Location: `sections/04_2_cobalt.md` through `sections/04_7_other_metals.md`.

Problem: Six of the ten metals in the scope have no mine or refined balance, no price with a named stage, and no producer structure. Five `TODO(data)` lines remain in the chapter. Cobalt facts that the aerospace section already uses are missing from section 4.2.

Fix: For each metal, give one dated mine or mineral figure, one refined or intermediate figure, one commercial form Münvest could ship, one price that names its stage, and the two or three producers that matter. Move the 11,499 tonne aviation cobalt figure, the 73% Congo share and the quota fact into section 4.2, and point section 5.4 back to them. Delete every `TODO(data)` line. Where a European mill balance cannot be logged, say so in one sentence and give the USGS or Comtrade figure that does exist. Do not paste the nickel identity again.

Grade impact: Research can move from 2.7 toward 2.0. That is about 0.2 on the overall grade, and it is the single largest lever because this criterion carries 25%.

### 2. Derive the 2035 rates and put a physical base under them

Location: `sections/07_scenarios.md` sections 7.1 and 7.2, and Appendix A2 in `sections/11_appendices.md`.

Problem: Table 11 is a reproducible sensitivity with unexplained rates. Product and region results are absent. The medical table, the aerospace cobalt path and the scenario index use different horizons, and the ceramic substitution risk never enters the aerospace downside.

Fix: Under each rate, write the driver, the year and the adjustment in one line. Examples that are already in the report: cars without a plug from 58.2 million to 42.9 million, fleet growth of 3.4% a year, committed low-emissions hydrogen just over 6 million tonnes against an announced pipeline of 27 million tonnes, implant growth of about 2.7% a year. Set the index on a tonne base wherever one exists, at least EU nickel flat products, EU nickel bar and aviation cobalt. Show all 21 unrounded values in A2. Build one automotive upside that keeps the hybrid path described in section 5.1, and one aerospace downside that applies a stated superalloy displacement. Use ten years from 2025 everywhere, or label the other window in the table title.

Grade impact: Model can move from 3.0 toward 2.0. That is about 0.2 on the overall grade.

### 3. Make Chapter 6 report the corridors, and let the bar deficit change Chapter 8

Location: `sections/06_trade.md`, then the first priority in `sections/08_recommendations.md`.

Problem: The trade chapter restates section 4.1. Partner structure, concentration and sourcing dependence are the chapter's job and are not in it. The most commercial trade fact, a flat-product surplus beside a bar deficit, never reaches the recommendation.

Fix: For HS 7505, 7506 and 7507, and for the cobalt, titanium, welding and aero-engine codes already tied to the three priorities, give the reporter, the year, the top partners and the net position from the clean extracts. Keep the pure-metal versus alloy caveat. Then say, in Chapter 8, whether the first European push is bar, rod and wire into the logged deficit or flat product into a market that already exports. One paragraph there is enough.

Grade impact: Research and storyline together, on the order of 0.1 to 0.15 overall, provided the numbers are taken from the logged extracts and the 57.8 million kilogram balance is not narrated a fourth time.

### 4. Separate the VDM benchmark from Münvest's orders

Location: `sections/05_1_automotive.md`, `sections/05_2_oil_and_gas.md`, `sections/05_4_aerospace.md`, `sections/05_7_medical_technology.md`. The rule is already stated in `sections/02_objective_and_scope.md`.

Problem: "The company sells" and "eleven of its alloys" are sourced to VDM Metals or, in aerospace, to Airbus, NASA and USGS. That treats a competitor catalogue, inside Acerinox, as the client's order book. Sections 5.3, 5.5 and 5.6 already use the right word, "benchmark".

Fix: Rewrite the application tables as benchmark applications a buyer can specify, with the VDM citation on the application and the materials. State in the same table, or in one column, that Münvest shipment, approval and share are unconfirmed. Keep "What the company should do" as a hypothesis to test against the A4 list. Count VDM and Haynes once wherever Acerinox is discussed.

Grade impact: This is a validity cap. Storyline stays at 2.3 until it is fixed, and actionability cannot move through 2.3 while the priorities describe the wrong firm's book. The wording change is short. The grade effect is large because it makes the later chapters believable.

### 5. Apply the eight criteria and name who already holds the account

Location: `sections/08_recommendations.md` section 8.1, using the criteria listed in `sections/03_framework_and_methodology.md`.

Problem: Chapter 8 ranks three priorities and does not test them. Acerinox High Performance Alloys, ATI, Carpenter, Alleima and Special Metals are absent from the chapter that is supposed to integrate competitive intensity and differentiation.

Fix: Add one comparison table for the three priorities plus the two "keep" positions (sour-service gas, profitable automotive) and the two "stop or do not fund" positions (engine-only parts, unfunded hydrogen and China implant bar). Rows are the eight criteria, scored high, medium or low in words, with one cited fact in each cell that claims a fact. The aerospace row has to say that ATI and Carpenter already take most of their sales from this industry and that Acerinox High Performance Alloys finished 47 thousand tonnes in 2025. End the section with an explicit stop list.

Grade impact: Actionability from 2.7 toward 2.0, and a further step on storyline. About 0.1 overall.

### 6. Give sections 5.3, 5.5 and 5.6 a numbered table and three spotlight facts

Location: `sections/05_3_energy_environment.md`, `sections/05_5_chemical_processing.md`, `sections/05_6_electronics_electrical.md`.

Problem: These sectors have no numbered table and no italic source line. They also miss the spotlight standard: who, what, where, when, which metal, which form, which supplier. Alloys are named (Nickel 201, Crofer 22, Alloy 59, C-276, 625, 29-18, 42) and buyers are categories.

Fix: Add the missing tables in the same shape as Tables 3, 5, 7 and 9, with an italic source line. For each sector, add three sentences that name a buyer or fabricator, a site or region, a year, a form and a metal. If the public record names only the alloy and not the account, say that the account is unconfirmed and still name the specification, the form and the competing grade.

Grade impact: About 0.05 to 0.1, mostly on research, and it removes a breach of the team's own sector rule. It also gives Chapter 8 something to rank besides a category label.

### 7. Size and own the 24 month roadmap

Location: `sections/08_recommendations.md` section 8.2.

Problem: The gates are timed and not sized. Ownership sits at the level of a function. Nothing is stopped on a date. The first gate only asks the company to go and find its own sales.

Fix: For each gate, name one owner, one input that already exists in this report, and one decision that does not wait for a full data room. Examples: in months 0 to 3, test the European bar deficit against the client's bar book, and take glow plugs off the growth list now. In months 3 to 12, cap the aerospace and Crofer qualification list at a stated number of accounts and a stated sample budget. In months 12 to 24, release finishing capacity only against a stated contribution per bottleneck hour under the Chapter 7 downside. Keep the company's hurdle rate. Do not invent one.

Grade impact: Actionability, on top of change 5. Together they are what separates a 2.7 strategy chapter from a 2.0 chapter.

### 8. Put the demand chapters in one voice and clear the style marks

Location: `sections/05_1_automotive.md`, `sections/05_2_oil_and_gas.md`, and the imperative paragraphs in sections 5.3, 5.5 and 5.6. Then a single pass on hyphens, the Table 7 semicolon, the "2013-2023" range, and citation form.

Problem: Two registers, one-sentence paragraphs in automotive and oil and gas, and a split between source-log markers and author-date. The sentence-length floor is largely met. Paragraph linkage is not.

Fix: Rewrite 5.1 and 5.2 so each paragraph takes up the previous claim and ends on the next one, in the voice already used in Chapters 3 and 8. Turn the imperatives in 5.3, 5.5 and 5.6 into "the company should". Write ranges as "2013 to 2023". Remove the semicolon in Table 7. Use one estimate label, "team estimate". Make every table source line italic. Point every new figure at a source-log marker so the reference list can be built in one system.

Grade impact: Writing from 3.0 toward 2.0, which is worth about 0.1 overall. Do this after the factual fixes, so the line edit does not have to be done twice.

### 10. Repair the four places where a table and a paragraph disagree

Location: Table 2 and the opening of `sections/05_1_automotive.md`, Table 4 in `sections/05_2_oil_and_gas.md`, Table 6 in `sections/05_4_aerospace.md`, Table 8 in `sections/05_7_medical_technology.md`.

Table 6 grows aviation cobalt in lockstep with the fleet while the prose flags ceramics. Make the source line list the works that support the cells, including the case names (Stated Policies, Current Policies) and the observation year. Footnote the cobalt path and the device-market path as team estimates with the proxy written in the footnote, not only in the appendix.
