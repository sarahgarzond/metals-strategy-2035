# Client CEO review, round 1

Reviewer role: CEO of Münvest (about EUR 400 million revenue, German producer of nickel alloys and specialty mill products).
Question used: Can the board decide on Monday what to invest in, where to sell, and what to stop?
Scope judged: `sections/*.md` against the central question in Chapter 2, the four dimensions in Chapter 3, and `data/toc.json`. The report itself was not edited.

## Verdict

**Overall grade: 2.7** (solid, and usable as a measurement paper, short of a capital paper).

I would leave a Monday meeting with three things I can use. The opportunity sits in qualified mill product, on a scale of tens of thousands of tonnes. World nickel mine output of 3.90 million tonnes and world stainless melt of 64.157 million tonnes are feedstock context. European apparent consumption of nickel flat products, 57.8 million kilograms in 2024, and of nickel bar, rod and wire, 5.4 million kilograms, is the physical layer this company can ship. I would also accept the proposed sequence: aerospace bar, sheet and repair wire first, chemical replacement plate and welding products second, precision electrical strip and wire third, with energy and powders kept as gated options.

I would not approve capital, headcount, or a change in the 2025 book from this text. Chapter 8 is a 24 month diagnostic. It has owners in outline and no euros, no tonnes of our own capacity, and no kill number. The seven industry closes each tell the commercial team to focus, keep, or grow. Medical technology tells us to focus on zirconium and cobalt implant bar and to approach Johnson and Johnson. That is a different company from the one Chapter 8 and the conclusion describe. Apparent consumption is calculated carefully for three nickel codes and is then left unsplit, so a director can still hear "58 thousand tonnes of demand" and set a plate target beside Acerinox and Outokumpu.

There is no opening the board can read in 15 minutes. The table of contents has no executive summary, and Chapter 1 teaches the measurement rule before it states the choice. The choice appears in Chapters 8 and 9, after seven sectors have each sounded like a priority.

## Apparent consumption versus stainless melt

This is the part I would keep even if Chapter 8 were rewritten.

The report holds five rungs apart and says which decisions each rung supports.

| Rung | Figure | What a director may use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nickel mine, contained metal | 3.90 million tonnes, 2025 | Ore availability. Indonesia is about two thirds. |
| Primary nickel | 3.875 million tonnes produced, 3.577 million tonnes used, surplus 299 thousand tonnes, 2025 | Class 1 feed and the surplus. A qualified alloy order is a thinner pool than this total. |
| Stainless melt, gross steel | 64.157 million tonnes world, 5.659 million tonnes in the EU, 2025 | The main first use of primary nickel and the home of ferrochromium. Feedstock and a scale check. |
| Named specialty capacity | Acerinox High Performance Alloys melted 83 thousand tonnes and finished 47 thousand tonnes in 2025 (VDM and Haynes counted once). Outokumpu describes about 13 thousand tonnes of added flat high nickel capacity at Avesta, against 1,751 thousand tonnes of stainless deliveries. | A supply ceiling. The text correctly refuses to turn the Acerinox melt into a share of EU apparent consumption, because the melt includes plants outside the Union. |
| EU mill apparent consumption, 2024 | Flat products (PRODCOM 24452200) 57.8 million kg, of which Germany 30.4 million kg. Bar, rod and wire (24452100) 5.4 million kg. | The only logged physical market for a European mill product. Identity is sold production plus imports minus exports. |

The comparisons I accept:

- EU stainless melt is on the order of a hundred times EU nickel flat apparent consumption. Chapter 4.3 says this is a gross tonne scale check, and that it neither converts melt into contained chromium nor sets a high performance market. That sentence should survive every later draft.
- The Union is a net exporter of the flat code (sold production 80.9 million kg, exports 37.5, imports 14.4) and a net importer of the bar code (sold production 2.5 million kg, imports about 5.6). Germany's flat tonnes nearly balance. Chapter 4.1 already draws the commercial line: a bar seller faces a European deficit, a flat seller faces a region that exports more than it imports.
- German HS 7506 exports of 17,775 tonnes sit close to the PRODCOM export leg of 18,087 tonnes. Using PRODCOM for the balance and Comtrade for corridors is the right split.
- Outokumpu's stainless deliveries and its 13 thousand tonne high nickel addition are shown together, so a stainless group total cannot be mistaken for the specialty book.
- Where a leg is missing, the text states no apparent consumption. Cobalt, titanium, tungsten, tube, and the copper and aluminium plate workings stay out of the prose. That discipline is why I trust the nickel figures.

Where a director can still misread the scale:

- 57.8 million kilograms is gross product weight of a code that mixes pure nickel and nickel alloy. Chapter 6 says so once. Chapter 8 then tells us to judge capacity against qualified orders "rather than against stainless volume" and cites the 58 thousand tonnes beside the melt figure. The sentence blocks the stainless mistake and leaves the next mistake open: treating the whole flat balance as qualified alloy demand, and as room under the Acerinox finish number.
- The 13 thousand tonne Avesta addition is described as below EU flat apparent consumption and therefore consistent with a multi supplier market. An addition of that size is a large slice of a 58 thousand tonne mixed balance that already exports. Crowding of the high nickel slice is the live question. The report softens it.
- Aerospace is sized with fleet growth and with 11,499 tonnes of aviation cobalt, because no official nickel use in aerospace is published. The caveat is honest. Table 6 still places that cobalt tonne next to the fleet, where it reads as the alloy market. It is a cobalt indicator.
- Oil and gas is sized with about USD 570 billion of upstream spending. Chemicals are sized with EU chemical sales of about EUR 635 billion. Medical technology leads with a device market of USD 529 billion and a bio health extrapolation that Appendix A3 itself tells the reader to treat as an illustration. Chapter 5.0 says these are activity, and the chapter openings often repeat the warning. The warning sits under the number. In a board pack the number wins.
- Tube apparent consumption is left open. Oil and gas tubes, and part of the chemical equipment case, therefore have no physical scale. The recommendation to sell more tube still stands in section 5.2.
- The scenario indices never touch these tonnes. Aerospace at 134 in the base case and 110 in the downside is a rate applied to an unstated 2025 book. A downside that still grows needs a reason once ceramic matrix composites and a missed approval are on the table.

Monday rule I will hold the team to: quote stainless melt only as feedstock context, quote apparent consumption only for a code whose three legs are logged, and quote a Münvest opportunity only after that balance is split into alloy versus pure metal and into the application we would actually ship.

## What I can decide, and what I cannot

I can decide the commercial sequence, as a hypothesis to test against our own 2025 shipments:

1. Qualified nickel alloy aerospace bar, sheet and repair wire, Europe and North America, Safran as the first European name.
2. Corrosion resistant plate and matching welding products for European replacement equipment, sold through fabricators.
3. Expansion, soft magnetic and resistance strip and wire, starting in Europe, with finishing capacity only after a repeat order and a margin.
4. Keep profitable oil and gas and automotive accounts. Take glow plugs off the growth list. Release energy and powder spending only against a financed customer programme.

I cannot decide capital. Chapter 8 tells the commercial lead and operations to map 2025 sales by grade, form, application, region, margin and approval in months 0 to 3, to run trials in months 3 to 12, and to add finishing, testing or inventory in months 12 to 24 only where qualified demand meets a constraint and clears our hurdle under the downside case. That is the correct order of work. It is the order I would have set without this report. The report should already contain the external half of that map: which tonnes in the 57.8 and 5.4 million kilogram balances are in play, who finishes them today, and which of our likely grades sit in a European deficit.

VDM Metals is handled correctly in Chapters 2, 4.7 and Appendix A4, and incorrectly in the industry chapters that a sales director will actually read. "The company sells", "eight of its eleven car applications", and "eleven of its alloys are approved for sour gas" treat a competitor portfolio inside Acerinox as our book and our approvals. I will not aim the sales force at a qualification list we may not hold.

## Five questions the board would ask that this report does not answer

1. Of the 57.8 million kilograms of EU nickel flat products and the 5.4 million kilograms of EU nickel bar, rod and wire, how many kilograms are nickel alloy rather than pure nickel, and how many sit in aerospace, chemical equipment, and electrical components? What is a defensible range for our share of each, in tonnes and in conversion revenue?
2. Europe already exports the flat code, Acerinox High Performance Alloys finished 47 thousand tonnes in 2025 on EUR 1,683 million of division sales, and Outokumpu is adding about 13 thousand tonnes of flat high nickel capacity. Where is the unfilled qualified demand, by grade and by corridor, that a EUR 400 million mill can win on delivery and approval rather than on price?
3. Which instruction binds the commercial team? Section 5.7 says focus on zirconium and cobalt implant bar, develop medical powders, and approach the separated Johnson and Johnson orthopaedics business. Section 5.2 says focus commercial effort on gas and offshore projects in the Americas and Africa. Chapter 8 ranks both behind the three core applications and withholds capacity until customer proof.
4. What do we stop selling or stop funding if the aerospace approval slips past the present parts shortage, and if the automotive book follows the base index of 90? Which grades, what share of 2025 revenue, and which plants feel it?
5. How much cash, bottleneck time and qualification cost may be spent before the first repeating aerospace order, given that the same paragraph says approval takes years and that we should use today's parts shortage to start? What single result kills a trial at month 12?

## What should stay

The refusal to publish one addressable market value is the correct call for this evidence, and it matches the team's own methodology. Counting VDM and Haynes once, inside Acerinox, is done in the metal, trade and appendix chapters and should be kept. The assignment of electrolysers to energy, process vessels to chemicals, and sensors to electronics is the right modelling rule and needs to be obeyed in the automotive close. Primary surplus, Indonesian mine concentration, Congo cobalt, ceramic composites, thinner linings, EU chemical share, and the China implant price cut are the right risks. They need a home in the stop list and in the downside case so they affect a number.

I can defend this report to the board as the way we will measure the market. I cannot yet defend it as the way we will spend.
