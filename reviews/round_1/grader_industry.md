# Industry grade, round 1

Grader: strategy VP, nickel alloys and specialty stainless (Acerinox test). Read only. Report text in `sections/*.md` judged against `data/toc.json`, the four dimension framework, and `reviews/research_audit.md`. Research files were used only to see whether a number in the report was already qualified in the working papers.

Weighted result: 2.3×0.25 + 2.7×0.20 + 3.0×0.20 + 3.0×0.15 + 2.3×0.10 + 3.0×0.10 = 2.70.

**Overall grade: 2.7.**

I would not forward this report to the board. I would keep the working papers. The discipline is real: stainless melt is not treated as alloy demand, VDM and Haynes are counted once, the German flat export leg is checked against customs, and nobody prints a fake euro market. The commercial conclusion then steps back over that discipline. A 2.7 is a solid research file with a join a melt shop director would send back.

## What an insider would actually use

The page I would put in front of the board has five rows and no other tonnes:

1. Stainless melt, gross steel. World 64.157 million tonnes and EU 5.659 million tonnes in 2025. Acerinox group melt shop output 1,866 thousand tonnes in 2025. Outokumpu deliveries 1,751 thousand tonnes in 2025. This is the stainless system.
2. Acerinox High Performance Alloys, VDM and Haynes as one division, 2025 output: 83 thousand tonnes melted, 47 thousand tonnes finished, sales EUR 1,683 million. That is about 4 percent of group melt tonnes and about 29 percent of group sales (EUR 5,781 million). Finished revenue is about EUR 36,000 per tonne. This is the peer for a qualified nickel alloy mill.
3. EU27 PRODCOM 24452200 quantity balance for 2024, shown only after a unit value check and after a split of HS 750610 (not alloyed) from HS 750620 (nickel alloys). Until that split, the 57.8 thousand tonnes is a customs balance, not a specialty market.
4. EU27 PRODCOM 24452100 bar, rod and wire, with the sold production figure marked as a rounded 2.5 million kilograms, and with UK Wiggin (Special Metals, Precision Castparts) kept outside EU27.
5. Aperam Alloys and Specialties, 61 thousand tonnes shipped in 2025 at a printed average selling price of EUR 17,475 per tonne. Different book from HPA. It belongs on the page so nobody thinks Acerinox HPA is the only European specialty mill.

Pricing on that page is net of metal. Nickel alloy plate is a conversion price plus a monthly alloy adjustment on the nickel, molybdenum, chromium, cobalt, tungsten and niobium in the grade, plus extras for width, testing, PMI and NDT. The Acerinox and Outokumpu stainless invoice, base plus alloy surcharge, is a different document. Qualification is named: AMS and the OEM approved supplier list for rotating parts, NADCAP for melt, heat treat and NDT, ASME and PED for chemical plate, NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 for sour service, RCC-M only where a nuclear job is actually in scope. VIM plus VAR for rotating grades, VIM plus ESR for corrosion plate, and a stainless AOD route for Crofer and for 5 to 12 percent nickel grades. Those routes are not interchangeable.

## What already meets the test

- Chapters 2, 3, 4 and 6 keep mine nickel, primary nickel, stainless melt and mill product balances on separate lines. EU stainless melt is about 100 times EU nickel flat apparent consumption (5.659 million tonnes against 57.8 thousand tonnes). That scale check is the right instinct.
- VDM and Haynes are one competitor. The report says so in Chapters 2, 4.1, 4.7, 6 and Appendix A4, and it does not add a standalone Haynes capacity to a standalone VDM capacity. Hold that rule in every sector list. Today aerospace and oil and gas drop the group that owns the benchmark portfolio.
- German HS 7506 exports of 17,775 tonnes and USD 578 million in 2024 sit close to the PRODCOM 24452200 export leg of 18,087 tonnes. The export quantity check is good. PRODCOM for the European balance and Comtrade for corridors is the right division of labour.
- No EUR 50 billion addressable market. Chapter 7 is an index with the formula written down. Appendix A2 can be recalculated.
- The chemical chapter names real grades (Nickel 200, Alloy 59, C-276, 625, 602 CA, zirconium), sends plate through fabricators, and uses the Acerinox 2025 comment that chemical processing was weak. That is the most credible sector.
- Carpenter medical sales of USD 224 million, down 24 percent, is the right kind of ceiling for an alloy mill. OEM hip and knee revenue is not.

The research audit is a pass on source counts for every material and sector note, with primary rows in double digits for nickel, cobalt, molybdenum, copper, aerospace and automotive. Open gaps are still large (20 on chromium, 22 on titanium, 24 on electronics, 19 on aerospace). The audit does not test whether a tonne in one basket was compared with a tonne in another. That is the failure mode of this draft, and it is not visible in the pass table.

Paid market reports are not carrying the nickel scale check. The stack is structured data plus filings. Depth is real. Judgment at the join is not.

## Errors, with corrections

### 1. Melt output is called a capacity ceiling

Sections 4.1 and 6 say supplier capacity is a ceiling, then cite Acerinox HPA melted tonnes and finished tonnes for 2025. Those are output figures in a year the same filings describe as weak in chemicals and sluggish in oil and gas. Output in a down year sits below nameplate. The VIM furnace, rotary forge and bar finishing aimed at 2028 are capacity that is not in the 2025 tonnes.

Correction: call 83 thousand tonnes and 47 thousand tonnes what they are, 2025 output. Ask for nameplate by plant and by route (VIM, VAR, ESR, hot mill, cold mill, wire). Put 2028 tonnes in a later column. Compare apparent consumption with finished shipments, not with melt. Finished HPA product is 47 thousand tonnes worldwide, all forms. EU flat apparent consumption is 58 thousand tonnes of one form in one region. The sentence "EU flat apparent consumption sits below that group melt" will be read as "one competitor can cover Europe." Finished shipments do not support that reading, and part of the melt is in Kokomo, Arcadia and Mountain Home, not in Werdohl, Altena or Unna.

Also confirm the melt definition. Stainless group melt of 1,866 thousand tonnes is one liquid steel count. If HPA "melt shop" adds a VIM heat and the VAR or ESR remelt of the same metal, 83 thousand tonnes double counts process steps. Until Acerinox's footnote is quoted, 83 is not a supply number.

### 2. The 58 thousand tonnes is not the specialty layer

Chapter 6 states the fact: neither PRODCOM 24452200 nor HS 7506 splits pure nickel from nickel alloy, and neither reports contained nickel. The next paragraph, and the conclusion, then call 57.8 million kilograms "specialty flat product" and place Münvest in "that last layer."

The team's own value columns fail a metal buyer's test. For 2024, EU sold production of 80.9 million kilograms is logged at EUR 1,171 million, about EUR 14,500 per tonne. German sold production of 30.6 million kilograms is logged at EUR 269 million, about EUR 8,800 per tonne. Import unit value on the same EU code is about EUR 47,800 per tonne and the export unit value is about EUR 31,000 per tonne. Apparent consumption value falls out at about EUR 12,000 per tonne. A nickel alloy mill does not sell plate at EUR 8,800 per tonne in a year when class 1 nickel itself is a five figure dollar price. HPA's own 2025 sales against 47 thousand finished tonnes are about EUR 36,000 per finished tonne. The quantity identity is reproducing something broader and cheaper than qualified alloy, or a production quantity whose value is suppressed. German production jumped from 15.9 thousand tonnes in 2019 to 36.5 thousand tonnes in 2020 while the logged value stayed near EUR 180 to 190 million. That is a series break, not a demand boom.

EU apparent consumption also counts domestic multi stage processing. PRODCOM imports and exports for the EU27 aggregate are extra EU trade. A melter who sells hot band to an EU re roller, who then sells cold strip, is inside sold production twice, and the intra EU sale is not subtracted. The 57.8 thousand tonnes is an upper bound on a mixed code, not end use of alloy plate.

Correction: publish the value next to the quantity in Table 10. Pull HS 750610 and HS 750620 for Germany and the EU before Chapter 8 uses the balance. Strip foil and coin and battery nickel if they dominate 750610. Say so in the conclusion. Until then, do not write that the opportunity sits in the 58 thousand tonnes.

### 3. Outokumpu's 13 thousand tonnes is the wrong basket, and it is not in the market yet

Sections 4.1, 4.3 and 6 set "about 13 thousand tonnes of flat high nickel alloy capacity" at Avesta beside the EU nickel flat balance and conclude that the addition "fits a market served by more than one mill." The working paper on Outokumpu, which the audit passed, places those tonnes on a stainless site (Avesta melt shop 500 thousand tonnes a year) in a two phase programme: remelt equipment early 2028, greenfield melt and casting only after a go or no go in the first half of 2027, production at the end of 2029. The same August 2026 presentation discusses high nickel alloys at 5.5 to 12 percent nickel. Duplex, 904L and 254 SMO are chapter 72 stainless. They compete with Acerinox Europa, NAS and Alleima. Alloy 825 can fall in chapter 75 and is a real overlap with VDM. The whole 13 thousand tonnes is not 13 thousand tonnes of 625 or C-276, and none of it belongs in a 2024 consumption comparison.

Correction: one sentence. Outokumpu is adding a specialty stainless pocket inside a 1.8 million tonne delivery system, timed for the late 2020s, overlapping a nickel alloy mill mainly where the grade is nickel predominant. It is evidence of competitive intent at the boundary, not evidence that EU chapter 75 demand has room for another 13 thousand tonnes of HPA plate.

### 4. Priority 1 is not inside the statistic used to justify it

Chapter 8 ranks qualified nickel alloy aerospace products first, corrosion resistant plate second, and magnetic, expansion and resistance strip third, then cites the 58 thousand tonne flat balance as the scale check. Aerospace wrought product for this industry is billet, bar, sheet and weld wire. EU bar, rod and wire apparent consumption is the 5.4 thousand tonne balance, and rotating billet is often declared as unwrought nickel under HS 7502, which the report correctly leaves out of the extract and then does not connect to the aerospace recommendation. Expansion alloys 29-18 and 42, Crofer 22, Aluchrom and much of the soft magnetic book are iron predominant. They are chapter 72. Konstantan is a copper nickel resistance alloy and usually chapter 74. Nickel 201 electrolyser strip is unalloyed nickel and does sit in HS 7506, at a different price and a different buyer from C-276 plate. One flat balance cannot size all three priorities.

The opening of Chapter 8 says dependence on declining engine only applications should be reduced, and the next section makes aerospace engines the first priority. The automotive chapter is the intended meaning (turbochargers, valves, plugs, exhaust). In this industry "engine" means the aero engine and the industrial gas turbine. A director will read a cut to the franchise the paper has just ranked first. Industrial gas turbines are also missing as a book. Haynes' current strength is aerospace and industrial turbines, while VDM is the one facing the weak chemical and oil and gas year. The report's own Acerinox notes say that. The recommendation chapter does not.

Correction: rewrite the ranking so each priority names the HS or PRODCOM code it actually ships in. Say "automotive engine and exhaust" when that is the book to harvest. Add industrial gas turbines beside aerospace, because that is where 718, 625, X and 230 are being pulled in 2026.

### 5. Aerospace product form and route

Section 5.4 says the company sells nickel superalloy bar and cobalt alloy sheet for blades, discs and combustors, and that parts shortages are the moment to start approval at Safran. Hot gas path blades are cast, single crystal or directionally solidified, from Howmet and PCC. A wrought mill does not sell blade bar into LEAP. Discs and rings are billet, then a forger. In Europe that forger is Aubert & Duval, and the qualified melters already on the approved supplier list include ATI, Carpenter, Special Metals and Aubert & Duval. Combustor and casing sheet is Haynes 188, 230, 625, X and similar. Haynes is Acerinox HPA, so the competitor and the benchmark portfolio are the same group. The sector list names ATI, Carpenter and Daido, and omits Aubert & Duval, Special Metals Wiggin, Howmet and Acerinox HPA.

GE Commercial Engines and Services at a 26.6 percent operating margin, with 76 percent of revenue from services, and Safran propulsion at 23 percent, are OEM margins on shop visits. The mill sees weld wire, some replacement hardware and the original billet. Carpenter at 27.7 percent and ATI forgings at 23.6 percent are real cycle margins. ATI standard alloy products at 16.3 percent is the closer comparison for a new source. NASA GRX-810 and a 1,000 times life claim are a laboratory alloy with its own atomisation route, not a powder volume for 2035. Ceramic matrix composites already fly on LEAP in the hottest static hardware. They do not retire the disc.

Approval for a rotating grade is AMS, NADCAP and an engine test, measured in years. A shortage in 2025 is a weld wire and a second source conversation for a grade the mill already makes. It is not an entry ticket to a LEAP disc.

Correction: split the offer into billet for forged discs through a named forger, sheet only where the mill is already approved, and weld wire for shop visits. Put MTU and Pratt & Whitney on the buyer list next to GE, Safran and Rolls-Royce. Move powders and GRX-810 to a watch item.

### 6. VDM's brochure is used as the company's qualification

Chapter 2 is correct: the VDM portfolio is an application benchmark, not evidence of Münvest's plants, customers or share. Sections 5.1, 5.2, 5.4 and 5.7 then say the company sells those products. Section 5.2 says eleven of its alloys are approved for sour gas and that this keeps many competitors out, citing VDM. A NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 listing names a producer and a route. It does not travel with a catalogue. Special Metals, Haynes, Alleima and the Japanese CRA mills are already inside that list. Sections 5.3 and 5.5 handle the same portfolio correctly by calling it a benchmark.

Correction: one rule in every sector. "The benchmark portfolio shows the application. Münvest can claim the approval only where its own mill, grade and form are on the specification."

### 7. The bar deficit is a rounded estimate

EU bar sold production for 2021 to 2024 in the extract is 6.0, 7.2, 3.5 and 2.5 million kilograms, round numbers. The 2024 figure is exactly 2,500,000 kilograms, and exports on the same row are 2.70 million kilograms. Exports above estimated production means stock, or an estimate that is not a census. Special Metals at Hereford is outside EU27 and is one of the main European sources of nickel alloy billet and bar. Planning "a product form deficit, therefore sell bar into Europe" on 5.4 thousand tonnes will not survive a meeting with the Altena or Hereford commercial team.

Correction: mark 24452100 production as confidential and rounded. Add UK supply. Add HS 7502 before any bar investment case.

## Gaps that keep this off the board agenda

- No split of the 83 and 47 thousand tonnes between VDM in Germany and Haynes in the United States, and no split by plate, cold strip, bar, wire, welding products and powder.
- No net of metal sales, conversion margin, yield or bottleneck hours. Chapter 7 already says shipment weights and net of metal weights answer different questions. Chapter 8 then cannot rank contribution per bottleneck hour because the number was never asked for in a form the model can use.
- No qualification calendar. Chemical fabricator trials can close inside a year. Sour service OCTG and rotating aerospace cannot. The months 3 to 12 trial language covers both as if they were the same process.
- Surcharge is one clause in section 8.2. The board will ask who carries nickel, molybdenum and cobalt between order and invoice, and whether scrap returns to the same mill.
- Cobalt, titanium, tungsten, zirconium, copper and aluminium have no logged European mill apparent consumption. The text is right to leave the number out. It is then thin. A board paper can live with a thin cobalt chapter. It cannot live with a thin titanium chapter beside an aerospace priority, because the same engine makers buy titanium mill product and the constraint there is sponge and approved melt, not the nickel flat balance.
- Chinese producers are absent. They matter for standard 625 plate, welding wire and stainless, and they matter less for a patented Haynes grade or a VAR disc billet. Say which contest is which.
- Medical oxidized zirconium is Smith & Nephew's OXINIUM route, not a generic zirconium bar the mill can offer to every implant maker. Carpenter's falling medical book is the better sizing tool, and the chapter already has it.
- Scenario rates in Chapter 7 are honest about being author choices. Aerospace at 3 percent a year in the base case follows the fleet, while LEAP deliveries rose 28 percent in 2025 and shop visits are the larger profit pool at the OEM. The index needs two lines, new engine hardware and shop visits, or it will be read as a forecast of alloy tonnes.

Framework and table of contents: Chapters 1 to 9 and appendices A1 to A4 match `data/toc.json`. The four dimensions keep their names, the metal first sequence holds, and the two Chapter 3 refinements are logged in `reviews/framework_changes.md`. No new dimension is required. The repair is inside sections 4.1, 6 and 8, and in the sector competitor paragraphs. The subsection title "Triangulation with trade, capacity and stainless melt" should be renamed to output, capacity and stainless melt once the words are fixed. That heading is below the fixed skeleton and can change.

## Top ten improvements, by impact on the grade

1. **Replace the triangulation paragraph with one basket table.** Location: sections 4.1, 4.3 and 6, and the first paragraph of the conclusion. Problem: stainless melt, HPA melt, HPA finish, Outokumpu's future 13 thousand tonnes and EU chapter 75 flat apparent consumption are read as one market. Fix: five rows, as in "What an insider would actually use," with Acerinox tonnes against Acerinox euros (about 4 percent of melt tonnes, about 29 percent of sales). Expected impact: research and storyline, about 0.4 on the overall grade. This is the difference between a paper I will discuss and a paper I will hand back.

2. **Put unit value beside every PRODCOM tonne and split HS 750610 from HS 750620.** Location: Table 10 and Appendix A1. Problem: German production at about EUR 8,800 per tonne cannot be qualified nickel alloy, and the strategy chapters still call the balance specialty. Fix: show quantity and euro per tonne, flag the 2020 German series break, and withhold the 58 thousand tonnes from Chapter 8 until the alloyed code is isolated. State the intra EU double count. Expected impact: about 0.3. Without this, every later recommendation sits on a number a buyer will reject in the first meeting.

3. **Tie each Chapter 8 priority to the code it ships in.** Location: sections 8.1, 7.2 and 9. Problem: aerospace bar, chemical plate and electrical iron nickel strip are sized on nickel flat apparent consumption. Fix: plate and alloyed strip against 750620, bar and billet against 7505 and 7502, electrical and Crofer against chapter 72, resistance copper nickel against chapter 74. Rewrite "declining engine only" as automotive engine and exhaust. Add industrial gas turbines. Expected impact: about 0.3 on storyline and actionability.

4. **Correct the aerospace route.** Location: section 5.4, Table 7 and the "what the company should do" line. Problem: wrought bar is offered for blades, the LEAP shortage is treated as an approval window, and OEM service margins are placed next to the mill. Fix: billet through Aubert & Duval or the incumbent forger, sheet only where the mill is already approved, weld wire into shop visits, powders as a watch item. Name Acerinox HPA once, plus Aubert & Duval, Special Metals, ATI, Carpenter and Howmet. Limit ceramic matrix composites to the hottest static parts. Expected impact: about 0.2. This is the section a technical director will read first.

5. **Keep one legal entity in every competitor list.** Location: sections 5.2, 5.4, 5.1, 5.5 and 4.7. Problem: the group rule is stated in the method chapters and then ignored where the buying decision sits. Oil and gas cites Nippon Steel and Sumitomo Metal 2015 and omits Haynes and VDM. Fix: one line, "Acerinox High Performance Alloys (VDM and Haynes)," on every list that includes 625, 718, C-276, 188 or 230. Split Europe and the United States inside that one line when the corridor differs. Do not add their tonnes together with anything already counted in the 83 and 47. Expected impact: about 0.2.

6. **Stop transferring VDM approvals to the company.** Location: sections 5.1, 5.2, 5.4 and 5.7. Problem: "eleven of its alloys are approved" treats a competitor brochure as Münvest's qualified manufacturing list. Fix: use the benchmark wording already used in sections 5.3 and 5.5. Sour service, AMS and ASME stay with the mill that poured the heat. Expected impact: about 0.2. A board will not sign a market entry that rests on another mill's stamp.

7. **Time the Outokumpu tonnes and park them in stainless.** Location: sections 4.1, 4.3 and 6. Problem: 13 thousand tonnes is written as current high nickel capacity below the EU flat balance. Fix: Avesta, stainless route, early 2028 for the first remelt step, overlap limited to nickel predominant grades such as 825. Expected impact: about 0.1, and it removes a false comfort that the European flat market has a measured gap.

8. **Write the price and the approval clock the way the invoice and the OEM actually work.** Location: section 8.2. Problem: "alloy surcharge clauses should be used where they fit" and a 3 to 12 month trial cover chemical plate and a rotating disc as if they were one sale. Fix: net of metal price, monthly alloy adjustment, extras, and two clocks. Fabricated chemical equipment can be trialed inside a year. Rotating parts and sour service OCTG need a multi year approved supplier path. Ask for contribution per bottleneck hour in the first 90 day sales map, in the company's own grade and form codes. Expected impact: about 0.2 on actionability.

9. **Mark the bar balance as an estimate and add the UK.** Location: sections 4.1 and 6, Table 10. Problem: a net import conclusion is drawn from a sold production figure of exactly 2.5 million kilograms, with exports higher than that production, and with Wiggin outside the customs territory. Fix: footnote the rounding, show a range, add HS 7502 and UK supply before anyone calls Europe short of bar. Expected impact: about 0.1, and it stops a bar mill proposal that the data cannot support.

10. **Split the aerospace index into new engines and shop visits, and leave GRX-810 out of the volume.** Location: Table 11 and section 5.4. Problem: a 3 percent base rate follows the fleet, while the metal is bought on deliveries and on shop visits, which are not the same series. Fix: two qualitative lines under the existing index, using the Safran, GE, MTU and Rolls-Royce figures already in the source log. Do not scale the Cobalt Institute's 11,499 tonnes of aviation cobalt with the parked fleet and then use it as the nickel indicator. Expected impact: about 0.1. The index is already honest. This makes it operational.

Doing items 1, 2 and 3, and fixing the aerospace route and the VDM approval leak, is what moves this from a 2.7 research file to a paper I would put on a board agenda. The rest is the difference between that agenda paper and a decision.
