## Can a producer decide on Monday?

- What to invest in: almost, but not with a cheque. The report says to qualify 718 billet with two forgers and remelt stock with one caster, and to add VAR, ESR, conversion and inspection after 1.5 kt is signed by Q4 2027. The board still cannot see what that step costs, when it produces approved tonnes, or what margin it earns, and the base case does not support the "one programme, priority 1" message.
- Where to sell: yes. The report names corridors (US and UK engine markets, about USD 111 m each), buyers (GE Aerospace and Safran forgers, turbine casting houses, Stryker and Zimmer Biomet, gas system makers for Applied Materials and Lam Research) and the competitors holding long term agreements.
- What to stop: yes. Diesel only parts (index 69), new European base chemical plants (75.0 percent utilisation), unfunded electrolyser capacity and captive zirconium cladding, with sour service at Monitor and chemical plate at Harvest.

## Five questions my board would ask that the report does not answer

1. What does the VAR, ESR, conversion and inspection step behind initiative 1 cost in EUR, and in which year does it ship its first approved tonne? If that is 2030 or later, are we still inside the window that 5.4.2 says narrows around 2030?
2. At peer margins of 14 to 25 percent, what operating profit do EUR 38 to 71 m of annual sales earn, and how many years does the capex take to pay back?
3. Table A5 puts the combined vacuum melt programme at 3.70, priority 2 at the base weights. Which is it, and should we fund 718 billet alone (priority 1 under all four weightings) first?
4. Is aerospace growing at 3.1 or 3.7 percent a year, and is energy growing at 2.8 or 3.6 percent? Which number drives the business case?
5. What is the likelihood of each risk in Table A15, and which measurable signal, with a threshold and a date, would make us stop the substitution and Asian capacity risks?
