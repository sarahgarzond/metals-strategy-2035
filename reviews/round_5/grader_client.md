## Can a producer decide on Monday?

- What to invest in: almost. The report clearly says to qualify triple melt 718 and 720Li billet with two forgers, qualify turbine remelt stock with a caster, and decide on melt capacity in Q2 2028 against 1.5 kt of signed agreements. What the board cannot see is the size of the cheque or the payback against the 193 kt market.
- Where to sell: yes. The report names engine corridors to the US and UK, turbine casting houses (CPP, PCC), implant makers in the US and Europe (Stryker, Zimmer Biomet), and East Asian mask makers as an option.
- What to stop: yes, the stop list is in chapter 2 and chapter 8. It is still unquantified (how much demand is at stake, for example combustion parts falling to index 69), and it is partly contradicted by the sector implications in 5.2 and 5.5.

## Five questions my board would ask that the report does not answer

1. What does a 3 kt VIM plus VAR step cost in EUR, and how many years pass between the Q2 2028 decision and the first approved tonne? If the answer is 2031, are we still inside the window that 5.4.2 says narrows around 2030?
2. What are the 193 kt of superalloy bar, billet and remelt stock and the 247 kt in 2035 worth in EUR, and what share would 1.5 kt or 3 kt be?
3. What tariff does a German nickel alloy bar actually pay at the US border in 2026 once the reciprocal tariff or the EU and US 15 percent ceiling applies, not only the HTS general duty?
4. Electronics grows fastest in your own Table 13. Why is it not a priority, and what alloy tonnage actually sits behind the 141?
5. Engine bar is priority 1 by 0.05 points and drops to priority 2 under either shock. If we treat engine billet and turbine remelt stock as one vacuum melt shop decision, is that combined position priority 1 under every weighting, and should we fund it as one programme?

## Smaller points

- In Table 15 the cobalt shock and the severe aerospace case each move engine bar to priority 2. Chapter 2 should say this in one clause, so that the board knows the priority is conditional.
- Table 18 initiative 3 combines CoCr and Alloy 690 under one gate, "two implant agreements", which does not test 690. Add a separate 690 gate, for example a named reactor programme order by a set date.
- The stop list would carry more weight with one number per item, for example combustion parts falling to index 69 and EU chemical utilisation at 75.0 percent.
- Aerospace P50 growth of 2.98 percent a year sits well below LEAP output growth of 28 percent in 2025. One sentence in 7.2 should say that the 2025 base already includes the ramp, so the board does not read the figure as pessimism.
