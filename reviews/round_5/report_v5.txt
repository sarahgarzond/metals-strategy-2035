1.     Introduction
For centuries, metals have been among the materials on which human societies are built, from
cities and transport networks to power grids, machinery and the technologies of modern
economies. Their uses have widened with each wave of industrial development, and the sector
remains large: according to McKinsey & Company's Global Materials Perspective, the metals
and mining industry generated about USD 3.0 trillion in revenue in 2024 after a decline of 6
percent and grew by only about 1 percent in 2025 (McKinsey & Company, 2025, 2026).
Within this broad market, high performance alloys serve the conditions that ordinary materials
cannot withstand, such as the hot section of a jet engine, a sour gas well, a chemical reactor or
a permanent implant. These engineered materials combine nickel, cobalt, chromium,
molybdenum, titanium, tungsten and other elements to provide strength at high temperature,
corrosion and oxidation resistance or biocompatibility, and they reach industry as qualified
bar, billet, sheet, strip, wire, tube and powder. Their raw materials, however, come from a
small number of countries, as Indonesia mines about 63 percent of the world's nickel, the
Democratic Republic of the Congo about 73 percent of its cobalt and China most of its
tungsten (International Energy Agency, 2026b; U.S. Geological Survey, 2026a, 2026e).

The outlook for these alloys to 2035 is positive but uneven, and its size is still poorly
measured. Commercial estimates place the segment between about USD 10 billion and USD
14 billion in 2025, a spread that reflects different market boundaries rather than different
markets (IMARC Group, 2025; The Insight Partners, 2025). Part of the future demand is
already visible in engine and turbine programmes, energy investment and the supply of
alloying metals that export policy increasingly shapes, while another part depends on
technologies such as hydrogen turbines, advanced reactors and additive manufacturing that
official statistics do not yet capture. This study therefore measures the first part from official
production, trade and industry data and treats the second as a set of emerging opportunities
whose evidence and timing are assessed openly rather than hidden in a single headline figure.

2.     Report Objective and Scope

This project follows selected metals that are essential to high performance alloys from the
mine to the industrial applications that depend on them, to assess how their markets may
develop through 2035. It asks one central question: how do the concentration of metal supply,
the conversion of metals into qualified product forms and the needs of seven industries
determine where the most attractive opportunities in high performance materials will lie
through 2035? Four subsidiary questions structure the answer, on supply and product forms,
on demand by industry and region and its measurable size, on growth to 2035 and its certainty,
and on where supply security, growth and entry barriers combine into the best opportunities.
                                                                                             4
The scope covers ten metals, namely nickel, cobalt, chromium, molybdenum, titanium,
tungsten, zirconium, copper, aluminium and manganese, and seven industries, namely
automotive, oil and gas, energy and environmental technology, aerospace, chemical
processing, electronics and electrical engineering, and medical technology. The analysis is
global, with regional comparisons for Europe, North America and Asia, and it uses the most
recent official data available in November 2026. The outlook separates a quantified estimate
for established applications from a structured assessment of emerging ones that official
statistics do not yet capture.

The report argues that value in this market is created by converting metals into qualified
products rather than by mining them. Nickel is in surplus, while cobalt, tungsten and
molybdenum are constrained by export policy, and the countries that convert metals into
qualified bar, strip and tube earn trade surpluses that the mining countries do not. The tier 1
model puts 2035 alloy demand at P50 indices of 141 in electronics and 136 in medical
technology, with aerospace at 134 and energy technology at 133 close behind, while
automotive reaches only 102 and oil and gas 98, with 2025 at 100 (Product Strategy 2035
Team, 2026a). Only triple melt engine bar and billet for aero engine forgers in the United
States and Europe reaches priority 1, and the rank is fragile because aerospace growth of 2.98
percent a year sits at the edge of a scoring band (Product Strategy 2035 Team, 2026a). Turbine
remelt stock, CoCr implant bar and Alloy 690 bar and hollows follow as priority 2, precision
FeNi foil for East Asian mask makers remains a gated option because no foil volume is
measured, and emerging applications stay in a register of options with signposts. Producers
should in turn stop investing in diesel only engine parts, European base chemical new build,
electrolyser capacity before funded orders and captive nuclear cladding zirconium. Chapter 3
explains how this argument is tested, chapters 4 to 7 follow the metals from supply through
industry demand and trade to growth, chapter 8 ranks the opportunities and chapter 9
concludes.

3.     Introduction to the Analytical Framework and Methodology

The analysis follows a metal value chain from material through product form, application,
industry and geography to market size and growth, and Table 1 shows how the chapters cover
its seven links. Figures are only compared within one stage, so contained metal is kept apart
from gross weight and output from capacity, and mine output, melt, mill products and revenue
are never added into one market value. The evidence starts with structured statistics from the
USGS, UN Comtrade, Eurostat PRODCOM, the World Bank, OECD, UNIDO and the IEA,
continues with industry associations, official outlooks and the 61 company profiles, and uses
commercial market reports only as a cross check. Figures that carry the argument are checked
against a second source where one exists, and single source figures are flagged in the source
register, which records the unit, stage, data type, source and formula of every value.

The outlook to 2035 is built in two tiers that are never mixed. The first tier is a demand model
for established applications, which multiplies the activity of each industry, such as engine
deliveries, gigawatts of gas turbines or joint replacements, by the metal content per unit and by
the share of units that use a high performance alloy. This model is anchored on measured 2025
values such as the cobalt used in aviation, back tested by fitting trends on data up to 2019 and
comparing them with outcomes for 2020 to 2025, and tested against earlier official outlooks
where they exist, while a Monte Carlo simulation yields the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles of
2035 demand and a supply overlay tests them against export quota and licence shocks.
The second tier covers opportunities that the data cannot yet confirm, such as hydrogen fuelled
turbines, supercritical carbon dioxide recuperators, molten salt and small modular reactors,
fusion magnet substrate tape or additively manufactured engine parts. Each candidate is drawn
from patents, pilot plants, standards under development or announced projects and assessed on
its technology readiness, evidence, required alloys and product forms and earliest date of
commercial volume. These options form a separate register with signposts, such as a final
investment decision or a first serial order, that would move them into the model, and chapter 8
scores both tiers on the same eight criteria while flagging the evidence behind each score, so
that measured positions are never confused with options to monitor rather than fund.

The scorecard sorts the metals into three groups, since nickel and chromium are available in
volume, cobalt, tungsten, molybdenum and aerospace grade titanium are scarce through policy
or qualification, and copper, aluminium and manganese matter mainly as complementary or
competing materials, so nickel, the base of the alloy family, comes first.

4.1    Nickel

Nickel is the foundation of the high performance alloy industry, and within a decade its supply
has moved to a single country. USGS estimates world mine output at 3.90 Mt of contained
nickel in 2025, with 2.60 Mt or about two thirds from Indonesia, while the IEA puts the total
at 4.21 Mt and Indonesia at 63 percent of mining and 45 percent of refining, so the series
differ in level but not in concentration (International Energy Agency, 2026b; U.S. Geological
Survey, 2026h). Primary production reached 3.875 Mt against usage of 3.577 Mt, yet just over
half of the primary supply in China and Indonesia was nickel pig iron for stainless steel rather
than the class 1 metal that vacuum melted alloys require (International Nickel Study Group,
2025, 2026). Class 1 metal itself is no longer scarce, as Chinese origin metal rose from 47 to
about 68 percent of LME open tonnage in the first nine months of 2025, so the constraint for
vacuum melting is qualified feed from approved sources such as Vale in Canada and Glencore
Nikkelverk in Norway (International Nickel Study Group, 2025; United Nations Statistics
Division, 2026a). Russian metal produced on or after 13 April 2024 may no longer enter the
United States or be warranted on metal exchanges under US and UK sanctions (U.S.
Department of the Treasury, 2024).
This oversupply explains the price, which averaged USD 25,834 per tonne in 2022 and USD
15,162 in 2025 in the World Bank series (World Bank, 2026a). Indonesia now manages output
through its ore quota, first cut to 260 to 270 Mt for 2026 and then raised to 290 to 300 Mt,
while the International Nickel Study Group cut its 2026 surplus forecast to 19 kt after 299 kt
in 2025, so the raw material price is now set in Jakarta as much as in London (Argus Media,
2026a; Indonesia Business Post, 2026; International Nickel Study Group, 2026).

Nickel reaches industry in two fundamentally different product forms, and the gap between
them reveals where value is created. Stainless steel absorbs most nickel, with 64.2 Mt of gross
melt shop output in 2025, and the IEA expects nickel demand outside energy technologies,
which covers stainless steel and alloys, to grow only from 2,906 kt in 2025 to 3,255 kt in 2035
(International Energy Agency, 2026b; worldstainless, 2026). Nickel alloys are sold as strip,
sheet, plate, bar, wire and tube, and German exports of nickel plate, sheet and strip reached
17,775 t worth USD 578 million in 2024, about USD 32,500 per tonne, a tonnage that Eurostat
confirms within 2 percent (Eurostat, 2026a; United Nations Statistics Division, 2026c).

That unit value is 1.9 times the 2024 nickel price, but a tonne of alloy is not a tonne of nickel.
Alloy C-276, with about 57 percent nickel, 16 percent molybdenum and 16 percent chromium,
holds metal worth about USD 19,000 per tonne at 2024 and 2025 prices before its tungsten, so
the unit value is about 1.7 times metal content in a team calculation (Haynes International,
2023; U.S. Geological Survey, 2026d, 2026g; World Bank, 2026a). Producers pass the metal
on through alloy surcharges or index clauses, as ATI does for most of its 70 million pounds of
nickel and Carpenter for all but the 43 percent of sales under firm prices, which it also reports
excluding surcharge (ATI, 2026a; Carpenter Technology, 2026). The margin therefore sits in
the base price, which Special Metals raised at Huntington eight times from April 2021 to
February 2024, by 5 to 20 percent each time (Special Metals Corporation, 2024). Buyers pay
for qualification and form rather than for nickel, a pattern that cobalt makes sharper still.


4.2    Cobalt
Cobalt is the scarcest of the major superalloy elements, and its scarcity is created by policy
rather than by geology. USGS estimates world mine output at 310 kt of contained cobalt in
2025, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo produced about 230 kt or 73 percent, while
China refined 78.6 percent of the world's 240 kt of refined cobalt (Cobalt Institute, 2026; U.S.
                                                                                              9
Geological Survey, 2026e). In February 2025 Congo banned exports and then imposed a quota
of up to 96,600 t a year for 2026 and 2027, CMOC, which mined 117,549 t in 2025, received
an export entitlement of only 31,200 t, and the European cobalt metal price rose 130 percent
during 2025 (CMOC Group, 2026; Cobalt Institute, 2026; Mining.com, 2026; U.S. Geological
Survey, 2026e). The IEA expects mine supply to fall to 225 kt by 2035 against demand of 300
to 322 kt, so the shortage is structural (International Energy Agency, 2026b).
The alloy industry is a small customer in this market, but it buys the most demanding product
form. Batteries for vehicles and portable electronics took 206 kt of cobalt in 2025 against only
20 kt for superalloys, which buy mostly high purity metal from a few Western refineries
(Cobalt Institute, 2026). Cobalt reaches its applications as superalloy remelt stock for turbine
blades and combustors, as cobalt chromium bar and powder for hip and knee implants, and as
wear resistant alloys. Because these applications cannot easily switch to another metal, a quota
decided in Kinshasa becomes a cost and continuity risk in Toulouse and Cincinnati, and
chromium shows a similar dependence one step later in the chain.

4.3    Chromium

Chromium gives every corrosion and heat resistant alloy its protective oxide layer, yet its
value chain is split between the countries that mine it and the country that smelts it. World
chromite ore output reached an estimated 51 Mt gross weight in 2025, of which South Africa
mined about 23 Mt on USGS figures and 25.2 Mt on the differently defined dashboard of the
International Chromium Development Association (International Chromium Development
Association, n.d.; U.S. Geological Survey, 2026d). China, with almost no chromite, smelts 9.9
Mt of ferrochrome on the same association's data, mostly for stainless steel, while the United
States imports 79 percent of its chromium needs (International Chromium Development
Association, n.d.; U.S. Geological Survey, 2026d; worldstainless, 2026).

The product form that high performance alloys require is far more specialised than the
ferrochrome that stainless mills consume. Vacuum melted nickel alloys use low carbon
ferrochrome and aluminothermic chromium metal, a small market whose U.S. price rose to an
estimated USD 5.90 per pound in 2025 while the ferrochrome price fell (U.S. Geological
Survey, 2026d). One maker of the purest grades is AMG Chrome in the United Kingdom,
which supplies low gas chromium metal for single crystal turbine blades, and losing one
qualified source of this input can halt a vacuum melt shop, a vulnerability that molybdenum
repeats in a more value intensive form (AMG Critical Materials N.V., n.d.).


4.4    Molybdenum
Molybdenum is what turns ordinary stainless steel into the alloys that survive hot acids and
seawater, and its intensity varies enormously between grades. One tonne of 316L stainless
                                                                                            10
contains about 21 kg of molybdenum, whereas one tonne of Alloy C-276 contains about 160
kg and Alloy 625 about 90 kg, so a small volume of nickel alloy carries a large molybdenum
bill (ATI, n.d.; Haynes International, 2023; Outokumpu, 2025).
Supply is tied to copper mining, which limits its response to price, and USGS estimates world
mine output at 260 kt of contained molybdenum in 2025, while the International Molybdenum
Association reports 672.6 million pounds, about 305 kt in a team conversion, and almost the
entire gap sits in China (International Molybdenum Association, 2026; U.S. Geological
Survey, 2026g). According to USGS only China and the United States produce from primary
molybdenum mines, and since February 2025 China has licensed exports of molybdenum
powders rather than of the oxide and ferromolybdenum that melt shops buy (Ministry of
Commerce of the People's Republic of China, 2025a; U.S. Geological Survey, 2026g). The
U.S. oxide price rose 7 percent to an estimated USD 51 per kg in 2025, while European use
fell 7 percent with the weak chemical cycle (International Molybdenum Association, 2026;
U.S. Geological Survey, 2026g). Molybdenum thus links corrosion alloys to copper mining
and Chinese policy, whereas titanium competes with nickel alloys more than it feeds them.


4.5    Titanium
Titanium replaces nickel alloys in lighter and cooler parts while sharing their aerospace
buyers, and its mineral and metal markets barely overlap, since world output of ilmenite and
rutile reached 9.8 Mt of TiO2 in 2025, mostly for pigment, while sponge metal reached only
370 kt (U.S. Geological Survey, 2026j, 2026k). China produced about 260 kt of that sponge
and holds 320 kt of the 470 kt of world capacity, but Chinese sponge is not the qualified
source for Western engines, and the United States has no operating sponge capacity (U.S.
Geological Survey, 2026j). Russia, whose VSMPO-AVISMA calls itself the leading supplier
of titanium for aviation, produced about 25 kt of sponge in 2025 and still shipped USD 715
million of titanium to the ten reporting countries in 2024 (U.S. Geological Survey, 2026j;
United Nations Statistics Division, 2026a; VSMPO-AVISMA Corporation, n.d.).
The scarce product form is aircraft grade sponge and the ingot, bar and plate made from it.
Osaka Titanium is raising aircraft grade capacity from 40 kt to 50 kt by fiscal 2027, Kobe
Steel sells titanium castings and forgings for aircraft and ATI quotes lead times of about 20
months for premium titanium, while Europe melts and fabricates titanium without producing
sponge, selling 22.6 kt of wrought titanium in 2024 against imports of 31.7 kt (ATI, 2026b;
Eurostat, 2024; Kobe Steel Ltd., 2026; Osaka Titanium Technologies, 2025). The same
shortage of qualified capacity constrains nickel superalloys, which strengthens every qualified
melter, and tungsten shows how quickly one government can reshape such a market.

4.6     Tungsten

Tungsten is the clearest example of a metal whose supply one state controls. World mine
output reached an estimated 85 kt of contained tungsten in 2025, of which China produced
about 67 kt, or 79 percent (U.S. Geological Survey, 2026l). China sets national mining quotas,
with a first batch of 58,000 t of concentrate for 2025, and since February 2025 it has required
export licences for ammonium paratungstate, tungsten oxides, unsintered carbide and specified
tungsten alloys, while sintered carbide tools and tungsten wire remain outside the control
(Ministry of Commerce of the People's Republic of China, 2025a, 2025b; Ministry of Natural
Resources, 2025).
Most tungsten ends up as carbide for cutting and mining tools, which takes 65 percent of first
use, while steels and superalloys take 14 percent (International Tungsten Industry Association,
n.d.). In superalloys tungsten strengthens cast turbine alloys and heat resistant sheet, so a
licence decision in Beijing soon reaches the gas turbine hot section, and Sandvik's powder
business more than doubled orders and revenues on higher tungsten prices in the third quarter
of 2026 (Sandvik, 2026). Tungsten thus closes the group of policy constrained metals before
the niche and competing metals of section 4.7.

4.7     Other Selected Metals

The remaining metals complete the picture in two ways, because zirconium and the minor
superalloy elements are niche inputs, while copper, aluminium and manganese are large
markets with a small high performance corner.


4.7.1   Zirconium
Zirconium shows why mineral output and specialty metal must be kept apart, since world
production of zirconium concentrates reached about 1.2 Mt gross weight in 2025, led by
Australia with 400 kt, mostly for ceramics, foundry sand and refractories (U.S. Geological
Survey, 2026m). The metal market is far smaller and divides into nuclear fuel cladding, which
fuel makers and integrated producers such as ATI control themselves, and zirconium plate and
tube for acid service, which is the open part of the market (ATI, 2026a).

4.7.2   Copper

Copper links the alloy market to electrification, although high performance products are a
small part of it. World mine production reached about 23.2 Mt in 2025 on ICSG and IEA
figures alike, flat rolled products take only 11 percent of first use against 63 percent for wire,
and the price, which averaged USD 9,947 per tonne in 2025, set records above USD 14,500 in
2026 (International Copper Study Group, 2025, 2026a; International Energy Agency, 2026b;
The Oregon Group, 2026; World Bank, 2026b). The IEA expects mine supply from current
                                                                                           12
projects to fall to 20.7 Mt by 2035 while demand rises from 27.8 Mt to 32.4 Mt under current
policies, and it sizes the primary deficit in 2035 at about 25 percent (International Energy
Agency, 2026a, 2026b). The specialised forms are copper nickel and high conductivity strip,
for example for the data centre busbars of section 5.6.


4.7.3   Aluminium
Aluminium matters here mainly as a competitor, because it replaces heavier alloys wherever
temperature and corrosion allow it. World primary production reached about 74 Mt in 2025, of
which China produced 61 percent, while European primary output has shrunk to about 955 kt
against demand of about 9 Mt a year (European Aluminium, n.d.; Federation of Aluminium
Consumers in Europe, 2024; International Aluminium Institute, 2026; U.S. Geological Survey,
2026c).

4.7.4   Manganese

Manganese is an alloying addition whose tonnage follows carbon steel, and world mine output
reached about 20 Mt of contained manganese in 2025, led by South Africa, Gabon, Ghana and
Australia, which together supplied about 81 percent, and the ore price fell to about USD 4.5
per metric ton unit, so it is neither a supply risk nor a growth market for alloy producers
(International Manganese Institute, 2026; U.S. Geological Survey, 2026f).

4.7.5   Additional Metals

Iron matters through FeNi alloys such as FeNi36 for display masks and FeNi42 for lead
frames, whose value is set by rolling capability rather than by iron supply. Niobium and
rhenium matter for superalloys, because Alloy 718 contains about 5 percent niobium, a metal
of which Brazil mines about 93 percent and which CBMM there sells as vacuum grade nickel
niobium, while single crystal turbine blades contain rhenium, of which only about 81 t were
mined worldwide in 2025 (CBMM, n.d.; RTX, 2026; U.S. Geological Survey, 2026b, 2026i).
The analysis now turns to the industries that convert these materials into demand for alloys.

5.      Industry Needs: Demand, Geography and Market Size

Metals create value only when an industry needs them, so the chain now moves from material
to demand, and each of the seven industries follows the same links from size and regions to
applications and product forms. Because industry revenue is not alloy demand, each first
subsection closes with the best measured size at a named stage, and industries without
published alloy tonnage enter chapter 7 as team indices, with indicator tables in appendix A5.
To avoid double counting, electrolysers and fuel cells belong to energy, process vessels to
chemicals, and sensors and magnetic parts to electronics.

5.1     Automotive
Automotive is the clearest case of high performance alloys losing ground, because
electrification removes the turbochargers, exhaust sensors, glow plugs and gaskets that have
long consumed heat resistant nickel alloys.

5.1.1   Demand, Geography and Market Size

Vehicle output still grows but shifts toward China and electric cars, as the world built 96.4
million motor vehicles in 2025, 34.5 million of them in China, while EU car registrations of
10.8 million stayed 17 percent below their 2019 level (European Automobile Manufacturers'
Association, 2026a; International Organization of Motor Vehicle Manufacturers, 2026).
Electric cars exceeded 20 million sales in 2025, a quarter of the market, and the IEA expects
about half of global car sales and more than 90 percent in China and the EU to be electric by
2035 (International Energy Agency, 2026c). The battery offers little compensation, since
lithium iron phosphate cathodes, which contain no nickel, took over 55 percent of electric
vehicle batteries in 2025 (International Energy Agency, 2026c).
European policy may soften the shift, since in October 2026 two European Parliament

5.2       Oil and Gas
Oil and gas is a stable market for high performance metals, in which investment moves from
oil to gas and sour gas, deep water and LNG need alloys that ordinary steel cannot replace.


5.2.1     Demand, Geography and Market Size
Upstream investment holds its level, but much of it only defends existing output. World
upstream oil and gas investment fell from USD 625 billion in 2019 to USD 542 billion in 2025
and about USD 546 billion in 2026, and about 40 percent of it only slows the decline of
existing fields (International Energy Agency, 2025b, 2026h, 2026j). Behind this flat total,
North America remains the largest region at USD 165 billion in 2026 but has fallen from USD
219 billion in 2019, while the Middle East spends above its 2019 level, at USD 93 billion
against USD 75 billion, and its gas investment roughly doubled from USD 22.9 billion to USD
45.4 billion (International Energy Agency, 2026j). Central and South America grew fastest,
from USD 42 billion to USD 68 billion, while LNG trade could grow from 560 bcm in 2024 to
880 bcm in 2035 under current policies (International Energy Agency, 2025c, 2026j).
Upstream investment is thus the best measured stage, since no public source counts the tonnes
of nickel alloy, duplex or clad pipe that the industry buys.


5.2.2     Applications and Opportunities
The real barrier to entry in this market is customer approval rather than metallurgy. ISO 15156
defines which corrosion resistant alloys in which metallurgical condition suit sour service, so a
listed grade is a commodity, whereas each producer and product form must pass NORSOK M-
650 qualification and operator approval (International Organization for Standardization, 2020;
Standards Norway, 2011). Orders then flow through the approved vendor lists of EPC
contractors and subsea majors such as SLB and TechnipFMC, which took USD 10.1 billion of
                                                                                                15
subsea orders in 2025 (SLB Limited, 2026; TechnipFMC, 2026). Product forms reach from
nickel alloy bar for wellheads and completion tools and corrosion resistant well tubing to
umbilical tube, in which Alleima's hyper duplex SAF 3007 succeeds super duplex SAF 2507,
and clad pipe, for which BUTTING reports turnover of EUR 880 million (Alleima, n.d.;
BUTTING, 2026). LNG carriers add a FeNi market, as Aperam doubled capacity at Imphy in
France in the first half of 2025 for the shipyards that build them, whose membrane tanks use
its Invar M93 cryogenic alloy (Aperam, 2025, n.d.). Outokumpu likewise adds about 13,000 t
of high nickel flat capacity at Avesta from 2028, a sign of the value of approved positions in a
flat market (Outokumpu, 2026c).

5.3       Energy and Environmental Technology

Energy technology holds both the most urgent shortage and the most visible disappointment in
the market, because gas turbines cannot be built fast enough while hydrogen has so far
produced more announcements than orders.

5.3.1     Demand, Geography and Market Size
Gas turbines are the tightest pocket, because data centres and grid stability created more orders
than the industry can build. Siemens Energy is sold out until fiscal 2028 and will raise its gas
turbine capacity from about 17 GW in fiscal 2024 to above 30 GW a year (Siemens Energy,
2025). Investment in gas fired power almost doubled from USD 64 billion in 2019 to USD 112
billion in 2025, and data centres could use about 945 TWh of electricity in 2030, more than
twice their 2024 level (International Energy Agency, 2025a, 2026j).

Hydrogen shows the opposite picture, as low emissions production was only about 1 Mt of
more than 100 Mt of demand in 2025 and the announced 2030 pipeline shrank to 27 Mt
(International Energy Agency, 2026d, 2026e). The IEA project database counts 4.3 GW of
electrolysis in operation, 19.3 GW at final investment decision or under construction, and
alkaline electrolysers, the nickel route, make up 60 percent of the capacity being built

5.4       Aerospace
Aerospace is the growth engine of high performance metals, because every jet engine hot
section is made of nickel and cobalt superalloys that the supply chain struggles to melt and
forge fast enough.

5.4.1     Demand, Geography and Market Size
Fleet growth sets the long term path, as Airbus expects the passenger fleet to grow from
23,310 aircraft in 2025 to 45,550 in 2045, which requires 42,060 new aircraft, while Boeing
expects 43,625 deliveries on a wider definition (Airbus, 2026b; Boeing, 2026b). Engines grow
faster than aircraft, as CFM delivered 1,802 LEAP engines in 2025, up 28 percent (GE
Aerospace, 2026a; Safran, 2026a). The aftermarket adds profit, since aviation services grow
from USD 209 billion in 2026 to USD 422 billion in 2045 and materials drive over 85 percent
of off wing maintenance cost (Airbus, 2026c).
Defence adds a third stream, with military expenditure of USD 2,887 billion in 2025 and GE
delivering 635 defence engines against 490 a year earlier (GE Aerospace, 2026a; Stockholm
International Peace Research Institute, 2026). The best measured size at the metal stage is
cobalt, as the Cobalt Institute counts 11,499 t of cobalt in aviation in 2025 within 20 kt used in
superalloys, and the U.S. Geological Survey reports that 51 percent of United States cobalt
consumption goes into superalloys, mainly for aircraft engines, while nickel alloy tonnage is
not published (Cobalt Institute, 2026; U.S. Geological Survey, 2026e).

5.4.2     Applications and Opportunities

Rotating parts set the highest barrier among the applications in Table 6. Disc and shaft billet is
premium melted by vacuum induction melting followed by electroslag and vacuum arc
remelting, as at ATI in Lockport and Special Metals in Huntington, and the most demanding

Whitney's geared turbofan exposed (ATI, 2026a; RTX, 2026; Special Metals Corporation,
n.d.). Each approved melt route is frozen in the engine maker's own specification on top of
AMS standards and NADCAP processes, and GE Aerospace states that qualifying a new
supplier takes substantial time, so a first rotating approval takes several years (GE Aerospace,

Chemical processing is the traditional heartland of corrosion resistant alloys, but in Europe it
has become a maintenance market while new plants are built in Asia.

5.5.1     Demand, Geography and Market Size
Europe's chemical industry is shrinking in volume and running with spare capacity. EU27
chemical sales were about EUR 635 billion in 2024, but Europe's share of world chemical
sales fell from 21 percent in 2009 to 13 percent, while China's rose from 24 to 46 percent

5.6       Electronics and Electrical Engineering
Electronics has the largest headline numbers and the smallest metal volumes, because

The semiconductor boom is real but must be read carefully before it is translated into metal.

5.7       Medical Technology
Medical technology offers the steadiest growth of all seven industries, because ageing
populations rather than business cycles drive the hip and knee replacements made from cobalt
chromium, titanium and zirconium alloys.

5.7.1     Demand, Geography and Market Size

Joint replacement is the most measurable driver of implant metal demand, as in 2023 the
OECD average was 198 hip and 156 knee replacements per 100,000 people, after a decade of
rising rates (OECD, 2025). Europe's medical technology market reached about EUR 173
billion in 2025, while the United States accounted for 46.4 percent of global device sales in
2024 (MedTech Europe, 2025, 2026). China is large but unprofitable for implant metals, since
central purchasing cut the prices of artificial hips and knees by about 82 percent (National
Healthcare Security Administration, 2022). The best measured size is at the procedure stage,
about 2.75 million hip and knee replacements in 2023 in the 35 OECD members reporting
both, excluding the United States and Japan, while implant alloy tonnage is not published
(OECD, 2025).

5.7.2     Applications and Opportunities
The buyers of implant metals are few and powerful, which makes approval and reliability
more important than price. Stryker reported orthopaedic revenue of USD 9,469 million in
2025, and the four leading orthopaedic groups exceed USD 20 billion of implant revenue
(Johnson & Johnson, 2025; Smith & Nephew, 2026; Stryker, 2026). The mill side is volatile,
as bar supplier Carpenter saw medical sales excluding surcharge fall 24 percent to USD 224.3
million in fiscal 2026 on customer destocking (Carpenter Technology, 2026).

6.3     Corridors and Dependencies
The geography of trade mirrors the geography of the industries that buy. German nickel bar
and wire go mainly to the United Kingdom and the United States, each with about USD 111 to
112 million in 2024, the engine corridors of section 5.4, while plate and strip go mainly to
Austria, Italy, China and the United States, where chemical and electronics fabricators work
(United Nations Statistics Division, 2026b, 2026c). Engine trade confirms the picture, with
British exports of turbojets and gas turbines at USD 39.8 bn in 2024, re exports included, and
trade policy now reshapes these corridors (United Nations Statistics Division, 2026e).

6.4     Trade Policy Reshapes Sourcing and Access
Trade policy now changes costs and market access faster than demand does, as the United
States raised its Section 232 tariffs on steel and aluminium to 50 percent in 2025 and taxed the
copper content of semifinished copper products at 50 percent from 1 August 2025, whereas its
tariff schedule lists general duties of only 2 to 3 percent for the nickel bars, plate, strip and
tubes of HS 7505 to 7507 (The White House, 2025; United States International Trade
Commission, 2026). The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, definitive since 1
January 2026, covers ferrochrome and ferronickel but not the nickel products of HS chapter
75, so it raises input costs without protecting nickel alloy mill products (Ernst & Young,
2025). The EU Critical Raw Materials Act lists nickel, cobalt and tungsten as strategic or
critical, on top of the export licences and quotas of chapter 4 (European Parliament and
Council, 2024). These measures reward producers that convert close to their customers and
secure several sources of each critical input, the setting in which chapter 7 projects demand to
2035.

9.        Conclusion
The central question was how supply, conversion and industry needs shape opportunities in
high performance materials through 2035, and the answer is that value lies in conversion and
qualification rather than in metal volume. Converting countries earn the trade surplus, and
because superalloys need only about 12 percent of 2035 cobalt mine supply, export controls
raise cost rather than cap volume (Product Strategy 2035 Team, 2026a).
Demand grows fastest where alloys are qualified into equipment, with P50 indices of 141 in
electronics, 136 in medical technology, 134 in aerospace and 133 in energy against 102 in
automotive and 98 in oil and gas (Product Strategy 2035 Team, 2026a). Engine bar and billet
for the United States and Europe is therefore the only priority 1 position, turbine remelt stock,
CoCr implant bar and Alloy 690 follow as priority 2 and FeNi foil remains a gated option.

These answers have limits, as the results are indices rather than tonnes, the haircut rests on
three tests, energy follows one turbine maker and no FeNi foil volume is published, but they
suffice to tell producers to qualify with engine and turbine makers now and decide on melt
capacity in 2027 and 2028 against signed agreements.

Appendices: Data, Sources, Calculations, and Assumptions

A1     Definitions and the stage ladder
The report uses Mt for million tonnes, kt for thousand tonnes, t for metric tonnes, GW for
gigawatts and bcm for billion cubic metres, and all prices are in current US dollars or euros of
the year stated. The stage ladder separates five rungs that are never added together: mine
output of contained metal, refined metal or intermediate such as nickel pig iron or cobalt
hydroxide, alloy melt, mill product such as strip, sheet, plate, bar and wire, and finished
component. Contained metal is kept apart from gross weight, actual output from capacity, and
commodity benchmarks such as the World Bank Pink Sheet from the selling price of qualified
alloy products.

A2     Outlook model, backtest and opportunity scoring
The tier 1 model of chapter 7 is fully reproducible from the repository, and this appendix
documents its formulas, parameters, backtest, simulation settings and the scoring rules of
chapter 8. Every value is a team estimate unless a source is given, and running python3
model/outlook_2035.py from the repository root rewrites data/clean/model_tier1_results.csv,
model_backtest.csv,              model_calibration.csv,             model_supply_overlay.csv,
model_supply_shocks.csv,         model_family_tonnes.csv,        model_tier2_register.csv,
research/model/outlook_results.md and Figure 3 with identical numbers. The same run calls
python3 model/scoring.py, which can also be run alone and rewrites
data/clean/model_scores.csv from data/clean/model_scoring_inputs.csv, while the option --no-
log skips the update of the source register (Product Strategy 2035 Team, 2026a).

A2.1 Formulas and simulation settings

For each industry j with components k, such as new engines and aftermarket in aerospace, the
2035 index is I(j) = 100 × Σ w(k) × H(j) × A(k) × (1 + i(k))^10 × S(k) × exp(10 × σ(j) × ε).
Here A is the activity ratio 2035 to 2025, H the optimism haircut on drivers that are official
guidance (aerospace, energy, oil and gas, automotive, otherwise 1), i the annual trend of alloy
mass per unit, S the ratio of the high performance alloy share, w the component weight and σ
the backtest error carried into the range, with ε a standard normal draw. Since round 5 no trend
shift is applied, because the drivers are guidance and scenarios rather than trend extrapolations
and a trend error must not be added on top of them, so σ = κ × the root mean square growth
error of the backtest series of the industry, with κ = 0.5 and a cap of 3 percentage points, only
widens the range. A product family index is the weighted sum of the industry indices with the
weights of Table A1, and cobalt tonnes multiply the 2025 base by the index and by the cobalt
content ratio.
The simulation draws 10,000 values with the random seed 20261108 from triangular
distributions with the low, mode and high values of Table A1. Activity drivers are linked by a
Gaussian copula with a global cycle weight of 0.2 and an industry weight of 0.3, intensity and
share draws are independent, and component and product weights vary around their mode in a
Dirichlet distribution with concentration 40. All of these settings, κ and its cap are team
assumptions, because the backtest windows are short and the test period contains the
pandemic.

A2.4 Product family tonnes, supply shocks and severe aerospace case
The tonnes of Table 14 are team estimates for each family that are never summed. Superalloy
bar, billet and remelt stock divides the 20.0 kt of cobalt used in superalloys in 2025 by a virgin
cobalt content of 4, 8 or 15 percent and multiplies by the 75, 85 or 95 percent of melt that
leaves the mill as bar, billet or remelt stock, which gives 138, 193 and 279 kt (Cobalt Institute,
2026; Product Strategy 2035 Team, 2026a). Chinese reported output of 64.5 kt is a third of the
P50 and the fifth VIM furnace at ATI Monroe adds about 9 kt, or 5 percent, so the range is
plausible mainly in its upper half because the cobalt method understates revert rich melt
(Argus Media, 2026b; Zhiyan Consulting, 2026). Bar, rod and wire multiplies the 82.6 kt of
HS 7505 exports of eight reporters in 2024 by 1.0, 1.5 or 2.5, while flat products and tubes
divide EU27 apparent consumption of 57.8 kt and 9.2 kt by an EU share of world use of 12, 20
or 30 percent (Product Strategy 2035 Team, 2026a). The 2035 tonnes multiply each draw by
the family index, and FeNi foil is not estimable because no Invar foil tonnes, trade code or
mask area are published.
The FeNi family index weights three drivers, OLED fine metal mask foil (P50 105), EV
magnetic sensor and shielding parts (158) and FeNi42 lead frames and glass seals (130), with
the inputs of Table A1, and the electronics only variant without EV parts gives 89, 118 and 153
(Product Strategy 2035 Team, 2026a). Round 4 had weighted SEMI fab equipment, which
uses no Invar, and the EV sub index, which gave 157 and is replaced.
The shock arithmetic of Table 15 is team arithmetic on logged prices. At 8 percent virgin
cobalt a tonne of superalloy holds 176 lb of cobalt, worth USD 3,704 at USD 21 per lb, so a
25 percent rise from USD 16.77 adds about USD 746 per tonne, and CoCr with about 600 kg
of cobalt per tonne adds about USD 5,595 (Product Strategy 2035 Team, 2026a). A tonne of C-
276 holds 40 kg of tungsten, or 5.04 mtu of WO3, which cost USD 1,271 at the 2024
concentrate price of USD 252 per mtu and USD 1,917 at USD 380 in 2025, an increase of
USD 646, and ex China mine supply of 18 kt of tungsten means that 10 kt of C-276 use 2.2
percent of it. Nickel passes through the monthly surcharge, at about USD 10 per tonne of alloy
per percentage point of nickel content for each USD 1,000 on LME nickel, so margin is
exposed only on fixed price contracts and inventory (ATI, 2026a). The score changes follow
from lowering supply security by one point for the affected positions in model/scoring.py.

A3     Data sources, product codes and reproducibility
All structured data were pulled with the project's fetch script, which stores raw files, writes
clean tables and logs every query with its date and URL in the source register of more than
1,000 entries. UN Comtrade data cover the product codes HS 7502, 7504, 7505, 7506, 7507,
8105, 8109, 7219, 7220, 7223, 7409, 7606, 8311 and 8411 for ten reporting countries from
2019 to 2024, and Eurostat PRODCOM data cover fifteen nickel, cobalt, stainless, copper and
aluminium product codes for the EU and five producer countries. USGS Mineral Commodity
Summaries 2026, the World Bank Pink Sheet, OECD STAN and ICIO, UNIDO INDSTAT for
Germany and the filings of 61 companies complete the evidence base.
The value range of Table 10 in chapter 6 is a team estimate that multiplies EU apparent
consumption in tonnes by the lower and the higher of the extra EU export and import unit
values of 2024, because the Eurostat production values fall below the value of the contained
nickel. Flat products give 57.8 kt × EUR 31.0 per kg = EUR 1.79 bn and 57.8 kt × EUR 47.8
per kg = EUR 2.76 bn, and tubes give 9.2 kt × EUR 26.1 per kg = EUR 0.24 bn and 9.2 kt ×
EUR 41.1 per kg = EUR 0.38 bn, while bars are not valued because their production cell is
confidential (Eurostat, 2025c, 2026d). The unit values are trade value divided by trade
quantity in PRODCOM DS-059358, so the range reflects the mix of grades traded across the
EU border rather than a list price.

A4     Limitations and further research
The study has three limitations that further research on this market should address. Official
statistics stop at the mill product and mix pure nickel with nickel alloys, so the market for
specific alloys can only be approximated, and a producer level survey or customs data at the
eight digit level would sharpen these estimates. The tier 1 model rests on published activity
drivers, but its intensity, share and product family weights are team assumptions, nickel alloy
tonnes by industry are not published, the optimism haircut rests on three plan against outcome
tests, energy follows one turbine maker and FeNi foil has no measured volume, so the model
should be rerun when the IEA World Energy Outlook 2026, the next Airbus and Boeing
outlooks and new SEMI forecasts appear. Finally, the trade analysis covers ten large reporting
countries, so a world mirror data analysis that includes Russia, Sweden and Austria would
complete the picture of global corridors.
