## 5.7 Medical Technology

Medical technology is a steady market for the company, driven by ageing populations rather than by fast growth, and its value is concentrated in a few implant makers. The best fit is cobalt chromium and zirconium bar and powder for joint implants, followed by stainless wire for instruments and leads.

### 5.7.1 Procedures, Ageing and Prices

Joint replacement is the most directly measurable driver of implant metal demand. In 2023 the OECD average was 198 hip and 156 knee replacements per 100,000 people, and rates rose over the decade in nearly every OECD country, with Germany, Switzerland, Australia, Finland and Denmark among the highest [MED_OECD_HIP_RATE_2023]. Europe's medical technology market is estimated at about EUR 173 billion in 2025, with spending growth of 1.8 percent, and the United States accounts for 46.4 percent of global device sales [MED_MTE_EUR_2025, MED_MTE_EUR_2024].

Prices are under pressure in China, where central purchasing cut the prices of artificial hips and knees by about 82 percent, so the profitable volume for a European supplier remains in Europe and the United States [MED_NHSA_VBP_2022].

### 5.7.2 Key Applications and Opportunities for the Company

Implant makers buy cobalt chromium, titanium and zirconium in bar and increasingly in powder for additively manufactured implants. Zimmer Biomet names cobalt chrome among its purchased commodities, and the FDA approved an additively manufactured cobalt chromium talus implant in 2023, which confirms that the alloy remains accepted [CO_ZBH_COMMODITY_2025, MED_FDA_RESTOR3D_2023]. Smith & Nephew's oxidized zirconium contains virtually no nickel, cobalt or chromium and shows 94.1 percent survivorship at 20 years, which makes it both a substitution threat to cobalt chromium and a zirconium opportunity [CO_SNN_OXINIUM_2025].

The alloy market is small and cyclical for suppliers, as Carpenter's medical sales fell to USD 278.4 million in fiscal 2026 because customers reduced inventory [MED_CRS_MED_EXSUR_2026]. The buyers are concentrated, with Stryker, Zimmer Biomet, Smith & Nephew and the orthopaedics business that Johnson & Johnson plans to separate accounting for most of the market [MED_JNJ_ORTHO_2024].

What the company should do:

* Focus on CoCr and zirconium bar for implant makers, with Germany, Switzerland and Austria as home markets.
* Develop medical grade CoCr powder for additively manufactured implants under ISO 13485 quality management.
* Approach the separated Johnson & Johnson orthopaedics business early, because a new organisation reviews its supplier base.
