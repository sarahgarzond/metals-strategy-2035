# 4. Metal Markets: Materials, Product Forms and Applications

Every high performance alloy begins as raw materials that the alloy maker does not control, so the analysis starts with the metals. This chapter follows ten metals from material through product form to application, the first three links of the framework, and Table 2 condenses them into a scorecard that already shows the pattern: supply is concentrated in a few countries, while the qualified product forms are made elsewhere.

The scorecard sorts the ten metals into three groups that structure the rest of the report. Nickel and chromium are the backbone of every alloy and are available in volume, cobalt, tungsten, molybdenum and aerospace grade titanium are scarce because of policy or qualification, and copper, aluminium and manganese matter mainly as complementary or competing materials, so nickel, the base of the whole alloy family, comes first.
