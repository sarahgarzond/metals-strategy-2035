## 5.4 Aerospace

Aerospace is a major growth source for high-performance metals. Jet engine hot sections use nickel and cobalt superalloys, which suppliers cannot melt and forge fast enough.

### 5.4.1 Demand, Geography and Market Size

Airbus expects the passenger fleet to grow from 23,310 aircraft in 2025 to 45,550 in 2045, needing 42,060 new aircraft, and Boeing expects 43,625 deliveries on a broader definition [CO_AIRBUS_DELIV_2025, AERO_BOEING_CMO2026_FLEET_2035]. Engine output grows faster, and CFM delivered 1,802 LEAP engines in 2025, up 28 percent [CO_GE_CES_2025, AERO_LEAP_2025].

Maintenance and defence add further demand. Airbus projects aviation services to grow from USD 209 billion in 2026 to USD 422 billion in 2045, with materials above 85 percent of off-wing maintenance cost [AERO_AIRBUS_GMF2026_DELIVERIES]. With world military spending at USD 2,887 billion in 2025, GE delivered 635 defence engines, up from 490 [CO_GE_CES_2025, AERO_SIPRI_MILEX_2025].

Cobalt is the best measured indicator. The Cobalt Institute estimates that aviation used 11,499 t of the 20 kt of cobalt in superalloys in 2025, and the USGS reports that 51 percent of US cobalt goes into superalloys, mainly for engines. Nickel alloy tonnage is not published [AERO_CI_AVIATION_CO_2025, AERO_USGS_CO_SUPER_SHARE].

### 5.4.2 Applications and Opportunities

Rotating parts set the highest entry bar in Table 6. At ATI in Lockport and Special Metals in Huntington, disc and shaft billet passes through vacuum induction melting, electroslag remelting, and vacuum arc remelting. The most demanding discs use powder, whose quality Pratt and Whitney's geared turbofan issue put in focus [AERO_ATI_JETENG_2025, CO_RTX_POWDER_2025, CO_SMC_HUN_EAF]. Engine makers also specify approved melt routes, and GE Aerospace says first approval for a rotating part can take several years [CO_GE_CES_2025].

Table 6: High performance metal applications in aerospace, outlook to 2035

| Outlook | Applications | Materials and product forms |
|---|---|---|
| Grows strongly | Discs, shafts, casings and rings for LEAP, GE9X, Trent and military engines | Triple melt superalloy bar and billet, powder for discs, sheet |
| Grows | Engine repair and overhaul | Welding wire, repair alloys |
| Grows | Additively manufactured combustor and nozzle parts | CoCr and nickel superalloy powder |
| Stable | Combustor and wear parts, with nickel alloys replacing some cobalt grades | Cobalt alloy sheet and plate |

*Sources: team assessment [CO_AIRBUS_DELIV_2025, AERO_ATI_JETENG_2025, AERO_LEAP_2025].*

Producers answer shortages with supply agreements and new capacity. ATI earned USD 1,762.9 million from commercial jet engines in 2025 under long-term agreements with GE Aerospace, Safran, Rolls-Royce, and Pratt and Whitney, and reports forging lead times above two years [AERO_ATI_JETENG_2025, CO_ATI_LEAD_2026]. Safran committed EUR 150 million to a 30,000 t press at Gennevilliers for about 14,000 hot section parts a year from 2029 [AERO_SAFRAN_GENNEVILLIERS_2026].

Other melters are expanding their capacity too. Austria's voestalpine Böhler holds about EUR 1 billion of aerospace orders over five years, Japan's Proterial sells gas turbine superalloys, and Aperam bought Universal Stainless for EUR 415 million in 2025 [CO_APERAM_INVEST_2026, CO_PROTERIAL_SUPERALLOY, CO_VOEST_AERO_ORDERS_2025]. Cannon-Muskegon, part of PCC, melts nickel and cobalt casting alloys for investment casters in five vacuum furnaces [CO_CANNON_MUSKEGON_VM]. In China, Western Superconducting's superalloy unit raised first half 2025 revenue 83 percent to RMB 282 million, and a market report puts national output near 64.5 kt [CO_WST_SEGMENTS_2025, CO_BAOWU_CHINA_SUPERALLOY_2025]. A new qualified European supplier can still enter, but that window narrows as this capacity arrives around 2030.

Implications for suppliers:

* Start part level qualifications with engine makers and independent forgers now, because rotating approvals take several years.
* Build the repair wire and powder business alongside, because off wing maintenance grows by 3.7 percent a year [AERO_AIRBUS_GMF2026_DELIVERIES].
