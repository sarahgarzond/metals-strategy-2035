## 5.4 Aerospace

Aerospace is the company's strongest growth market, because engine production, aftermarket services and defence demand all rise while qualified superalloy capacity stays short. Nickel and cobalt superalloys for jet engines are the core products, and ceramic matrix composites in the hottest engine sections are the main long term substitution risk.

### 5.4.1 Fleet, Engines and Services to 2035

Airbus expects the passenger fleet to grow from 23,310 aircraft at the end of 2025 to 45,550 in 2045, which requires 42,060 new passenger aircraft, and Boeing expects 43,625 deliveries over the same period on a wider fleet definition [AERO_AIRBUS_GMF2026_FLEET_2025, AERO_AIRBUS_GMF2026_DELIVERIES, BOEING_CMO_2026]. Engine output grows faster than aircraft output, as CFM delivered 1,802 LEAP engines in 2025, up 28 percent, and Rolls-Royce still describes parts availability as constrained [AERO_LEAP_2025].

Services are the profit pool, since Airbus says that over 85 percent of off wing maintenance cost is driven by materials, and GE Aerospace took 75 percent of its commercial engine revenue from services in 2025 [AERO_AIRBUS_GSF2026_SERVICES_2045, CO_GE_CES_2025]. Qualified capacity remains concentrated in the United States, the United Kingdom, France and Germany, while deliveries move toward Asia, so the buyers of bar and billet remain Western engine makers and forgers.

### 5.4.2 Key Applications and Opportunities for the Company

The company sells nickel superalloy bar, sheet and welding wire for hot section parts such as discs, shafts, casings and combustors, and cobalt alloy sheet for combustors and wear parts. Its direct buyers are engine makers and their forgers rather than the airframers.

Customers are investing in captive capacity, which defines the window for a new supplier. Safran committed EUR 150 million to a 30,000 t press at Gennevilliers that will forge about 14,000 superalloy and titanium parts a year from 2029 [AERO_SAFRAN_GENNEVILLIERS_2026]. ATI earned USD 1,762.9 million from commercial jet engines in 2025 under long term agreements with GE Aerospace, Safran, Rolls-Royce and Pratt and Whitney, and quotes forging lead times of more than two years [AERO_ATI_JETENG_2025, CO_ATI_LEAD_2026]. Daido Steel is building a JPY 36 billion high alloy melting and forging base in Japan, so the window for European capacity is open now but will narrow by 2030 [CO_DAIDO_HIGHALLOY_2026].

What the company should do:

* Qualify superalloy bar and billet as an additional source for the Safran Gennevilliers press before its 2029 start.
* Use the current shortages to win long term agreements with MTU, Rolls-Royce and GE Aerospace forgers, because approval takes years.
* Grow welding wire and repair alloys for the maintenance market, which grows by 3.8 percent a year to 2045.
* Secure cobalt from more than one region and recover customer revert, because the Congolese quota limits alloy grade supply.
