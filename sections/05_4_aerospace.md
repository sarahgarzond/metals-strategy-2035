## 5.4 Aerospace

Aerospace is the engine of growth for high performance metals, in the literal sense, because the hot section of every jet engine is made of nickel and cobalt superalloys. Fleets are growing, engines are being built faster than ever, and the supply chain that melts and forges these alloys is struggling to keep pace.

### 5.4.1 Demand, Geography and Market Size

The long term demand path is set by fleet growth and fleet renewal. Airbus expects the passenger fleet to grow from 23,310 aircraft at the end of 2025 to 45,550 in 2045, which requires 42,060 new aircraft, and Boeing expects 43,625 deliveries on a wider fleet definition over the same period [AERO_AIRBUS_GMF2026_FLEET_2025, AERO_AIRBUS_GMF2026_DELIVERIES, BOEING_CMO_2026]. Engines grow faster than aircraft, as CFM delivered 1,802 LEAP engines in 2025, up 28 percent, while Rolls-Royce still describes parts availability as constrained [AERO_LEAP_2025, CO_RR_FY2025]. The aftermarket adds a second, even more profitable stream, since the aviation services market grows from USD 209 billion in 2026 to USD 422 billion in 2045, and Airbus states that over 85 percent of off wing maintenance cost is driven by materials [AERO_AIRBUS_GSF2026_SERVICES_2045].

Defence adds a third stream of demand and at the same time changes its geography. World military expenditure reached USD 2,887 billion in 2025, with Europe up 14 percent, GE delivered 635 defence engines against 490 a year earlier, and MTU earned EUR 614 million of military revenue [AERO_SIPRI_MILEX_2025, CO_GE_DEFENG_2025, AERO_MTU_MIL_2025]. Although aircraft deliveries shift toward Asia, qualified melting, forging and casting capacity remains concentrated in the United States, the United Kingdom, France and Germany, which is where the buyers of superalloy bar and billet sit.

### 5.4.2 Applications and Opportunities

The applications range from discs and shafts to combustors and repair, and each requires AMS specifications, NADCAP accredited processes and part level approval by the engine maker.

Table 6: High performance metal applications in aerospace, outlook to 2035

| Outlook | Applications | Materials and product forms |
|---|---|---|
| Grows strongly | Discs, shafts, casings and rings for LEAP, GE9X, Trent and military engines | Superalloy bar and billet, sheet |
| Grows | Engine repair and overhaul | Welding wire, repair alloys |
| Grows | Additively manufactured combustor and nozzle parts | Superalloy powder |
| Stable | Combustor and wear parts where nickel alloys replace cobalt | Cobalt alloy sheet and plate |

*Source: team assessment based on Airbus, Boeing, CFM and ATI evidence [AERO_AIRBUS_GMF2026_DELIVERIES, AERO_LEAP_2025, AERO_ATI_JETENG_2025].*

The shortage is visible in the behaviour of the market leaders, who are locking in supply and building captive capacity. ATI earned USD 1,762.9 million from commercial jet engines in 2025 under long term agreements with GE Aerospace, Safran, Rolls-Royce and Pratt and Whitney and quotes forging lead times of more than two years [AERO_ATI_JETENG_2025, CO_ATI_LEAD_2026]. Safran committed EUR 150 million to a 30,000 t press at Gennevilliers for about 14,000 hot section parts a year from 2029 and co owns the French melter Aubert & Duval with Airbus, while Daido Steel is building a JPY 36 billion high alloy base in Japan [AERO_SAFRAN_GENNEVILLIERS_2026, CO_AD_WEB_2025, CO_DAIDO_HIGHALLOY_2026]. The window for a new qualified European source is open today, but it will narrow as these investments come on stream around 2030.

Implications for suppliers:

* Start part level qualifications with engine makers and independent forgers now, because approvals take years.
* Build the repair wire and powder business alongside, because the maintenance market grows by 3.8 percent a year.
