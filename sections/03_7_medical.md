### Market size and growth to 2035

Medical technology is the smallest sector in the Client's taxonomy by metal value, yet it pays the highest price per kilogram and grows faster than the portfolio average. The team model puts demand for the Client's addressable high performance metals in medical devices at EUR 2.0bn in 2025, rising at 6.0% p.a. to about EUR 3.6bn in 2035, which is a team estimate built from volume and semifinished value per material family [MODEL_MED_2025]. Within this total, the cell for CoCr, MP35N type alloys and 316LVM in bar and fine wire is worth about EUR 1,100m and grows at 6.5% p.a. [MODEL_M1_2025]. The device layer above is far larger, because MedTech Europe sizes the European market at EUR 170bn in 2024, which is 26.4% of the world market and implies a global total of about EUR 644bn [MTE_EU_MEDTECH_2024, MTE_EU_SHARE_2024, TEAM_WORLD_MEDTECH_2024]. Orthopaedics, the largest metal consumer, reached USD 64.9bn in 2025 and should pass USD 78bn by 2029, which is about 4.7% p.a. [OW_ORTHO_2025, OW_ORTHO_2029]. Published revenues give a partial cross check on the metal layer, since Carpenter Technology sold USD 351.2m into medical in fiscal 2025 and ATI sold USD 139.4m in 2025 [CRS_MED_FY2025, ATI_MED_2025]. Private wire makers do not publish figures, so the EUR 2.0bn remains an estimate with medium confidence, and the drivers below explain why the metal layer should grow faster than orthopaedic devices.

### Drivers and risks

Because metal intensity is highest in joints and cardiovascular devices, demographics and new cardiac therapies drive most of the growth. The World Health Organization expects the number of people aged 60 and over to rise from 1.1bn in 2023 to about 2.1bn by 2050 [WHO_60PLUS_2050]. For United States Medicare patients, primary hip replacements are projected to rise by 176% and knee replacements by 139% between 2019 and 2040, while revision knees rise by 149% [SHICHMAN_THA_TKA_2040, SHICHMAN_REV_2040]. Cardiology adds momentum, since Edwards Lifesciences sold USD 1.16bn of transcatheter valves in the fourth quarter of 2025 alone and Medtronic grew pulsed field ablation by 145% in fiscal 2026 [EW_TAVR_Q4_2025, MDT_FY26].

These drivers face four risks that the Client must price into any medical push. Under the EU Medical Device Regulation, substances classified as carcinogenic or toxic for reproduction above 0.1% by weight require a specific justification, and cobalt has carried the Carc. 1B classification in devices since October 2021 [EU_MDR_CMR, OBELIS_COBALT_CMR]. China's national tender of 2021 cut hip and knee implant prices by about 82% on average [CN_VBP_JOINTS_2021]. The United States opened a Section 232 investigation into medical device imports in September 2025, which may favour local supply [US_S232_MED_2025]. Finally, Carpenter's medical sales fell 6% in fiscal 2025 as device makers cut inventories, which shows how volatile ordering can be [CRS_MED_FY2025]. Taken together, the risks argue for products where the Client's metallurgy is hard to replace, which leads to the technology question.

### Emerging technologies and materials required

The fastest growing device families use thin, strong and fatigue resistant metal in small cross sections, which matches the Client's wire and strip forms. Balloon expandable heart valves such as the Edwards SAPIEN 3 use a frame of MP35N cobalt chromium alloy, while self expanding valves and ablation baskets rely on nickel titanium [FDA_SAPIEN3_MP35N, FWM_NITINOL_VAR]. Printed porous titanium is spreading in spine cages and acetabular cups, as shown by Stryker's 156,000 square foot printing site in Cork [SYK_CORK_AM]. The core material set is therefore CoCrMo to ASTM F75 and F1537 for femoral heads and knee components, MP35N and L605 for frames, stents and pacing leads, 316LVM for trauma plates and wire, Zr2.5Nb for oxidised zirconium bearings, and CoCr and 316L powder for dental and orthopaedic printing. Each grade is specified by standard and frozen in the device approval, which turns qualification into the decisive barrier and shapes where demand sits.

### Geographic demand and customers

Since approvals and production sit with a small number of large device makers, demand clusters in a few regions. The United States is the largest market and Europe the second [MTE_EU_SHARE_2024], and at Zimmer Biomet about 55% of knee and hip sales come from the United States [TEAM_ZBH_US_SHARE]. Plants concentrate in Warsaw in Indiana, Minneapolis, Irvine, the Irish cluster around Cork and Galway, Tuttlingen and Switzerland, which is a team assessment from company site lists. The leading customers are Stryker with sales of USD 25.1bn, Medtronic with USD 36.4bn and Zimmer Biomet with USD 8.2bn [SYK_2025, MDT_FY26, ZBH_2025], together with Johnson and Johnson MedTech, Smith+Nephew, Edwards, Boston Scientific, Abbott and contract makers such as Integer and Tecomet. These buyers rarely buy mill products directly, so the competitor map runs through specialist converters.

### Competitor map and spotlight facts

The competitive field is split by product form, and each layer has a clear leader that the Client must either partner with or bypass. Carpenter Technology leads in CoCr and titanium bar, Fort Wayne Metals in fine wire and nitinol, Zapp and Alleima in European precision wire and strip, and Resonetics in nitinol tubing after buying the SAES medical business for USD 900m in 2023 [CRS_MED_FY2025, FWM_NITINOL_VAR, ALLEIMA_PENANG_2025, RESONETICS_SAES_2023]. Montagu bought the Johnson Matthey medical components unit for USD 700m in 2024, which confirms that investors value certified medical metal capacity [JM_MDC_2024]. ### Where high performance metals gain or lose relevance

The spotlights show that metal demand is moving rather than shrinking, with losses in bearings and gains in cardiovascular devices. CoCr femoral heads keep losing share to ceramics, and the UK registry already showed ceramic heads at 40% in 2016 [NJR_HEADS_2016]. Printed titanium replaces some wrought bar in spine and cups [SYK_CORK_AM], and the EU cobalt rules push new designs towards titanium and oxidised zirconium [EU_MDR_CMR]. Against this, MP35N and L605 gain in valves and stents, and CoCr knee components gain from rising revisions [FDA_SAPIEN3_MP35N, SHICHMAN_REV_2040]. Medical demand for the Client's zirconium in Zr2.5Nb bar is plausible, but volumes remain a TODO(data) because Smith+Nephew does not publish them, which leaves value chain position as the decisive choice.

### Most attractive value chain steps for the Client

Given this mix, the Client should concentrate on remelted CoCr and MP35N type bar, fine wire and thin strip delivered with full medical traceability. This step earns the highest value per kilogram in the portfolio, locks in customers through device approvals and benefits directly from ATI's retreat [MODEL_M1_2025, ATI_MED_2025]. A second step is tube hollows supplied to tube drawers such as Resonetics and Fort Wayne Metals, plus CoCr and 316L powder for dental and orthopaedic printing. Nitinol and titanium sit outside the Client's melt base and face consolidated incumbents, and commodity instrument bar and the Chinese joint market offer low margins, so the team ranks them lower.
