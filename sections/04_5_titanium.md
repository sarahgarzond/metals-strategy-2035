## 4.5 Titanium

Titanium replaces nickel alloys in lighter and cooler parts while sharing their aerospace buyers and qualification bottlenecks, and its mineral and metal markets barely overlap, since world output of ilmenite and rutile reached 9.8 Mt of TiO2 in 2025, mostly for pigment, while sponge metal reached only 370 kt [TI_CONC_WORLD_2025, TI_SPONGE_WORLD_2025]. China produced about 260 kt of that sponge and holds 320 kt of the 470 kt of world capacity, but Chinese sponge is not the qualified source for Western engines, and the United States has no operating sponge capacity [TI_SPONGE_CHINA_2025, TI_SPONGE_CAP_WORLD_2025, TI_US_NET_IMPORT_SPONGE_2025].

The scarce product form is aircraft grade sponge and the ingot, bar and plate made from it, as Osaka Titanium is raising aircraft grade capacity from 40 kt to 50 kt by fiscal 2027, ATI quotes lead times of about 20 months for premium titanium, and Europe melts and fabricates titanium without producing sponge, selling 22.6 kt of wrought titanium in 2024 against imports of 31.7 kt [TI_OSAKA_CAP_2025, CO_ATI_LEAD_2026, TI_PRODCOM_WROUGHT_EU_2024]. The same shortage of qualified capacity constrains nickel superalloys, which strengthens every qualified melter, and tungsten shows how quickly one government can reshape such a market.
