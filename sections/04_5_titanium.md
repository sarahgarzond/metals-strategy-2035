## 4.5 Titanium

Titanium matters to the company as a substitute for nickel alloys in lighter parts and as a complementary material that the same aerospace buyers qualify. Mineral concentrates and titanium metal are different markets, since world output of ilmenite and rutile was 9.8 Mt of TiO2 in 2025, mostly for pigment, while sponge metal reached only 370 kt [TI_CONC_WORLD_2025, TI_SPONGE_WORLD_2025].

Sponge supply is concentrated in China, which produced about 260 kt and holds 320 kt of the 470 kt of world capacity [TI_SPONGE_CHINA_2025, TI_SPONGE_CAP_WORLD_2025]. Chinese sponge is not the qualified aerospace source for Western engines, however, and the United States has no operating sponge capacity at all [TI_US_NET_IMPORT_SPONGE_2025]. Osaka Titanium is raising aircraft grade capacity from 40 kt to 50 kt by fiscal 2027, and ATI quotes lead times of about 20 months for premium titanium [TI_OSAKA_CAP_2025].

Europe melts and fabricates titanium without producing sponge, with EU sold production of wrought titanium of 22.6 kt in 2024 against imports of 31.7 kt [TI_PRODCOM_WROUGHT_EU_2024]. The lesson for the company is that aerospace buyers face the same qualified capacity shortage in titanium as in nickel superalloys, which strengthens the bargaining position of every qualified melter. Tungsten, the next metal, shows how export controls can reshape a market within one year.
