## 4.5 Titanium

Titanium is the metal that cannot stay a caveat beside an aerospace priority, because the same engine makers buy titanium mill product and the constraint is sponge and approved melt rather than the nickel flat balance. World sponge production is estimated at 370,000 tonnes in 2025, of which China produced an estimated 260,000 tonnes, and the United States has no operating sponge capacity in that year [TI_SPONGE_WORLD_2025, TI_SPONGE_CHINA_2025, TI_SPONGE_CAP_WORLD_2025]. The price at the sponge stage is the estimated United States landed duty paid import unit value of USD 12 per kilogram in 2025 [TI_SPONGE_US_PRICE_2025]. The commercial form a mill ships is wrought bar, sheet or tube after melting, and EU sold production of wrought titanium was 22,617 tonnes in 2024 against unwrought sold production of only 1,110 tonnes, which is a mixed code and not a qualified aerospace balance [TI_PRODCOM_WROUGHT_EU_2024, TI_PRODCOM_UNWROUGHT_EU_2024].

No European mill apparent consumption is asserted as aerospace demand, because those PRODCOM codes still mix forms. German HS 8108 trade in 2024 was a net import, led by value from the Russian Federation and the United States, and that corridor is reported in Chapter 6 without being converted into a Münvest tonne [CT_DE_8108_2024]. Japan and China are the sponge volume centres, and a qualified melt route remains the gate for engine billet, which is why tungsten is taken next as a separate carbide chain rather than as another reading of this sponge balance.
