## 4.5 Titanium

Titanium replaces nickel alloys in lighter and cooler aerospace parts. In 2025, world ilmenite and rutile output reached 9.8 Mt as TiO2, mostly for pigment, but titanium sponge output was only 370 kt [TI_SPONGE_CAP_WORLD_2025, TI_CONC_WORLD_2025].

China produced about 260 kt of sponge and held 320 kt of the world's 470 kt of capacity. Western aircraft engines use other qualified sources, and the United States has no operating sponge plant [TI_SPONGE_CAP_WORLD_2025]. Russia produced about 25 kt of sponge in 2025 and shipped USD 715 million of titanium to the ten reporters in 2024 [TI_SPONGE_CAP_WORLD_2025, COMTRADE_MIRROR_EXTRA6_2024].

The scarce forms are aircraft-grade sponge and the ingot, bar, and plate made from it. Osaka Titanium is raising aircraft-grade capacity from 40 kt to 50 kt by fiscal 2027, and ATI reports lead times of about 20 months for premium titanium. Europe makes no sponge but sold 22.6 kt of wrought titanium in 2024 and imported 31.7 kt [CO_ATI_LEAD_2026, TI_PRODCOM_WROUGHT_EU_2024, TI_OSAKA_CAP_2025]. Nickel superalloys face the same shortage of qualified melting capacity.
