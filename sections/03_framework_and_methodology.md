# 3. Introduction to the Analytical Framework and Methodology

Because those boundaries are easy to cross, the report uses one metal centred value chain with four dimensions: Supply, Trade, Demand and Outlook. Supply is assessed metal by metal in Chapter 4 through commercial forms, production stages, producer structure, availability and prices. Trade is assessed in Chapter 6 through product classifications, exporting and importing markets, and sourcing dependence. Demand is examined industry by industry in Chapter 5, linking drivers to components, materials, delivery forms and buyers. Outlook is assessed in Chapter 7 through explicit scenarios to 2035, and Chapter 8 integrates the four dimensions for Münvest. The sequence is metal first and industry second, so the reader sees what the material is before judging who buys it.

The mapping keeps each figure in the step it measures, so a mine statistic belongs to Material, a PRODCOM plate balance belongs to Product form, and a component and its buyer belong to Application and Industry. A corridor belongs to Geography, and a 2035 sensitivity belongs to Market size and Growth, while prices stay inside each metal market because a commodity benchmark is not the selling price of a qualified alloy.

Apparent consumption is the mill product check inside Supply and Trade, and for one product code, one area and one year it equals sold production plus imports minus exports, with the same identity available in euro where all three value series exist [AC_METHOD]. The result records the trade balance of that code, while end use alloy demand and stainless melt tonnage remain separate series. Chapter 4 applies the identity to EU and German nickel flat products and to EU nickel bar, and Chapter 6 compares those results with Comtrade, with named mill capacity and with stainless melt. Where any leg is missing, no apparent consumption figure is stated in the text.

Sources stay at the stage they measure. Commodity definitions and mine statistics follow the United States Geological Survey Mineral Commodity Summaries, and company filings and industry sources support applications and competitive structure. Two figures are not treated as comparable merely because both name the same metal. Contained metal stays distinct from gross shipment weight, mine output from refined output, primary metal from scrap, actual output from capacity, and group output from an ownership share. Missing observations stay missing rather than being filled from a neighbouring series. Geographic concentration is calculated separately for extraction and for processing.

Chapter 8 reads the four dimensions against eight assessment criteria: market attractiveness, growth to 2035, strategic fit, competitive intensity, technology attractiveness, customer attractiveness, entry barriers and differentiation potential. The project model holds the weights, and this text does not collapse the criteria into one score, because shipment shares by application were not supplied. Coverage is deeper for nickel and cobalt alloys and for selected corrosion resistant materials than for every metal in the scope, and the later profiles mark that limit instead of filling it with an estimated total. The industry chapters then take the forms that survive this screen and ask who buys them.
