# 3. Introduction to the Analytical Framework and Methodology

Answering a question that spans ten metals and seven industries requires a framework that keeps different markets on one line of reasoning, and this study therefore follows a value chain that the team built specifically for it. The chain starts with the material and ends with growth, and every chapter of the report occupies one or more of its links: Material, Product form and Application in chapter 4, Industry, Geography and Market size in chapters 5 and 6, and Growth in chapter 7. Table 1 shows what each link asks and which evidence answers it.

Table 1: The metal value chain framework

| Link | Guiding question | What is measured | Main evidence | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Material | Where is the metal mined and refined, and how concentrated is supply? | Production by stage and country, prices, quotas and export controls | USGS, study groups, World Bank Pink Sheet, producer reports | 4 |
| Product form | In which commercial forms does the metal reach industry? | Strip, sheet, plate, bar, wire, tube, powder and their producers | Eurostat PRODCOM, company filings | 4 |
| Application | Which components need the material, and why? | Properties that make the alloy necessary, substitutes | Industry associations, standards, filings | 4 and 5 |
| Industry | Which industries buy these applications, and how fast do they grow? | Activity indicators such as engine deliveries, fab spending, implant rates | Airbus, Boeing, IEA, OICA, Cefic, SEMI, OECD | 5 |
| Geography | Where are production, demand and trade concentrated? | Country shares, trade corridors, regional investment | UN Comtrade, OECD ICIO, IEA | 5 and 6 |
| Market size | How large is the measurable market today? | Trade values, apparent consumption, industry and company revenue | UN Comtrade, PRODCOM, filings | 5 and 6 |
| Growth | How large could demand be in 2035? | Indexed demand scenarios with explicit rates | Sector evidence translated into rates | 7 |

*Source: team framework.*

The first three links describe the supply side, and chapter 4 applies them to every metal in the same order: where the material is mined and refined, in which product forms it reaches industry, and which components depend on it. This order exposes what a single production figure hides, namely that a metal can be abundant at the mine and scarce in the qualified form that an engine or an implant requires.

The next three links turn to demand, as chapter 5 follows each industry from its demand driver to its regional centres and buyers and chapter 6 measures the size of the market through trade flows and apparent consumption. Chapter 7 translates the evidence into growth scenarios to 2035, and chapter 8 assesses each opportunity on eight criteria: market attractiveness, growth to 2035, supply security, competitive intensity, technology attractiveness, customer attractiveness, entry barriers and the potential for differentiation.

A chain is only as reliable as its weakest comparison, so one discipline applies to every link. A figure is only compared with a figure from the same stage, which means that mine output is never added to refined metal, alloy melt or mill product, that contained metal is kept apart from gross weight, that output is kept apart from capacity, and that a commodity benchmark is never read as the price of a qualified alloy. This stage discipline explains why the report shows nickel mine output, primary nickel and stainless melt on separate lines, and why it does not claim one aggregate market value where the underlying series are not comparable.

The evidence follows an equally strict hierarchy, which determines how much weight each number carries. Structured statistics come first and were pulled directly, with every query logged, from UN Comtrade, Eurostat PRODCOM, the World Bank Pink Sheet, the USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026, OECD STAN and ICIO and UNIDO INDSTAT. Industry associations and official outlooks such as the IEA, Airbus, Boeing, OICA, Cefic and SEMI come second, and the annual reports and filings of 61 producers, customers and suppliers come third. Commercial market reports serve only to triangulate, and a missing observation stays missing rather than being estimated, which is the standard the following chapter applies to each metal in turn.
