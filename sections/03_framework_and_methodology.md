# 3. Introduction to the Analytical Framework and Methodology

The study follows the seven links of the metal value chain: material, product form, application, industry, geography, market size, and growth. Table 1 shows which chapters cover each link. Figures are compared only at the same stage, so contained metal is kept apart from gross weight and output from capacity.

Structured data from the USGS, UN Comtrade, Eurostat PRODCOM, the World Bank, OECD, UNIDO, and the IEA form the base. Associations, official outlooks, and 61 company profiles add detail, and commercial reports serve only as a cross-check. Key figures are checked against a second source where one exists, and the source register flags single-source figures.

Table 1: The metal value chain and its evidence

| Link | Guiding question | Main evidence | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Material | How concentrated is supply? | USGS, IEA, World Bank prices | 4 |
| Product form | In which qualified forms does it reach industry? | PRODCOM, producer filings | 4 |
| Application | Which components need it, and why? | Standards, associations, filings | 4 and 5 |
| Industry | Which industries buy, and how fast do they grow? | Airbus, Boeing, IEA, OICA, Cefic, SEMI | 5 |
| Geography | Where are the forms made and bought? | IEA regions, OECD, UN Comtrade | 5 and 6 |
| Market size | How large is measurable demand today? | Apparent consumption, trade values, family tonnes | 5 and 6 |
| Growth | How large could demand be in 2035? | Demand model, emerging options register | 7 |

Tier 1 estimates established demand as industry activity times metal content per unit times the share of units using a high-performance alloy. Activity measures such as engine deliveries, gas turbine gigawatts, and joint replacements start from measured 2025 values. To test the model, the team fitted trends to data through 2019 and compared them with 2020 to 2025 outcomes and with earlier official outlooks. A Monte Carlo simulation gives the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles of 2035 demand, and a supply analysis tests export quotas and licensing changes.

Tier 2 records emerging applications that current data cannot yet confirm. Examples are hydrogen-fueled turbines, supercritical CO2 recuperators, molten salt and small modular reactors, fusion magnet tape, and printed engine parts, drawn from patents, pilot plants, draft standards, and announced projects. Each is rated for technology readiness, evidence, alloys and forms, and earliest commercial volume.

The register names the event, such as a final investment decision or first serial order, that would move each candidate into tier 1. Chapter 8 scores both tiers on the same eight criteria, so producers can see which opportunities to monitor and which merit funding.
