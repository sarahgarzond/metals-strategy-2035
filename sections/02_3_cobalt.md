## 2.3 Cobalt alloys

Cobalt matters for the Client because it is the scarce and politically exposed element behind its cobalt alloys, so the section starts with where the metal comes from and then follows it through trade, use, price and outlook.

### Production

Cobalt is mined mostly as a byproduct of copper and nickel, and mine output reached 310 kt in 2025 after 302 kt in 2024 [USGS_CO_MINE_2025]. The Democratic Republic of the Congo supplied 230 kt or 73% of this total, while Indonesia raised its output from 35 kt to 44 kt and now holds 14% [USGS_CO_DRC_2025, USGS_CO_IDN_2025]. Because all other countries each produce less than 8 kt, with Russia at 7.7 kt and Madagascar, Australia, the Philippines and Canada between 3.5 and 3.9 kt, the supply base is narrower than for nickel [USGS_CO_IDN_2025]. Reserves of 12 Mt are similarly concentrated, with 6.0 Mt in the Congo [USGS_CO_RESERVES]. China leads refining and consumption, mainly for lithium ion batteries [USGS_CO_MINE_2025]. ### Trade

Since the mined material leaves Africa as intermediate product, the largest flow is from the Congo to China, which in 2024 imported goods under HS 8105 worth USD 3.2bn and 640 kt gross weight, of which USD 3.0bn came from the Congo [CT_8105_CN_IMP_2024, CT_8105_CN_IMP_DRC_2024]. The implied unit value of about USD 5 per kg (team estimate) shows that this is mattes and intermediates, not metal. China re-exports only USD 266m or 9.6 kt, with the Netherlands as the first buyer at USD 140m [CT_8105_CN_EXP_2024]. Western trade in refined and processed cobalt is smaller and more valuable, with the United States exporting USD 418m, the United Kingdom USD 218m and Germany USD 149m under HS 8105 [CT_8105_US_2024, CT_8105_UK_2024, CT_8105_DE_2024].

The wrought cobalt articles of HS 810590 are the closest customs proxy for alloy semifinished products. In 2024 the United States exported USD 263m for 1,889 t, the United Kingdom USD 142m for 1,744 t and Germany USD 99m for 1,056 t, while China exported only USD 13m [CT_810590_US_EXP_2024, CT_810590_UK_EXP_2024, CT_810590_DE_EXP_2024]. German wrought exports therefore earned about USD 93 per kg and US exports about USD 139 per kg, a value ladder of roughly 19 to 28 times the Chinese import unit value (team estimate). This ladder explains why the Client wants to sit at the semifinished step and not in the raw material step, and why Western alloy makers dominate the high value end of the flow.

### Consumption

As the semifinished flows show, the Western consumption pattern differs from the Chinese battery pattern. The only complete split available is for the United States, where 51% of the cobalt consumed in 2025 went into superalloys for aircraft gas turbines, 25% into chemicals, 15% into other metallic uses and 9% into cemented carbides, with a total value of USD 320m [USGS_CO_US_USE_SUPERALLOY, USGS_CO_US_VALUE]. Net import reliance reached 79% and scrap covered 25% of consumption, so recycling of alloy returns is a real supply source for producers [USGS_CO_US_IMPREL, USGS_CO_US_SCRAP]. ### Applications

Because jet engines need metal that keeps strength and resists oxidation at high temperature, cobalt base alloys serve combustors and hot section parts, and USGS lists nickel base alloys and ceramics as the only substitutes in this use [USGS_CO_MINE_2025]. In cemented carbides cobalt is the binder, in magnets it provides high temperature magnetic stability, and in implants CoCr alloys compete with titanium [USGS_CO_US_USE_SUPERALLOY]. ### Market size

Since customs and USGS data do not isolate alloys, the market size is bounded from two sides. US cobalt consumption was worth USD 320m in 2025 [USGS_CO_US_VALUE], and the 2024 wrought article exports of the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany and China sum to about USD 516m (team estimate, [CT_810590_US_EXP_2024, CT_810590_UK_EXP_2024, CT_810590_DE_EXP_2024, CT_810590_CN_EXP_2024]). ### Prices

Because the Congo sets the marginal supply of the metal, cobalt prices swing widely from year to year. The LME cash price averaged USD 23.17 per lb in 2021, peaked at USD 28.83 in 2022, fell to USD 11.84 in 2024 and recovered to USD 15 in 2025, a 59% fall from peak to trough (team estimate) [USGS_CO_LME_PRICE]. The US spot cathode price stood at USD 21 per lb in 2025 [USGS_CO_LME_PRICE]. A market forecast sees intermediates near USD 28.8 per lb at the end of 2026, but with low confidence and a range of USD 24.5 to 33.0 [SMM_CO_PRICE_2026]. ### Capacity and supply

The export policy of the Congo is the dominant supply constraint for every cobalt user. After a temporary ban in February 2025, the country set quotas of 18.1 kt for the rest of 2025 and up to 96.6 kt per year for 2026 and 2027, including 9.6 kt for strategic reserves [USGS_CO_QUOTA_2025, ARGUS_CO_QUOTA]. This cap equals about 42% of the 2025 Congo mine output (team estimate), and the uncertainty pushed US imports to 14,000 t, up from 10,800 t in 2024 [USGS_CO_DRC_2025, USGS_CO_US_IMPREL]. The United States cancelled a 2025 solicitation for 7,480 t of stockpile cobalt, which shows that government buying is also unstable [USGS_CO_MINE_2025]. ### Outlook 2035

Looking ahead, demand growth for alloys is tied to aircraft output, and the Airbus and Boeing forecasts of 43,420 deliveries to 2044 and 43,625 to 2045 give a stable base for hot section demand [AIRBUS_GMF_2025, BOEING_CMO_2026]. On the supply side, USGS notes that battery cobalt content is falling and that iron phosphate chemistries hold a large share in China, which eases competition for metal from alloy makers [USGS_CO_MINE_2025]. ### Spotlight facts

In February 2025 the Congo, which mined 230 kt or 73% of world cobalt, banned exports and replaced the ban in October 2025 with quotas, which raises raw material cost risk for alloy makers in Europe and the United States [USGS_CO_QUOTA_2025, USGS_CO_DRC_2025]. In 2025 the United States raised cobalt imports to 14,000 t and industry stocks to 1,500 t while cancelling its 7,480 t stockpile solicitation, which is consistent with buyers hedging against the ban [USGS_CO_LME_PRICE, USGS_CO_US_IMPREL]. Between 2022 and 2024 the LME cobalt price lost 59% before partially recovering, which shows why fixed price semifinished contracts carry metal risk [USGS_CO_LME_PRICE]. In 2024 German wrought cobalt articles sold at about USD 93 per kg against USD 5 per kg for the intermediate imported into China [CT_810590_DE_EXP_2024, CT_8105_CN_IMP_2024].
