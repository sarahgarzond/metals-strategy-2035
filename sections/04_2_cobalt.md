## 4.2 Cobalt

Cobalt shares aerospace, energy and medical applications with nickel alloys, and it does not share the European mill balance just calculated for nickel plate and bar. World mine production of contained cobalt was an estimated 310,000 tonnes in 2025, of which the Democratic Republic of the Congo accounted for about 230,000 tonnes, and refined production was a separate 240 thousand tonnes with China the leading refined producer [CO_USGS_MINE_WORLD_2025, USGS_CO_MINE_2025, CO_CI_REFINED_2025]. The price that matches the metal stage is the estimated London Metal Exchange cash average of USD 15 per pound in 2025, which is not a hydroxide price and not an alloy selling price [CO_USGS_LME_2025]. The commercial form a mill can buy for superalloys is alloy grade metal, and superalloys took 20 thousand tonnes of contained cobalt in 2025, while aviation use was 11,499 tonnes, of which 72 percent was commercial [AERO_CI_SUPERALLOY_CO_2025, AERO_CI_AVIATION_CO_2025].

Availability through 2027 is a quota rather than a mine capacity number. Export quotas replace the 2025 ban at up to 96,600 tonnes of contained cobalt a year in 2026 and 2027, including a strategic reserve [CO_USGS_QUOTA_2026]. No European mill apparent consumption of cobalt is stated, because the PRODCOM code previously mapped to cobalt is labelled bismuth and cadmium and has empty value arrays [CO_PRODCOM_24453030]. Section 5.4 therefore uses the 11,499 tonnes only as a cobalt indicator, and a path of about 16,100 tonnes in 2035 is a team illustration that holds 2025 intensity constant with the fleet interpolation, not a Cobalt Institute forecast and not the nickel alloy market [TE_AERO_CO_2035]. German HS 8105 trade in 2024 was a small net export of a mixed matte, unwrought, powder and article code, which is the corridor Chapter 6 reports and not a superalloy balance [CT_DE_8105_2024]. Chromium is next, because it is the bulk alloying element in the stainless melt that Table 2 keeps apart from this metal.
