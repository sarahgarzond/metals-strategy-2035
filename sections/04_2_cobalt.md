## 4.2 Cobalt

Export restrictions and concentrated mining make cobalt scarce. The USGS estimates 2025 mine output at 310 kt of contained cobalt. The Democratic Republic of the Congo mined about 230 kt, which the USGS puts at 73 percent of the world total. China refined 78.6 percent of the world's 240 kt of refined cobalt [USGS_CO_MINE_2025, AERO_CI_AVIATION_CO_2025].

In February 2025, Congo banned cobalt exports, then set an annual quota of up to 96,600 t for 2026 and 2027. CMOC mined 117,549 t in 2025 and received an export allowance of 31,200 t. European cobalt metal prices rose 130 percent in 2025 [CO_CMOC_QUOTA_2026, AERO_CI_AVIATION_CO_2025, CO_CMOC_CO_2025, AERO_USGS_CO_SUPER_SHARE]. The IEA expects 2035 mine supply of 225 kt against demand of 300 to 322 kt, a lasting gap [IEA_CM_CO_DEMAND_2025_2035].

Superalloys use a small share of cobalt but need high-purity metal. In 2025, batteries used 206 kt and superalloys 20 kt. A few Western refineries supply most of the superalloy grade [AERO_CI_AVIATION_CO_2025]. Its uses in turbine remelt stock, cobalt-chromium implants, and wear-resistant alloys are hard to switch, so a quota set in Kinshasa can halt vacuum melt shops such as Cannon-Muskegon, which melts cobalt based casting alloys [CO_CANNON_MUSKEGON_VM].
