## 4.3 Chromium

Chromium gives every corrosion and heat resistant alloy its protective oxide layer, yet its value chain is split between the countries that mine it and the country that smelts it. World chromite ore output reached an estimated 51 Mt gross weight in 2025, of which South Africa mined about 23 Mt on USGS figures and 25.2 Mt on the differently defined dashboard of the International Chromium Development Association [CR_MINE_ORE_2025_WORLD, CR_MINE_ORE_2025_ZAF, CR_ICDA_ORE_2025_ZAF]. The same association reports 9.9 Mt of ferrochrome alloy for China, a smelter product that cannot be set against ore tonnes, from a country with almost no chromite [CR_ICDA_FECR_2025_CHN]. Stainless steel absorbs most of this ferrochrome, and the United States imports 79 percent of its chromium needs [CR_SS_MELT_2025_WORLD, CR_US_NIR_2025].

The product form that high performance alloys require is far more specialised than the ferrochrome that stainless mills consume. Vacuum melted nickel alloys use low carbon ferrochrome and aluminothermic chromium metal, a small market whose U.S. price rose to an estimated USD 5.90 per pound in 2025 while the ferrochrome price fell [CR_PRICE_METAL_2025]. A single qualified source of this small input can therefore halt a vacuum melt shop, a vulnerability that molybdenum repeats in an even more value intensive form.
