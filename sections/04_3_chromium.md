## 4.3 Chromium

Chromium gives corrosion-resistant and heat-resistant alloys a protective oxide layer. Mining and smelting are concentrated in different countries. World chromite ore output reached an estimated 51 Mt gross weight in 2025. The USGS estimates that South Africa mined about 23 Mt. The International Chromium Development Association reports 25.2 Mt using a different definition [CR_ICDA_FECR_2025_CHN, CR_MINE_ORE_2025_WORLD]. The association reports that China, which has little chromite, smelts 9.9 Mt of ferrochrome, mostly for stainless steel. The United States imports 79 percent of its chromium needs [CR_ICDA_FECR_2025_CHN, CR_MINE_ORE_2025_WORLD, CR_SS_MELT_2025_WORLD].

Vacuum-melted nickel alloys require more specialized inputs than the ferrochrome used in stainless steel mills. These inputs include low-carbon ferrochrome and aluminothermic chromium metal. The U.S. chromium metal price rose to an estimated USD 5.90 per pound in 2025 while the ferrochrome price fell [CR_MINE_ORE_2025_WORLD]. AMG Chrome in the United Kingdom supplies low-gas chromium metal for single-crystal turbine blades. A vacuum melt shop can stop production if it loses a qualified source of this input (AMG Critical Materials N.V., n.d.).
