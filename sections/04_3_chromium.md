## 4.3 Chromium

Chromium gives every corrosion and heat resistant alloy its protective oxide layer, yet its value chain is split between the countries that mine it and the country that smelts it. World chromite ore output reached an estimated 51 Mt gross weight in 2025, of which South Africa mined about 23 Mt on USGS figures and 25.2 Mt on the differently defined dashboard of the International Chromium Development Association [CR_MINE_ORE_2025_WORLD, CR_MINE_ORE_2025_ZAF, CR_ICDA_ORE_2025_ZAF]. China, with almost no chromite, smelts 9.9 Mt of ferrochrome on the same association's data, mostly for stainless steel, while the United States imports 79 percent of its chromium needs [CR_ICDA_FECR_2025_CHN, CR_SS_MELT_2025_WORLD, CR_US_NIR_2025].

The product form that high performance alloys require is far more specialised than the ferrochrome that stainless mills consume. Vacuum melted nickel alloys use low carbon ferrochrome and aluminothermic chromium metal, a small market whose U.S. price rose to an estimated USD 5.90 per pound in 2025 while the ferrochrome price fell [CR_PRICE_METAL_2025]. One maker of the purest grades is AMG Chrome in the United Kingdom, which supplies low gas chromium metal for single crystal turbine blades, and losing one qualified source of this input can halt a vacuum melt shop, a vulnerability that molybdenum repeats in a more value intensive form [CO_AMG_CHROME_2026].
