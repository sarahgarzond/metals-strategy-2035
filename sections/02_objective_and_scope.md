# 2. Report Objective and Scope

This project follows selected metals that are essential to high performance alloys from the mine to the industrial applications that depend on them, to assess how their markets may develop through 2035. It asks one central question: how do the concentration of metal supply, the conversion of metals into qualified product forms and the needs of seven industries determine where the most attractive opportunities in high performance materials will lie through 2035? Four subsidiary questions structure the answer, asking where the metals are mined and refined and in which qualified forms they reach industry, which industries and regions drive demand and how large the measurable market is, how large and how certain demand could become by 2035, and where supply security, growth and entry barriers combine into the most attractive opportunities.

The scope covers ten metals, namely nickel, cobalt, chromium, molybdenum, titanium, tungsten, zirconium, copper, aluminium and manganese, and seven industries, namely automotive, oil and gas, energy and environmental technology, aerospace, chemical processing, electronics and electrical engineering, and medical technology. The analysis is global, with regional comparisons for Europe, North America and Asia, and it uses the most recent official data available in November 2026. The outlook separates a quantified estimate for established applications from a structured assessment of emerging ones that official statistics do not yet capture.

The report argues that value in this market is created by converting metals into qualified products rather than by mining them. Nickel is in surplus, while cobalt, tungsten and molybdenum are constrained by export policy, and the countries that convert metals into qualified bar, strip and tube earn trade surpluses that the mining countries do not. The most attractive measured segments are therefore superalloy remelt stock and combustor sheet for gas turbines and triple melt bar and billet for aero engines in the United States and Europe, while precision FeNi foil for East Asian mask makers is a candidate whose rank chapter 8 tests. The tier 1 model puts 2035 alloy demand at P50 indices of 133 in energy technology, 134 in aerospace and 102 in automotive, with 2025 at 100 [MODEL_T1_RESULTS_2035], while emerging applications remain a register of options with signposts. Producers should in turn stop investing in diesel only engine parts, European base chemical new build, electrolyser capacity before funded orders and captive nuclear cladding zirconium. Chapter 3 explains how this argument is tested, chapters 4 to 7 follow the metals from supply through industry demand and trade to growth, chapter 8 ranks the opportunities and chapter 9 concludes.
