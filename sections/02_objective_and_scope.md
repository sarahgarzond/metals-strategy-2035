# 2. Report Objective and Scope

The objective follows from that separation of stages and identifies where changes in metal supply, trade and industrial demand create commercially relevant opportunities for Münvest through 2035. The central question is how the production, availability, trade and industrial uses of selected metals shape opportunities in high performance materials through 2035.

The scope that answers the question covers ten named metals and a residual category, together with seven industries: automotive, oil and gas, energy and environmental technology, aerospace, chemical processing, electronics and electrical engineering, and medical technology. The metal chapters provide the production and form context, and the industry chapters keep the application evidence for those buyers and close each sector with what the company should do. VDM Metals’ published portfolio is an application benchmark only, and it is not evidence of Münvest’s plants, customers or share, while VDM and Haynes are counted once because both sit inside Acerinox.

Observation years stay attached to each figure, because mined metal, primary metal, mill product shipments and industry revenue have different boundaries and are not interchangeable measures of market size. No aggregate addressable market value is asserted where comparable data are unavailable. Apparent consumption, defined in the next chapter as sold production plus imports minus exports, is one of those non interchangeable measures and is applied only where all three legs are logged.

The scenarios in Chapter 7 are sensitivities for relevant application demand, and they are not forecasts of metal prices, company revenue or investment returns. Internal sales, margins, approvals, capacity and customer lists are required before those findings become a business plan, which is why Chapter 3 states how the four dimensions will be read together.
