# 2. Report Objective and Scope

A turbine disc spinning at the heart of a jet engine, a valve deep inside a sour gas well and a hip implant that must last thirty years have one thing in common: each depends on a handful of alloys whose raw materials come from a handful of countries. Nickel now comes mainly from Indonesia, cobalt from the Democratic Republic of the Congo and tungsten from China, while the customers who pay for the finished alloys sit in Europe, North America and East Asia. This distance between where metals are mined and where they create value is the starting point of the study.

The study therefore asks one central question: how do the production, availability, trade and industrial uses of selected metals shape the opportunities in high performance materials through 2035? To answer it, the report follows ten metals, namely nickel, cobalt, chromium, molybdenum, titanium, tungsten, zirconium, copper, aluminium and manganese, into seven industries, namely automotive, oil and gas, energy and environmental technology, aerospace, chemical processing, electronics and electrical engineering, and medical technology. The analysis covers the global market, with particular attention to Europe, North America and Asia, and it uses the most recent official data available in November 2026.

The report argues that value in this market is created by conversion and qualification rather than by metal volume. Nickel is in surplus while cobalt, tungsten and molybdenum are constrained by export policy, and the countries that convert metals into qualified products earn trade surpluses that the mining countries do not. Demand to 2035 grows fastest in aerospace engines and gas turbines, where qualified superalloy capacity is already short, while automotive demand for engine alloys declines and European chemical demand shifts to maintenance and to Asian construction. The most attractive opportunities therefore lie in vacuum melted superalloy bar, billet and wire for engine and turbine supply chains, followed by precision, medical and corrosion resistant niches that are protected by approvals.

Because production, melt, mill product and revenue figures measure different stages, the report never adds them into one aggregate market value, and its scenarios to 2035 are sensitivities of application demand rather than forecasts of prices or revenues. The chapters follow the analytical chain introduced in chapter 3, moving from the metals to the industries that use them, the size of their markets, their growth and, finally, the recommendations.
