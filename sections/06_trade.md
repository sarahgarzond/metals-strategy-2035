# 6. Global Market Size and Trade

Trade data show how large the market for qualified metal products is and where its value is created, before this chapter turns to trade policy.

### 6.1 Measuring the Market for Qualified Nickel Products

The only product level market that official statistics measure for Europe is the apparent consumption of nickel mill products, which runs to tens of kilotonnes. EU production plus imports minus exports came to 57.8 kt of nickel plate, sheet, strip and foil and 9.2 kt of tubes in 2024, while the bar figure rests on a confidential production cell [AC_EU_NI_FLAT_2024, AC_EU_NI_TUBE_2024, AC_EU_NI_BAR_2024]. Because Eurostat production values fall below the value of the contained nickel, Table 10 values these tonnes at EU border unit values, a team estimate of about EUR 1.8 to 2.8 bn for flat products and EUR 0.24 to 0.38 bn for tubes [AC_EU_NI_UNITVAL_2024]. The codes cover only alloys in which nickel predominates, so the qualified market is larger, and the unit values show the conversion premium that section 6.2 explains.

### 6.2 Conversion Creates the Trade Surplus

Trade flows show that value is earned where metals are converted, and among the ten reporting countries Germany is the largest exporter of nickel plate, sheet and strip and the second largest of bars and wire, and its exports of the three mill product codes rose from USD 0.968 bn in 2019 to USD 1.418 bn in 2024, an increase of about 47 percent [COMTRADE_7505_2019_2024, COMTRADE_7506_2019_2024, COMTRADE_7507_2019_2024].

The pattern in Table 11 is the market's central economic fact. Germany imports USD 736 million more raw nickel than it exports, yet it is a net exporter of every nickel mill product, and its plate and strip sell at about twice the value of the contained metal, as do EU exports at EUR 31.0 per kg [NI_CT_DE_7506_2024_T, AC_EU_NI_UNITVAL_2024, NI_WB_AVG_2024]. Reporter data alone suggest that China leads unwrought nickel exports, but mirror flows show Canada, Norway, Russia and Australia, the main class 1 origins, each shipping about USD 1 bn to the ten reporters, more than Indonesia reports [COMTRADE_MIRROR_EXTRA6_2024]. Austria and Sweden appear in the mirror data as converters on the German pattern, so value accrues where nickel is melted, rolled and drawn, which is where the corridors of section 6.3 run.

### 6.3 Corridors and Dependencies

The geography of trade mirrors the geography of the industries that buy. German nickel bar and wire go mainly to the United Kingdom and the United States, each with about USD 111 million in 2024, the engine corridors of section 5.4, while plate and strip go mainly to Austria, Italy, China and the United States, where chemical and electronics fabricators work [COMTRADE_7505_2019_2024, COMTRADE_7506_2019_2024]. Engine trade confirms the picture, with British exports of turbojets and gas turbines at USD 39.8 bn in 2024, re exports included, and trade policy now reshapes these corridors [COMTRADE_PORTFOLIO_WORLD_2019_2024].

### 6.4 Trade Policy Reshapes Sourcing and Access

Trade policy now changes costs and market access faster than demand does, as the United States raised its Section 232 tariffs on steel and aluminium to 50 percent in 2025 and taxed the copper content of semifinished copper products at 50 percent from 1 August 2025 [US_232_COPPER_2025]. The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, definitive since 1 January 2026, covers ferrochrome and ferronickel but not the nickel products of HS chapter 75, so it raises input costs without protecting nickel alloy mill products [EU_CBAM_2026]. The Chinese licences, Congolese cobalt quota and Indonesian ore quota of chapter 4 add supply risk, while the EU Critical Raw Materials Act lists nickel, cobalt and tungsten as strategic or critical [W_MOFCOM_2025_10, CO_USGS_QUOTA_2026, NI_RKAB_2026_REV, W_EU_CRMA_2024]. These measures reward producers that convert close to their customers and secure several sources of each critical input, the setting in which chapter 7 projects demand to 2035.
