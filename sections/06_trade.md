# 6. Global Market Size and Trade

Chapter 5 measured each industry at its own stage. This chapter sizes the global market at the mill product stage and then follows trade routes and policies.

### 6.1 Global Market Size by Product Family

Six digit customs data separate nickel alloys from pure nickel. In 2024, ten major reporters exported 95.1 kt of alloy bar, rod and wire (HS 750512 and 750522), 41.3 kt of alloy flat products (750620), and 18.0 kt of alloy tubes (750712). They also shipped 27.9 kt of unwrought alloy (750220), the code for VIM remelt ingot and casting stock [COMTRADE_750512_750522_750712_2023_2024, COMTRADE_750610_750620_2019_2024, COMTRADE_750210_750220_2023_2024]. For the eight reporters with four digit data, alloys make up 97, 85 and 87 percent of bar and wire, flat and tube exports by weight, so the totals in section 6.2 track alloy trade (team calculation).

Unit values rise with each conversion step, which gives a first price ladder by form. Unwrought alloy averaged USD 23.4 per kg in 2024, flat products 34.0, wire 36.5, bar 40.6 and tube 58.3, or 2.5 times remelt ingot [MODEL_HS6_PRICE_LADDER_2024].

Exports are only a floor, so world output has to be estimated. EU apparent consumption, production plus imports minus exports, was 57.8 kt of flat products and 9.2 kt of tubes in 2024. Bar data are confidential [AC_EU_NI_TUBE_2024, AC_EU_NI_BAR_2024, AC_EU_NI_FLAT_2024, AC_EU_NI_UNITVAL_2024].

Table 10 scales these anchors to world team estimates. Superalloys divide the 20 kt of cobalt used in superalloys in 2025 by an assumed cobalt content. Flat products and tubes divide EU use by an assumed EU share of 12 to 30 percent, and bar applies a multiple of 1.0 to 2.5 to exports. The families overlap, so the rows are never summed [MODEL_TON_SUPERALLOYBARBIL, MODEL_TON_EU_SHARE, MODEL_TON_BAR_MULT].

Germany is a net importer of USD 736 million of raw nickel, yet a net exporter of every mill product in Table 11. EU flat product exports reach EUR 31.0 per kg, against EUR 15.5 for the contained nickel, which is the conversion premium of section 4.1 [AC_EU_NI_UNITVAL_2024, COMTRADE_7506_2019_2024, NI_WB_AVG_2022, FX_ECB_USD_EUR_2024].

Partner import data also show where class 1 nickel comes from. Canada, Norway, Russia, and Australia each shipped about USD 1 bn to the ten reporters, more than Indonesia reports [COMTRADE_MIRROR_EXTRA6_2024]. Austria and Sweden appear as converters like Germany, and section 6.3 follows the routes to their buyers.

### 6.3 Corridors and Dependencies

The main trade routes follow the buyers. In 2024, Germany exported about USD 111 to 112 million of nickel bar and wire each to the United Kingdom and the United States, both key engine markets. German plate and strip go mainly to Austria, Italy, China, and the United States, where chemical and electronics fabricators operate [COMTRADE_7505_2019_2024, COMTRADE_7506_2019_2024]. British turbojet and gas turbine exports reached USD 39.8 bn in 2024, including re-exports [COMTRADE_PORTFOLIO_WORLD_2019_2024]. The United States exported the most alloy bar, 26.4 kt at USD 44.2 per kg, against 10.4 kt at USD 34.4 per kg from Germany [COMTRADE_750512_750522_750712_2023_2024]. Remelt ingot follows the casting houses. The United States and the United Kingdom are each other's largest suppliers of unwrought alloy, at USD 66 and 62 million in 2024 [COMTRADE_750210_750220_2023_2024]. These corridors are exposed to the trade policies described next.

### 6.4 Trade Policy Reshapes Sourcing and Access

Trade policy can change costs and access quickly. In 2025, the United States raised Section 232 tariffs on steel and aluminium to 50 percent and, from 1 August, taxed the copper content of copper semis at 50 percent [US_232_COPPER_2025]. The general US duty on nickel mill products (HS 7505 to 7507) is 2 to 3 percent [USITC_HTS_75_2026]. Iron based grades differ, because customs classify an alloy by the metal that predominates by weight. FeNi36 and Alloy 800H, with at least 39.5 percent iron, are therefore steel and carry the 50 percent Section 232 tariff [USITC_HTS_XV_NOTE5_2026, MO_SM_800H_COMP, US_PROC9705_2018].

Tariffs on EU goods have shifted several times. The joint statement of 21 August 2025 capped the combined tariff on most EU goods at 15 percent, and from February 2026 a 10 percent surcharge applied for 150 days [US_EU_JOINT_STATEMENT_2025, US_EO14389_2026, US_PROC11012_2026]. Since 24 July 2026, a Section 301 action sets the combined EU rate at 10 percent where the general duty is lower. Goods under Section 232 are exempt, and the exemption lists could not be checked for HS 7505 to 7507 [USTR_301_2026_EU]. EU mills supplying US forgers may therefore pay up to 10 percent on nickel alloys that US mills such as ATI and Carpenter avoid.

The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism entered its definitive phase on 1 January 2026. It covers ferrochrome and ferronickel but not the nickel products of HS chapter 75, so it raises input costs without protecting alloy mill products [EU_CBAM_2026]. With the quotas and licences of chapter 4, these measures favour conversion near customers and several qualified sources per critical input, conditions that chapter 7 carries into the growth scenarios.
