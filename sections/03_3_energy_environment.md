## 3.3 Environmental and energy technology

<!-- sector-analyst energy and environment, research notes in research/sectors/energy_environment.md -->

Environmental and energy technology is the largest growth sector open to the Client, with an addressable market of about EUR 8.5bn in 2025 that grows at 6.5% p.a. to about EUR 16.0bn in 2035 (team estimate) [MODEL_SEC_ENERGY], but the growth sits in a few qualified niches rather than across the sector. The pull from capital spending is strong, since clean energy investment reached about USD 2.2 trillion in 2025 [IEA_WEI_2025] and electricity demand is projected to rise by around 40% by 2035 [IEA_WEO_2025_NUC]. The sector value is a top down allocation, and the four energy cells of the market model explain only EUR 3.85bn of it, so the remaining EUR 4.65bn, mainly gas turbine superalloys and copper for wind and grids, is marked TODO(data) for bottom up validation. Because investment headlines and alloy tonnage diverge, the analysis first separates the sub markets by metal intensity.

### Market size and growth to 2035

Following the money into metal shows that gas turbines, nuclear and hydrogen carry most of the high performance alloy growth, while wind and solar add volume mainly in copper and aluminium. GE Vernova held 116 GW of gas turbine backlog and slot reservations in mid 2026 and plans to raise output from 20 GW to 30 GW per year by 2030 [GEV_2Q26], and Siemens Energy reported a firm backlog of 69 GW, about a quarter of it linked to data centres [SE_Q3FY26]. Nuclear capacity under construction exceeded 70 GW in 2025 [IEA_NUC_2025], and the IEA expects global capacity to grow by one third to 70% by 2035 [IEA_WEO_2025_NUC]. Wind added a record 165 GW in 2025 [GWEC_GWR_2026], and renewables are set to add about 4,600 GW between 2025 and 2030 [IEA_REN_2025]. Hydrogen is the outlier, because operating and committed projects deliver only about 4.2 Mt per year by 2030 against 37 Mt announced [IEA_H2_2030_FID], and this gap between ambition and orders frames the drivers and risks.

### Drivers and risks

The dominant driver behind these numbers is firm power for data centres and electrified industry, which governments now back with large nuclear commitments. The United Kingdom took the final investment decision for Sizewell C at GBP 38bn in July 2025 [SZC_FID_2025], and the United States agreed a partnership worth at least USD 80bn for new Westinghouse reactors in October 2025 [WEC_US_80BN]. The risks are equally concrete, because electrolyser factories ran at about 9% utilisation in 2025 and Chinese systems cost USD 500 to 1,100 per kW against USD 1,900 to 2,500 per kW elsewhere [ARGUS_ELY_2026]. Gas turbine slot reservations may not all convert into firm orders [GEV_2Q26], and India relaxed its sulphur dioxide norms again in 2025, which makes desulphurisation retrofits beyond the first 22 GW uncertain [PRAYAS_FGD_2025]. These risks decide which emerging technologies deserve the Client's scarce qualification effort.

### Emerging technologies and applications

Against this risk profile, four emerging technologies matter because each creates a new qualified component rather than more of an existing one. Solid oxide fuel cells and electrolysers need ferritic chromium interconnect strip that resists oxidation up to about 900 °C [CROFER_TK_2003], and Bloom Energy aims for 2 GW of annual stack production by the end of 2026 [BLOOM_2GW]. Small modular and Generation IV reactors add demand for Alloy 690, Alloy 800 and specialty stainless, with Kairos Power building its salt cooled reactor in 316H stainless steel [KAIROS_316H]. Fusion needs copper chromium zirconium, and ITER alone tendered up to 270 t of CuCrZr-IG for its first wall panels [ITER_CUCRZR]. Geothermal and carbon storage wells also run hotter and wetter, as Fervo casings rated to 460 °F and the Northern Lights expansion to at least 5 Mt of CO2 per year by 2028 show [FERVO_CAPE_2026, EQUINOR_NL_2025]. Each of these applications maps onto specific alloys and product forms, which the following table summarises.

| Application | Alloy and grade | Product form | Why this material |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steam generators (large reactors and SMR) | Alloy 690, Alloy 800 | Tube from bar and hollows, support strip | Resists stress corrosion cracking in primary water |
| Fuel cladding and spacer grids | Zircaloy, ZrNb alloys | Tube, strip | Low neutron absorption |
| Gas turbine combustors and transition ducts | HASTELLOY X, HAYNES 282 type | Sheet, plate, weld wire | Oxidation and creep strength with good formability |
| Alkaline electrolysers | Nickel 200 and 201 type | Strip, expanded mesh | Corrosion resistance in alkaline electrolyte without precious metals |
| Solid oxide stacks | Ferritic Cr steel (Crofer type) | Thin strip | Thermal expansion matched to ceramic cells |
| Desulphurisation ducts and stacks | Alloy C-276, Alloy 59, 6Mo | Plate, sheet, wallpaper lining | Acid chloride condensate |
| Waste to energy boilers | Alloy 625, 686 type filler | Weld wire, overlay strip | Chloride deposit corrosion on waterwalls |
| Fusion first wall and divertor | CuCrZr-IG | Plate, pipe, forging | Heat conduction combined with strength |

### Geographic concentration of demand

Demand for the materials in this table splits into a Chinese volume pole and a Western qualification pole. China holds 65% of installed or committed electrolyser capacity [IEA_H2_2025], installed more than 120 GW of the 165 GW of new wind in 2025 [GWEC_GWR_2026] and accounts for nearly 60% of new renewables to 2030 [IEA_REN_2025]. North America and Europe host about 80% of the carbon capture capacity due by 2030 [IEA_CCUS_2025] and most nuclear new build that is open to Western suppliers, led by the four BWRX-300 units at Darlington in Canada [OPG_DARLINGTON]. The United States placed about half of the record 38 GW of gas turbine orders in the second quarter of 2026 [TM_GT_ORDERS_Q2], while Saudi Arabia and India add hydrogen and desulphurisation demand [NEOM_H2, PRAYAS_FGD_2025]. This pattern places the Client's realistic customers mostly in Europe and North America, where the competitive field is narrow.

### Customers, competitors and competitor map

Within these regions, customers are a small group of OEMs and EPCs, namely GE Vernova, Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Power for turbines, Westinghouse, EDF, Doosan Enerbility, NuScale and Rolls-Royce SMR for nuclear, and thyssenkrupp nucera, ACWA Power, Air Products and Bloom Energy for hydrogen and fuel cells. The strongest competitor is Acerinox, whose high performance alloys division has combined VDM Metals and Haynes since November 2024 [ACX_HAYNES] and owns several of the reference grades listed above. Alleima raised steam generator tube capacity by about 60% in 2026 [ALLEIMA_TM2026], which shows that tube makers invest ahead of the reactor orders. ### Where high performance metals gain or lose relevance

The competitor map already hints at substitution, because high performance metals gain where temperature, radiation or chlorides rule out coated steel and lose where a cheaper system exists. Proton exchange membrane electrolysers shift value to titanium and platinum group metals, with IRENA estimating 5,500 t of titanium for the 2030 fleet [IRENA_H2_2020], while alkaline stacks keep nickel. In waste to energy plants, Alloy 625 overlay has lasted ten years or more on waterwalls [KAWAHARA_2007], yet thermal spray cladding replaced it at the Swiss Renergia plant after early corrosion [IGS_HVTS]. In desulphurisation, glass block and titanium clad linings undercut Alloy C-276 on cost, and 6Mo grades replace it in milder zones [NI_FGD]. Coal phase out in Europe and the United States shrinks this market further, which steers the Client toward value chain steps protected by qualification.

### Most attractive value chain steps

Given these shifts, the most attractive steps are qualified semifinished products sold close to the component maker. First, nuclear grade strip, bar and wire in Alloy 690, Alloy 800 and zirconium form a EUR 1.7bn cell growing 7% p.a. (team estimate) [MODEL_SEC_ENERGY]. Second, thin interconnect strip and nickel strip and mesh for electrolyser stacks is small at EUR 0.25bn but grows 18% p.a. (team estimate) [MODEL_SEC_ENERGY]. Third, combustor sheet and repair welding wire follow the gas turbine fleet into maintenance, although alloy volumes for this aftermarket remain TODO(data). Weld overlay consumables for boilers are worth defending at EUR 0.9bn and 4% p.a. (team estimate) [MODEL_SEC_ENERGY], whereas commodity copper for wind and solar offers little differentiation against Chinese scale.
