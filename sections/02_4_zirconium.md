## 2.4 Zirconium

Zirconium reaches the Client through two distinct value chains, because the same element is mined as zircon sand for tiles and foundries and is refined to hafnium free metal for nuclear fuel and corrosion resistant process equipment. The commercial core for a producer of semifinished products is the metal chain, which in 2024 moved about USD 507m of goods in HS 8109 across 98 reporting economies [CT_8109_M_2024], while the mineral chain sets the cost base and the price signal for everything downstream.

### 2.4.1 Production

Because zirconium metal can only be made from zircon, mine supply defines the upper limit for every semifinished product, and that supply is concentrated in a handful of heavy mineral sands provinces. World zircon concentrate output was estimated at 1,200 kt gross weight in 2025 after 1,300 kt in 2024 [USGS_ZR_2025], with Australia at 400 kt [USGS_ZR_AUS_2025], South Africa at 270 kt [USGS_ZR_ZAF_2025] and Mozambique at 160 kt [USGS_ZR_MOZ_2025]. China, Senegal and Indonesia together added about 222 kt (team estimate from the country table in [USGS_ZR_OTHER_2025]). Reserves exceed 70,000 kt of ZrO2 content [USGS_ZR_RES_2025], so geology is not the constraint and the risk lies in mine closures and project delays.

Downstream of the mine, the number of producers shrinks steeply, since only a few plants in France, the United States, Russia and China separate hafnium from zirconium and reduce the oxide to sponge. The United States has one zirconium and one hafnium metal producer, in Oregon and Utah, and at least five other firms make zirconium chemicals [USGS_ZR_2025]. Framatome claims about 30% of the fuel cladding and sponge market [FRAM_SHARE], and four fabricators (Framatome, Global Nuclear Fuel, TVEL and Westinghouse) dominate light water reactor fuel. ### 2.4.2 Trade

Since the mine and the metal plant are rarely in the same country, trade flows follow two clear corridors. For ore, South Africa led exports in 2024 with USD 610m of a USD 1,404m reporter total, followed by Indonesia, Senegal, Australia and Mozambique [CT_261510_X_2024], while China took about USD 1,368m of USD 2,196m reported imports [CT_261510_M_2024]. Reported ore imports rose from USD 1,820m in 2019 to USD 2,196m in 2024, which is 3.8% p.a. (team estimate from [CT_261510_M_2024]).

For metal, the picture reverses, because the United States is the largest exporter at USD 198m in 2024 [CT_8109_X_USA_2024] and the largest importer, with France supplying USD 79.7m of its USD 118.4m purchases [CT_8109_M_USA_FRA_2024]. USGS confirms that France provided 72% of US wrought zirconium imports in 2021 to 2024, while China provided 55% of unwrought imports [USGS_ZR_US_WROUGHT_2025]. The hafnium free subheadings introduced in HS 2022 give a nuclear proxy. Exports of hafnium free unwrought zirconium were USD 107.8m in 2024, led by China (USD 64.0m) and the United States (USD 36.8m) [CT_810921_X_2024], and exports of hafnium free articles reached USD 180.5m, of which the United States held USD 125.2m [CT_810991_X_2024]. These sums cover only the economies that reported to the public preview endpoint and omit France, so they understate the true flows.

Between 2019 and 2024 the reported import value of HS 8109 grew from USD 386m to USD 507m, which is 5.6% p.a. (team estimate from [CT_8109_M_2024]), while reported net weight fell from 9,018 t to 5,217 t. The implied unit value therefore more than doubled, which points to a mix shift toward nuclear and other high value items, although changing reporter coverage limits how far this reading can be pushed.

### 2.4.3 Consumption

Because zircon is a mineral commodity with a metal by product, consumption splits into a large ceramic block and a small but valuable metal block. Ceramics take about 54% of zircon according to an association figure relayed by a market summary [ZIA_CERAMICS_SHARE], and foundries and refractories together take about 27% [KEN_ZIRCON_USES]. Chemicals, zirconia and metal share the remainder, and TODO(data): the exact tonnage of zircon that ends up as nuclear grade sponge was searched but not found in public sources.

China is the decisive consumer of ore, as the import data above show, and it is also a growing consumer of nuclear zirconium. The Client's addressable consumption is the metal block, where nuclear fuel assemblies in France, the United States, Russia, China, Japan and South Korea and chemical plants in Europe, the United States and Asia are the main buyers, as the trade data for Japan, Sweden and South Korea in [CT_810991_X_2024] indicate.

### 2.4.4 Applications

Because each use depends on one property, it is easiest to read applications through the property that cannot be replaced. Zirconium alloys such as Zircaloy are used for fuel cladding and channel components since they combine a low neutron absorption cross section with corrosion resistance in hot water, and this requires hafnium to be removed, as hafnium occurs at about 1 part per 50 parts zirconium in ore [MP_HF_PRICE_2026]. Commercially pure Zr 702 and the niobium alloyed Zr 705 serve chemical processing, where they resist hot sulfuric acid up to about 70% for Zr 702 [ZR702_H2SO4] and organic acids such as acetic acid, but they fail in hydrofluoric acid, wet chlorine and ferric or cupric chloride [ZR702_H2SO4]. Titanium and synthetic materials may substitute in some chemical plants, and niobium or stainless steel in some nuclear uses [USGS_ZR_SUBST].

Hafnium, the by product, is a separate market that matters because it comes out of the same separation plants, and its leading use is in superalloys [USGS_ZR_2025], while nuclear control rods and chip demand add pull [MP_HF_PRICE_2026].

### 2.4.5 Market size

The overall zirconium market is quoted at about USD 2.2bn in 2025 by paid reports [SNS_ZR_2035], but these figures include ore and chemicals and serve only as a cross check. For the semifinished level the better anchors are the reported HS 8109 imports of USD 507m in 2024 [CT_8109_M_2024] and a commercial estimate of USD 593m for zirconium metal in 2025 [DIR_ZRMETAL_2025]. A further commercial estimate puts nuclear grade metal at USD 531m in 2024 [SPH_NUCZR_2035]. Because the two metal anchors agree within about 15%, the team uses USD 0.5bn to 0.6bn as the working size of the zirconium metal and semifinished market (team estimate, assumption documented in Appendix 06). This is about 1% of the roughly EUR 50bn market in which the Client competes, which makes zirconium a niche with high margins rather than a volume pillar.

### 2.4.6 Prices

Because metal producers buy mineral feed and sell into long contracts, prices show a cost push from the mine and a spike risk from by products. Premium zircon in China rose from USD 1,530 per t in 2021 to a peak of USD 2,300 in 2022 and eased to USD 1,800 in 2025 [USGS_ZR_PRICE_2025]. Zirconium sponge ex works China followed the same shape, from USD 25 per kg in 2021 to USD 30 in 2022 and USD 22 in 2025 [USGS_ZR_SPONGE_2025]. The mineral price direction turned again in 2026, with Chinese zircon sand up 4.3% in July [CHEMANALYST_ZIRCON_2026] after Iluka cut volumes. Hafnium behaves differently from the other zirconium products, as USGS reports annual averages of USD 781 per kg in 2021, USD 6,130 in 2023 and USD 3,800 in 2025 [USGS_HF_PRICE_2025], while spot reports show a rise from about USD 5,000 per kg to roughly USD 13,115 by April 2026 [MP_HF_PRICE_2026]. The two figures differ in basis and date and the spot source is a trade news summary, so the spot level is treated as low confidence.

### 2.4.7 Capacity and supply

Since the mine side is cyclical and the metal side is concentrated, supply risk sits at both ends. Iluka guides to about 180 kt of zircon in 2026 with the Cataby mine and the SR2 kiln idle [ILU_2026_ZIRCON], and the USGS lists exploration and development projects in Australia, Mozambique, South Africa, Sri Lanka and Tanzania [USGS_ZR_2025]. On the metal side, China tightened licensing of hafnium exports, and reported unwrought hafnium exports fell from 5,001 kg in January 2025 to 499 kg in September 2025 [MP_HF_PRICE_2026], which shows how an export policy on a by product can reach buyers of superalloys. Global pure hafnium supply is estimated at only 70 to 75 t per year [MP_HF_PRICE_2026]. The United States applies a 4.2% duty on unwrought zirconium and 3.7% on other articles [USGS_ZR_TARIFF]. ### 2.4.8 Outlook to 2035

Because nuclear fuel is the largest value pool for metal, the outlook follows reactor build. The IEA expects global nuclear capacity to grow by at least a third and up to 70% by 2035 under current policies, with more than 70 GW under construction [IEA_NUC_2035]. If cladding demand scales with capacity, it would grow by about 2.9% p.a. in a base case (team estimate: 1.33 to the power 0.1 minus 1, from [IEA_NUC_2035]). Commercial forecasts for the whole zirconium market range from 5.2% to 7.9% p.a. to 2035 [SNS_ZR_2035], and for nuclear grade metal 6.3% p.a. to about USD 1.04bn [SPH_NUCZR_2035].

Technology shifts could reduce the nuclear pull on zirconium, since silicon carbide cladding and some small modular reactor designs do not use zirconium cladding [ZIA_NUCLEAR], although the Zircon Industry Association expects the nuclear market for zirconium alloys to remain stable for many years [ZIA_NUCLEAR]. ### 2.4.10 Spotlight facts

1. In 2025, China cut reported exports of unwrought hafnium from 5,001 kg in January to 499 kg in September, which affects gas turbine superalloy and nuclear buyers in Japan, South Korea and the United States [MP_HF_PRICE_2026].
2. In July 2026, Iluka guided to about 180 kt of zircon for the year, keeping the Cataby mine and the SR2 kiln idle in Western Australia, which tightens the feed for Chinese ceramics and metal producers [ILU_2026_ZIRCON].
3. In 2024, China exported 912 t of hafnium free unwrought zirconium worth USD 64.0m, ahead of the United States at 801 t, according to the public preview of UN Comtrade [CT_810921_X_2024].
4. In 2024, France supplied USD 79.7m of the USD 118.4m of zirconium products the United States imported under HS 8109, which is consistent with Framatome and other French fabricators serving US reactor fleets [CT_8109_M_USA_FRA_2024].
