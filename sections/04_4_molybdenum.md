## 4.4 Molybdenum

Molybdenum follows chromium as an alloying addition whose mine stage is a different unit from the nickel mill products in section 4.1. World mine output of contained molybdenum was an estimated 260,000 tonnes in 2025 [MO_USGS_MINE_WORLD_2025]. Part of that output comes from primary molybdenum mines and part is recovered with copper, so the two routes have to be read separately before any availability claim is made. United States apparent consumption of molybdenum is estimated at 34,000 tonnes of contained metal in 2025, built from production, trade and stocks [MO_USGS_APPARENT_US_2025]. That national metal balance is a different identity from the European mill product apparent consumption used for nickel plate and bar.

The price that matches this metal is the United States molybdic oxide benchmark, estimated at 51 dollars per kilogram in 2025, up from 47.72 dollars in 2024 [MO_USGS_PRICE_OXIDE_2025, MO_USGS_PRICE_OXIDE_2024]. That quotation is for oxide, so ferromolybdenum or a molybdenum bearing alloy needs its own price before anyone treats the oxide figure as a selling price. The commercial form inside a corrosion grade is that alloy addition, not the oxide benchmark, which is why titanium is taken next as sponge and approved melt rather than as another nickel flat identity.
