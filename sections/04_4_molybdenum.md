## 4.4 Molybdenum

Molybdenum helps alloys resist hot acids and seawater, in amounts that depend on the grade. One tonne of 316L stainless steel contains about 21 kg, but Alloy C-276 holds about 160 kg and Alloy 625 about 90 kg [MO_ATI_625_MO, MO_HAYNES_C276_COMP, MO_OUTOKUMPU_316L_MO].

Much of the world's molybdenum is a by-product of copper mining, so supply responds slowly to price. The USGS estimates 2025 mine output at 260 kt, while the International Molybdenum Association reports 672.6 million pounds, about 305 kt, with almost all of the gap in China [MO_IMOA_PROD_2025, MO_USGS_MINE_CHINA_2025].

According to the USGS, only China and the United States produce from primary molybdenum mines. Since February 2025, China has required licences for molybdenum powder exports. The oxide and ferromolybdenum that melt shops buy are excluded [W_MOFCOM_2025_10, MO_USGS_MINE_CHINA_2025]. The US oxide price rose 7 percent to about USD 51 per kg in 2025, while European use fell 7 percent with the chemical slowdown [MO_IMOA_PROD_2025, MO_USGS_MINE_CHINA_2025].
