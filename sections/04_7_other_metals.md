## 4.7 Other Selected Metals

The remaining metals complete the picture in two ways, because zirconium and the minor superalloy elements are niche inputs, while copper, aluminium and manganese are large markets with a small high performance corner.

### 4.7.1 Zirconium

Zirconium shows why mineral output and specialty metal must be kept apart, since world production of zirconium concentrates reached about 1.2 Mt gross weight in 2025, led by Australia with 400 kt, mostly for ceramics, foundry sand and refractories [ZR_MINE_GROSS_2025_WORLD, ZR_MINE_GROSS_2025_AU]. The metal market is far smaller and divides into nuclear fuel cladding, which fuel makers and integrated producers such as ATI control themselves, and zirconium plate and tube for acid service, which is the open part of the market [ZR_ATI_ZR_SITES_2025].

### 4.7.2 Copper

Copper links the alloy market to electrification, although high performance products are a small part of it. World mine production reached about 23.2 Mt in 2025 on ICSG and IEA figures alike, flat rolled products take only 11 percent of first use against 63 percent for wire, and the price, which averaged USD 9,947 per tonne in 2025, set records above USD 14,500 in 2026 [CU_ICSG_MINE_2025, IEA_CM_CU_BALANCE_2025_2035, CU_IWCC_FIRSTUSE_2024, CU_WB_CU_2025, LME_CU_RECORD_2026]. The IEA expects mine supply from current projects to fall to 20.7 Mt by 2035 while demand rises from 27.8 Mt to 32.4 Mt under current policies, and it sizes the primary deficit in 2035 at about 25 percent [IEA_CM_CU_BALANCE_2025_2035, CU_IEA_GAP_2035]. Data centres illustrate the new demand, since a 1 MW rack at 54 VDC needs up to 200 kg of copper busbar, and the specialised forms are copper nickel and high conductivity strip [NVDA_800VDC].

### 4.7.3 Aluminium

Aluminium matters here mainly as a competitor, because it replaces heavier alloys wherever temperature and corrosion allow it. World primary production reached about 74 Mt in 2025, of which China produced 61 percent, while European primary output has shrunk to about 955 kt against demand of about 9 Mt a year [USGS_MCS2026_AL, IAI_PRIMARY_2025, FACE_EU_AL_2024, EA_DEMAND_PRIMARY].

### 4.7.4 Manganese

Manganese is an alloying addition whose tonnage follows carbon steel, and world mine output reached about 20 Mt of contained manganese in 2025, led by South Africa, Gabon, Ghana and Australia, which together supplied about 81 percent, and the ore price fell to about USD 4.5 per metric ton unit, so it is neither a supply risk nor a growth market for alloy producers [USGS_MCS2026_MN, IMNI_AR_2025].

### 4.7.5 Additional Metals

Iron matters through FeNi alloys such as FeNi36 for display masks and FeNi42 for lead frames, whose value is set by rolling capability rather than by iron supply. Niobium and rhenium matter for superalloys, because Alloy 718 contains about 5 percent niobium, which Brazil mines at about 93 percent of world output and CBMM there sells as vacuum grade nickel niobium, while single crystal turbine blades contain rhenium, of which only about 81 t were mined worldwide in 2025 [NB_USGS_BRAZIL_2025, CO_CBMM_NINB, USGS_MCS2026_RHENIUM, CO_RTX_RAWMAT_2025]. The analysis now turns to the industries that convert these materials into demand for alloys.
