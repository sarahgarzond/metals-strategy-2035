## 4.7 Other Selected Metals

The remaining metals complete the picture in two different ways, because zirconium and the minor superalloy elements are niche inputs, while copper, aluminium and manganese are large markets in which high performance products occupy only a small corner.

### 4.7.1 Zirconium

Zirconium shows most clearly why mineral output and specialty metal must be kept apart. World production of zirconium concentrates reached about 1.2 Mt gross weight in 2025, led by Australia with 400 kt, mostly for ceramics, foundry sand and refractories [ZR_MINE_GROSS_2025_WORLD, ZR_MINE_GROSS_2025_AU]. The metal market is far smaller and divides into nuclear fuel cladding, which fuel makers and integrated producers such as ATI control themselves, and zirconium plate and tube for acid service, which is the open part of the market [ZR_ATI_ZR_SITES_2025].

### 4.7.2 Copper

Copper connects the alloy market to electrification, but high performance products occupy only a small corner of a vast market. World mine production reached about 23.2 Mt in 2025, flat rolled products account for only 11 percent of first use against 63 percent for wire, and the price set LME records above USD 14,500 per tonne in 2026 while the IEA expects a primary supply deficit of about 25 percent by 2035 [CU_ICSG_MINE_2025, CU_IWCC_FIRSTUSE_2024, LME_CU_RECORD_2026, CU_IEA_GAP_2035]. Data centres illustrate the new demand, since a 1 MW rack at 54 VDC needs up to 200 kg of copper busbar, and the specialised forms are copper nickel and high conductivity strip [NVDA_800VDC].

### 4.7.3 Aluminium

Aluminium matters to the high performance metal market mainly as a competitor, because it replaces heavier alloys wherever temperature and corrosion allow it. World primary production reached about 74 Mt in 2025, of which China produced 61 percent, and the price averaged USD 2,632 per tonne, while European primary output has shrunk to about 955 kt against demand of about 9 Mt a year [USGS_MCS2026_AL, IAI_PRIMARY_2025, AL_PINK_2025, FACE_EU_AL_2024, EA_DEMAND_PRIMARY].

### 4.7.4 Manganese

Manganese is an alloying addition whose tonnage follows carbon steel rather than high performance alloys. World mine output reached about 20 Mt of contained manganese in 2025, led by South Africa, Gabon, Ghana and Australia, which together supplied about 81 percent, and the ore price fell to about USD 4.5 per metric ton unit, so manganese is neither a supply risk nor a growth market for alloy producers [USGS_MCS2026_MN, IMNI_AR_2025].

### 4.7.5 Additional Metals

A few further metals enter the market through specific applications and complete the alloy family. Iron matters through FeNi alloys such as FeNi36 for display masks and FeNi42 for lead frames, whose value is set by rolling capability rather than by iron supply. Niobium and rhenium matter for superalloys, because Alloy 718 contains about 5 percent niobium and single crystal turbine blades contain rhenium, of which only about 81 t were mined worldwide in 2025, mostly as a byproduct of copper and molybdenum [USGS_MCS2026_RHENIUM, CO_RTX_RAWMAT_2025]. With the supply side of all metals established, the analysis now turns to the industries that turn these materials into demand.
