## 4.7 Other Selected Metals

Zirconium and the minor superalloy elements serve narrow but demanding uses, while copper, aluminium, and manganese have large markets but small high-performance roles.

### 4.7.1 Zirconium

World output of zirconium concentrates reached about 1.2 Mt gross weight in 2025, led by Australia with 400 kt. Most goes into ceramics, foundry sand, and refractories, and the metal market is far smaller [ZR_MINE_GROSS_2025_AU]. Fuel makers and integrated producers such as ATI control their own nuclear cladding supply, so the open market covers mainly plate and tube for acid service [AERO_ATI_JETENG_2025].

### 4.7.2 Copper

Copper links high-performance materials to electrification, with 2025 mine production of about 23.2 Mt. Flat-rolled products take 11 percent of first use, against 63 percent for wire. Copper averaged USD 9,947 per tonne in 2025 and set records above USD 14,500 in 2026 [CU_IWCC_FIRSTUSE_2024, CU_ICSG_MINE_2025, IEA_CM_CO_DEMAND_2025_2035, LME_CU_RECORD_2026, CU_WB_CU_2025]. Under current policies, the IEA expects output from current mines to fall to 20.7 Mt by 2035, while demand rises from 27.8 Mt to 32.4 Mt. That implies a primary supply deficit of about 25 percent [CU_IEA_GAP_2035, IEA_CM_CO_DEMAND_2025_2035]. Specialized forms include copper-nickel and high-conductivity strip, such as the busbars in section 5.6.

### 4.7.3 Aluminium and Manganese

Aluminium can replace heavier alloys where temperatures and corrosion allow. World primary output reached about 74 Mt in 2025, 61 percent of it in China. European primary output has fallen to about 955 kt, against demand of about 9 Mt [EA_DEMAND_PRIMARY, FACE_EU_AL_2024, IAI_PRIMARY_2025, USGS_MCS2026_AL].

Manganese demand follows carbon steel output. Mines produced about 20 Mt of contained manganese in 2025, about 81 percent from South Africa, Gabon, Ghana, and Australia. The ore price fell to about USD 4.5 per metric ton unit, so supply risk and growth potential for alloy producers are limited [IMNI_AR_2025, USGS_MCS2026_MN].

### 4.7.4 Additional Metals

Iron matters in FeNi36 for display masks and FeNi42 for lead frames, whose value lies mainly in rolling to tight specifications. Alloy 718 contains about 5 percent niobium. Brazil mines about 93 percent of the world's niobium, and CBMM sells it as vacuum-grade nickel-niobium. Single-crystal turbine blades contain rhenium, of which mines produced only about 81 t in 2025 [CO_CBMM_NINB, CO_RTX_POWDER_2025, USGS_MCS2026_RHENIUM, NB_USGS_BRAZIL_2025]. These supply facts frame the industry demand in chapter 5.
