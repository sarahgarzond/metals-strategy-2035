# 9. Conclusion

This study asked how supply concentration, conversion and industry needs shape the opportunities in high performance materials through 2035, and its answer is that value lies in conversion and qualification rather than in metal volume. Nickel is in surplus while cobalt, tungsten and molybdenum are bound by quotas and licences, yet the countries that convert metals into qualified bar, strip and tube earn the trade surplus, and cobalt is a cost risk rather than a volume ceiling for superalloys.

The tier 1 model then locates demand, with energy technology and aerospace at P50 indices of 156 and 147 in 2035 and automotive flat. The most attractive measured opportunities are therefore turbine remelt stock, engine billet and precision FeNi foil in the United States, Europe and East Asia, followed by approval protected franchises, while solid oxide strip, fusion magnet tape, additive powder and small reactor tubing are tier 2 options to monitor against their signposts.

These findings have limits, because official statistics stop at the mill product and most indices rest on activity drivers and team weights, not measured alloy tonnes. Within those limits the decade to 2035 rewards producers that secure their inputs, qualify early with engine and turbine makers and invest only where signed demand has arrived.
