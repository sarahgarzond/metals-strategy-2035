# 9. Conclusion

The central question was how supply, conversion and industry needs shape opportunities in high performance materials through 2035, and the answer is that value lies in conversion and qualification rather than in metal volume. Converting countries earn the trade surplus, and because superalloys need only about 12 percent of 2035 cobalt mine supply, export controls raise cost rather than cap volume [MODEL_SUPPLY_OVERLAY_2035].

Demand grows fastest where alloys are qualified into equipment, with P50 indices of 141 in electronics, 136 in medical technology, 134 in aerospace and 133 in energy against 102 in automotive and 98 in oil and gas [MODEL_T1_RESULTS_2035]. Engine bar and billet for the United States and Europe is therefore the only priority 1 position, turbine remelt stock, CoCr implant bar and Alloy 690 follow as priority 2 and FeNi foil remains a gated option.

These answers have limits, as the results are indices rather than tonnes, the haircut rests on three tests, energy follows one turbine maker and no FeNi foil volume is published, but they suffice to tell producers to qualify with engine and turbine makers now and decide on melt capacity in 2027 and 2028 against signed agreements.
