## 5.6 Electronics and Electrical Engineering

Electronics is the industry in which the headline numbers are largest and the metal volumes smallest, and that contrast defines the opportunity. Semiconductor sales are booming and data centres are rewriting the rules of power distribution, yet the metals involved are precision strip, foil and high purity alloys measured in tonnes rather than in thousands of tonnes.

### 5.6.1 Demand, Geography and Market Size

The semiconductor boom is real, but its numbers must be read carefully before they are translated into metal. Semiconductor sales reached USD 795.6 billion in 2025, up 26.2 percent, and WSTS forecasts USD 1.51 trillion for 2026 because memory prices and sales more than triple, which shows that sales value can rise without a comparable rise in wafer or metal volume [WSTS_WORLD_2025, WSTS_WORLD_2026_SPRING]. Equipment spending is the closer indicator, and SEMI expects 300 mm fab equipment spending of USD 149 billion in 2026 and USD 227 billion in 2029, with the toolmakers concentrated in the United States, Japan and Europe and the fabs in Taiwan, Korea, China and increasingly the United States [SEMI_300MM_2026, SEMI_300MM_2029]. Germany's electrical and electronics industry, by contrast, added EUR 112.2 billion of value in 2023 but showed flat real output in 2025 [STAN_DEU_C26C27_B1G_2023, ZVEI_PROD_REAL].

### 5.6.2 Applications and Opportunities

Data centres and displays create the most interesting new applications for high performance metals. NVIDIA states that a 1 MW rack at 54 VDC needs up to 200 kg of copper busbar and plans 800 VDC distribution with its Kyber racks in 2027, together with partners such as Infineon, Vertiv, Eaton and Schneider Electric, and Vertiv raised its copper hedge to 12,946 t by September 2026 [NVDA_800VDC, CO_VERTIV_COPPERHEDGE_2026]. Dai Nippon Printing doubled its capacity for OLED metal masks with an 8th generation line at Kurosaki and holds patents on the invar material that it expects to remain protected for the foreseeable future [DNP_KUROSAKI_2024, DNP_INVAR_2024].

| Outlook | Applications | Materials and product forms |

| Grows | OLED fine metal masks for IT panels | FeNi36 ultra thin foil |
| Grows | Current sensors and shielding in power electronics and data centres | Soft magnetic FeNi strip |

| Stable | Glass seals, lead frames, resistance heating | FeNi42 and FeNi29Co18 strip, resistance wire |

*Source: team assessment based on NVIDIA, Dai Nippon Printing, SEMI and Infineon evidence [NVDA_800VDC, DNP_KUROSAKI_2024, SEMI_300MM_2026, CO_INFINEON_FAB_2026].*

The filings of TSMC, Applied Materials and Lam Research name no specific alloy, which means that suppliers reach this market through component makers rather than through the toolmakers themselves. Infineon's EUR 5 billion Smart Power Fab in Dresden, opened in July 2026, shows that Europe still adds capacity for power electronics, the segment that uses the most soft magnetic and lead frame alloys [CO_INFINEON_FAB_2026].

Implications for suppliers:

* Partner with mask makers to qualify FeNi36 ultra thin foil rather than compete with protected processes.
* Qualify high purity alloy strip and tube with the component suppliers of fab gas systems.
