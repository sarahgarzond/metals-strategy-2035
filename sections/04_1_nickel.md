## 4.1 Nickel

Nickel is the base of the company's portfolio, and its supply has moved decisively to one country within a decade. World mine production of contained nickel reached an estimated 3.90 Mt in 2025, of which Indonesia supplied about 2.60 Mt, or roughly two thirds [NI_USGS_MINE_W_2025, NI_USGS_MINE_ID_2025]. Mine output is not the metal a mill buys, however, because primary nickel is a later stage, and the International Nickel Study Group puts world primary production at 3.875 Mt in 2025 against usage of 3.577 Mt [NI_INSG_PROD_2025, NI_INSG_USE_2025]. Just over half of primary supply in China and Indonesia is nickel pig iron, which feeds stainless steel and is not the class 1 metal that vacuum melted alloys require [NI_INSG_NPI_2025].

That oversupply explains the price, and the price explains why raw material cost is not the company's main constraint. The World Bank nickel price averaged USD 25,834 per tonne in 2022 and USD 15,162 in 2025, a fall of about 41 percent [NI_WB_AVG_2022, NI_WB_AVG_2025]. The study group reported a surplus of 299 kt in 2025 but cut its 2026 surplus forecast to only 19 kt, because Indonesia reduced its ore mining quota [NI_INSG_BAL_2025, NI_INSG_BAL_2026]. The initial 2026 quota of 260 to 270 Mt of ore was later revised to 290 to 300 Mt, which shows that Jakarta now manages the price through volume [ARGUS_NI_RKAB_2026, NI_RKAB_2026_REV].

For the company the relevant market starts after the commodity, where conversion, qualification and product form create value. Stainless steel remains the first use of nickel, with world melt shop output of 64.2 Mt in 2025, of which the European Union produced 5.7 Mt [NI_WS_MELT_2025]. German exports of nickel plate, sheet and strip (HS 7506) reached 17,775 t worth USD 578 million in 2024, an average unit value near USD 32,500 per tonne, about twice the exchange price, and Eurostat records the same product family within 1.8 percent [NI_CT_DE_7506_2024_T, NI_PC_DE_SHEET_X_2024]. Competitors confirm the scale of the qualified segment, as Acerinox's High Performance Alloys division, which combines VDM Metals and Haynes, melted 83 kt in 2025 and sold EUR 1,683 million [NI_ACX_HPA_2025], while ATI consumed about 70 million pounds of nickel and passes most price moves through surcharges [NI_ATI_NI_LB_2025].

The implication is that nickel availability is secure in volume but not in grade, so the company should secure class 1 metal and qualified revert under surcharge contracts rather than take price positions. Cobalt, the second superalloy element, shows the opposite pattern of scarcity created by policy.
