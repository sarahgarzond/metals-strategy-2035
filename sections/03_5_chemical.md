## 3.5 Chemical process industry

Because it builds and maintains acid, chlorine and organic acid plants worldwide, the chemical process industry is the largest sector in the market model, with about EUR 9.0bn of high performance metal demand in 2025 rising to about EUR 11.5bn in 2035 at 2.5% p.a. [MODEL_CHEM_SECTOR]. This low confidence team estimate is allocated top down from the EUR 50.6bn addressable market [MODEL_TOTAL_2025] and still needs a bottom up check by alloy. The host industry sold roughly EUR 5.1 trillion of chemicals in 2024 by team arithmetic on Cefic data [CEFIC_WORLD_2024], but the American Chemistry Council sees global output growth slowing from 2.6% in 2025 to 0.5% in 2026 after the Gulf conflict, before 3.2% in 2027 [ACC_JAN_2026, ACC_MIDYEAR_2026, ATRADIUS_CHEM_2026]. A sourced forecast to 2035 is still missing and marked TODO(data), because searches for UNEP and Cefic scenarios returned no usable figure. Mill data support caution, as Haynes reported chemical processing revenue of USD 54.7m in the nine months to June 2024, down 20.7% and about 12% of net revenue [HAYNES_10Q_2024], which raises the question of what drives demand.

Remaining growth comes from capacity additions outside Europe, while the risks sit in Europe and in Chinese capital spending. On the driver side, global acetic acid capacity is forecast to rise from 20.51 Mtpa in 2023 to 29.09 Mtpa in 2028, with China adding 6.70 Mtpa and more than 90% of the total [GD_ACETIC_2028], and the Gulf is building chlor alkali and PVC complexes such as TA'ZIZ in Ruwais with 500 kt/yr of PVC [NUCERA_TAZIZ]. On the risk side, Europe has closed 37 Mt of chemical capacity since 2022 [CEFIC_CLOSURES] and EU output in April 2026 stood 16% below its pre crisis level [CEFIC_IRIS_2026_04]. Chinese fixed asset investment in chemical raw materials fell 8.1% in the first eight months of 2026 [NBS_FAI_CHEM_2026], and Acerinox, owner of VDM Metals and Haynes, reported delayed chemical projects in 2025 [ACX_Q3_2025_CALL, ACX_FY2025_HPA], so new process technologies matter more for alloy demand than a cyclical recovery.

Beyond capacity cycles, three emerging technologies are changing which alloys the sector buys and in which product forms. Electrified base chemicals come first, led by the electrically heated steam cracker demonstration of BASF, SABIC and Linde in Ludwigshafen [BASF_ECRACKER_2024]. Battery materials hydrometallurgy is the second, with high pressure acid leach (HPAL) plants in Indonesia feeding nickel and cobalt sulphate [VALE_POMALAA_2026]. The third is PFAS free plant design, because the ECHA committees backed a universal PFAS restriction with targeted derogations in March 2026 [ECHA_PFAS_2026] and an average European chemical plant holds about 20,000 PFAS containing elements outside piping [CEFIC_PFAS_EQUIP_2026]. Each shift translates into specific grades and product forms, which the next paragraph sets out.

The resulting material basket is broad, but it maps closely onto the Client's portfolio of alloys and forms. NiCrMo alloys such as Alloy C-276, C-22 and Alloy 59 in plate, sheet, clad plate and matching filler wire serve hydrochloric acid, chlorinated organics and pharmaceutical reactors, because molybdenum and tungsten resist reducing acids and pitting. Alloy 31, 904L and 6Mo stainless steels cover sulfuric and phosphoric acid at lower cost, while pure nickel 200 and 201 in strip and mesh resists hot caustic soda and forms the cathodes of membrane chlor alkali cells [INEOS_MEMBRANE_NI]. Zirconium 702 and 705 plate and tube are standard for hot acetic acid and line bimetallic urea stripper tubes [ALLEIMA_STRIPPER], while Alloy 800H/HT and 617 serve crackers and reformers. In the model these needs condense into three cells, NiCrMo plate and clad at EUR 2.6bn, battery materials refining at EUR 0.6bn and zirconium at EUR 0.3bn in 2025 [MODEL_CHEM_CELLS], whose location now follows.

Demand for these materials follows new chemical capacity, and that capacity has moved towards Asia and the Gulf. China accounted for 46% of world chemical sales in 2024 against 13% for the EU [CEFIC_FF2025_SHARES], and Asia adds 9.92 Mtpa of acetic acid capacity from 2025 to 2030, led by India [GD_ACETIC_2030]. The Gulf leads in chlor alkali and PVC [NUCERA_MIDEAST_2025] and Indonesia in HPAL, while Europe and the United States remain installed bases dominated by maintenance, a split that defines which customers matter.

Within these hubs plant owners rarely fix materials alone, because licensors and EPCs set the specification early. Owners include BASF, SABIC, INEOS, TA'ZIZ, Huayou and Vale Indonesia, while licensors and EPCs include Stamicarbon, Casale, thyssenkrupp nucera, Linde Engineering and China ENFI. Fabricators such as De Dietrich, Alfa Laval and Sulzer convert plate and sheet into equipment for them. These competitive positions shift with substitution, which cuts both ways for high performance metals. Metals gain where PFAS rules push lined equipment towards solid or clad NiCrMo alloys, since the Cefic study ranks nickel alloys and glass closest to fluoropolymers in chemical compatibility [CEFIC_PFAS_EQUIP_2026], yet an industry funded study for refiners finds only about 10% of fluoropolymer piping replaceable [RICARDO_PFAS_REFINING], so the upside is uncertain in size. Metals lose where titanium clad steel remains the standard for HPAL autoclaves [HPAL_TI_CLAD] and where super duplex grades such as SAF 2906, launched by Alleima in June 2025, take over urea high pressure sections [ALLEIMA_SAF2906]. Glass lined steel, graphite and silicon carbide also compete in acid service, which narrows the attractive positions in the value chain.

The most attractive value chain steps for the Client are therefore those where specification and service count for more than the price per tonne. Specification work with licensors such as Stamicarbon and thyssenkrupp nucera fixes grades in standard designs for years, and thin NiCrMo and zirconium sheet for clad plate and nickel strip for electrodes match the Client's strengths. Welding consumables and maintenance supply for the European and US installed base keep their value even while European capacity closes [CEFIC_CLOSURES], whereas commodity C-276 plate for Chinese new builds faces price pressure, with Haynes chemical prices down 19% in one quarter [HAYNES_10Q_2024]. Four spotlight projects show how these material and value chain choices look in concrete plants.

1. Vale Indonesia, Huayou and Ford received the first two of five autoclaves for the 120 kt/yr Pomalaa HPAL plant in Sulawesi in January 2026, with start up planned for the third quarter of 2026 [VALE_POMALAA_2026]. Autoclaves are titanium clad steel, while nickel alloy and super duplex plate, pipe and filler serve flash vessels and piping, likely from Chinese mills and explosion cladders (team view).
2. Thyssenkrupp nucera signed its largest new build chlor alkali order for a Middle East plant in December 2025, with completion due at the end of 2028 [NUCERA_MIDEAST_2025]. Membrane electrolysers need nickel cathode sheet and mesh next to titanium anodes [INEOS_MEMBRANE_NI], so nickel strip mills such as VDM Metals are likely suppliers.
3. BASF, SABIC and Linde have run the 6 MW electric cracker demonstration in Ludwigshafen since April 2024 [BASF_ECRACKER_2024], which in the team's view needs creep resistant NiCrFe tube and heating alloys, although alloy and suppliers are undisclosed (TODO(data)).
4. INEOS and Zhejiang Petrochemical plan to start their 1.0 Mtpa Daishan acetic acid plant in 2026 [GD_ACETIC_2028], a process that by industry practice relies on Zr 702 plate and tube, likely from ATI or Chinese zirconium producers.
