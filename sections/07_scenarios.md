# 7. Market Scenarios to 2035

The Outlook dimension turns the sector evidence into a common yardstick, so that industries measured in different units can be compared on one scale. The scenarios are a sensitivity analysis of demand for the specialty metal applications identified in chapter 5, not a forecast of industry revenue, metal prices or company sales, and no probabilities are assigned.

## 7.1 Market Model and Scenario Assumptions

Demand in each industry is indexed to 100 in 2025 and compounded for ten years with the formula I(2035) = 100 × (1 + g)^10, where g is the annual growth rate of relevant alloy demand. Each rate is a team assumption that is anchored to the strongest indicator in chapter 5 and adjusted for material intensity and substitution, as Table 11 shows. Metal prices and exchange rates are held constant, so the indices measure volume and mix rather than value.

Table 11: Scenario assumptions and their evidence anchors

| Industry | Downside / base / upside, % a year | Evidence anchor for the base case |
|---|---|---|
| Aerospace | 2.0 / 4.5 / 6.0 | Passenger fleet about 3.4% a year, engine output and services growing faster (services 3.8% a year) |
| Energy and environmental technology | 0.5 / 4.0 / 6.5 | Gas turbine capacity up from 17 GW to above 30 GW a year, nuclear from about 2030, hydrogen only in the upside |
| Electronics and electrical engineering | 0.0 / 3.0 / 5.0 | 300 mm equipment spending to USD 227 bn in 2029, flat German real output |
| Medical technology | 1.0 / 2.5 / 4.0 | Rising joint replacement rates, European spending growth of 1.8% in 2025 |
| Chemical processing | −1.0 / 1.0 / 2.5 | EU utilisation of 75%, growth concentrated in Asia |
| Oil and gas | −1.0 / 0.5 / 1.5 | Upstream investment flat near USD 550 bn, gas up, oil down |
| Automotive | −3.0 / −1.5 / 0.0 | Electric share of car sales rising from 25% to about 50% by 2035 |

*Source: team assumptions based on the evidence in chapter 5 [AERO_AIRBUS_GSF2026_SERVICES_2045, CO_SIEMENSENERGY_CMD_2025, SEMI_300MM_2029, MED_MTE_EUR_2025, CEFIC_CAPUTIL_EU27_Q3_2026, OG_WEI2026_UPSTREAM_OG_2026, IEA_GEVO_EV_SHARE_2035].*

The downside case assumes delayed projects, weak replacement spending and faster substitution, for example by ceramic matrix composites in engines or lithium iron phosphate in batteries. The base case assumes steady replacement demand, gradual electrification and the engine and turbine ramp up that manufacturers have announced. The upside case adds faster adoption of qualified energy components, including electrolysers and solid oxide fuel cells, and a stronger European defence build up.

Two results of the scenario analysis matter most for the strategy of the company. Aerospace and energy technology together are the only industries that grow by more than 40 percent in the base case, and both depend on the same product family of vacuum melted superalloy bar and billet, so one capacity decision serves two markets. The spread between downside and upside is widest in energy technology, which argues for staged investment that follows signed orders. Regionally the growth sits in European and American engine and turbine supply chains, in Asian chemical construction and in European and American implant markets, while European automotive and base chemical demand declines, which is the geography that chapter 8 turns into priorities. Converting the indices into company revenue requires the company's own sales mix by industry, which appendix A4 lists as the first internal data request.
