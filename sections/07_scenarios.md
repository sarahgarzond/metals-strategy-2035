# 7. Market Scenarios to 2035

The 2035 outlook tests demand for the relevant applications under three cases, and it is not a forecast of industry revenue, company sales or alloy tonnes. The 2025 physical base for each priority is unknown, pending the shipment map, because no defensible share of the mixed flat code or of the rounded bar code can be stated [AC_EU_NI_FLAT_2024, AC_EU_NI_BAR_2024]. The indices are therefore not multiplied by a tonne range in this chapter or in Chapter 8. Metal prices are held constant, and no company total is printed, because 2025 shipment weights were not available.

## 7.1 Market Model and Scenario Assumptions

For an industry and a scenario, the 2035 index equals 100 times (1 plus the annual rate) to the power 10. A company weighted index would multiply each industry index by that industry's 2025 shipment share. Those weights were not supplied, and the rates are author choices, so each line below names the driver, the year and the adjustment, using figures already in the report.

Automotive uses the team estimate that cars without a plug fall from 58.2 million to 42.9 million, and a car with a plug can still carry an engine. The downside of 2 percent a year is the faster loss of diesel only parts. The base of 1 percent a year is that car path with turbo content slowing the decline. The upside of 0.5 percent a year is the hybrid path, so the upside is not a flat zero [TE_AUTO_UPSIDE_2035]. The October 2026 committee vote stays out of the model because it is not a logged value row.

Oil and gas uses the 2025 upstream level of just under USD 570 billion as context for replacement, not as a 2035 spending path [OG_WEI2025_UPSTREAM_OIL_2025]. The downside of 1 percent a year is slower replacement, the base of 0.5 percent a year is replacement that holds, and the upside of 1.5 percent a year is that replacement plus funded gas projects. None of these rates is the USD 65 billion Current Policies gap.

Energy uses committed low emissions hydrogen of 4.3 million tonnes a year by 2030 as the bankable class [EE_H2_COMMIT_2030]. The downside of zero is that committed class with no extra alloy, the base of 3 percent a year allows some replacement equipment on committed projects, and the upside of 6 percent a year is the conditional case above 6 million tonnes if further projects are decided, which is not a stock already committed. Electricity growth is not applied to alloy tonnes in any of the three cases.

Aerospace separates new engine hardware from shop visits, and LEAP deliveries rose 28 percent in 2025, and shop visits are the larger OEM profit pool, while the mill sees weld wire, some replacement hardware and the original billet [CO_SAFRAN_LEAP_2025, CO_GE_CES_2025, CO_SAFRAN_PROP_2025]. The fleet path of about 3.4 percent a year is a team interpolation from 23,310 to 45,550 and is not the alloy rate [TE_AERO_FLEET_2035]. The base of 3 percent a year sits near that interpolation and is still not a tonne forecast. The upside of 4.5 percent a year assumes both hardware and shop visits stay tight. The downside of 1 percent a year still compounds because ceramic matrix composites take the hottest static parts and do not retire the disc. Growth in that downside stops if an approval is missed, if substitution reaches the disc, or if both occur. The index is not a forecast of alloy tonnes, and 11,499 tonnes of aviation cobalt are not scaled into a nickel indicator [AERO_CI_AVIATION_CO_2025].

Chemicals uses replacement rather than EU chemical sales, so the downside of 0.5 percent a year is the weak 2025 chemical year persisting [ACERINOX_HPA_SALES_2025]. The base of 1.5 percent a year is replacement of named grades. The upside of 3 percent a year is funded export projects through fabricators.

Electronics uses component alloys rather than the 26.2 percent chip growth [WSTS_WORLD_2025]. The downside is a zero rate because chip growth is not the alloy rate, the base of 2.5 percent a year is repeat orders for controlled properties, and the upside of 4.5 percent a year is that book with a wider set of named stampers. Chapter 72 tonnes are not taken from the nickel flat code in any of those cases.

Medical procedures compound at 3.36 percent a year on the constant set from 2013 to 2023, while the device illustration runs at 5.3 percent a year from 2023 to 2035 [MED_OECD_HK_COUNT_2023]. The application row here is 2 percent a year from 2025 in the base, which is a slower author choice because the mill ceiling is the Carpenter medical book and China tenders are stopped [MED_CRS_MED_EXSUR_2026]. The downside is 0.5 percent a year and the upside is 3.5 percent a year. The two windows are different, and neither window is converted into a bar tonne.

Products stay tied to the codes in Chapter 6, and regions stay qualitative because shipment shares are unavailable. In the base case the priorities are aerospace billet, approved sheet and repair wire in Europe and North America, chemical plate and welding products through fabricators, and precision strip and wire for electrical components where the code is the one the grade ships in. Energy products follow committed projects rather than the announced pipeline, and the 2025 tonne range for each priority is unknown, pending the shipment map, so Chapter 8 does not convert Table 16 into a capacity tonne. Chapter 8 turns the same ranking into a sequence of commercial actions.
