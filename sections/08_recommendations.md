The first priority is vacuum melted superalloy bar, billet and repair wire for jet engines and gas turbines, sold to engine makers and their forgers in Europe and the United States, because it combines the highest growth with the highest entry barriers. Siemens Energy's capacity is sold out to 2028, Safran's new press starts in 2029, and ATI quotes forging lead times beyond two years, so qualified capacity is the scarce resource [CO_SIEMENSENERGY_CMD_2025, AERO_SAFRAN_GENNEVILLIERS_2026, CO_ATI_LEAD_2026].

The second tier protects and grows the existing franchises where the company's approvals, not volume, create value. Chemical plate and welding consumables remain the largest business but must follow construction to Asia through EPC contractors, while precision FeNi strip, medical CoCr and zirconium bar and sour service products grow selectively with named buyers such as Infineon, DNP, Zimmer Biomet, Stryker, BUTTING and TechnipFMC.

The third tier consists of options that are released for investment only after customer milestones. Fuel cell interconnect strip, nuclear Alloy 690 and zirconium, and electrolyser nickel each have credible demand but uncertain timing, and thyssenkrupp nucera's collapse in green hydrogen orders shows why the company should not build capacity ahead of signed volumes [CO_TKNUCERA_ORDERS_2025].

## 8.2 Recommended Actions and Implementation Roadmap

The roadmap sequences five initiatives over three horizons, so that each investment is released only after the previous step has proven demand.

*Source: team recommendation based on chapters 4 to 7.*

Execution of the roadmap follows three governance rules that protect the company's capital. Capacity is added only where qualified demand shows a constraint and the investment earns its hurdle rate in the downside scenario of chapter 7. Commercial resources follow the tiers of Table 20, which means fewer resources for diesel engine parts and European new build chemicals. Progress is reviewed quarterly against approvals, repeat orders, contribution margin per bottleneck hour and on time delivery.

The main risks to this strategy are known, and each of them can be managed with a specific response. Cobalt export quotas and Indonesian nickel policy are addressed by multi sourcing and revert recovery, the substitution of nickel alloys by ceramic matrix composites and titanium aluminide by early participation in customer development programmes, and new Asian capacity at Daido and Chinese producers by fast qualification before 2030 [CO_DAIDO_HIGHALLOY_2026].
