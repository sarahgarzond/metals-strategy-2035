# 8. Strategic Recommendations

The previous chapters followed the chain from material to growth, and this chapter brings its links together to identify where the most attractive opportunities lie. The recommendations address producers of high performance metals in general, because a market study can show where value lies but not which producer is best placed to capture it.

The ranking points to one clear priority, which is vacuum melted superalloy bar, billet, remelt stock and repair wire for the engine and turbine supply chains of Europe and the United States. This segment combines the highest growth with the highest barriers, and its scarce resource is qualified capacity rather than metal, since Siemens Energy's turbine capacity is sold out to 2028 and ATI quotes forging lead times beyond two years [CO_SIEMENSENERGY_CMD_2025, CO_ATI_LEAD_2026]. The leaders are moving fast, as Safran and Airbus co own Aubert & Duval, the American engine makers rely on ATI, PCC and Carpenter under long term agreements, and Daido is building new capacity in Japan, so the realistic role for a new entrant is that of a qualified second source [CO_AD_WEB_2025, AERO_ATI_JETENG_2025, CO_DAIDO_HIGHALLOY_2026]. The peer benchmark in appendix A6 adds the financial argument, because the producers most exposed to aerospace earn operating margins of 14 to 25 percent, while the European flat product groups earn single digit margins [CO_HWM_SALES_2025, CO_CRS_OPINC_2026, CO_ATI_SALES_2025, CO_OUTOKUMPU_SALES_2025].

Precision FeNi strip and foil completes the first tier, because it combines secure raw materials with technology that few producers master. The second tier holds franchises protected by approvals, such as sour service products, chemical plate and cobalt chromium implant bar, and options with uncertain timing, such as Alloy 690 for nuclear tube makers and coated strip for solid oxide cells, while electrolyser nickel and engine alloys for cars close the ranking [CO_TKNUCERA_ORDERS_2025, IEA_GEVO_EV_SHARE_2035]. Each position depends on approvals whose gates differ by industry, as appendix A7 shows, so approvals rather than capacity set the pace of any strategy in this market.

## 8.2 Recommended Actions and Implementation Roadmap

A producer that wants to capture these opportunities should sequence its moves so that capacity follows signed demand, and Table 15 sets out such a sequence over three horizons.

Table 15: Implementation roadmap 2026 to 2035 for high performance metal producers

| Initiative | 2026 to 2028 | 2029 to 2031 | 2032 to 2035 | Investment benchmark and gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Superalloy engine and turbine programme | Qualify bar, billet, remelt stock and sheet with forgers and casting houses, sign long term agreements | Add remelt and bar finishing capacity against signed volumes | Become an established second source for hot section material | Outokumpu invests about EUR 150 m for 13 kt of high nickel capacity. Gate: signed agreements for at least half of new capacity |
| 2. Repair and additive manufacturing | Launch repair wire, qualify powder | Supply repair shops in Europe and Asia | Powder for serial printed parts | Atomiser and wire lines. Gate: first engine powder approval |
| 3. Asian fabricator network | Key accounts with engineering contractors in India and Southeast Asia | Stock and distribution in Asia | Share of new build projects | Working capital rather than plant. Gate: rising order intake outside Europe |
| 4. Precision and medical materials | Partnerships with mask makers, sensor makers and implant makers | Grow FeNi foil and CoCr powder | Leading European source | Rolling and atomising upgrades. Gate: signed partnership |
| 5. Raw material resilience | Several sources of cobalt, class 1 nickel and chromium metal, surcharge clauses | Closed loop revert with aerospace customers | Low carbon product offer | Contract and stock costs. Gate: no single source above half of any critical input |

*Source: team recommendation. Investment figures are external benchmarks from company reports [CO_OUTOKUMPU_HINI_CAP_2026], not cost estimates.*

Focus also means letting go, and four activities deserve fewer resources in this market: diesel only engine parts such as glow plugs, new build projects in European base chemicals, speculative electrolyser capacity before funded projects place signed orders, and nuclear fuel cladding zirconium, which remains captive to the fuel makers. The main risks to this sequence, from slow qualification to substitution by ceramic matrix composites and new Asian capacity, are listed with early signals, responses and kill criteria in appendix A8, so that each initiative carries a defined point at which to stop.
