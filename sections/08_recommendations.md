# 8. Strategic Recommendations

Chapter 8 integrates the four dimensions of the framework and answers the central question of where supply, trade and demand create the most attractive opportunities for the company through 2035. Each opportunity combines a material, a product form, an application, an industry and a region, it is scored on the eight criteria introduced in chapter 3, and it is checked against the capability it requires and the competitors that already hold the position.

Each opportunity also passes a capability gate that internal data must confirm before investment (appendix A4): vacuum induction melting with vacuum arc or electroslag remelting and bar finishing for the superalloy opportunities, atomisation and wire drawing for powder and repair wire, thin gauge cold rolling for FeNi strip and foil, and the NORSOK, API and nuclear approvals for the sour service and Alloy 690 options.

The first priority is vacuum melted superalloy bar, billet, remelt stock, sheet and repair wire for engine makers, turbine builders and their forgers and casting houses in Europe and the United States, because it combines the highest growth with the highest entry barriers. Siemens Energy's capacity is sold out to 2028 and ATI quotes forging lead times beyond two years, so qualified capacity is the scarce resource [CO_SIEMENSENERGY_CMD_2025, CO_ATI_LEAD_2026]. The case is a second source strategy, because Safran and Airbus co own Aubert & Duval and the American engine makers rely on ATI, PCC and Carpenter under long term agreements [CO_AD_WEB_2025, AERO_ATI_JETENG_2025].

The peer benchmark in appendix A6 shows that the leaders in this segment earn much higher margins than the European flat product groups and are all adding melt and forge capacity now, which both validates the priority and narrows the window.

The second tier protects and grows the franchises where the company's approvals, rather than volume, create value. Precision FeNi strip and foil, CoCr implant bar and powder, chemical plate with welding consumables, and sour service products each have named buyers, such as Infineon and Dai Nippon Printing, Zimmer Biomet and Stryker, the fabricators of Asian chemical plants, and clad pipe makers such as BUTTING. The third tier consists of options that are released only after customer milestones, because coated interconnect strip, Alloy 690 for nuclear tube makers and electrolyser nickel each have credible demand but uncertain timing, as thyssenkrupp nucera's collapse in green hydrogen orders shows [CO_TKNUCERA_ORDERS_2025].

Each tier depends on qualifications whose owners and gates differ by industry, as Table 14 shows, and these approvals rather than capacity set the speed of the strategy.

## 8.2 Recommended Actions and Implementation Roadmap

The roadmap sequences five initiatives over three horizons, and each capacity step is released only when its gate is met, so that the company never builds ahead of signed demand.

Table 15: Implementation roadmap 2026 to 2035

Superalloy engine and turbine programme (head of aerospace and energy) | Qualify bar, billet, remelt stock and sheet with forgers and casting houses, sign long term agreements | Add remelt and bar finishing capacity against signed volumes | Second European source for hot section material | VDM's comparable programme cost EUR 67 m for about 15% more sales. Gate: signed agreements for at least half of the new capacity by end 2028 |

*Source: team recommendation. Investment figures are external benchmarks from Table A9 in appendix A6, not company estimates.*

Focus also means stopping, and five activities should release commercial and production resources for the tiers above:

* Stop new development and capacity for diesel only engine parts such as glow plugs, whose volume falls fastest.
* Stop chasing European new build projects in base chemicals, where plants are closing rather than expanding.
* Stop speculative capacity for electrolysers until funded projects place signed orders with stack makers.
* Stop pursuing nuclear fuel cladding zirconium, which is captive to the fuel makers and not an open market.
* Stop price led volume business in standard grades that competes with stainless and duplex alternatives.

Execution of the roadmap follows three governance rules that protect the company's capital. Capacity is added only where qualified demand shows a constraint and the investment passes the company's hurdle rate on its own downside volumes, commercial resources follow the tiers of Table 13, and progress is reviewed quarterly against approvals, repeat orders, contribution margin per bottleneck hour and on time delivery.
