# 8. Strategic Recommendations

## 8.1 Priority Products, Industries, Regions, and Customers

Table 17 scores each opportunity from 1 to 5 for a qualified producer that already melts and rolls these alloys, weighting market 15 percent, growth 20, supply security 15 and the others 10 percent each, with the first three computed by rule from chapters 5 to 7, the rest team judgement on the anchors of Table A4 and unmeasured opportunities capped as options [MODEL_SCORING_2026].

Engine bar and billet is the only priority 1 position, because every link points the same way, as triple melt Alloy 718 and 720Li billet is mostly surplus nickel with cobalt as a minor element (chapter 4) and serves the largest measured end use, 11,499 t of aviation cobalt, through forgers bound to GE Aerospace, Safran, Rolls-Royce and Pratt and Whitney (chapter 5) [AERO_CI_AVIATION_CO_2025, AERO_ATI_JETENG_2025]. German nickel bar and wire exports of about USD 111 m each to the United Kingdom and the United States follow these engine plants (chapter 6) [COMTRADE_7505_2019_2024]. The rank is fragile, as aerospace growth of 2.98 percent sits at the 3.0 percent band edge and the score clears its threshold by 0.05, so either shock case of Table 15 moves it to priority 2.

Turbine remelt stock and combustor sheet follow at 3.65 as the second leg of the same vacuum melt shop, serving 8.5 kt of non aviation superalloy cobalt, mainly in gas turbines, while Siemens Energy is sold out to 2028 on a path from 17 to 30 GW a year [MODEL_CO_SUPER_NONAV_2025, CO_SIEMENSENERGY_CMD_2025]. It rests on one turbine maker's guidance but rises to priority 1 under equal weights (3.750) or entry barriers at 20 percent (3.800), and casting houses such as CPP, which GE Aerospace agreed to buy in 2026, source from ATI, PCC, Carpenter and Aubert & Duval [CO_GE_CPP_2026].

The other priority 2 positions are approval protected franchises with less open demand, as CoCr implant bar to ASTM F1537 and F75 scores 3.45 on hip and knee procedures growing by 3.0 to 4.1 percent a year and Carpenter medical sales of USD 278.4 m in fiscal 2026, while a cobalt content of about 60 percent caps supply security at 2 for buyers such as Stryker and Zimmer Biomet [MED_OECD_HK_COUNT_2023, CO_CRS_END_2026, CO_ZBH_COMMODITY_2025]. Alloy 690 scores 3.15 on secure inputs, but its market score of 2 is an upper bound, because Alleima melts its own alloy for the steam generator tube of section 5.3.2 and most tube makers are integrated, so forgings and hollows suit only named reactor programmes [CO_ALLEIMA_NUCLEAR_2025]. Crowded AM powder stays a by product of the bar business, FeNi36 foil remains a gated option because no foil volume exists and mask free OLED routes are under test, and the stop list covers diesel only parts, European base chemical new build, unfunded electrolysers and captive cladding zirconium [DNP_INVAR_2024].

## 8.2 Recommended Actions and Implementation Roadmap

The roadmap in Table 18 qualifies before it builds, starting with superalloy because the window for a new source narrows as ATI's fifth Monroe VIM furnace adds about 9 kt a year from 2027 and Safran's Gennevilliers press starts in 2029, so the go or no go decision on melt capacity falls in 2027 and 2028 [ARGUS_SUPERALLOY, AERO_SAFRAN_GENNEVILLIERS_2026]. Only Outokumpu's Avesta gives a cost per tonne, about EUR 150 m for 13 kt or EUR 11,500 per annual tonne of flat capacity, while Carpenter's capex of USD 243 m in fiscal 2026 and USD 355 to 375 m guided for 2027, mainly Athens, and Aperam's EUR 160 m for four sites disclose no tonnes [CO_OUTOKUMPU_AVESTA_NI_2026, CO_CRS_CAPEX_2026, CO_APERAM_INVEST_2026].
