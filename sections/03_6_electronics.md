## 3.6 Electronics and electrical engineering

Electronics and electrical engineering is the fastest growing sector in the market model, with demand for the Client's type of high performance metals estimated at EUR 6.5bn in 2025 and EUR 13.4bn in 2035, which equals 7.5% per year (team estimate) [MODEL_SEC_ELEC]. The end market grows far faster in value, because global semiconductor sales reached USD 795.6bn in 2025 [WSTS_SEMI_2025] and are forecast at USD 1.51tn in 2026 after market growth of almost 90% in a single year, led by memory [WSTS_FC_2026]. McKinsey places the 2030 base case at USD 2.3tn but attributes most of the rise to higher prices and product mix while unit shipments stay nearly flat [MCK_SEMI_2030], and figures for 2035 exist only as executive remarks of USD 2tn to 3tn [SEMI_2035_OUTLOOK]. Metal tonnage therefore follows physical capacity rather than chip revenue, and SEMI expects installed 300mm wafer capacity to grow by about 7% per year from 2026 to 2029 [SEMI_WFF_2026], which is the more reliable anchor for the Client and leads to the question of what drives that capacity.

Because capacity follows capital spending, the strongest driver is the build out of artificial intelligence, with the four largest hyperscalers guiding USD 720bn to 745bn of capital expenditure for 2026 against about USD 410bn in 2025 [HYPER_CAPEX_2026]. Fab equipment spending is set to reach USD 152bn in 2026 and USD 166bn in 2027 [SEMI_WFF_2026], while data centre electricity use is projected to rise from about 415 TWh in 2024 to 945 TWh in 2030 [IEA_EAI_2025]. BloombergNEF expects data centres to absorb about 400 kt of copper per year over the next decade, with a peak of 572 kt in 2028 [BNEF_DC_CU_2025]. The risks are equally concrete, since a memory led boom invites a correction, China held equipment spending flat in 2025 [SEMI_EQUIP_2025], and new architectures reduce metal intensity per unit of computing power. These technology shifts decide which alloys win, so they are examined next.

Among the emerging technologies, three change the metal demand that matters to the Client in a direct and measurable way. NVIDIA's 800 VDC architecture for Kyber racks from 2027 promises up to 45% less copper than 415 VAC distribution [NVDA_800VDC_CU45], direct liquid cooling with copper cold plates is projected to rise from about 33% to 53% of AI chips between 2025 and 2026 [TF_LIQCOOL_2026], and 8.6 generation IT OLED lines require Invar masks of 20 µm or thinner [TREND_FMM, THELEC_FMM_20UM]. Power devices on silicon carbide and gallium nitride add a fourth layer, with forecasts of USD 11bn and USD 3.5bn by 2031 [YOLE_SIC_2026, YOLE_GAN_2026], although these market report figures serve only as triangulation. Table 3.6a shows that the Client's strongest fit lies in nickel alloy strip and FeNi36 precision strip, where purity and surface quality matter more than tonnage. Demand for these products is concentrated in East Asia, because China, Taiwan and South Korea took 79% of the USD 135.1bn of equipment billings in 2025, with Taiwan up 90% to USD 31.5bn [SEMI_EQUIP_2025]. Data centre growth centres on the United States, which could add up to 240 TWh of demand by 2030, followed by China with 175 TWh [IEA_EAI_2025]. Trade in specialty copper alloy strip confirms the split between producers and consumers, since Germany and Japan supplied 28.7% and 26.4% of world exports under HS 740990 in 2024 while China, Singapore, Mexico and Malaysia led imports [COMTRADE_740990_X_2024, COMTRADE_740990_M_2024]. Europe is smaller but closer, and ESMC in Dresden will run 40,000 wafers per month from 2027 [ESMC_DRESDEN_CAP], which shapes the customer list that follows.

Following the geography, the buyers of the Client's alloys are rarely the chipmakers themselves, because fabs such as TSMC, Samsung and SK hynix specify materials but purchase complete gas and chemical delivery systems. The relevant customers are therefore subsystem makers such as Ichor, which earned USD 948m in 2025 mostly from fluid delivery [ICHOR_2025], alongside Ultra Clean Holdings and Swagelok, and tube makers such as Dockweiler in Neustadt-Glewe with EUR 164m of sales in 2023 [DOCKWEILER_2023]. In data centres the buyers are Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider Electric and Delta for power [NVDA_800VDC_CU45] and Cooler Master, AVC, Boyd and Auras for cold plates [TF_COLDPLATE_SUPPLIERS], while DNP dominates the market for fine metal masks [ASIAE_POONGWON_2025]. Against this competitor map, the relevance of high performance metals shifts in both directions across the sector. Nickel alloys gain where etch and deposition chemistries grow, because C-22 corrodes at about 0.1 mpy in hot HCl vapour where 316 stainless loses 28.8 mpy [HAYNES_C22_HCL], and copper gains in cold plates as liquid cooling spreads [TF_LIQCOOL_2026]. Copper loses intensity in power distribution, since 800 VDC removes up to 45% of the conductor [NVDA_800VDC_CU45] and the 200 kg of busbar per 1 MW rack at 54 VDC will not scale with rack power [NVDA_800VDC]. The camp of OLED makers that patterns pixels without fine metal masks is accelerating [TREND_FMM], which would remove Invar from those lines, while aluminium busbars and ceramic chamber parts are further threats whose adoption rates remain TODO(data): no share found in the searched sources. The balance of these gains and losses points to a small number of specific value chain steps.

The most attractive step for the Client is qualified ultra high purity strip in C-22 and C-276 for welded gas tube and valve components, which scores 4.45 in the opportunity matrix and ranks second of all eighteen cells (team estimate) [MODEL_E1]. This cell is worth about EUR 900m in 2025 and could reach EUR 2.1bn in 2035, and its barrier is qualification rather than capacity, which favours a specialist producer with control of melting and surface finish. The second step is FeNi36 precision strip as foil feedstock, a EUR 400m cell scoring 4.05 (team estimate) [MODEL_E3], where Korean localisation by Hyundai BNG Steel and Poongwon Precision opens a door that the dominance of Proterial has kept narrow [ASIAE_POONGWON_2025]. Copper strip for connectors and cold plates remains a selective option at a score of 3.55 [MODEL_E2], because busbar volume competes on cost against Chinese scale, and the spotlights below show where these opportunities take concrete form.

### Spotlights

The first spotlight concerns NVIDIA, whose 800 VDC Kyber racks from 2027 replace 54 VDC distribution that needs up to 200 kg of copper busbar per 1 MW rack [NVDA_800VDC], with Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider Electric and Delta as power partners and Infineon, STMicroelectronics, Texas Instruments and Navitas as device partners [NVDA_800VDC_CU45]. The likely suppliers of copper flat bar and strip are Ningbo Jintian, Wieland and Aurubis (team assessment), which shows that the Client's copper offer should target connectors and cold plates rather than busbar volume.

The second spotlight concerns ESMC, the TSMC joint venture with Bosch, Infineon and NXP, which topped out its Dresden fab in 2026 and targets 40,000 wafers of 300mm per month from 2027 [ESMC_DRESDEN_CAP, ESMC_TOPOUT_2026]. Its gas and chemical lines will need electropolished 316L and C-22 tube, likely through Dockweiler, Ichor or Swagelok with alloy from the Acerinox mills (team assessment), which makes ESMC the nearest reference fab for a German strip producer.

The third spotlight concerns Samsung Display at A6 in Asan from 2026 and BOE at B16 in Chengdu from 2027, whose 8.6 generation IT OLED lines need FeNi36 masks of 20 µm or thinner made mainly by DNP [TREND_FMM, THELEC_FMM_20UM]. Proterial supplies most of this foil today, with Zapp and Hyundai BNG Steel as the alternative sources that panel makers are now qualifying [MT_FMM_2026, OLEDINFO_ZAPP, ASIAE_POONGWON_2025].

The fourth spotlight concerns Toho Titanium of the JX Advanced Metals group, which announced in September 2026 that it will double ultrafine nickel powder output at Chigasaki from 2027 for MLCCs in AI servers [TOHO_NIPOWDER_2026]. This nickel use sits outside the Client's gas atomised powder route, which keeps the focus on strip and sets up the contrast with medical technology, where volumes are smaller and qualification barriers are higher still.
