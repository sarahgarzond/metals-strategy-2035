# 5. Industry Needs

The seven industry blocks that follow keep the application evidence and examine demand from the industry driver through the component, the material, the delivery form and the buyer. Their role is to show how the metals in Chapter 4, including the basket in Table 2, become orders a buyer can place. Industry revenue measures economic activity in the sector, and it is not read here as addressable alloy demand, just as a named account stays a prospect until a relationship is documented. To limit overlap in the scenario model, electrolysers and fuel cells are assigned to energy, process vessels to chemicals, and sensors and magnetic components to electronics.

Each sector closes with the same four verbs that Chapter 8 uses, so the chapters do not issue seven different mandates. The company should qualify for repeating orders in aerospace billet, approved sheet and repair wire, and in chemical replacement plate and welding products where a named grade clears the capacity rule. The company should keep and harvest oil and gas accounts only where an approval the company itself holds supports the margin, and automotive accounts that are already profitable. The company should treat electrolysers, powders and medical implant bar as options against a financed programme or a regulatory path that is the company's own. The company should stop adding effort on undifferentiated stainless, on China implant tenders, and on any electrolyser or powder programme without a financed buyer. Chapter 7 then turns those readings into demand sensitivities, and it does not multiply them by an unmeasured share of the mixed flat code.
