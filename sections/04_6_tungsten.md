## 4.6 Tungsten

Tungsten is the clearest example of a metal whose supply one state controls. World mine output reached an estimated 85 kt of contained tungsten in 2025, of which China produced about 67 kt, or 79 percent [USGS_MCS2026_TUNGSTEN]. China sets national mining quotas, with a first batch of 58,000 t of concentrate for 2025, and since February 2025 it has required export licences for ammonium paratungstate, tungsten oxides, unsintered carbide and specified tungsten alloys, while sintered carbide tools and tungsten wire remain outside the control [W_QUOTA_CN_2025_BATCH1, W_MOFCOM_2025_10, W_MOFCOM_FAQ_2025].

Most tungsten ends up as carbide for cutting and mining tools, which takes 65 percent of first use, while steels and superalloys take 14 percent [W_ITIA_FIRSTUSE]. In superalloys tungsten strengthens cast turbine alloys and heat resistant sheet, so a licence decision in Beijing soon reaches the gas turbine hot section, and Sandvik's powder business more than doubled orders and revenues on higher tungsten prices in the third quarter of 2026 [W_SANDVIK_Q3_2026]. Tungsten thus closes the group of policy constrained metals before the niche and competing metals of section 4.7.
