## 4.6 Tungsten

Tungsten is a small but essential element for the company, because cast superalloys and heat resistant sheet grades contain several percent of it, and it is the clearest case of a metal whose supply is controlled by one state. World mine output was an estimated 85 kt of contained tungsten in 2025, of which China produced about 67 kt, or 79 percent [USGS_MCS2026_TUNGSTEN]. China sets national mining quotas, with a first batch of 58,000 t of concentrate for 2025, and since February 2025 requires export licences for tungsten intermediates, carbide and certain tungsten nickel alloys [W_QUOTA_CN_2025_BATCH1, W_MOFCOM_2025_10].

Most tungsten is used as carbide, which takes 65 percent of first use, while steels and superalloys take 14 percent [W_ITIA_FIRSTUSE]. European producers respond by recycling, as Sandvik reported that its powder business more than doubled orders and revenues on higher tungsten prices in the third quarter of 2026 [W_SANDVIK_Q3_2026]. For the company tungsten is a sourcing and export compliance topic for its casting and sheet superalloys rather than a market, and the same is true for the other metals in section 4.7, which are analysed with a lighter profile.
