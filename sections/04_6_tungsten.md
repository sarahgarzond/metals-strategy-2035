## 4.6 Tungsten

China controls most of the world's tungsten supply. In 2025, mines produced an estimated 85 kt of contained tungsten, of which China produced about 67 kt, or 79 percent [USGS_MCS2026_TUNGSTEN]. China's first 2025 mining quota covered 58,000 t of concentrate. Since February 2025, it has required export licences for ammonium paratungstate, tungsten oxides, unsintered carbide, and specified tungsten alloys. Sintered carbide tools and tungsten wire remain outside these controls [W_MOFCOM_2025_10, W_MOFCOM_FAQ_2025, W_QUOTA_CN_2025_BATCH1].

Carbide for cutting and mining tools takes 65 percent of first use, and steels and superalloys take 14 percent (International Tungsten Industry Association, n.d.). In superalloys, tungsten strengthens cast turbine parts and heat-resistant sheet, so export licences can affect gas turbine output. Sandvik's powder business more than doubled orders and revenue in the third quarter of 2026 as tungsten prices rose [W_SANDVIK_Q3_2026].
