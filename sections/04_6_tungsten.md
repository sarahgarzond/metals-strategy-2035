## 4.6 Tungsten

Tungsten is the clearest example of a metal whose supply one state controls, and its small share in superalloys makes it easy to overlook. World mine output reached an estimated 85 kt of contained tungsten in 2025, of which China produced about 67 kt, or 79 percent [USGS_MCS2026_TUNGSTEN]. China sets national mining quotas, with a first batch of 58,000 t of concentrate for 2025, and since February 2025 it has required export licences for tungsten intermediates, carbide and certain tungsten nickel alloys [W_QUOTA_CN_2025_BATCH1, W_MOFCOM_2025_10].

Most tungsten ends up as carbide for cutting and mining tools, which takes 65 percent of first use, while steels and superalloys take 14 percent [W_ITIA_FIRSTUSE]. In superalloys tungsten strengthens cast turbine alloys and heat resistant sheet, so a licence decision in Beijing reaches the hot section of a gas turbine within months. European producers respond with recycling, and Sandvik reported that its powder business more than doubled orders and revenues on higher tungsten prices in the third quarter of 2026 [W_SANDVIK_Q3_2026]. Tungsten thus completes the group of policy constrained metals, while the remaining metals in section 4.7 matter mainly as niche inputs or competing materials.
