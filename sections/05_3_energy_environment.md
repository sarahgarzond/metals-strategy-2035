## 5.3 Energy and Environmental Technology

Energy technology holds both the most urgent shortage and the most visible disappointment in the high performance metal market. Gas turbines for power and data centres cannot be built fast enough, while the hydrogen economy that was expected to consume large amounts of nickel has so far produced more announcements than orders.

### 5.3.1 Demand, Geography and Market Size

Gas turbines are the tightest pocket of demand, because data centres and grid stability have created more orders than the industry can build. Siemens Energy told investors in November 2025 that its gas turbine capacity is sold out until fiscal 2028 and will grow from about 17 GW in fiscal 2024 to above 30 GW a year from fiscal 2028 [CO_SIEMENSENERGY_CMD_2025]. Heavy duty rotor discs are mostly steel, so the nickel demand sits in the casting remelt stock for blades and vanes and in combustor sheet, and the binding constraint is hot section castings rather than nickel ore.

Hydrogen shows the opposite picture, a wide gap between political ambition and the orders that reach equipment suppliers. Global hydrogen demand surpassed 100 Mt in 2025, but low emissions production was still only about 1 Mt, and the announced 2030 pipeline shrank to 27 Mt [EE_H2_DEMAND_2025, EE_H2_LOWEM_2025, EE_H2_PIPELINE_2030]. thyssenkrupp nucera, the leading European maker of alkaline electrolysers, saw green hydrogen orders fall from EUR 356 million to EUR 26 million in fiscal 2024/25 and abandoned solid oxide stack mass production in August 2026 [CO_TKNUCERA_ORDERS_2025, CO_TKNUCERA_SOEC_2026]. Nuclear power offers a slower but steadier pull, with 81 reactors under construction worldwide, 40 of them in China, and a United States framework of October 2025 covering at least USD 80 billion of new AP1000 reactors [EE_NUC_WNA_UC, CO_WESTINGHOUSE_USGOV_2025].

### 5.3.2 Applications and Opportunities

The energy transition therefore creates three different kinds of opportunity, which differ in timing rather than in size.

Table 5: High performance metal applications in energy technology, outlook to 2035

| Outlook | Applications | Materials and product forms |
|---|---|---|
| Grows strongly | Gas turbine blades, vanes, combustors and repair | Superalloy remelt stock, combustor sheet, welding wire |
| Grows from 2030 | Nuclear steam generator tube | Alloy 690 bar and hollows for tube makers |
| Option | Solid oxide cell interconnects, alkaline electrolyser electrodes | Coated ferritic strip, nickel strip and mesh |
| Stable | Flue gas desulphurisation, waste to energy boilers | Corrosion resistant plate, weld overlay |

*Source: team assessment based on Siemens Energy, World Nuclear Association, IEA and thyssenkrupp nucera evidence [CO_SIEMENSENERGY_CMD_2025, EE_NUC_WNA_UC, EE_H2_COMMIT_2030, CO_TKNUCERA_ORDERS_2025].*

Fuel cells illustrate how a promising application can still be a weak market for metal suppliers. Bloom Energy is doubling its solid oxide output from 1 GW to 2 GW a year by the end of 2026 after revenue of USD 2,024 million in 2025, but it does not disclose its interconnect material, so the strip opportunity lies with the stack makers that use coated ferritic strip [CO_BLOOM_REV_2025, CO_BLOOM_CAP_2026]. In nuclear power Alleima has reopened a tube mill at Sandviken for steam generator tube, which shows that the role of an alloy producer lies in supplying such tube makers rather than in cladding, which the fuel makers keep in house [CO_ALLEIMA_NUCLEAR_2025].

Implications for suppliers:

* Qualify superalloy remelt stock and combustor sheet with turbine casting houses while capacity is sold out to 2028.
* Treat electrolysers, fuel cells and nuclear tube as options that are released only against signed customer volumes.
