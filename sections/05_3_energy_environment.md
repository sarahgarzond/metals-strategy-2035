## 5.3 Energy and Environmental Technology

Energy technology holds both the most urgent shortage and the most visible disappointment in the market, because gas turbines cannot be built fast enough while hydrogen has so far produced more announcements than orders.

### 5.3.1 Demand, Geography and Market Size

Gas turbines are the tightest pocket, because data centres and grid stability created more orders than the industry can build. Siemens Energy is sold out until fiscal 2028 and will raise its gas turbine capacity from about 17 GW in fiscal 2024 to above 30 GW a year [CO_SIEMENSENERGY_CMD_2025]. Investment in gas fired power almost doubled from USD 64 billion in 2019 to USD 112 billion in 2025, and data centres could use about 945 TWh of electricity in 2030, more than twice their 2024 level [IEA_WEI_GASPOWER_2025, EE_IEA_DATACENTRE_TWH_2030].

Hydrogen shows the opposite picture, as low emissions production was only about 1 Mt of more than 100 Mt of demand in 2025 and the announced 2030 pipeline shrank to 27 Mt [EE_H2_DEMAND_2025, EE_H2_LOWEM_2025, EE_H2_PIPELINE_2030]. The IEA project database counts 4.3 GW of electrolysis in operation, 19.3 GW at final investment decision or under construction and 858 GW at concept or feasibility stage, and alkaline electrolysers, the nickel route, make up 60 percent of the capacity being built [IEA_H2DB_ELECTROLYSIS_STATUS_2026, IEA_H2DB_ALK_SHARE_2026]. thyssenkrupp nucera, the leading European alkaline electrolyser maker, saw green hydrogen orders fall from EUR 356 million to EUR 26 million in fiscal 2024/25 [CO_TKNUCERA_ORDERS_2025].

Nuclear power offers a slower but steadier pull, with 81 reactors under construction, 40 of them in China, and a United States framework of October 2025 covering at least USD 80 billion of AP1000 reactors [EE_NUC_WNA_UC, CO_WESTINGHOUSE_USGOV_2025]. At the metal stage, the IEA puts nickel demand for hydrogen technologies at only 2.3 kt in 2025 and 5.2 kt in 2035 in its stated policies case, or 32 kt in its high demand case, while gas turbine superalloy tonnage is not published [IEA_CM_NI_H2_2025_2035].

### 5.3.2 Applications and Opportunities

The three resulting opportunities differ in timing more than in size, as Table 5 shows. Gas turbine parts are needed now, whereas fuel cells remain a weak metal market, because Bloom Energy, which doubles its solid oxide output to 2 GW a year by the end of 2026, does not disclose its interconnect material [CO_BLOOM_CAP_2026]. In nuclear power Alleima is reopening a Sandviken tube mill to raise steam generator tube capacity by about 60 percent, but it melts and remelts its own alloys there, so this tube creates little open demand for bar or hollows from other producers [CO_ALLEIMA_NUCLEAR_2025, CO_ALLEIMA_VAR_2025].

Table 5: High performance metal applications in energy technology, outlook to 2035

| Outlook | Applications | Materials and product forms |
|---|---|---|
| Grows strongly | Gas turbine blades, vanes, combustors and repair | Superalloy remelt stock, combustor sheet, welding wire |
| Grows from 2030 | Nuclear steam generator tube | Alloy 690 tube, mostly from integrated mills |
| Option | Solid oxide cell interconnects, alkaline electrolyser electrodes | Coated ferritic strip, nickel strip and mesh |

*Sources: team assessment [CO_SIEMENSENERGY_CMD_2025, EE_NUC_WNA_UC, IEA_H2DB_ELECTROLYSIS_STATUS_2026, CO_TKNUCERA_ORDERS_2025].*

Implications for suppliers:

* Qualify superalloy remelt stock and combustor sheet with turbine casting houses while capacity is sold out to 2028.
* Treat electrolysers, fuel cells and nuclear tube as options released only against signed volumes, and in nuclear tube only where a tube maker buys outside hollows.
