## 5.3 Energy and Environmental Technology

Energy technology shows two trends: gas turbine makers struggle to fill orders, while many hydrogen projects have yet to place any.

### 5.3.1 Demand, Geography and Market Size

Gas turbine orders grow as data centres need power and grids need stability, and all three large makers are adding capacity. Siemens Energy is sold out until fiscal 2028 and plans to raise annual capacity from about 17 GW in fiscal 2024 to more than 30 GW [CO_SIEMENSENERGY_CMD_2025]. GE Vernova reached a 20 GW annual run rate in 2026 and plans 30 GW in 2030. In July 2026 it held 116 GW under contract, 63 GW of it slot reservations [CO_GEV_WEBCAST_Q2_2026]. Mitsubishi Heavy Industries booked 16 GW in fiscal 2025, held a 37 GW backlog in September 2026, and plans to double its new unit capacity [CO_MHI_GT_2026]. Gas-fired power investment rose from USD 64 billion in 2019 to USD 112 billion in 2025, and data centres may use about 945 TWh in 2030, twice their 2024 use [EE_IEA_DATACENTRE_TWH_2030, IEA_WEI_GASPOWER_2025].

Hydrogen demand for alloys moves far more slowly. Low-emissions hydrogen output was about 1 Mt in 2025, and the 2030 pipeline fell to 27 Mt [EE_H2_DEMAND_2025, EE_H2_COMMIT_2030]. The IEA lists 4.3 GW of operating electrolysis and 19.3 GW at final investment decision or under construction, 60 percent of it nickel-using alkaline units [IEA_H2DB_ALK_SHARE_2026]. Green hydrogen orders at thyssenkrupp nucera fell from EUR 356 million to EUR 26 million in fiscal 2024/25 [CO_TKNUCERA_ORDERS_2025]. The IEA puts hydrogen nickel use at 2.3 kt in 2025 and 5.2 kt to 32 kt in 2035 [IEA_CM_CO_DEMAND_2025_2035].

Nuclear power offers steadier but slower demand. Of 81 reactors under construction, 40 are in China, and an October 2025 US framework covers at least USD 80 billion of AP1000 reactors [CO_WESTINGHOUSE_USGOV_2025, EE_NUC_WNA_UC]. The best metal measure is cobalt: the team puts turbine superalloys at about 6.0 kt of cobalt in 2025, 30 percent of the superalloy total (team estimate) [MODEL_CO_ENERGY_BASE_2025].

### 5.3.2 Applications and Opportunities

Table 5 shows when each energy opportunity generates demand. Bloom Energy plans to double solid oxide output to 2 GW a year by the end of 2026 but does not name its interconnect material [CO_BLOOM_CAP_2026].

Alleima is reopening its Sandviken mill to raise steam generator tube capacity by about 60 percent. It melts its own alloys there, so the expansion creates little open demand for hollows [ALLEIMA_CHEM_2025].

Table 5: High performance metal applications in energy technology, outlook to 2035

| Outlook | Applications | Materials and product forms |
|---|---|---|
| Grows strongly | Gas turbine blades, vanes, combustors and repair | Superalloy remelt stock, combustor sheet, welding wire |
| Grows from 2030 | Nuclear steam generator tube | Alloy 690 tube, mostly from integrated mills |
| Option | Solid oxide cell interconnects, alkaline electrolyser electrodes | Coated ferritic strip, nickel strip and mesh |

*Sources: team assessment [IEA_H2DB_ALK_SHARE_2026, CO_SIEMENSENERGY_CMD_2025, CO_TKNUCERA_ORDERS_2025, EE_NUC_WNA_UC].*

Implications for suppliers:

* Qualify superalloy remelt stock and combustor sheet with turbine casting houses while capacity is sold out to 2028.
* Treat electrolysers, fuel cells and nuclear tube as options released only against signed volumes or outside hollow purchases.
