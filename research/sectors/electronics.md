### Data centre power

The NVIDIA post of 20 May 2025 was opened. It says today's racks use 54 VDC, that this architecture is not built for megawatt racks, and that NVIDIA is moving to 800 VDC to support 1 MW racks and beyond, with full scale production coinciding with Kyber rack scale systems in 2027. The copper sentence is specific: the physics of using 54 VDC in a single 1 MW rack requires up to 200 kg of copper busbar, and the rack busbars alone in a 1 GW data centre could require up to 200,000 kg. ## 2. Key growth drivers and risks

The demand driver that the official sources agree on is AI infrastructure. WSTS names AI, high bandwidth memory and accelerated computing as the reason memory dominates the 2026 sales jump. SEMI names AI, high performance computing and government incentives as reasons for the 300 mm build. NVIDIA's Data Center revenue and Vertiv's sales growth (USD 10.2 billion in 2025, up 27.7 percent, with backlog of USD 15.0 billion against USD 7.2 billion a year earlier) are the company scale evidence [CO_VERTIV_REVENUE_2025, CO_VERTIV_BACKLOG_2025].

The risk for a metal supplier is that sales growth and equipment dollars are poor multipliers. A 90 to 108 percent rise in semiconductor sales against a 7 to 8 percent rise in 300 mm capacity means price, especially memory price, is doing most of the work. Package counts, lead frame strip and chamber parts will not scale with the sales number. DNP's own point on masks is the same shape of warning: a jig does not scale with panel units.

A second risk is geography and policy. Tool revenue is concentrated in China, Taiwan and Korea. TSMC's geographic projects (Arizona, Kumamoto, Dresden) move some capacity, and SEMI's Americas equipment path more than doubles from a base that is still about 12 percent of 2026 300 mm equipment spending (18.1 / 149, team arithmetic). That is not a forecast of alloy shipments to Arizona.

A third risk is substitution inside the pockets that could have used the company's alloys. Mask free OLED routes are already funded at Visionox and TCL CSOT (TrendForce, triangulation only) and are acknowledged by DNP. Higher conductivity copper lead frames displace low expansion iron nickel where heat, not seal matching, is the constraint. 800 VDC is designed to use less copper per unit of distributed power, even while rack power goes toward 1 MW. Which copper pool shrinks, the row busway or the rack busbar, is not a number this note can split beyond the two statements NVIDIA actually made.

A fourth risk is qualification and single sources. Applied Materials says some key parts come from one qualified supplier or a small group. Lam Research says rare earths, minerals and metals are available in a limited number of countries. Neither statement is a shortage of the company's grades.

Cyclical demand in the broader electrical industry is already visible. Aperam's H1 2025 report, as recorded in the company profile, said electronics final demand was still healthy while electrical components suffered from weak demand or lower investment. ZVEI real production was down sharply in 2024 and only stabilising in 2025 and 2026. https://www.aperam.com/sites/default/files/documents/Aperam_HYR_2025.pdf

## 8. Where high performance metals gain or lose relevance

They gain where a physical constraint forces a specialty alloy and a specification locks it in. The evidenced case is invar for fine metal masks: dimensional stability of a thin etched plate, protected by DNP patents, with a new generation of larger glass raising mask area per jig. That is a gain in specification intensity, not a proven gain in tonnes.

They lose, or never enter, where conductivity, cost or a redesign removes the specialty metal. Copper lead frames and connectors are the mainstream electrical path. Low expansion iron nickel is the niche that remains when glass or ceramic seals set the expansion, which is the company's benchmark application, and that niche was not quantified. Mask free OLED (inkjet at TCL CSOT, ViP at Visionox) is the direct threat to invar masks. DNP says the threat is not immediate because yields and process length are still problems. TrendForce says the mask free camp is investing anyway. Both statements can be true.

In data centres, copper gains kilograms when racks move from tens of kilowatts toward 1 MW at 54 VDC (the 200 kg statement). Copper loses intensity when distribution moves to 800 VDC at row level (the 45 percent statement versus 415 VAC). Net copper per gigawatt is not calculated here, because the two statements cover different parts of the power chain and only NVIDIA provides them. Aluminium is already a USGS listed substitute for copper in electrical equipment and power cable. Vertiv hedges aluminium as well as copper. Nickel alloys are not part of this substitution story in the documents opened.

In tools, high purity stainless and nickel alloys would gain if halogen and ultra clean gas service is specified more often as fabs multiply. The filings do not show that specification, so this is not a gain the note can claim. Composites and ceramics are not evidenced as substitutes for the company's electronics alloys in the sources opened.

## 9. Where the value chain is attractive for the company

The attractive step is a specified, low volume, hard to qualify form that the company can melt and finish, sold to a converter the OEM already qualifies. Three of the four pockets fail that test on present evidence, and the fourth passes only part of it.

Semiconductor equipment fails for now. The dollars are large and the alloy content is unstated. The company would sit behind a machine shop, in a chain whose revenue is concentrated in China, Taiwan and Korea, with long qualification and no kilogram to size a furnace. Revisit only with a tool maker or tier one parts drawing that names an alloy and a form.

Lead frames and connectors fail as a volume bet. The evidenced metal is copper. The company's expansion alloys fit the seal and lead frame niche only if a stamper still qualifies them. Europe is the accessible relationship (Infineon, and Aperam already reporting weak electrical component demand), which argues for a sample and margin test, not for capacity. Do not read Infineon's AI revenue targets as strip demand.

Data centre power fails as a nickel alloy market and is real as a copper market the company does not lead. Busbar, switchgear and cable follow Vertiv, Eaton and Schneider, and 800 VDC is explicitly a copper reduction architecture at row level. The company should track it so that a copper heavy story is not mistaken for a nickel alloy story. A connector or magnetic part inside the 800 VDC shelf would be a different, still undocumented, opportunity.

Fine metal masks are the pocket that matches the company's alloy family, with the limits written down. DNP has the customer position, the invar patents and a doubled line aimed at 8th generation glass. The company can be relevant only as a foil or strip source to a mask etcher, or to the mill that already supplies DNP, and only if it can hold the gauge, surface and expansion that the patent holder requires. Tonnes are likely small because the mask is a jig. The commercial test is a named foil specification and a named buyer, not the growth rate of OLED panel area. Until Proterial or another mill is confirmed as the incumbent, the competitive intensity of this step is unknown. Entry barriers are the patents, the etching know how (which sits at DNP, not at a melt shop) and the foil rolling step.

What the company should not do, on this evidence, is treat WSTS or SEMI growth as a multiplier for alloy volume, or build a 2035 electronics tonnage from chip sales.
