## Key findings

1. In 2025 the world built 96.4 million motor vehicles, up from 92.7 million in 2024, and sold 99.8 million, up from 95.3 million [OICA_VEH_PROD_2025, OICA_VEH_SALES_2025]. Passenger car registrations were 77,554,919, rounded by ACEA to 77.6 million, up 3.5 percent, and passenger car production was 78,661,926, rounded to 78.7 million, up 4.2 percent [ACEA_CAR_REG_2025, ACEA_CAR_PROD_2025]. The two pairs measure different things. OICA counts all motor vehicles. ACEA counts passenger cars, and its global tables are provisional figures from S&P Global Mobility.

2. Production is concentrated in Asia. Asia Pacific built around 59.2 million vehicles, more than 61 percent of world output, and China built 34.53 million, which is 35.8 percent of the OICA total [OICA_APAC_PROD_2025, OICA_CHINA_PROD_2025]. Inside that Chinese total, new energy vehicle production was 16.626 million, up 29 percent, or 48.2 percent of Chinese vehicle output [OICA_CHINA_NEV_2025]. On the car definition, China built 29,426,897 cars (37.4 percent of world car output) and registered 24,219,235 (31.2 percent of world car registrations) [ACEA_CHINA_CAR_PROD_2025, ACEA_CHINA_CAR_REG_2025]. The current draft's "China built 36 percent" is the all vehicle production share, not the car registration share.

3. Europe is not growing in volume. OICA puts European vehicle production at 17.2 million, down 0.8 percent [OICA_EUR_PROD_2025]. ACEA puts European car production at 14,377,017, down 0.3 percent, and EU car production at 11,470,235, up 0.3 percent [ACEA_CAR_PROD_2025]. EU car registrations were 10,822,831, up 1.8 percent, and still 2.2 million units, or 17 percent, below 2019 [ACEA_EU_CAR_REG_2025]. The Eurostat production volume index for NACE C29, 2021 equal to 100, was 106.3 for the EU27 in 2025 against 123.6 in 2019, a 14.0 percent shortfall. Germany was 106.6 against 127.3. Italy was 74.5 against 109.2 [EUROSTAT_C29_EU27_2025, EUROSTAT_C29_DE_2025, EUROSTAT_C29_IT_2025]. Italy's registration decline was only 2.1 percent, so the Italian collapse is in factory output, not in cars sold.

4. Electric cars, defined by the IEA as battery electric plus plug in hybrid and not as conventional hybrids, exceeded 20 million sales in 2025 and reached 25 percent of new car sales [IEA_GEVO_EV_SALES_2025, IEA_GEVO_EV_SHARE_2025]. Battery electric cars were 65 percent of those electric car sales [IEA_GEVO_BEV_OF_EV_2025]. In the EU, battery electric cars were 17.4 percent of registrations [ACEA_BEV_2025], plug in hybrids 9.4 percent, conventional hybrids 34.5 percent, and petrol plus diesel 35.5 percent, down from 45.2 percent in 2024 [ACEA_EU_PHEV_2025, ACEA_EU_HEV_2025, ACEA_EU_PETROL_DIESEL_2025]. The 17.4 percent and the 25 percent are not the same statistic. Conventional hybrids are now the largest EU powertrain and they still need exhaust, sensor and, usually, turbocharger alloys.

5. For 2035 the IEA does not publish one car sales total. In the Global EV Outlook 2026 the share of electric cars in global car sales is around 50 percent in its two exploratory scenarios, and sales of internal combustion cars do not return to the 2017 peak in any scenario [IEA_GEVO_EV_SHARE_2035]. The November 2025 World Energy Outlook has the same Stated Policies Scenario above 50 percent by 2035, and the Current Policies Scenario at around 40 percent in the summary and over 40 percent in the transport chapter [IEA_WEO_EV_STEPS_2035, IEA_WEO_EV_CPS_2035]. The draft table that takes car sales from 77.6 million to 85.7 million at 1 percent a year, and cars without a plug from 58.2 million to 42.9 million, is not in either IEA book. Those three 2035 counts should not be carried as sourced results.

6. The regional split matters more than the world average. China electric cars and vans exceed 90 percent of sales by 2035 in both IEA scenarios, from close to 55 percent in 2025 [IEA_GEVO_CN_CARS_2035]. EU electric light duty vehicles exceed 90 percent in the Stated Policies Scenario and approach 100 percent in the Current Policies Scenario [IEA_GEVO_EU_LDV_STEPS_2035]. Europe as a whole, which includes the United Kingdom and Türkiye, is close to 85 percent in the Stated Policies Scenario and around 90 percent in the Current Policies Scenario, not a uniform "more than 90 percent" [IEA_GEVO_EUR_CARS_STEPS_2035]. The United States stays the holdout. Electric vehicles excluding two and three wheelers were 9 percent of sales in 2025, reach around 20 percent by 2035 if stated policies are counted, and stay under 10 percent for the whole horizon if only current policies are counted [IEA_GEVO_US_STEPS_2035]. Japan electric cars were under 3 percent of sales in 2025, while conventional hybrids were about one third [IEA_GEVO_JP_EV_2025].

7. No primary source in this pass gives a euro value, or a tonne figure, for high performance nickel alloy, iron nickel strip or iron chromium aluminium foil consumed by the auto industry. What can be supported is the direction. Turbocharger units were close to 50 million in 2025, up from about 49 million in 2024, and Garrett expects them to fall back toward the 2022 volume by 2030, citing S&P Global Mobility [CO_GARRETT_TURBOMKT_2025]. Gasoline turbo penetration on light vehicles is expected to peak at 54 percent by 2032 and then plateau [CO_GARRETT_GAS_PEN_2032]. Lithium iron phosphate, which contains no nickel, was over 55 percent of electric vehicle batteries deployed in 2025 [IEA_GEVO_LFP_2025]. That chemistry shift hits battery grade nickel sulphate. It does not, by itself, measure wrought alloy demand.

8. The attractive remainder for the company is narrow. It is heat resistant nickel alloy and special stainless for the engines, turbos, exhaust sensors and spark plugs that hybrids and North American gasoline cars still use, plus soft magnetic iron nickel alloys for resolvers and shielding on electric motors. The buyers with filed evidence are Garrett and BorgWarner. The mills with a real auto overlap are Aperam Alloys Imphy and Höganäs. Carpenter's transportation business is only USD 100.4 million, 3 percent of fiscal 2026 sales, so it is not an automotive specialist.

## 2. Key growth drivers and risks

The volume driver is Asia, and inside Asia it is China and India. India built 6.49 million vehicles in 2025 on the OICA press figures, and Chinese new energy vehicle output rose 29 percent. Policy is the second driver. The 2025 step up in EU CO2 standards, a 15 percent cut versus 2021, coincided with European electric car sales of 4.2 million, up more than 30 percent, and with almost 3 million in the EU at just under 27 percent of sales (Global EV Outlook 2026, p. 21). Germany sold about 850,000 electric cars, around 30 percent of its car market. ACEA's German split is 19.1 percent battery electric and 10.9 percent plug in hybrid, which sums to the same 30 percent [ACEA_DE_BEV_2025].

The risk to the company's current auto book is that this policy mix removes engines. In the EU Stated Policies Scenario, which already includes the Commission's December 2025 Automotive Package, conventional cars including hybrids keep less than 10 percent of the market beyond 2035, and battery electric light duty vehicles exceed 85 percent [IEA_GEVO_EU_LDV_STEPS_2035]. A further political risk cuts the other way, but only at the margin. The package is not law. Parliament's environment committee voted on 4 November 2026, by 48 to 30 with 2 abstentions, for an 87.5 percent CO2 cut for new cars by 2035 rather than 100 percent, with a possible further credit for EU low carbon steel up to 95 percent [EP_ENVI_CAR_CO2_2035]. The plenary mandate is expected in December 2026, and the Council still has to agree. Until then the 100 percent rule stands. The IEA book, closed at the end of March 2026, uses the Commission proposal of 90 percent, not the November committee text. Even the looser committee text still leaves only a small combustion tail.

Other risks are already visible in the 2025 numbers. EU car registrations remain 17 percent below 2019. Italian factory output has fallen to an index of 74.5. ACEA reports EU car exports down 6.2 percent and the surplus at 76 billion euro, with exports to the United States down 21.4 percent on tariffs and exports to China down 43 percent, while Chinese built cars reached 7 percent of EU sales and Chinese imports passed 1 million cars (Economic and Market Report, full year 2025, pp. 3 and 10). North American car production fell 0.9 percent to 11.2 million, and US car production fell 0.5 percent to 7,338,071 (ACEA Table 4). OICA still shows the United States selling 16.7 million vehicles against production of 10.24 million, so the market is larger than the factory base [OICA_US_PROD_2025].

Oil prices are a swing factor, not a settled one. The Global EV Outlook 2026 says a prolonged disruption at the Strait of Hormuz would push oil importing countries, especially emerging markets, toward electric cars (pp. 193 to 194). That would shrink the engine alloy base faster. It would not create a wrought nickel market in the battery.

## 3. Emerging technologies and applications

Four technology shifts show up in the primary sources.

Electrification of cars is furthest in China, then Europe, and is slow in the United States and Japan. Electric trucks are the new growth leg. Sales more than doubled in 2025 to 9 percent of world truck sales, one in four trucks in China was electric, and current policies take electric trucks to at least 20 percent of world truck sales by 2035 and about 60 percent in China (Global EV Outlook 2026, pp. 13 and 196). CATL supplied 80 percent of the batteries in Chinese electric trucks (p. 13). Truck electrification removes diesel engine alloys. It does not specify a nickel mill product.

Batteries are moving to lithium iron phosphate. That chemistry was over 55 percent of EV batteries deployed in 2025, against nearly 50 percent in 2024, and it contains no nickel [IEA_GEVO_LFP_2025]. In the EU, lithium iron phosphate was more than 10 percent of EV battery demand and nearly all of it was imported from China. In the United States the lithium iron phosphate share almost halved in 2025 from a low base (pp. 96 to 97). LFP packs were more than 40 percent cheaper per kilowatt hour than nickel manganese cobalt packs (p. 95). The nickel that remains in batteries is mid and high nickel cathode material, which is a chemical plant product, not strip or wire.

Software defined vehicles and 1,000 volt architectures are raising the semiconductor and power electronics content (Global EV Outlook 2026, pp. 14 to 15). The IEA does not name a nickel alloy for that content. The VDM brochure, dated from a September 2024 file, is the only primary product map in hand for the electric motor sensor. It says ordinary electrical sheet is usually no longer suitable once a resolver runs at 10 kHz, and it names the soft magnetic grades VDM MAG 50 and VDM MAG 7904 for angle sensors, current sensors and shielding. That is the growth application that matches a nickel alloy mill. It is a benchmark, not a customer list.

The EU low carbon steel credit is a carbon steel story. If every manufacturer used the full credit, the IEA puts induced demand at 9 to 23 million tonnes of EU low carbon crude steel in 2035, equal to 7 to 17 percent of projected EU crude steel output in the Stated Policies Scenario [IEA_GEVO_LC_STEEL_2035]. The finished steel in the car would be lower because of process losses. The emissions definition is not set. About 1 million tonnes of existing near zero steel commitments are linked to auto manufacturers and suppliers, inside almost 6 million tonnes of announced commitments (p. 199). This is not a nickel alloy market, and it should not be scored as one.

## 5. Geographic concentration of demand

Vehicle demand and the alloy demand do not sit in the same place.

Vehicle output in 2025 is Asian. Asia Pacific was more than 61 percent of OICA production. Asia was 62.1 percent of ACEA car production. China alone was 35.8 percent of all vehicle production and 37.4 percent of car production. The United States produced 10.24 million vehicles and sold 16.7 million. Europe produced 17.2 million vehicles and 14.4 million cars, and the EU registered 10.8 million cars.

Alloy demand for the engine book follows the cars that still have an engine, and the factories that build the parts.

China is already a weak place to add engine alloy exposure. New energy vehicles were 16.626 million of 34.53 million vehicles built there, and electric cars were close to 55 percent of Chinese car sales. The IEA has that share above 90 percent by 2035 in both scenarios. Chinese electric truck batteries are 80 percent CATL. A European nickel mill meets this market, if at all, through global tier ones that cast and machine in China, not by selling strip to a Chinese car plant.

Europe is a large but shrinking factory base for the engine alloys, and it is the main footprint of the two named buyers. Garrett took 49 percent of 2025 sales in Europe, 29 percent in Asia and 19 percent in the United States (Form 10-K, p. 34). BorgWarner employs 14,800 of its 37,500 people in Europe (Form 10-K, p. 12). EU policy takes electric light duty vehicles above 90 percent by 2035 even after the Commission flexibility. The hybrid bridge is real in 2025, at 34.5 percent of EU registrations, and it is not a 2035 plan.

North America is the holdout. US electric car sales were just under 10 percent in 2025. The Current Policies Scenario keeps the broader electric vehicle share under 10 percent. The Stated Policies Scenario reaches only about 20 percent by 2035. US vehicle sales of 16.7 million against production of 10.24 million also mean a large park and a large aftermarket. That is the region where turbo, exhaust sensor and plug alloys last longest.

Japan is a hybrid holdout rather than a battery electric market. Electric cars were under 3 percent of sales and conventional hybrids about one third. The Stated Policies electric light duty share is only around 20 percent by 2035. Japanese specialty mills, not a European strip mill, are the natural suppliers. Daido's auto revenue split was not verified in this pass.

India is a growth vehicle market with a still small electric car share. The IEA says electric car sales reached a 4 percent share in 2025 and electric light duty vehicles reach nearly 25 percent by 2035 in the Stated Policies Scenario, or over 2 million cars and vans, and only 5 percent in the Current Policies Scenario (Global EV Outlook 2026, p. 204). The engine alloy tail is longer than in Europe. No filed link from Garrett or BorgWarner to an Indian alloy mill was found. BorgWarner owns 32.6 percent of Turbo Energy Private Limited in India (Form 10-K).

Höganäs, which is the volume powder competitor rather than a nickel alloy mill, already sells 41.8 percent of its business in Asia Pacific, 29.5 percent in the Americas and 28.7 percent in Europe, the Middle East and Africa (Annual and Sustainability Report 2025, p. 66). That is a map of sintered auto parts, which are mostly iron powder.

## 8. Where high performance metals gain or lose relevance

They lose relevance wherever the engine, the turbocharger, the catalyst and the spark plug leave the car.

The loss is fastest in China, where new energy vehicles are already 48 percent of production and electric cars and vans are projected above 90 percent by 2035 in both IEA scenarios. It is nearly as fast in the EU, where battery electric plus plug in hybrid is already 26.8 percent and the Stated Policies path leaves conventional cars, including hybrids, under 10 percent after 2035. Diesel only parts, above all glow plugs, go first. ACEA has diesel at 8.9 percent of EU registrations. Catalyst foil goes with gasoline and diesel aftertreatment, not with a battery car. Turbo units are the largest single engine alloy pool that is actually counted, at close to 50 million in 2025, and the filed outlook is a decline to 2022 volumes by 2030, even though gasoline penetration can still rise to 54 percent by 2032 because the remaining engines are more often turbocharged. That penetration peak does not offset a shrinking engine fleet. The draft claim that more alloy per unit slows the decline is unsupported.

They hold where a combustion engine remains. Conventional hybrids are 34.5 percent of EU registrations and about one third of Japanese car sales, and they still have exhaust, sensors and usually a turbo. The United States, under current policy, keeps electric vehicles under 10 percent of sales. Plug in hybrids are inside the IEA electric definition and they still have an engine. Because electric cars were 25 percent of world sales, more than three quarters of new cars were not electric, and the plug in hybrids inside that 25 percent also keep an engine.

They do not win the battery. Lithium iron phosphate has no nickel and is now the majority of EV batteries. The nickel that stays in nickel manganese cobalt cathodes is a sulphate, sold by chemical and mining companies, not a wrought mill product. Recycling is not a near term offset for that chain either. The IEA says end of life batteries lag production by about 15 years, and in the Stated Policies Scenario recycling might cover 10 to 20 percent of lithium and nickel demand only by 2050 (Global EV Outlook 2026, pp. 222 to 223).

They can gain in a small electrical pocket. Soft magnetic iron nickel for resolvers, current sensors and shielding is the application the brochure ties to electric drives, precisely because electrical steel stops being good enough at about 10 kHz. Copper for motors and cables also rises with electric cars. Copper is not the company's core flat nickel business, and no auto copper tonne was taken from a primary source in this pass. Höganäs is trying to push the other way in coatings, by replacing nickel and cobalt alloys with iron. That is a loss for nickel alloy powder, not a gain.

Substitution by titanium, composites or coated carbon steel is not evidenced for these particular parts in the sources read here. The substitution that is evidenced is electrical steel and ferrite magnets and lithium iron phosphate against nickel, and iron powder against nickel and cobalt in coatings. ## 9. Where the value chain is attractive for the company

The attractive step is not the car plant and not the battery plant. It is the qualified strip, wire and forging stock sold to tier one component makers and to their casters.

First, defend the gasoline and hybrid hot end while the awards are still being placed. Garrett and BorgWarner are spending on zero emission products, and those products are still the smaller pool. BorgWarner Turbos and Thermal is USD 5,772 million against PowerDrive at USD 2,347 million and battery systems at USD 590 million. Both companies booked gasoline work that runs into 2027 and, for one BorgWarner pickup program, into 2029. The region to defend is North America, then the hybrid programs in Europe and Japan. The commercial job is to stay specified on the unnamed casting and forging suppliers, because neither tier one buys mill bar as its main turbo input. Entry barrier is the qualification of the heat resistant grade, not a new plant.

Second, put commercial effort on the soft magnetic grades for resolvers and shielding. That is the only auto application in the benchmark brochure that grows when the engine disappears. The competition is other iron nickel mills, including Imphy and, on the evidence gap, Daido's magnetic materials business. The tonne market is unknown, so this is a specification campaign, not a volume plan. TODO(data): a shipment figure for MAG 50 type strip into auto electronics before anyone sizes the prize.

Third, do not spend a growth plan on four adjacent chains. Diesel glow plugs fall with an 8.9 percent and shrinking EU diesel share. Battery cathode nickel is a different product, and most new EV batteries no longer use it. Sintered iron powder is Höganäs's scale game, at hundreds of thousands of tonnes and falling auto demand. Low carbon body steel is a carbon steel credit of 9 to 23 million tonnes that is not yet law and is not a nickel alloy. Commodity exhaust stainless is a flat stainless fight with Aperam and Outokumpu in a European market whose factory index is still below 2019 and whose auto demand Aperam already calls depressed.

The practical order is therefore hold the specified turbo, sensor and plug alloys through the hybrid and North American tail, and use that customer access to place soft magnetic strip on the electric content of the same platforms.
