# Medical technology

Scope: implants, surgical and implantable instruments (including wire), and capital equipment. The client is called the company. VDM Metals application pages are a benchmark of uses only, not evidence of what the company sells or whom it supplies. Growth below is taken from procedure counts, demographic projections and company segments. Paid market reports are not used as the growth rate.

## Key findings

1. `WHOSIS_000001` in `data/clean/who_WHOSIS_000001.csv` is life expectancy at birth, confirmed from the WHO GHO indicator catalogue (IndicatorName "Life expectancy at birth (years)"). It is the wrong series for implant demand. Both sexes, global, is 73.3 years in 2023 (`WHO_WHOSIS_000001`). The UN World Population Prospects 2024 summary also gives 73.3 years in 2024. Life expectancy has risen slowly. It does not measure hip or knee volume. `WHOSIS_000015` is life expectancy at age 60, also not a population count and not used. `data/clean/who_UHC_HRHSURGEONDENSITY.csv` has a header and no rows, so surgeon density is not cited. A search of the GHO catalogue found no osteoarthritis, hip or knee indicator. The one additional pull allowed here is `WHS6_102`, hospital beds per 10,000 population (`data/clean/who_WHS6_102.csv`, `WHO_WHS6_102`), a capital equipment capacity series.

2. In 2023 the OECD unweighted averages were 198 hip replacements and 156 knee replacements per 100,000 population (OECD, Health at a Glance 2025, Figure 5.25, source OECD Health Statistics 2025). The same chapter says rates rose over the decade in every OECD country with data except New Zealand and Mexico, and that Germany, Switzerland, Australia, Finland and Denmark were among the highest. The local count file `data/clean/oecd_health_hip_knee_procedures.csv` sums to 1,471,061 hip and 1,280,211 knee procedures in 2023 in the 35 OECD members that report both (2,751,272 together). The United States, Japan and Chile are missing, so this is not world implant volume and not a full OECD total. On a constant set of 29 OECD members reporting both procedures in both 2013 and 2023, the combined count rose from 1,797,396 to 2,501,707, a compound rate of 3.4 percent a year (team arithmetic on that file).

3. The current section's "2.7 million procedures in 28 countries" is close to the 35 country 2023 total but the country count is not. The section's 2.7 percent a year path to about 3.8 million in 2035 should not be reused. Health at a Glance 2025 does not publish a 2.7 percent rate, nor the 2013 averages of 158 and 116 that the section table implies. The 2025 text also does not print Germany at 351 and 268 per 100,000. Germany's 2023 counts in the file are 292,143 hips and 223,133 knees. Lithuania's hip count rose from 3,776 to 7,199, which is almost a doubling of counts. Korea's hip count rose from 10,159 to 36,035.

4. OEM revenue is not mill revenue. In fiscal 2025 Stryker orthopaedics were USD 9,469 million, of which knees were USD 2,656 million and hips USD 1,865 million (`CO_STRYKER_ORTHO_2025`). Zimmer Biomet knees were USD 3,322.3 million and hips USD 2,093.5 million (`CO_ZBH_PRODUCTS_2025`). Smith & Nephew orthopaedics were USD 2,437 million (`CO_SNN_ORTHO_2025`). Johnson & Johnson said its orthopaedics business had about USD 9.2 billion of sales in fiscal 2024. These figures overlap. They are competitor segments, not a market to be added. Carpenter's medical end use in the year to 30 June 2026 was USD 278.4 million including surcharge, down 21 percent, and USD 224.3 million excluding surcharge, down 24 percent. The section's "fell 24 percent to USD 224 million" is the ex surcharge pair, not total medical sales.

5. Europe's medical technology market is estimated at roughly EUR 170 billion in 2024 and roughly EUR 173 billion in 2025 (MedTech Europe). Those totals cover devices and diagnostics, not high performance metal. MedTech Europe's device geography for 2024 is United States 46.4 percent, Europe 26.4 percent, China 6.5 percent, Japan 4.7 percent. The 6 October 2026 facts page says European spending on medical technology grew 1.8 percent in 2025, against a decade average of about 5.6 percent, and that Europe's world share was 25.6 percent, down from 26.4 percent. The underlying market file is Fitch Solutions. It is an association estimate, not the growth rate used here.

6. FDA has not removed cobalt chromium or titanium from orthopaedic use. The December 2017 additive manufacturing guidance sets process and test expectations. The January 2024 draft on metallic and calcium phosphate coatings is not for implementation and does not ban an alloy. On 17 November 2023 FDA approved restor3d's patient specific total talus, additively manufactured Co28Cr6Mo powder to ASTM F3213, then polished. Smith & Nephew's OXINIUM oxidized zirconium is the sourced substitution case against cobalt chromium, because the 2025 annual report says it has virtually no nickel, cobalt and chromium and needs no CMR labelling.

7. For the company, the metal that is evidenced is implant bar and powder (cobalt chromium, titanium, oxidized zirconium) and stainless wire and strip for instruments and implantable leads. Capital equipment is a large device market and a small, poorly measured metal market. USGS MCS 2026 puts medical implants inside the non aerospace remainder for titanium, with no share, and does not separate medical devices inside US cobalt use (51 percent superalloys, mainly aircraft turbines, 25 percent chemicals, 15 percent other metallic uses, 9 percent cemented carbides).

## 2. Growth drivers and risks

Drivers that are measured. Age is the strongest predictor of osteoarthritis in the OECD chapter, with obesity, inactivity and injury also named. Replacement is mainly, not only, in people aged 60 and over. World Population Prospects 2024, medium scenario, is the demographic source. World population is 8.2 billion in 2024 and is projected to peak around 10.3 billion in the mid 2080s. Persons aged 65 or older reach 2.2 billion in the late 2070s. Persons aged 80 or older reach 265 million by the mid 2030s. In the 63 countries whose populations had already peaked by 2024, a group that includes China, Germany and Japan and that held 28 percent of world population in 2024, the share aged 65 or older nearly doubles from 17 percent in 2024 to 33 percent in 2054, and the number aged 65 or older reaches 409 million by 2027. In that same group the number aged 80 or older rises from 85 million in 2024. The summary does not print a single global headcount of people aged 60 or 65 in 2035. The section's "1.0 billion in 2020 to 1.4 billion in 2030" aged 60 is a different age cut and was not in `WHOSIS_000001`. It is not carried forward. `TODO(data): WPP 2024 single age file for the global population aged 60 and over and aged 65 and over in 2024 and 2035. The Data Portal API returned 401 in this session.`

Procedure rates rose with that ageing, and with obesity, across almost all OECD countries. Volume growth in the count file was fastest where rates were catching up, not in the already high German count. Korea, Ireland, Poland and Lithuania lead the hip count growth rates in the 29 country set. Germany's hip count still rose, from 228,162 in 2013 to 292,143 in 2023, about 2.5 percent a year.

OEM segments in 2025 grew in the mid single digits for hips and knees, faster for trauma (Zimmer S.E.T. up 15.2 percent, Stryker trauma and extremities the largest orthopaedic line). Stryker's orthopaedic price effect was slightly negative, so 2025 growth was volume. Guidance then softened. Stryker cut 2026 organic growth guidance to 6.5 to 7.0 percent at the third quarter, with only modestly positive pricing. Smith & Nephew cut full year underlying growth guidance to about 4 percent at the first half of 2026, from about 6 percent. Zimmer raised 2026 organic constant currency guidance to 3.0 to 3.5 percent even as knees fell in the quarter. The planning range for implant OEM revenue is low to mid single digits, not the historical 6 percent European device average.

Risks. China volume based procurement is in the Stryker, Zimmer Biomet and Smith & Nephew filings. The National Healthcare Security Administration, reported by China Daily on the State Council English site on 12 February 2022, said prices of artificial hips and knees were down 82 percent and drug prices in the programme were down 53 percent on average. Smith & Nephew's chief executive said the trading margin rose while the group overcame some 1,000 basis points of macro pressure from China VBP and inflation, and that VBP already covers orthopaedics and sports medicine, with ENT expected in 2026. Inventory is the mill risk. Carpenter said FY2026 medical shipments fell because the supply chain managed inventory, partly offset by price realisation in dental. Raw material clauses are short. Zimmer buys cobalt chrome, titanium and tantalum on contracts that generally run 12 to 24 months where available, and names dependence on a limited number of suppliers. Stryker says raw materials are generally available from multiple sources but that some come from single suppliers, and the 10 K names no alloy. A separation of DePuy Synthes, targeted by Johnson & Johnson within 18 to 24 months of 14 October 2025, changes the buyer, not the procedure. Regulatory cost in Europe is a device industry risk (MedTech Europe's 2026 page on MDR and IVDR revision), not a direct alloy ban.

## 3. Emerging technologies and applications

Additive implants are real and still a specification business. FDA's December 2017 guidance, Technical Considerations for Additive Manufactured Medical Devices, covers any device with at least one additively manufactured component. It asks for process validation, material characterisation and cleaning. It does not choose an alloy. The January 2024 draft, Characterization of Metallic Coatings and/or Calcium Phosphate Coatings on Orthopedic Devices (docket FDA-2023-D-5430), is labelled not for implementation. It tightens what a submission should show for metallic and calcium phosphate coatings. It does not withdraw cobalt chromium or titanium.

The concrete clearance is restor3d, Durham, North Carolina. FDA's humanitarian device exemption H230003, approved 17 November 2023, is a patient specific total talus, laser powder bed fusion in Co28Cr6Mo meeting ASTM F3213, then polished on the articulating surface. Clinical data in the FDA overview showed 96.3 percent device survivorship and a 14.8 percent rate of additional surgery. Instruments shipped with it are polymer trials, not metal. Powder supplier is not named.

Robotics and cementless fixation pull instruments and some titanium, but tonnage is not published. Stryker's Mako system is in more than 45 countries, with a US commercial launch of Mako Shoulder planned for the first quarter of 2026. Smith & Nephew's LANDMARK knee is due in the second half of 2026. Neither filing gives a metal weight. Oxidized zirconium is the commercial material shift that is documented, under section 8.

Imaging and sensing are emerging for wire more than for nickel plate. Alleima describes ultra fine wire, stainless and precious metal, for glucose monitors, pacemakers and neurostimulation, in the medical portion of Kanthal, and medical demand as still high in FY2025. That is an instrument and implant lead application, not an MRI magnet.

## 5. Geographic concentration of demand

Device revenue is concentrated in the United States and western Europe. MedTech Europe's 2024 manufacturer price shares are United States 46.4 percent and Europe 26.4 percent, and within Europe Germany alone is 25.2 percent. Japan is 4.7 percent and China 6.5 percent of the world device market in that chart. The 2026 MedTech Europe page lowers Europe's world share to 25.6 percent but the datahub body still prints the United States at 46.4 percent with a Fitch 2024 footnote, so a 2025 US share is not updated here.

Procedure intensity, which is the better metal indicator, is a different map. The OECD narrative puts the highest hip and knee rates in Germany, Switzerland, Australia, Finland and Denmark, and the lowest, among reporters, in Mexico, Colombia and Costa Rica. Count volume in the 2023 file is led by Germany (515,276 hip plus knee), France (324,311), the United Kingdom (240,658), Italy (231,914) and Canada (165,696). The United States is absent from the file, so the revenue centre and the counted procedure centre do not match. That is the main geographic gap.

Growth geography inside the file is central and eastern Europe and Korea, not a further surge in the German rate. Korea, Poland and Lithuania are the large hip count gains. China is a population and hospital capacity story and a price story. WHO hospital beds per 10,000 population were 56.3 in China in 2023, up from 33.2 in 2013, against 75.5 in Germany in 2023, 26.8 in the United States in 2022, 125.9 in Japan in 2022 and 128.1 in Korea in 2022 (`WHS6_102`). More beds are not more implant metal, and the 82 percent hip and knee price cut is why volume in China can rise while the alloy ticket falls. Zimmer's largest plants outside the United States are in Switzerland, Ireland and China, which matches a European manufacturing base plus a China cost exposure.

## 8. Where high performance metals gain or lose relevance

Gains. Procedure counts in the OECD file rose 3.4 percent a year from 2013 to 2023 on a constant country set, and OEM hip and knee revenue rose again in 2025. Trauma and extremities are growing faster than primary hips and knees inside Stryker and Zimmer, and trauma uses titanium and stainless plate, screw and nail forms. Additive cobalt chromium has a cleared path, so powder gains a specification role even if tonnes stay small. Stainless and precious metal wire gains where sensing and leads grow, which is Alleima's statement that medical demand increased in FY2025. Ageing in the already peaked countries, including Germany, supports the rate base. Catch up in Korea and central and eastern Europe supports the count growth.

Losses and substitution. OXINIUM is a deliberate substitute for cobalt chromium bearings at one of the four large OEMs, sold on the absence of nickel, cobalt and chromium and on a 20 year registry result. Any cobalt chromium bar forecast that ignores that franchise overstates the company's cobalt opportunity at Smith & Nephew. Ceramics and highly cross linked polyethylene take the wear surface in many hips. The company does not sell those. Titanium does not disappear when a head becomes ceramic or oxidized zirconium, because stems and cups remain, but the cobalt kilograms per hip fall. The section's line that titanium alloys can replace cobalt in some prostheses is directionally the same point. USGS 2026 does not quantify it. China VBP cuts the price of the implant, which squeezes the alloy invoice even when the operation still happens. Carpenter's 21 percent medical decline shows the mill can lose a year of shipments while procedures do not.

Capital equipment does not rescue the nickel book on current evidence. Imaging is a large device category in MedTech Europe's old sector ranking and a blank bill of materials for nickel alloys. MRI magnet wire is not the company's nickel strip. Until a sourced kilogram per system exists, imaging should be treated as a place high performance nickel can lose attention, not as a growth pool.

## 9. Where the value chain is attractive for the company

The attractive steps are qualified remelt, bar, wire, tube and powder, sold against a specification, not the implant OEM margin. Stryker's GAAP operating margin in 2025 was 19.5 percent (USD 4,889 million of operating income on USD 25,116 million of sales, team arithmetic). The section's "adjusted operating margins of 26 to 28 percent" for Stryker and Zimmer is not the GAAP margin in the 10 Ks used here. Zimmer's GAAP operating margin was 13.3 percent (USD 1,098.1 million on USD 8,231.5 million). Most of the value sits with the OEM. The company's slice is the Carpenter sized book, a few hundred million dollars at the largest listed US specialty mill, and it fell in FY2026.

What is still attractive. Cobalt chromium and titanium bar for the four orthopaedic OEMs, because the procedure base is rising in Europe and the filings show multi year OEM growth, with Germany, Switzerland and the other high rate systems as the technical home market and the United States as the revenue market once the missing registry counts are filled. Oxidized zirconium bar, if the company can qualify it, because Smith & Nephew has made the substitution commercial and the zirconium supplier is unnamed. Stainless wire and precision tube or strip for instruments and leads, because Alleima's medical demand rose while its oil and gas and industrial book was only stable or softer, and because instrument sales are less exposed to the 82 percent China implant price cut than femoral heads are. Additive powder as a qualification, using the 2017 FDA guidance and the 2023 CoCr clearance as the regulatory frame, not as a volume plan.

What is not attractive on this evidence. A broad China implant push while VBP holds. Treating Stryker MedSurg growth as stainless demand. Building a 2035 case on nickel strip for imaging from the VDM page. Adding OEM revenues into an addressable market. Using life expectancy, or a 5 to 6 percent device CAGR from Fitch via MedTech Europe, as the metal growth rate.

Practical priorities for the later section, still as the company. Keep zirconium and cobalt chromium bar, and titanium bar, in front of Zimmer Biomet, Stryker, Smith & Nephew and the separated DePuy Synthes business. Quote Carpenter's inventory decline next to the procedure growth so the board sees the difference. Grow stainless wire and tube beside the implant bar. Treat powder as a named development, with ASTM F3213 as the cobalt chromium reference. Be selective in China. Do not promise an imaging nickel franchise.
