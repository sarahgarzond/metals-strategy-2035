1. Global hydrogen demand surpassed 100 million tonnes in 2025, up almost 3%, but low emissions production was still almost 1 million tonnes. Installed electrolysis doubled in 2025 to more than 4 GW. Committed low emissions production for 2030 is 4.3 million tonnes, and the announced pipeline has shrunk to 27 million tonnes (International Energy Agency, Global Hydrogen Review 2026). The bankable equipment market is the committed slice, not the 100 million tonne molecule market and not the 27 million tonne pipeline. See EE_H2_DEMAND_2025, EE_H2_LOWEM_2025, EE_H2_ELEC_GW_2025, EE_H2_COMMIT_2030 and EE_H2_PIPELINE_2030.
2. The only named European buyer of nickel strip and mesh in the company universe is thyssenkrupp nucera, and its green hydrogen orders collapsed to EUR 26 million in fiscal 2024/25 from EUR 356 million. Chlor alkali orders rose. Nucera dropped solid oxide electrolyser stack mass production in August 2026. Alkaline nickel demand should be qualified project by project.
3. Fuel cell metal demand is a strip story at Bloom Energy, not a hydrogen tonne story. Bloom revenue was USD 2,024 million in 2025, with 2026 guidance of USD 4.4 billion to 4.6 billion, and Fremont capacity is being doubled from 1 GW to 2 GW by the end of 2026. The interconnect is ferritic chromium steel strip. Bloom does not name the grade or the mill. Near term Bloom systems are being bought to make power, including on natural gas, because gas turbine slots are scarce.
4. The operating nuclear fleet, not the new build headline, is the nearer metal market. IAEA operating experience for 2025 puts 413 reactors and 377,076 MWe net in operation at 31 December 2025. The World Nuclear Association table shows a larger "operable" stock and 81 reactors under construction (88,463 MWe gross), of which China is 40 reactors and 45,230 MWe gross. The product forms are zirconium alloy cladding tube and Alloy 690 steam generator tube. Tonnes per plant were not found.
5. Gas turbine hot section parts are the tight high performance metal pocket. Siemens Energy told investors in November 2025 that gas turbine capacity is sold out until fiscal 2028, with a manufacturing path from about 17 GW in fiscal 2024 to above 30 GW in fiscal 2028 to 2030. The World Energy Outlook 2025 already recorded that the surge in gas turbine orders had strained supply chains. The metal form is nickel base superalloy castings and forgings. Nickel ore is not the bottleneck.
6. Wind and grids are large and are growing, and they are the wrong unit for the company's alloy plan. IRENA records 5,149 GW of renewable power at the end of 2025, including 1,291 GW of wind. GWEC counts 165 GW of new wind in 2025 and 969 GW of new wind from 2026 to 2030. Grid investment is about USD 400 billion a year and would need to be about 50% higher by 2030 (IEA Electricity 2026). Those chains use structural steel, electrical steel, copper and rare earth magnets. Nickel alloy content is marginal.
7. OECD STAN output for electrical equipment (C27) and machinery (C28) is large and is not an alloy forecast. Germany's 2023 output was EUR 140.7 billion in C27 and EUR 315.6 billion in C28, in current prices. Electricity, gas, steam and air conditioning supply (D35) is absent from the clean STAN files. China is absent. UNIDO INDSTAT was not available.
8. The existing section draft is right to refuse a single energy growth rate for nickel alloys. Two figures in that draft should be updated. Electricity 2025 said demand rose 4.3% in 2024 and that emerging economies would make up 85% of growth through 2027. Electricity 2026 revises 2024 growth to 4.4%, records 3% growth in 2025, and puts average growth at 3.6% a year from 2026 to 2030, with emerging economies near 80% of the extra consumption through 2030. The hydrogen sentences in the draft match Global Hydrogen Review 2026 and should be kept, with the tighter wording below.

## 1. Market size and growth to 2035

The sector is not one market. Electricity demand is the backdrop. High performance metal demand sits in six much smaller equipment pockets. Sizes below are not additive.

Electricity backdrop. Worldwide electricity demand grew 4.4% in 2024 and 3% in 2025. The IEA forecast average growth is 3.6% a year over 2026 to 2030. Emerging economies account for nearly 80% of additional consumption through 2030, and China for close to half of the global increase (Electricity 2026). The mid year update puts consumption at 27,739 TWh in 2024, 28,583 TWh in 2025 and 30,737 TWh in 2027. In the World Energy Outlook 2025, both the Current Policies Scenario and the Stated Policies Scenario reach about 37,800 TWh in 2035, described as about 40% above the level used in that report (p. 30). Dividing 37,800 by the later 27,739 TWh history gives about 36%, so the "around 40%" line is a rounded scenario statement, not a second history. Renewables reach nearly 55% of generation by 2035 in the Stated Policies Scenario. None of these terawatt hours is an alloy tonne.

Hydrogen, molecule versus equipment. Demand surpassed 100 million tonnes in 2025. Low emissions production was almost 1 million tonnes, concentrated in China, Europe and North America, and is expected to exceed 1% of global production in 2026. Installed electrolysis surpassed 4 GW in 2025 after doubling, with more than 2.5 GW under construction for 2026 operation. New final investment decisions fell below 0.8 million tonnes a year in 2025, after two years near 1 million tonnes a year. Committed production (operating, under construction or at final investment decision) reaches 4.3 million tonnes by 2030, up 3%, and could exceed 6 million tonnes if projects with strong potential take a decision in 2026 or 2027. The announced pipeline fell by 10 million tonnes to 27 million tonnes by 2030. More than 100 GW of announced electrolysis could miss 2030 if decisions are not taken before the end of 2027. Capital spending on low emissions hydrogen projects was nearly USD 7 billion in 2025 and could be nearly USD 10 billion in 2026, still under 1% of energy supply investment. The World Energy Outlook 2025, using an earlier project cut, put the same committed class at 4.2 million tonnes a year by 2030 (p. 92). The later review is the one to use. thyssenkrupp nucera sales were EUR 845 million in fiscal 2024/25, of which green hydrogen was EUR 459 million, with a backlog of EUR 606 million. That is the observed European equipment pocket, and it is shrinking on the green hydrogen side.

Fuel cells. Bloom Energy revenue was USD 2,024.0 million in 2025. Guidance for 2026 was raised to USD 4.4 billion to 4.6 billion. Total backlog was about USD 20 billion at the end of 2025 and product backlog about USD 6 billion. Factory capacity at Fremont is doubling from 1 GW to 2 GW a year by the end of 2026. The IEA also records a fuel cell electric vehicle stock of almost 130,000 at the end of 2025, up 20%, with a projected tripling by 2030 led by trucks in China and cars in Korea. Stationary oxide systems for data centre power and vehicle stacks are different products. Only the stationary ramp is large enough, on the evidence in hand, to matter for interconnect strip before 2035. A tonne forecast is TODO(data).

Nuclear. At 31 December 2025 the IAEA counted 413 reactors in operation with 377,076 MWe net, plus 23 licensed reactors in suspended operation with 19,687 MWe (Operating Experience 2026). The World Nuclear Association table, which draws on IAEA PRIS plus other estimates, lists 443 operable reactors and 405,667 MWe net, and 81 reactors under construction with 88,463 MWe gross. Its World Nuclear Outlook Report 2026, with a cutoff of 1 August 2026, says capacity under construction has risen from 76 GWe to 88 GWe. Reactors already under construction are projected to add 56 GWe by 2030 and a further 32 GWe by 2035. Government ambitions would reach 1,467 GWe gross in 2050 only if operable units run up to 80 years, identified projects are all built, and a further 548 GWe of target capacity also appears. That 2050 figure is not a forecast for alloy orders. The World Energy Outlook 2025, written earlier, said more than 70 GW were under construction and that global nuclear capacity increases by at least one third to 2035 across its scenarios (pp. 19 to 20). A 70% increase by 2035 is stated only for the Net Zero Emissions by 2050 Scenario (p. 40) and should not be used as the base case. Framatome revenue of EUR 5,399 million in 2025 is a company size, from trade press, not a metal market. Cameco's 49% share of Westinghouse revenue was CAD 3,458 million in 2025. A 100% gross up is not published and is not used here.

Gas turbines. The pocket is sized with orders, backlog and manufacturing slots, because a clean segment revenue for Siemens Energy Gas Services is still marked unverified in the company profile (about EUR 12.2 billion, aggregator only). What is on the company's own capital markets day deck of November 2025 is a sold out position until fiscal 2028, about 78 GW of new unit backlog including reservations, and a capacity path from about 110 units or 17 GW in fiscal 2024 to about 160 units or 22 GW on average in fiscal 2025 to 2027 and 210 to 230 units or above 30 GW in fiscal 2028 to 2030 (pp. 4, 5 and 9 of that deck). A later secondary report put the firm backlog at 69 GW by August 2026, with slots mainly in 2029 and 2030. The two gigawatt figures differ because reservations are in the first. In the World Energy Outlook, gas fired generation serving data centres rises by 220 TWh to 2035 in the Stated Policies Scenario and by 285 TWh in the Current Policies Scenario (p. 53). Coal and gas capacity additions together were about 80 GW in 2024, half the 160 GW of 2015, while the announced gas plant pipeline has jumped (p. 92). There is no audited world market value for hot section alloys in these sources. ATI's conventional energy plus specialty energy shares were 7% and 6% of fiscal 2025 sales of USD 4,587.4 million. That is about USD 600 million if the percentages are applied to sales (team calculation). The 10-K does not say how much of that is gas turbine superalloy rather than other energy or oil and gas work, so it is an upper context figure for one competitor, not the market.

Wind. IRENA Renewable Capacity Statistics 2026, maximum net generating capacity at year end, in megawatts: world wind 1,132,690 in 2024 and 1,291,368 in 2025, so 158,678 MW added in 2025. The highlights brochure rounds this to 159 GW and to 1,291 GW. It should not be subtracted from the IRENA stock.

Grids. Annual grid spending has risen more slowly than generation investment and stands at about USD 400 billion, against about USD 1 trillion a year for electricity generation (World Energy Outlook 2025, p. 19). Electricity 2026 says meeting demand through 2030 would require annual grid investment to rise by roughly 50% from today's USD 400 billion, and that more than 2,500 GW of projects sit in connection queues. Siemens Energy Grid Technologies sold more than EUR 2 billion to hyperscalers in fiscal 2025. In the third quarter of fiscal 2026 a secondary call summary gives EUR 5.4 billion of orders, a EUR 51 billion backlog and a 19.9% margin. ## 2. Growth drivers and risks

Drivers differ by pocket. Electricity growth of 3.6% a year to 2030, data centres, electric vehicles, cooling and industry pull generation and grids (Electricity 2026). Data centre investment is expected to reach USD 580 billion in 2025, above the USD 540 billion spent on global oil supply, but a tripling of data centre electricity to 2035 is still less than 10% of global electricity demand growth and is concentrated in the United States, China and the European Union (World Energy Outlook 2025, pp. 19 to 20). In the United States, data centres are about half of electricity demand growth to 2030. That concentration is why gas turbines, fuel cells and some nuclear deals are being bought as firm power, not why wind gigawatts become nickel strip.

Hydrogen's firm demand is still refining and industry. Fuel cell vehicles and shipping are policy led and small in tonnes. New offtake agreements were 1.7 million tonnes a year in 2025, and only about one fifth were firm. Fossil hydrogen stays cheaper than renewable hydrogen in most places in the near term, so support policies still decide final investment decisions (Global Hydrogen Review 2026).

Nuclear drivers are life extension of the 377 GWe in operation, the 88 GWe gross already under construction, and a political pipeline that is much larger than the construction start rate. Gas turbine drivers are data centre firm power, Middle East fuel switching and replacement of older plants, against a manufacturing system that is already sold out.

Risks. Hydrogen project cancellations and a thinner decision rate can persist past 2027, which would strand any strip capacity built on the 27 million tonne pipeline. Nucera's order intake is the European evidence that this risk is already in the accounts. China holds most electrolysis manufacturing capacity and can price European alkaline stacks out of export markets. The IEA notes excess Chinese manufacturing capacity and offers below cost.

Fuel cell risk is technology and fuel choice. If gas turbine lead times ease, the premium for an oxide system running on gas can fade. The interconnect grade may stay with a US mill. Chromium poisoning of cathodes is managed by coatings, which ties the sale to a coated strip qualification rather than to a commodity coil.

Nuclear risk is schedule. Framatome's 2025 growth was tied to EPR projects, while Flamanville 3, Hinkley Point C and Sizewell C have faced delays and cost overruns. A plant that slips five years does not consume steam generator tubes early. Accident tolerant cladding, if it moves from coated zirconium to iron chromium aluminium or silicon carbide, would change the tube alloy on a fuel cycle timescale. No share of the fleet on those claddings was found in the sources opened.

Gas turbine risk for a metal producer is qualification and substitution, not a shortage of orders. Ceramic matrix composites already take some hot section work. Siemens Energy has bought a ceramics business. Casting and forging slots, not nickel cathode, are what the trade press calls the constraint. A mill without the released route does not capture the sold out position.

Wind and grid risk for this company is analytical. Treating 969 GW of wind or a EUR 51 billion grid backlog as alloy demand would overstate the opportunity. Connection queues of more than 2,500 GW, and the IEA estimate that about 20% of data centre additions to 2030 could be delayed (World Energy Outlook 2025, p. 52), also mean that generation headlines arrive later than the capacity statistics suggest. Dublin has paused new data centre connections until 2028. Northern Virginia and parts of the United Kingdom and Europe report multi year queues.

## 3. Emerging technologies and applications

Alkaline electrolysis remains the nickel strip and mesh route and is the route nucera sells, including the NEOM scale of project as described by the company. Proton exchange membrane electrolysis, which is Siemens Energy's Silyzer family and the Linde joint venture with ITM Power, uses titanium components and platinum group metals in an acid environment. It is not a nickel mesh market. Solid oxide electrolysis would have used ferritic interconnect strip and a nickel cermet. Nucera stopped plans for stack mass production in August 2026, kept an 8 MW pilot at Arnstadt with Fraunhofer IKTS, and is not building the planned 300 MW factory. Anion exchange membrane designs are discussed in the industry as a way to cut precious metals. No commercial nickel form or tonne figure was found, so they stay TODO(data).

Stationary solid oxide fuel cells are being sold as quick firm power for data centres. The IEA investment chapter of the Global Hydrogen Review 2026 notes that Bloom Energy's valuation moved because data centre electricity demand and long gas turbine backlogs improve the case for its fuel cells, initially on natural gas. That is a power product. It should not be filed under hydrogen demand. Vehicle fuel cells remain a separate, smaller stack market, led by China and Korea on the IEA stock figures.

Small modular reactors appear as agreements and expressions of interest for 30 GW, mainly for data centres (World Energy Outlook 2025, p. 20). Construction starts in the World Nuclear Association list are still dominated by large water reactors in China, plus early small units such as Darlington SMR-1 in Canada. Expressions of interest are not tube orders. Higher assay low enriched uranium fuel, which Framatome is licensed to fabricate at Richland with production possible from 2027, changes enrichment more than it changes zirconium tonnes. The tonne effect is TODO(data).

In gas turbines the emerging material is ceramic matrix composite hardware in the hottest sections, beside nickel superalloy blades, vanes and discs. In grids the emerging response to the queue is not only new lines. Electricity 2026 estimates that non firm connection agreements could free roughly 750 GW to 900 GW and that grid enhancing technologies, including reconductoring and voltage uprating, could free roughly 450 GW to 700 GW. Those are aluminium, copper and steel conductor projects, not nickel alloy projects, unless a specific high temperature connector is specified. No such specification was found.

## 4. Materials, grades and product forms

Nickel strip and mesh, alkaline electrolysis and chlor alkali. The portfolio benchmark names Nickel 201 strip for alkaline electrolyser cathodes (VDM Metals, n.d.-e, the team's existing citation). Shop practice uses woven wire, expanded mesh and thin strip, often with a Raney type nickel surface, because the potassium hydroxide electrolyte needs a nickel electrode that stays stable and provides area. The buyer in the company universe is thyssenkrupp nucera. Nucera's sustainability report names nickel and titanium among minerals sourced from Asia, Africa, Europe and North America, without tonnes. Chlor alkali electrodes are the same family and, on 2025 and 2026 orders, the more reliable one. This form does not transfer to proton exchange membrane stacks.

Solid oxide interconnect strip. The same VDM benchmark names Crofer 22 grades for solid oxide fuel cell and electrolyser interconnects. Crofer 22 APU is a ferritic chromium steel, on the order of 22% chromium, supplied as cold rolled strip, usually coated so that chromium species do not poison the cathode. The thermal expansion is chosen to match the ceramic cell at roughly 600 to 850 degrees Celsius. It is not a nickel superalloy and it is not Nickel 201. Bloom is the named buyer and has not disclosed grade, coating, kilogram per kilowatt or mill. Nickel still enters the cell as a nickel ceramic anode powder. That powder volume is TODO(data) and is a different product from strip.

Zirconium alloy tube and nickel alloy tube, nuclear. Fuel rods use zirconium alloy cladding (Zircaloy and ZIRLO type alloys) as seamless tube because the neutron penalty is low and the water side corrosion resistance is proven on the operating fleet. Steam generators on pressurised water reactors use thermally treated Alloy 690 tube (UNS N06690), with Alloy 800 also in some designs, because the alloy resists primary water stress corrosion cracking. Alleima offers Alloy 690 and Alloy 800 steam generator tube from Sandviken to ASME and RCC-M. Framatome and Westinghouse buy tubes and also fabricate fuel, so they are customers and, in fuel, competitors in the fabrication step. ATI is a zirconium and hafnium producer as well as a superalloy mill. Tube count and tube weight per steam generator were not found. Replacement and reload on the 377 GWe in operation dominate any tonne account before the 56 GWe due by 2030 is all online. That split was not quantified and is TODO(data).

Superalloys, gas turbine hot section. Nickel base superalloys are used as investment castings for blades and vanes, including directional and single crystal parts, and as forgings and billet for discs and structural parts. The reason is creep and oxidation above the range of stainless steel. Siemens Energy is the named buyer in the company universe. GE Vernova and Mitsubishi Power are the other large frame OEMs and were not profiled in this pass. The Acerinox group, through the Haynes portfolio, is one competitor in this alloy family, together with VDM flat products. It is not a second independent group. ATI, Howmet, Special Metals inside Precision Castparts and Berkshire Hathaway, Carpenter Technology and Aubert & Duval are the other named competitors for mill products, forgings, castings and powder. Which of those routes the company can sell is a capability question for the client, not a fact in these sources.

Wind and grids, for contrast. A wind turbine's metal mass is tower and foundation steel, electrical steel and copper in the generator, and rare earth magnets in many direct drive machines. Blades are composites. Nickel alloys show up in fasteners, bearings and some offshore corrosion parts, not in the gigawatt. A transformer is grain oriented electrical steel and copper or aluminium. High performance nickel is a niche (bellows, expansion joints, special connectors) inside the grid backlog. Environmental treatment equipment, such as flue gas and scrubber service, uses corrosion resistant nickel alloys in plate, sheet and welded tube. The VDM benchmark lists that route. No market size was found, so it is not given a pocket value.

## 5. Geographic concentration of demand

Electrolysis capacity and manufacturing are concentrated in China. The 2025 review said China accounted for 65% of electrolyser capacity installed or at a final investment decision, and nearly 60% of manufacturing capacity. The 2026 review says China is more than 60% of capacity by 2026 but only about 25% of estimated investment, while Europe is less than 20% of capacity and about 45% of investment, because European capital cost per unit is higher. A European mill that prices off European projects is looking at the smaller, higher cost slice. Nucera's order collapse shows that slice can pause even when the global installed base is growing.

Fuel cell power demand in the evidence is concentrated in the United States, and in data centre clusters. Bloom's named counterparties in secondary reports include American Electric Power (Wyoming), Oracle and Brookfield. Those names are aggregator sourced in the company profile and should be confirmed in the 10-K before they are used as a target list.

Nuclear construction capacity is concentrated in China: 40 of 81 units under construction and 45,230 of 88,463 MWe gross, about 51% (team calculation from the World Nuclear Association table). The operating fleet, which sets reload and replacement demand, is still spread across the United States (94 reactors, 96,952 MWe net), France (57 reactors, 63,000 MWe net) and China (65 reactors, 65,085 MWe net) in that same table. Steam generator tube qualifications are national and code specific (ASME, RCC-M). A European tube mill does not automatically sell into a Chinese domestically supplied island.

Gas turbine ordering for the current boom is pulled by the United States and, in the World Energy Outlook, the Middle East, while a large part of Siemens Energy manufacturing sits in Europe. The sold out window is a global slot constraint, not a German demand statistic.

Wind installations in 2025 were about three quarters Chinese on the IRENA difference calculated above. GWEC says Asia, led by China and India, commissioned 131 GW, about 80% of its 165 GW total, and that China is about 63% of expected installations in 2026, with more than half of growth outside China only by the end of the decade. Either definition leaves China as the volume market and leaves high performance nickel as a minor input.

Grid equipment orders in the Siemens Energy accounts are visibly pulled by hyperscalers, which the World Energy Outlook places mainly in the United States, China and the European Union. OECD STAN cannot locate this, because China and D35 are missing and because C27 mixes all electrical equipment.

## 8. Where high performance metals gain or lose relevance

They gain where temperature, corrosion or a nuclear code forces a nickel or zirconium alloy, and where the buyer is short of qualified parts. That is the gas turbine hot section through at least the sold out window into fiscal 2028 to 2030, Alloy 690 tube on new and replacement steam generators, zirconium cladding for as long as the water reactor fleet reloads with zirconium, and nickel mesh on alkaline stacks that actually reach a contract. Chlor alkali is the gain that does not depend on the hydrogen pipeline.

They lose relevance, or never had it, where another material does the job at lower cost. Wind blades are composites. Towers and most foundations are steel. Generators are electrical steel and copper. Grids are electrical steel, copper and aluminium. Solar modules have essentially no high performance nickel content in the sources used here. Proton exchange membrane electrolysis substitutes titanium and platinum group metals for nickel electrodes. Solid oxide interconnects substituted a ferritic steel for ceramic or for a nickel alloy because the steel is cheaper and matches expansion. Ceramic matrix composites substitute for some superalloy hardware in the hottest turbine sections. Coated zirconium, iron chromium aluminium and silicon carbide are the substitution path in fuel cladding, still a minority topic in the sources opened. In each of these cases the gigawatt can rise while the nickel alloy tonne does not.

A second loss is economic rather than technical. Announced electrolysis that is not at a final investment decision does not buy mesh. Nucera's retreat from solid oxide stack factories removes a European interconnect volume case that the portfolio benchmark would otherwise have suggested.

## 9. Where the value chain is attractive for the company

Most attractive, if the quality system is already in reach: nickel superalloy billet and forgings tied to gas turbine discs and structural hot section parts, sold to Siemens Energy and the other frame OEMs. The buyer's manufacturing slots are sold out into the late 2020s, the World Energy Outlook records strained turbine supply chains, and the constraint the trade press describes is qualified castings and forgings. This is a qualification and release problem with a small number of buyers. It is not a reason to add commodity melt capacity. Ceramic substitution caps the hottest parts. The company should treat cast blade alloys and forged disc alloys as separate releases.

Next, and only with an existing nuclear release: Alloy 690 steam generator tube, and zirconium alloy tube if the company already melts or fabricates zirconium. The operating fleet is the demand. New build is real but China heavy and slow. Alleima already occupies the European tube position. Entering from zero before 2035 is not supported by the order evidence in hand. ATI is the zirconium competitor to assume, not a customer.

Selective, and small until a contract is firm: nickel strip and mesh for nucera and other alkaline and chlor alkali plants. The attractive step is qualified strip, mesh and surface quality on repeat chlor alkali and on the few electrolysis projects with a final investment decision, not a new plant sized to the 27 million tonne pipeline. Europe is the practical collaboration region. Asian volume has to clear a Chinese cost position that the IEA describes as excess manufacturing capacity. Proton exchange membrane projects of Siemens Energy and Linde should be screened out of the nickel mesh account.

A qualification programme, not a capacity decision: ferritic interconnect strip for solid oxide fuel cells, using the Crofer benchmark as the specification to meet or to displace. Bloom's doubling to 2 GW is the demand signal. The undisclosed US supply base, the coating step and the fact that the product is stainless strip rather than a nickel superalloy all argue for a small funded trial. Release more money only if a buyer names a grade, a volume and a delivery year.

Not attractive as an alloy strategy: wind gigawatts, solar gigawatts, and the grid transformer backlog. Those are large, well documented and mostly someone else's metal. They belong in the electricity narrative so the board does not confuse them with the four product forms above. Environmental corrosion resistant plate for flue gas and scrubbers stays a niche until a market size is found. It should not be grown by applying the electricity growth rate.

## Corrections to the existing section draft

The draft's warning, that electricity and renewable capacity growth cannot be applied to nickel alloy tonnes, is confirmed by the pocket split above and should stand.

Hydrogen. The draft said demand exceeded 100 million tonnes in 2025, low emissions output was about 1 million tonnes, the 2030 pipeline fell to 27 million tonnes, committed projects plus those with strong potential were just over 6 million tonnes, and installed electrolysis exceeded 4 GW. Global Hydrogen Review 2026 supports that wording. The more precise committed figure is 4.3 million tonnes by 2030, which could exceed 6 million tonnes with the strong potential projects. "Just over 6" is the upside case, not the committed case alone. The draft already said "committed projects and those with strong potential", so the sense is right. Use 4.3 and "more than 6" when the sentence is next edited. The older World Energy Outlook figure of 4.2 million tonnes is the previous vintage, not a contradiction.

Electricity. Replace the draft's 4.3% growth in 2024, the 3.9% a year path for 2025 to 2027, and the 85% emerging economy share with the Electricity 2026 figures: 4.4% in 2024, 3% in 2025, 3.6% a year from 2026 to 2030, and nearly 80% of additional consumption in emerging economies through 2030. Keep the draft's point that these are electricity indicators, not alloy forecasts. The 4.3% and 85% lines were accurate for the 2025 edition and are now superseded.

No other number in the short draft was found to be wrong. The portfolio benchmark sentence on Nickel 201 and Crofer 22 is retained as a benchmark, with the product forms separated above so that ferritic interconnect strip is not described as a nickel alloy.
