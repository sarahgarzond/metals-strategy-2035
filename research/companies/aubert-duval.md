## Snapshot
FY2024 revenue EUR 844 million (reported by press in August 2025 citing 2024 figures; not an audited standalone filing), approximately 4,200 employees at October 2025. The company website shows EUR 962 million in revenues and 4,400 employees (undated; likely a 2025 estimate, with management targeting above EUR 1 billion by end 2025). Aubert and Duval is a private company and does not publish audited standalone reports in English. Owned since April 2023 in equal thirds by Airbus, Safran and Tikehau Capital, with the French state holding a golden share. Headquarters at Issy-les-Moulineaux (Hauts-de-Seine). Source: Armees.com (August 2025, secondary), Aubert and Duval website (undated), company press releases.

## Segments and end markets
Aubert and Duval does not report public segmental revenue splits. By activity and customer disclosure, aerospace accounts for approximately two thirds of revenues and is the primary demand driver. Additional markets are energy (including nuclear), defence, medical and other high-performance industrial applications. The company describes itself as a strategic partner for civil and space aeronautics, defence, healthcare and nuclear energy, participating in securing supply chains for critical sectors and qualified to the most demanding buyer specifications, including Airbus, Safran and other Tier 1 aerospace primes. It masters four alloy families: nickel-based superalloys, titanium alloys, high-performance steels and aluminium alloys.

## Relevance to the Client
Aubert and Duval competes with the company in nickel-based superalloys and titanium alloys in bar, forging and powder form for aerospace, defence and energy. It is one of the few European producers to master the full route from melting to closed-die forging in all four alloy families. Its powder metallurgy capability (gas atomization under vacuum, VIM gas atomization) produces AM powders for nickel superalloys (grades Ni625, Ni718, AD730, ABD-900AM, ABD-1000AM developed with Alloyed Ltd) and high-performance steels competing in the additive manufacturing market. The Interforge subsidiary in Issoire operates the 65,000-tonne forging press, the most powerful in the western world, which is a strategic capability barrier that few competitors can replicate. Aubert and Duval's customer and shareholder overlap (Airbus and Safran as both owners and primary buyers) creates deep qualification lock-in that is difficult for new entrants to displace.

## Capacity and investments
Main French production sites (10 industrial sites, 11 international commercial offices): Les Ancizes (Puy-de-Dome) for primary melting and titanium processing including PAM-CHR cold crucible plasma arc fusion for titanium ingots; Pamiers (Ariege) for large closed-die forgings of nickel and titanium alloys and high-performance steels (approximately 1,200 employees); Issoire (Puy-de-Dome) for the Interforge 65,000-tonne forging press and aluminium forgings; Firminy (Loire) and Heyrieux for steels. Furnace equipment: electric arc furnaces and AOD/VOD converters up to 60 tonnes, vacuum induction melting (VIM) up to 20 tonnes, ESR and VAR remelting furnaces up to 30 tonnes, titanium plasma arc melting. Die-forging presses from 4,500 to 65,000 tonnes closed-die, open-die presses from 1,200 to 10,000 tonnes; rolling mill for bar 7 to 200 mm diameter. Major investments underway as part of a EUR 350 million four-year modernization plan: new 6,000-tonne die-forging press at Pamiers (EUR 75 million investment, SMS Group supplier, operational from Q2 2026, with isothermal forging module), new surface inspection workshop (Atelier de Controle de Surface) at Pamiers rebuilt after a 2021 fire (EUR 29 million investment supported by France 2030/ADEME, operational October 2025, capacity raised to 12,500 parts per year by 2027 from 6,000 before the fire). Electrification of the Pamiers furnace park is planned alongside the new press, targeting 50% CO2 reduction. The new ACS received Airbus qualification in April 2025. Aubert and Duval is a shareholder in EcoTitanium (Les Ancizes), which recycles aeronautical-grade titanium scrap using PAM/VAR technology.

## Market statements
In a press release of May 2025, CEO Etienne Galan stated: "If the operating result of Aubert and Duval was just positive last year, we will not generate positive cash flow until 2027. We therefore still have much work to do to continue investing, modernizing and developing." The company targets revenues above EUR 1 billion by end 2025 and production of more than 53,000 parts in 2026. The Armees.com article of August 2025 noted that the aerospace recovery after COVID and rising defence orders are the two main demand drivers, with Airbus production ramp and military turbine engine orders cited specifically. Les Echos (March 2025) described the company as experiencing an "unexpected industrial rebound" driven by aeronautics and defence, two thirds of its activity.

## Customers and suppliers
Primary customers are Airbus (also a co-owner since April 2023), Safran (also a co-owner), and other aerospace primes and engine manufacturers. Aubert and Duval holds a shareholder position in EcoTitanium alongside ADEME and Credit Agricole Centre France, ensuring recycled titanium feedstock. Raw material suppliers for nickel, titanium sponge and alloy scrap are not publicly named.
