# Howmet Aerospace (customer and competitor, United States)
## Snapshot
- 2025: sales USD 8,252m (+11%), operating income USD 2,046m, margin 24.8% (calculated), segment adjusted EBITDA USD 2,507m [1, p. 21, 28].
- About 25,430 employees in 23 countries [1, p. 7].
- Q3 2026: sales USD 2,703m versus 2,089m (+29%), operating income USD 777m; nine months sales USD 7,563m [2, p. 27].

## Segments and end markets
- Third party sales 2025: Engine Products USD 4,320m (adjusted EBITDA margin 33.3%), Fastening Systems 1,745m (30.4%), Engineered Structures 1,148m (21.2%), Forged Wheels 1,039m (28.5%) [1, p. 25 to 27].
- End markets: aerospace about 70%, commercial transportation 15%, gas turbines 11% [1, p. 2].
- Q3 2026 Engine Products USD 1,451m (+33%), driven by commercial aerospace, gas turbines and engine spares [2, p. 29].

## Relevance to the Client
Mainly a customer: investment castings (airfoils) and seamless rolled rings made of nickel superalloys, forgings of nickel and titanium, and fasteners in nickel superalloy. It buys vacuum melted master alloy, bar and billet. It is also a competitor in titanium and rings. ATI lists Howmet as a competitor in titanium [ATI 10-K]. Several of its largest customers run captive superalloy furnaces for castings [1, p. 7].

## Capacity and investments
- Capex USD 453m in 2025 (2024: USD 321m), mainly Engine Products capacity; about 5% of sales in 2026 [1, p. 31].
- Casting sites in France, Germany, Hungary, UK, Japan, the US and others; UK sites Glossop and Ickles produce alloy metal and billets [1, p. 4].
- Acquisitions: Consolidated Aerospace Manufacturing (fasteners) for about USD 1,812m, closed 6 Apr 2026; Brunner Manufacturing 6 Feb 2026 [2, p. 10].

## Market statements
- "demand in the commercial aerospace, defense aerospace, and gas turbines markets is expected to increase, including engine spares" [1, p. 26].
- Gas turbines serve "growing demand for electricity generation, driven by accelerating data center build-out" [1, p. 2].
- "Product price increases are in excess of material and inflationary cost pass through" [1, p. 21].
- "we depend on a number of limited source or sole source suppliers, such as for titanium sponge and specialized metal alloys" [1, p. 10].
- Raw materials: nickel, cobalt, titanium, aluminum purchase obligations up to five years [1, p. 32].
