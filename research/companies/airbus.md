# Airbus SE (customer, France)

## Snapshot
Fiscal 2025 group revenue was EUR 73,420 million (up 6%), with EBIT adjusted of EUR 7,128 million (2024: EUR 5,354 million) and free cash flow before customer financing of EUR 4,574 million [CO_AIRBUS_REV_2025]. Source: FY 2025 results press release, pp. 1 to 5. The group had 165,294 employees at end 2025 (2024: 156,921). Order book stands at EUR 619 billion (8,754 commercial aircraft). H1 2026 delivered 547 aircraft (9M 2026: 547 aircraft versus 507 in 9M 2025), with a full year 2026 delivery target of approximately 870 and EBIT adjusted guidance of approximately EUR 7.5 billion [CO_AIRBUS_9M_2026].

## Segments and end markets
Commercial Aircraft revenue was EUR 52,600 million in 2025, with EBIT adjusted EUR 5,470 million [CO_AIRBUS_DELIV_2025]. Helicopters contributed EUR 9,000 million (EBIT adjusted EUR 925 million) and Defence and Space EUR 13,400 million (EBIT adjusted EUR 798 million, order intake EUR 17.7 billion). Commercial deliveries in 2025 were 793 aircraft: A220 93, A320 family 607, A330 36, A350 57 [CO_AIRBUS_DELIV_2025]. Source: press release, pp. 1 to 2. The A320 family rate target is 70 to 75 aircraft per month by end 2027, and A220 rate 13 per month by 2028 [CO_AIRBUS_GUIDE_2026]. Gross commercial orders in 2025 were 1,000 aircraft (up 14% from 2024).

## Relevance to the Client
Airbus is an indirect demand driver for all high-performance metals in scope: titanium and aluminium for airframe structures (skin, frames, spars, pylons), nickel superalloys in engines supplied by CFM International, Pratt and Whitney and Rolls-Royce, and titanium in landing gear and engine pylons. Each A350 uses approximately 15% titanium by structural weight. Airbus purchases raw structural titanium and aluminium directly; engine hot-section nickel alloys reach Airbus through the engine OEMs as assembled units. The A320 family ramp-up to 70 to 75 aircraft per month by end 2027 directly increases the volume of nickel superalloy consumed by CFM LEAP engines and Pratt and Whitney GTF engines. Airbus in December 2025 acquired Spirit AeroSystems facilities (US, France, Morocco, Northern Ireland, Scotland) to stabilise key work packages [CO_AIRBUS_DELIV_2025].

## Capacity and investments
Airbus opened new A320 final assembly lines (FAL) in China and the United States in Q4 2025 to support the rate ramp [CO_AIRBUS_DELIV_2025]. Titanium circularity: since 2024, EcoTitanium has been recycling titanium production scraps at Airbus Saint-Eloi (Toulouse); the programme extended in 2025 to Varel and Augsburg (Germany) [CO_AIRBUS_TICI_2025]. In June 2025 EcoTitanium and IMET Alloys produced the first ingot from end-of-life titanium pylon material qualified for aerospace-grade use. ## Market statements
CEO Faury stated at the FY 2025 results that Airbus is "facing significant Pratt and Whitney engine shortages" negatively impacting delivery guidance [CO_AIRBUS_GUIDE_2026]. Source: press release, pp. 1 to 2. The annual report (Airbus Operations Ltd) confirms "continued supply-chain challenges" and that deliveries were achieved "against the backdrop of an operating environment that continues to be complex and fast-changing" [CO_AIRBUS_DELIV_2025]. Airbus has diversified titanium sourcing to reduce reliance on Russian-origin material (post-2022) and is building recycled titanium capability through EcoTitanium to reduce primary supply pressure. ## Customers and suppliers
Key airline customers include Air France, Lufthansa, Ryanair, IndiGo, Singapore Airlines and American Airlines. Engine OEM customers receiving Airbus airframes: CFM International (A320neo LEAP), Pratt and Whitney (A320neo/A220 GTF), Rolls-Royce (A350 Trent XWB).
