# TSMC (customer and end user, Taiwan)

Access note: the 20-F for 2025 was read from SEC EDGAR as text without page numbers, so Item references and the printed page numbers found in the text are given. The 2026 capex update and Q3 2026 statements come from search summaries because investor.tsmc.com returned HTTP 403, and need a check against the management report.

## Snapshot
* 2025 net revenue was NT$ 3,809,054 million (US$ 121,423 million) and income from operations NT$ 1,936,092 million, an operating margin of 50.8% (Item 5) [CO_TSMC_REV_2025, CO_TSMC_OPINC_2025].
* TSMC had 90,557 full-time employees at year end 2025, up from 83,825 (Item 6, p. 54) [CO_TSMC_EMP_2025].

## Segments and end markets
* High Performance Computing contributed 58% of 2025 revenue (2024: 51%), smartphone 29%, Internet of Things 5%, automotive 5% and digital consumer electronics 1% (Item 5, p. 20) [CO_TSMC_REV_2025].
* Revenue grew by NT$ 914,746 million, mainly from North America (up 42%). Wafer fabrication was about 86% of revenue.

## Relevance to the Client
* TSMC buys no alloy directly in volume. It is an indirect demand driver for high purity stainless and nickel alloy gas lines, valves and chamber parts, which are bought through Applied Materials, Lam Research, ASML and fab construction contractors.
* The 20-F names silicon wafers, gases, chemicals and photoresist as raw materials and no metal (Item 3) [CO_TSMC_RAWMAT_2025]. Piping and tool alloy content per fab: TODO(data).
* Vendor pages describe 316L electropolished stainless as the standard for ultra high purity gas lines and Hastelloy C-276 and C-22 for halogen service. These are supplier marketing claims and need a standards source such as SEMI F20.

## Capacity and investments
* Capital expenditure was NT$ 1,272,411 million in 2025 against NT$ 956,007 million in 2024 [CO_TSMC_CAPEX_2025].
* The 20-F guides 2026 capex to US$ 52 billion to 56 billion (Item 5, p. 35) [CO_TSMC_CAPEX_2026G]. Press reports of the July 2026 call give US$ 60 billion to 64 billion, unchanged in October [CO_TSMC_CAPEX_2026G2].
* Annual capacity exceeded 17 million 12-inch equivalent wafers in 2025 (Item 4) [CO_TSMC_CAPACITY_2025].
* Arizona: first fab in high volume at the end of 2024, second under construction, third started in 2025 and more land bought in 2026. The second fab is reported to start volume production in Q3 2027. CHIPS Act support is up to US$ 6.6 billion grants and US$ 5 billion loans (Item 4) [CO_TSMC_FABS_2025].
* Japan: JASM Fab 23 in Kumamoto began volume production in December 2024, and construction of the second site began in October 2025. TSMC owns 72.6% [CO_TSMC_FABS_2025].
* Germany: ESMC in Dresden began construction in 2024 with Bosch, Infineon and NXP at 10% each, and up to EUR 5 billion state aid. Start of production: TODO(data), since the chairman said it depends on customer needs.

## Market statements
* "Shortages in the supply of some materials ... have resulted in occasional industry-wide price adjustments and delivery delays" (Item 3, Raw materials risk) [CO_TSMC_RAWMAT_2025].
* The 2026 capex focus is 2 nm and 3 nm capacity in Fabs 20, 21 and 22 plus specialty and advanced packaging in Fabs 23 and 24 (Item 5, p. 35).
* Management reportedly said AI demand exceeds supply and plans prudently for 2027 (secondary).
