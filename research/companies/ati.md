# ATI Inc. (competitor, United States)
## Snapshot
- Fiscal 2025 (year to 28 Dec 2025): sales USD 4,587.4m (2024: 4,362.1m), operating income USD 640.9m, margin 14.0% (calculated), adjusted EBITDA USD 859.3m (18.7%) [1, p. 21].
- About 7,600 active employees, 15% outside the US [1, p. 7].
- Latest quarter (Q3 2026): sales USD 1,423.7m versus 1,125.5m (+26%), backlog about USD 4.3bn, adjusted EBITDA USD 328m [2, p. 27; 3, p. 3].

## Segments and end markets
- HPMC USD 2,441.7m (EBITDA margin 23.6%), AA&S USD 2,145.7m (16.3%) [1, p. 24].
- Aerospace and defense 68% of 2025 sales (62% in 2024): commercial jet engines 39% (USD 1,762.9m), commercial airframes 17%, defense 12%. Other: conventional energy 7%, specialty energy 6%, automotive 5%, electronics 4%, medical 3% [1, p. 22].
- Nickel based and specialty alloys 46% of revenue, precision forgings, castings and components 22% [1, p. 22].
- Q3 2026: jet engines 42% of sales, up 39% year on year [2, p. 27; 3, p. 4].

## Relevance to the Client
Direct competitor in nickel base alloys and superalloys for jet engines (premium melted billet, powder, disc forgings), plus flat rolled nickel alloys (AA&S, plate, sheet, strip) and zirconium, hafnium, niobium. ATI names Berkshire Hathaway (PCC), Howmet and Carpenter as HPMC competitors [1, p. 5]. It also supplies the same customers as the Client would target (GE Aerospace, Safran, Rolls-Royce, Pratt and Whitney). Strip and thin flat products for electronics via a Shanghai PRS joint venture (60%) [1, p. 5].

## Capacity and investments
- Nickel melting: Monroe and Bakers NC and Richland WA (VIM, VAR, ESR, plasma, electron beam); Lockport NY (VIM, VAR, ESR); titanium VAR in Albany OR [1, p. 17].
- Capex USD 280.6m in 2025 (2024: 239.1m), aimed at aerospace and defense capacity [1, p. 26]. Nine months 2026: USD 216.6m [2, p. 20].
- Q3 2026 call: ramp of a sixth nickel based superalloy cost about 200 basis points of HPMC margin; announced capex in nickel superalloys and premium titanium; no "monolithic" new assets planned [3, p. 6, 12].

## Market statements
- "backlog provides visibility" with "extended lead times for our most differentiated products" [3, p. 3].
- Lead times: premium quality titanium "about 20 months", isothermal forgings "more than two years" [3, p. 11].
- Customers "need qualified capacity, differentiated technology and certainty of supply" [3, p. 4].
- Nickel use about 70 million lb in 2025, a USD 1.00/lb move equals about USD 70m cost, mostly passed on by surcharge and index [1, p. 37].
- Risk factors name shortages of graphite electrodes, helium and argon, and Chinese export controls on inputs [1, p. 6, 10].

## Customers and suppliers
- Long term agreements with GE Aviation and Safran (titanium, nickel and vacuum melted alloys), Rolls-Royce (disc quality mill products, forgings), Pratt and Whitney (isothermal and conventional forgings) [1, p. 26].
- Suppliers: nickel, cobalt, chromium, titanium sponge and zirconium sourced partly abroad; graphite electrodes and gases from third parties [1, p. 6, 10].
