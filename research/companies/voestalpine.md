# voestalpine (Böhler) (competitor, AT)

## Snapshot
Business year 2025/26 (ended 31 March 2026): voestalpine Group revenue EUR 15,063 million (EUR 15,744m in BY2024/25), group EBITDA EUR 1,486 million, group EBIT EUR 724 million (4.8% margin), approximately 48,800 employees across more than 500 group companies in over 50 countries. The High Performance Metals (HPM) Division, which houses Böhler and Uddeholm, generated revenue of EUR 2,749.7 million (EUR 3,182.2m in BY2024/25), EBITDA EUR 216.2 million (7.9% margin) and EBIT EUR 57.3 million (2.1% margin), with 11,062 FTE employees. The revenue decline of 13.6% reflects the January 2025 divestment of Buderus Edelstahl (Wetzlar, Germany) and weak European industrial demand. Shares listed on the Vienna Stock Exchange since 1995. Source: Annual Report 2025/26 p. 2, 6, 62 and 63.

## Segments and end markets
The HPM Division does not report sub-segments by revenue in the annual report but describes three market segments in its divisional commentary. Tooling (the largest by both volume and value) supplies tool steel and high-speed steel to the mould-making, cutting tool and stamping die industries; this segment faced intense global competition and price pressure in BY2025/26. Industrials encompasses specialty steels and machined components in automotive (valve steels, engine components), food and beverage, medical technology, mining and racing applications; automotive demand was subdued while medical and racing performed well. Aerospace and Power Industries covers specialty materials and forged parts for the civil and military aerospace and power generation sectors and was described as the strongest-performing segment in BY2025/26, driven by European aerospace production ramp and increased North American aircraft manufacturer volumes. By customer sector (BY2025/26): aerospace 21%, automotive 20%, energy 18%, mechanical engineering 18%, white and consumer goods 11%, other 8%, construction 4%. By region: EU 43% (Austria 4%), Asia 20%, USMCA 16%, South America 8%, rest of world 13%.

## Relevance to the Client
Böhler and Uddeholm (the production brands within HPM) compete with the company in nickel-based alloys and titanium as long products, forgings and powder metallurgy. The division describes itself as a leading provider of "nickel-based alloys and titanium" alongside its dominant tool steel franchise, and positions itself as "No. 1 manufacturer in Europe and strategic partner of the U.S. aerospace industry" for these materials (CMD presentation October 2025). BÖHLER AMPO AM powders, produced at Kapfenberg (Austria) and Hagfors (Sweden), include Ni625 (BÖHLER L625) and Ni718 (BÖHLER L718) powder grades that compete with the company's powder offerings. The Kapfenberg special steel plant (opened 2023, 205 kt/yr capacity, the most advanced special steel plant in the world by digital and sustainability standards) supports high-precision tool steel and specialty alloy production. voestalpine Böhler Aerospace supplies titanium sheet as the sole European supplier to the aerospace industry (also first contracts secured in North America and Asia from BY2023/24). The division has secured approximately EUR 1 billion in aerospace orders for the next five years, a large part with Airbus, covering high-performance materials, complex forged parts and logistics services.

## Capacity and investments
Production sites within the HPM Division: Kapfenberg (Austria) is the flagship site with 205,000 t/yr special steel capacity, opened 2023, EUR 467 million total investment (overrun from original EUR 350 million due to COVID and equipment delays), featuring automated electric arc furnace route, centrally controlled melting, Industry 4.0 digital process data acquisition and certification processes for aerospace and energy sectors. Villares Metals, Sumaré (Brazil) is the second EAF-based special steel mill, with new AOD plant investment in BY2025/26. Hagfors (Sweden) is the Uddeholm site and also a powder production location for AM. The division previously operated 450 kt total crude steel capacity across three mills (Austria, Sweden, Brazil) per CMD 2025 presentation. voestalpine BÖHLER Bleche (Mürzzuschlag, Austria) has had capacity adjusted as part of portfolio streamlining. Buderus Edelstahl (Wetzlar, Germany) was divested in January 2025 and voestalpine BÖHLER Profil (Austria) was sold in BY2025/26. Capital expenditure BY2025/26: EUR 81.8 million (BY2024/25: EUR 126.1 million); investments in Kapfenberg automated warehouse and Villares AOD. The CMD 2025 presentation described the asset base as "well invested" with utilization headroom meaning "little to no CAPEX" required for growth.

## Market statements
The annual report (BY2025/26) stated: "The High Performance Metals Division operated in a consistently challenging economic environment... Market conditions were shaped by a persistently weak economy in Europe and restrictive trade policies implemented by the U.S. administration." Management noted that aerospace performed very strongly: "A large portion of these orders was agreed with the European aircraft manufacturer Airbus and covers the entire range of services from high-performance materials and complex forged parts to a comprehensive global logistics service. Nearly every newly built civil aircraft flies with a high-tech product from voestalpine." On tooling: "Intense global competition and associated price pressure throughout the 2025/26 business year" with European demand "stable but subdued" and North American ordering behavior "cautious." On automotive: "demand from the automotive industry for valve steels and engine components remained subdued." The LME nickel price was described as approximately USD 15,000/t for three quarters of BY2025/26, rising to approximately USD 18,000/t late in the year, then settling near USD 17,000/t at March 2026; nickel is described as the most important alloying element for the HPM Division.

## Customers and suppliers
Airbus is named as a major customer for aerospace forgings and materials (terms extending approximately five years). No other individual customer names are disclosed in the annual report. Suppliers of nickel and other alloying elements are not named; LME nickel price is specifically tracked as a primary input cost indicator.
