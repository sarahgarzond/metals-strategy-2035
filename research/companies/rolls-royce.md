# Rolls-Royce Holdings (customer, United Kingdom)

## Snapshot
Fiscal 2025 underlying revenue was GBP 20,059 million and statutory revenue was GBP 21,207 million, with underlying operating profit of GBP 3,503 million (17.3% margin, 2024: 13.8%) and free cash flow of GBP 3,270 million [CO_RR_REV_2025]. Source: 2025 full year results press release. The group had 43,162 employees at 31 December 2025 (2024: 42,211) [CO_RR_EMP_2025]. Source: 2025 Annual Report. H1 2026 underlying operating profit was GBP 2,500 million (margin 22.5%), up 46%, on revenue of GBP 11,280 million (up 26%), with guidance raised to GBP 4.7 to 4.9 billion operating profit and GBP 3.8 to 4.0 billion free cash flow for full year 2026 [CO_RR_H1_2026].

## Segments and end markets
Civil Aerospace revenue was approximately GBP 10,400 million in 2025 (about 52% of underlying revenue) with a margin of 20.5% [CO_RR_CIVIL_2025]. Defence revenue grew with a margin of 14.4%, and Power Systems (MTU-branded industrial engines and power generation) delivered a margin of 17.4%. Large engine flying hours grew 8% in 2025. The 2026 guidance includes 550 to 600 new OE engine deliveries, 1,480 to 1,550 shop visits, and flying hours at 115 to 120% of the 2019 level. Mid-term (2028) targets are underlying operating profit of GBP 4.9 to 5.2 billion (margin 18 to 20%) and free cash flow of GBP 5.0 to 5.3 billion [CO_RR_TARGETS].

## Relevance to the Client
Rolls-Royce is a primary customer for nickel superalloy and uses the following alloys: polycrystalline and single-crystal blade alloys (CMSX-4, RR1000, DD26 class) for Trent XWB-84, Trent XWB-97, Trent 1000 and Trent 7000; titanium forgings and discs for fan and compressor stages. Power Systems uses nickel alloys for industrial gas turbines. The HPT blade improvement programme for Trent 1000 XE and Trent 7000 (phase one certified June 2025, phase two certified December 2025) requires new blade alloy stock and increases demand for repair alloys in MRO. ## Capacity and investments
Rolls-Royce announced in March 2024 a GBP 55 million investment in large engine assembly, test and shop visit capacity at Derby (UK) and Dahlewitz (Germany), enabling over 40% more new engine output from 2025 versus the ten-year average [CO_RR_INV_2024]. Dahlewitz assembles and tests new Trent XWB-84 engines from 2026. A GBP 300 million UK investment package announced in March 2026 includes over GBP 140 million in Derby (completion 2028), a GBP 19.3 million Advanced Blade Casting Facility at Rotherham producing Trent XWB-84 and Trent 1000 XE blades, and further R&D and engineering sites [CO_RR_UK_2026]. The MRO network capacity is targeted to increase by an additional 20% by the mid-term: Beijing (BAESL JV with Air China, up to 250 Trent overhauls per year by mid 2030s), Istanbul (Turkish Technic JV, up to 200 shop visits per year, operational by end 2027), Singapore and other sites added capacity in 2025 [CO_RR_CIVIL_2025].

## Market statements
The 2025 Annual Report states: "Our transformation continues with pace and intensity ... we have navigated challenges from supply chain to tariffs." The 2025 full year results release confirms: "supply chain constraints which we are actively managing." The 2026 full year guidance includes a cash impact of GBP 150 to 200 million related to the supply chain, "similar to 2025, with parts availability improving but still constrained" [CO_RR_CIVIL_2025]. The time on wing programme targets more than 100% durability improvement across all in-production Trent engines by end 2027, with more than half already delivered in 2025 [CO_RR_CIVIL_2025]. The Trent XWB-84EP, introduced in 2025, improves fuel efficiency by over 1% and also extends time on wing for new engines.

## Customers and suppliers
Major airline customers include British Airways, Singapore Airlines, Air France and Cathay Pacific on Trent XWB-84 (A350-900), plus Etihad and Qatar Airways on Trent XWB-97 (A350-1000). Trent 1000 powers Air New Zealand, All Nippon Airways and United Airlines B787 fleets.
