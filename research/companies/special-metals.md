- Special Metals is not reported separately. Its parent PCC had 2025 revenues of USD 10.8bn (+4.6%), with aerospace revenue up 7.5% and pre-tax earnings up 34.2% [1, Item 7, industrial products group].

## Segments and end markets
- PCC makes investment castings, forgings, fasteners and aerostructures, and "nickel, titanium and cobalt alloys in all standard mill forms" for aerospace, chemical processing, oil and gas and pollution control [1, Item 1].

- 2025: power products in oil and gas and general industrial declined, industrial gas turbine rose [1, Item 7].

## Relevance to the Client
The most direct competitor for nickel alloys. Trademarks Inconel and Incoloy are Special Metals brands (company list). Product forms: billet, bar, rod, wire, flat (sheet, strip, plate), tubulars, powder, welding products [2]. Overlap with the Client is high in aerospace rotating parts, chemical processing, oil and gas. PCC is also vertically integrated into castings and forgings, so it is both supplier and customer of alloy to its own downstream plants, and a competitor of the Client's customers. ATI, Howmet and Haynes all name PCC as their principal competitor.

## Capacity and investments
- Huntington WV (130 acres): two 70,000 lb (31.8 t) electric arc furnaces, one 48,000 lb (21.8 t) VIM furnace, AOD, VAR and ESR, 5,000 ton forge press, 6,000 ton extrusion press, hot strip mill up to 60 inch coils, cold rolled strip down to 0.005 inch [2].
- New Hartford NY: 45,000 lb VIM, VAR ingots 9 to 30 inch (1,000 to 20,000 lb), ESR ingots 17 to 30 inch (4,000 to 30,000 lb) [3]. Dunkirk NY: 4,500 ton open die press [3].
- Other sites listed: Hereford UK (Wiggin, IncoTest), Livingston Scotland (revert), Canning Vale Australia, Newton NC (welding products) [4].

## Market statements
- "Long-term industry forecasts continue to show growth and strong demand for air travel and aerospace products" [1, Item 1].
- Nickel, titanium, cobalt, tantalum, hafnium, vanadium, rhenium and molybdenum "are found in only a few parts of the world"; "Future shortages or price fluctuations in raw materials could have a material adverse effect" [1, Item 1].
- Pricing: Huntington raised base prices 5 to 7% (May 2021), 10 to 15% (March 2022), 10 to 20% (July 2023), 10 to 15% on long products (Sept 2023) and 8 to 15% on rounds (Feb 2024), the last notice listed [5].
