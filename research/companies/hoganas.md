# Höganäs (competitor in metal powders and additive manufacturing, Sweden)
## Snapshot
Fiscal year 2025 (calendar year). Revenue SEK 11,045 million (2024: SEK 11,826 million), operating profit SEK 1,148 million, a margin of 10.4 percent (2024: 9.0 percent). EBITDA before non recurring items SEK 1,663 million. Average employees 2,245 (about 2,200 stated). Sales volume 393,000 tonnes (2024: 412,000 tonnes). Sources [1] p. 66 and p. 71. Höganäs Holding AB is owned by Lindéngruppen and Wallenberg interests, so no stock exchange filing exists.
## Segments and end markets
Five divisions: Powder Metallurgy Technologies (PMT, sintered automotive parts, the core and largest), Electro Magnetic Materials (EMM, loss making start up), Metal Consumables & Elements (MCE, welding and metallurgy powders), Coating & Brazing Technologies (CBT, thermal spray and brazing alloys) and Additive & Ceramic Technologies (ACT). Revenue is not split by division in the report. Sales by region 2025: Asia Pacific 41.8 percent, Americas 29.5 percent, Europe, Middle East and Africa 28.7 percent [1] p. 66. The report says ACT "currently accounts for a limited portion" of the business but offers good potential in 3D printing [1] p. 66. Volumes rose 2 percent in CBT and 3 percent in EMM, and fell in PMT, MCE and ACT [1] p. 66.
## Relevance to the Client
Höganäs is a powder competitor and potential partner. ACT sells nickel, titanium, aluminium, iron and copper powders for powder bed fusion, directed energy deposition, binder jetting and cold spraying, with aerospace parts, gas turbine burners, ducts and blades, heat exchangers and fuel cells as applications [1] p. 18. CBT sells iron, nickel and cobalt based alloys including MCrAlY plus tungsten, chromium and titanium carbides for thermal spray, laser cladding and weld overlay in gas turbines, aero engines and valves [1] p. 16. MCE supplies welding consumable powders and stainless steel powder [1] p. 17. Höganäs states that developing iron based solutions to replace cobalt and nickel based materials is one pathway it pursues [1] p. 16. ## Market statements
"For CBT, the energy sector remains strong and is expected to stay that way, supported by AI-driven demand for electricity" (CEO comment) [1] p. 6. Demand from the energy sector increased in 2025 while automotive demand was restrained [1] p. 66. ACT notes reliance on critical materials that often come from outside Europe and are exposed to geopolitical risk or export restrictions, and that local alternatives are "significantly more expensive" [1] p. 18. CBT cites limited availability of critical raw materials such as tungsten [1] p. 16. Tariffs and geopolitical manoeuvring are named as general demand risks [1] p. 9.
