# Safran (customer, France)

## Snapshot
Fiscal 2025 adjusted revenue was EUR 31,329 million, up 14.7% (14.8% organic), with a recurring operating income of EUR 5,197 million (16.6% margin, up 26%) and free cash flow of EUR 3,921 million [CO_SAFRAN_REV_2025]. Source: FY 2025 results and investor update presentation, slide 3. The group employed 110,778 people at 31 December 2025, up from 103,058 in 2024 [CO_SAFRAN_EMP_2025]. Source: 2025 Universal Registration Document (URD), p. 409. H1 2026 adjusted revenue was EUR 17,571 million (up 19.0%), with recurring operating income of EUR 3,237 million (18.4%) [CO_SAFRAN_H1_2026].

## Segments and end markets
Propulsion (CFM International 50/50 joint venture with GE Aerospace, plus Safran Aircraft Engines for military) generated EUR 15,668 million in fiscal 2025, or 50% of group revenue, with a recurring operating income of EUR 3,600 million (23.0% margin) [CO_SAFRAN_PROP_2025]. Source: presentation slide 10. Equipment and Defence contributed EUR 12,300 million (39% of group) and Aircraft Interiors EUR 3,300 million (11%). LEAP deliveries were 1,802 in 2025 (up 28% from 1,407 in 2024), with H1 2026 deliveries reaching 1,030 (up 41%) [CO_SAFRAN_LEAP_2025, CO_SAFRAN_H1_2026]. The 2028 ambition targets recurring operating income of EUR 7.0 to 7.5 billion and cumulative free cash flow of approximately EUR 21 billion for 2024 to 2028, with propulsion margin of 22 to 24% per year [CO_SAFRAN_GUIDE_2026].

## Relevance to the Client
Propulsion is the primary demand pull for nickel superalloy castings and forgings, used in LEAP, GEnx and GE9X combustors, HPT blades, vanes and discs, and military engine hot sections (M88 for Rafale, TP400 for A400M). Equipment and Defence draws on titanium for aircraft structures and landing gear. CFM56 shop visits will total 2,300 to 2,400 per year from 2025 to 2028, driving alloy demand for MRO work scopes. Safran states it is the only engine maker with complete in-house forging, casting and machining capability, meaning its Gennevilliers facility competes with independent forge houses for alloy conversion volume.

## Capacity and investments
Safran is investing EUR 150 million in a new 30,000 tonne hydraulic press at its historic Gennevilliers forging site (France), to produce approximately 14,000 parts per year at full capacity, becoming operational by 2029 [CO_SAFRAN_GENN_2026]. The investment will nearly double site production by 2035 across LEAP, GE9X and military engine families. The Gennevilliers site handles hot-section forgings in nickel superalloy and titanium. ## Market statements
The FY 2025 investor presentation (slide 18) names "supply chain production capabilities" as a named watch item for achieving 2028 targets [CO_SAFRAN_GUIDE_2026]. CEO Andries stated in April 2026 that more than 2,000 LEAP engines would be delivered in 2026 and that demand is expected to keep exceeding supply for the coming years [CO_SAFRAN_CEO_2026]. Civil spare parts grew 17.6% in USD terms and MRO services 30% in H1 2026, driven by LEAP rate per flight hour (RPFH) contracts [CO_SAFRAN_H1_2026].

## Customers and suppliers
Safran supplies engine modules to GE Aerospace through CFM International for LEAP-1A and LEAP-1B integration. Key MRO airline customers include Air France, Lufthansa Technik and other CFM-authorised centres.
