## Segments and end markets
Net sales 2025: Turbos & Thermal Technologies USD 5,772 million (adjusted margin 15.2%), Drivetrain & Morse Systems 5,654 million (18.4%), PowerDrive Systems 2,347 million (minus 3.5%), Battery & Charging Systems 590 million (minus 6.6%) (10-K p. 7 and 46). End markets: light vehicle 82%, commercial vehicle 10%, off highway 5%, aftermarket 3% (p. 10). Turbochargers for light vehicles were about 21% of group sales (p. 7). eProducts reached about USD 2.6 billion, 18% of revenue, up from 14% in 2023 (p. 6). Turbos & Thermal sales fell 2% on lower volumes in Europe (p. 46).

## Relevance to the Client
BorgWarner lists nickel among its raw materials with aluminum, copper, steel and "certain alloy elements", bought as castings, powder metal, forgings and bar stock (10-K p. 14 and p. 21 risk factors). Turbine wheels and housings for turbochargers and eTurbos are the nickel alloy touchpoint, a direct competitor of Garrett in the same alloy grades. Grades and tonnes: TODO(data). ## Capacity and investments
81 manufacturing, assembly and technical locations, of which 26 in Turbos & Thermal (7 Americas, 11 Europe, 8 Asia) (10-K Item 2, p. 33). Capex including tooling USD 469 million in 2025 against 671 million in 2024 (cash flow statement). Restructuring expense USD 101 million in 2025, after a 2023 plan of USD 130 to 150 million with gross savings of USD 80 to 90 million by 2027, including a European facility closure affecting about 200 staff (10-K p. 41 and segment note). Exit of the charging business removed USD 56 million of sales.

## Market statements
1. Q3 2026: net sales USD 3,487 million, down 2.9%, adjusted operating margin 10.9%. Turbos & Thermal USD 1,372 million against 1,437 million [CO_BORGWARNER_GUIDE_2026].
2. 2026 guidance: net sales USD 14.0 to 14.2 billion, weighted light vehicle market down 1% to 2%.
3. Awards: three conquest turbocharger awards for gasoline platforms in South America and Europe, start 2027, plus an internal combustion pickup engine control award for 2029 (Q3 2026 release). This shows turbo content persists in hybrids and gasoline.
4. Tariffs added about USD 108 million to cost of sales in 2025, with customer recoveries of about USD 80 million (10-K p. 41).
5. Strategy: "technology focused product portfolio that supports electric, hybrid and combustion vehicles" (10-K p. 6). Commodity cost is passed through "wherever possible", with selective hedging (risk factors).
