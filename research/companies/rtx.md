# RTX (Pratt & Whitney) (customer, United States)

## Snapshot
* Fiscal 2025 net sales were USD 88,603 million, operating profit USD 9,300 million (10.5%), and about 180,000 employees [CO_RTX_SALES_2025, CO_RTX_EMPLOYEES_2025]. Source: Form 10-K 2025, pp. 7 and 36.
* Backlog was USD 268 billion at end 2025 (commercial 161, defense 107) and USD 340 billion at 30 September 2026 (commercial 176, defense 164) [CO_RTX_BACKLOG_2025, CO_RTX_9M26_BACKLOG].
* Nine months 2026 net sales were USD 71,749 million (10-Q Q3 2026).

## Segments and end markets
* Collins Aerospace: sales USD 30,196 million, operating profit USD 4,923 million (16.3%).
* Pratt & Whitney: sales USD 32,916 million (2024: 28,066), operating profit USD 2,596 million (2024: 2,015). Organic growth of USD 4.8 billion came from commercial aftermarket (+2.9), commercial OEM (+0.9) and military (+1.0, F135) [CO_RTX_PW_2025]. Pages 44 to 45.
* Raytheon: sales USD 28,043 million, operating profit USD 3,227 million.
* Pratt nine months 2026: sales USD 26,293 million (up 12%), operating profit USD 2,056 million. Aftermarket +USD 2.5 billion, OEM down USD 0.3 billion on engine mix [CO_RTX_9M26_PW].
* Defense bookings at Pratt: USD 9 billion in 2025 (F135 production 2.9, sustainment 2.4) and USD 9 billion in nine months 2026 plus USD 3.8 billion F135 production.

## Relevance to the Client
* Pratt engines (GTF PW1100G, PW1500G, F135, F119, PW4000 legacy) use nickel superalloy discs, blades, vanes and cases, including powder metal discs.
* Pratt is also a competitor on the powder route, since it qualifies its own powder metal sources. The Client's powder or disc alloy supply to Pratt: TODO(data).
* Defense and the F135 ramp are less cyclical demand for nickel alloys than the commercial OEM line.

## Capacity and investments
* GTF aftermarket network expanded to 21 facilities, lifting PW1100G-JM shop visit output about 26% in 2025 (10-K p. 5). Q3 2026 total GTF overhauls were up 35% [CO_RTX_Q3CALL_2026] (secondary).
* GTF Advantage certified by FAA and EASA in 2025, first delivery to United in October 2026 (secondary).
* Casting and forging output: isothermal forgings up 16% and structural castings up 29% in Q3 2025 year on year [CO_RTX_Q3CALL_2025] (Aviation Week, 2025, secondary). Absolute tonnes and furnace counts: not published, TODO(data).

## Market statements
* "We are largely dependent upon foreign sources for certain raw materials, such as cobalt, tantalum, chromium, rhenium, nickel, and titanium" and "Some of our products require relatively scarce raw materials" (10-K p. 8).
* RTX will keep "expanding the use of long-term agreements" and "extending our contractually negotiated raw material pricing to higher-tier suppliers" (10-K p. 8). This is a direct lever for alloy price pass through.
* "We expect to experience prolonged delays for certain critical component parts and sub-systems" (10-K p. 19).
* Powder Metal Matter: elevated aircraft on ground for the A320neo and incremental shop visits expected through the end of 2026, with a 2026 cash impact of about USD 0.7 billion [CO_RTX_POWDER_2025, CO_RTX_9M26_BACKLOG]. Affected parts are HPT and HPC parts from the affected raw material (10-K pp. 19 and 34).
* Airbus names Pratt engine commitments as the main constraint on the A320 ramp [CO_AIRBUS_GUIDE_2026].

## Customers and suppliers
* Customers: Airbus (A320neo, A220), Embraer E2, Lockheed Martin (F35), US government. GTF fleet over 2,600 aircraft and 90 operators [CO_RTX_GTF_2025].
* Reported GTF backlog above 8,000 engines and over 800 orders and commitments in 2026 (call transcript summary, unverified).
