# Bloom Energy (customer, United States)
## Snapshot
• Revenue in fiscal 2025 was USD 2.02bn, up 37.3%, with GAAP operating income of USD 72.8m, an operating margin of about 3.6% (team calculation), and non GAAP operating income of USD 221.0m [1].
• Employees were reported as 2,214 by an aggregator, which needs checking in the 10-K [2]. Page numbers: TODO(data), sec.gov could not be opened by WebFetch, so the release text found by search is used.
• Latest fiscal year is calendar 2025, and the 10-K was filed in February 2026 (tools/data/fetch.py lists it) [3].
• Third quarter 2026 revenue was USD 1.24bn, up 138.1%, with GAAP operating income of USD 225.4m [4].

## Segments and end markets
• Bloom sells solid oxide fuel cell systems (Energy Server) with product, installation, service and electricity revenue. The revenue split by product, service and electricity line is TODO(data) here.
• Product revenue was about USD 1.07bn in the third quarter of 2026, around 87% of revenue [4].
• Data centres are the growth driver that explains the jump in orders and revenue. Management said that demand which began with hyperscalers is moving across market segments [4].
• Guidance for 2026 was raised to revenue of USD 4.4bn to 4.6bn and non GAAP operating income of USD 960m to 1,030m [4]. At the start of the year guidance was USD 3.1bn to 3.3bn [1].

## Relevance to the Client
• Each Energy Server stack needs metallic interconnects, which in SOFC practice are ferritic chromium steels such as Crofer 22 APU or AISI 444 type grades, with coatings against chromium poisoning of the cathode.
• Bloom does not name its interconnect alloy, strip supplier or kg per kW in the sources reached, so alloy, tonnage and supplier are TODO(data).
• Nickel enters through the anode (nickel cermet) and balance of plant alloys, but volumes are TODO(data).
• A German nickel alloy producer is therefore relevant to Bloom mainly for hot box and heat exchanger alloys, and only if a supplier base outside the United States is accepted. This is the analyst assessment and it is not a statement made by the company.

## Market statements
• Total backlog was about USD 20bn and product backlog about USD 6bn at the end of 2025, roughly 2.5 times the prior year [1].
• Fourth quarter 2025 GAAP gross margin fell to 30.8% from 38.3%, so ramp up costs are visible [1].
• Spotlight: AEP agreed in January 2026 to procure up to 1 GW for USD 2.65bn, with a first phase of 900 MW for a data centre in Wyoming (aggregator, low confidence) [6].
• Spotlight: Oracle agreement expanded to 2.8 GW in April 2026, and a Brookfield financing framework of USD 5bn (aggregator, low confidence) [6].
