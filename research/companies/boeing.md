# Boeing (customer, United States)

## Snapshot
* Fiscal 2025 revenue was USD 89,463 million, earnings from operations USD 4,281 million (4.8%), and the workforce was about 182,000 [CO_BA_REVENUE_2025, CO_BA_EMPLOYEES_2025]. Source: Form 10-K 2025, pp. 2 and 26.
* Total backlog was USD 682.2 billion at end 2025 and USD 745 billion at 30 September 2026, with Commercial Airplanes at USD 612 billion and over 6,300 aircraft [CO_BA_BACKLOG_2025, CO_BA_Q3_2026].
* Q3 2026 revenue was USD 24.2 billion with a Commercial Airplanes operating margin of minus 6.5% (press release 27 October 2026).

## Segments and end markets
* Commercial Airplanes: revenue USD 41,494 million, loss from operations USD 7,079 million, including USD 5.3 billion reach forward losses on 777X and 767 (10-K p. 33).
* Defense, Space & Security: revenue USD 27,234 million (p. 38). Global Services: revenue USD 20,923 million (p. 41).
* Deliveries 2025: 600 aircraft (737: 447, 767: 30, 777: 35, 787: 88), versus 348 in 2024 [CO_BA_DELIV_2025]. Nine months 2026: 475 (search summary).
* About 85% of Commercial Airplanes backlog is with non US airlines (p. 31).

## Relevance to the Client
* Boeing is an indirect customer. Nickel alloys reach Boeing aircraft through engines (CFM LEAP on 737, GE9X on 777X, GEnx on 787, Rolls-Royce Trent 1000 on 787), through fasteners, and through the titanium and nickel structures of the defense platforms.
* Its published raw material focus is aluminium and titanium, not nickel. Nickel exposure is TODO(data) beyond the engine route.
* Boeing's rate plans set the call-off for engine OEMs one tier down, so they are the demand driver for alloy volumes.

## Capacity and investments
* 737: rate 42 per month in Q4 2025 after a low of below 38, planned increase to 47 in 2026 with FAA concurrence, further increases and a new 737 line are planned [CO_BA_RATES_2025]. Pages 36 to 37.
* 787: 7 rising to 8 per month from Q4 2025, stable at 8 in Q3 2026. 767: about 3 per month, commercial production ends 2027. 777X: first 777-9 delivery 2027, 777-8 passenger not before 2030.
* Spirit AeroSystems acquisition completed on 8 December 2025 (10-K p. 37), bringing fuselage and wing work packages in house.

## Market statements
* "The most important raw materials required for our aerospace products are aluminum (sheet, plate, forgings and extrusions), titanium (sheet, plate, forgings and extrusions) and composites." Alternative sources "generally exist" but "qualification of the sources could take a year or more" [CO_BA_RAWMAT_2025]. 10-K p. 4.
* "Many major components and product equipment items are procured or subcontracted on a sole-source basis" (p. 4).
* "We and our suppliers have experienced supply chain disruptions and constraints, labor instability and inflationary pressures" (p. 8).
* Q3 2026: the margin reflects "higher estimated supply chain costs". Wing supply is reported as the bottleneck for the 737 and stabilisation at 47 per month is expected to take "into next year" [CO_BA_737RATE_2026] (secondary).
* A potential durability issue was found on the 777X engine during certification flight testing (10-K p. 37), which ties to GE9X midseal certification at GE.

## Customers and suppliers
* Customers: Turkish Airlines, Ethiopian Airlines, Lufthansa (Q3 2026 orders), US government for defense. Non US customers were 46% of 2025 revenue (p. 10).
* Suppliers: GE Aerospace and CFM, Rolls-Royce, Spirit (now owned), sole source suppliers not named. Long term metal agreements: not disclosed, TODO(data).
* Defense: F/A XX Navy fighter selection in Q3 2026, defense backlog USD 101 billion.
