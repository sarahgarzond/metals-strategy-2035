## Segments and end markets
Nornickel sells nickel, palladium, platinum and copper, with precious metals driving the 2025 gain while the LME nickel average fell 10 percent (1). Sales were redirected to Asia. End market split: TODO(data).

## Relevance to the Client
Nornickel is the largest class 1 nickel producer and a source of cathode, briquettes and powder that fits alloy melting. For a German producer, direct purchase is constrained by the sanctions context described below and by customer and buyer compliance policy. Nornickel also sells cobalt and copper.

## Capacity and investments
- 2026 guidance: nickel 193,000 to 203,000 t, copper 336,000 to 356,000 t, palladium 2.41 to 2.46 million oz, platinum 616,000 to 636,000 oz, reaffirmed with H1 2026 [CO_NORNICKEL_GUIDANCE_2026] (3).
- H1 2026 nickel output 85 kt, down 2 percent on repairs and a high base [CO_NORNICKEL_H1_NICKEL_2026] (4). Q1 figures conflict across reports (about 41.7 to 42.1 kt) so none is used here.
- CEO Potanin said output could rise about 20 percent in nickel equivalent by 2030 and a recovery is expected in 2028 (5). Furnace and refinery detail (Kola, Harjavalta): TODO(data).

## Market statements
- Nornickel expects a global nickel surplus of 275 kt in 2026 if Indonesia keeps the status quo, after about 240 kt in 2025, with 2025 supply 3.86 Mt and demand 3.62 Mt [CO_NORNICKEL_SURPLUS_2026] (1). These are company estimates.
- Sanctions: the company is not directly sanctioned. Restrictions limit Western equipment, payments and logistics, and some foreign clients avoid Russian metal (5). Under the EU 20th package, transactions with the port of Murmansk are banned, which could affect an export hub (5).
