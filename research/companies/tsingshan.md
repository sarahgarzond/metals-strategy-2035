# Tsingshan Holding Group (nickel pig iron, matte, HPAL and stainless producer, China)
Research note. Tsingshan is private and publishes no annual report, so nearly all evidence is trade press and Project Blue. Company reported group figures: TODO(data).

## Snapshot

- Tsingshan holds about 51 percent of Weda Bay Nickel, with Eramet at about 38 percent [CO_TSINGSHAN_WBN_STAKE_2026] (1).
- IWIP park is owned by Tsingshan (40 percent), Huayou (30 percent) and Zhenshi (30 percent) (2).

## Segments and end markets
Activities are stainless steel (including PT Indonesia Tsingshan Stainless Steel at IMIP), NPI, high grade matte and HPAL. End market split and group tonnage of NPI, matte and stainless: TODO(data), nothing reliable found.

## Relevance to the Client
Tsingshan sets the marginal cost of nickel units through Indonesian NPI and is a stainless competitor. For a nickel alloy producer it matters because NPI and matte cuts move the LME nickel price that sets alloy surcharges. It does not produce high performance Ni base alloys in the sources read.

## Capacity and investments
- The Blue Flame HPAL project at IWIP is expected to start in 2026 with 120 kt Ni per year, per Project Blue. Ownership attribution needs checking [CO_TSINGSHAN_HPAL_2026] (3).
- Weda Bay received an initial 2026 RKAB of 12 Mwmt, about 71 percent below the late 2025 level and far below a 42 Mwmt target (1). Ore mining stopped in May and June while the WBN NPI plant ran from stockpiles (4).
- National 2026 ore target is about 260 to 270 Mt against 379 Mt approved for 2025. Another source gives about 250 Mt [CO_TSINGSHAN_RKAB_NATIONAL_2026] (5).
- Some high grade NPI lines at IWIP entered maintenance and cuts from March and April 2026, and 10 to 15 percent of high grade NPI capacity at IWIP was expected to stay idle (6).
- In October 2026 producers at IMIP discussed coordinated cuts of up to 50 percent for MHP and about 20 percent for NPI. Sizes are unconfirmed and several firms denied taking part [CO_TSINGSHAN_IMIP_CUTS_2026] (7).

## Market statements
- Reuters coverage says nickel prices dropped nearly 25 percent, from about USD 20,000 to USD 15,070 per tonne, in the week before 23 October 2026 (7). The exact price benchmark was not stated: TODO(data).
- Project Blue expects a 2026 market surplus of about 275 kt Ni, the same figure Nornickel cites (3).

## Customers and suppliers
