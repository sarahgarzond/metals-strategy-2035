# Sandvik (competitor, SE)

## Snapshot
Sandvik AB, listed on Nasdaq Stockholm, reported revenues of SEK 120,680 million for the year ended 31 December 2025, against SEK 122,878 million in 2024, which was an organic increase of 5 percent. Adjusted EBITA was SEK 23,309 million, a margin of 19.3 percent, and reported EBITA was SEK 22,616 million, a margin of 18.7 percent. The average number of full time equivalent employees was 41,801. Source: Annual Report 2025, directors' report, printed p. 37. In the third quarter of 2026 revenues were SEK 37,728 million and the adjusted EBITA margin was 22.5 percent, with 42,831 employees (Interim report third quarter 2026, p. 1 and p. 22).

## Segments and end markets
The group had three business areas in 2025. Mining contributed SEK 62,971 million of revenue and an adjusted EBITA margin of 20.7 percent. Rock Processing contributed SEK 10,435 million and 14.8 percent. Machining and Intelligent Manufacturing contributed SEK 47,273 million and 19.9 percent, and that area includes tools, software and metal powder (Annual Report 2025, p. 37). From 1 January 2026 the last area is reported as two businesses. On that restated basis, 2025 Machining revenue was SEK 44,003 million and Intelligent Manufacturing revenue was SEK 3,117 million (Q3 2026 report, p. 19). Shares of 2025 external revenue were mining 52 percent, infrastructure 9 percent and manufacturing 39 percent. Inside manufacturing, general industry was 25 percent of group revenue, aerospace and defense 5 percent, light vehicles 4 percent, mining and energy 2 percent, transportation 2 percent, and medical and electronics 1 percent (Q3 2026 report, p. 3).

## Relevance to the Client
The overlap with the company sat in the Osprey powder and controlled expansion alloy unit, which Sandvik reported inside Machining until it left the group. On 29 May 2026 Sandvik signed an agreement to sell the Additive Manufacturing business unit to Mimir. The unit makes metal powders for additive manufacturing, metal injection moulding and hot isostatic pressing, and it also makes controlled expansion alloys (press release, p. 1). Mimir completed the purchase on 1 October 2026, and Osprey now trades as an independent company with plants in Neath and Sandviken (Osprey release, 5 October 2026). The published grade list includes nickel and superalloy powders, titanium, cobalt chromium grades such as Osprey Co212 (UNS R30075, ASTM F75), copper alloys including Cu30Ni and CuCrZr, and Osprey Alloy 36 (UNS K93600, EN 1.3912), a low expansion alloy used in aerospace and electronic components. Those families match the application benchmark for nickel, cobalt, titanium and controlled expansion alloys, but the product form is powder and expansion alloy rather than plate, bar or tube. Sandvik kept its tungsten powder business for cutting tools, which is a different product. The purchase price and the revenue of the sold unit were not disclosed. ## Capacity and investments
Osprey describes Neath, at Red Jacket Works on Milland Road, as the original foundry, in continuous operation since 1974, and as the home of the fine powder plants. Sandviken does speciality atomising and downstream processing for European customers (Osprey company page, retrieved 7 November 2026). When the sale was signed, Sandvik recorded an impairment of about SEK 230 million, mainly on property, plant and equipment, with no cash effect (press release, p. 1). The third quarter report adds a write down of SEK 9 million related to the Additive divestment (p. 20). TODO(data): VIM, VAR or ESR equipment is not described for the powder plants.

## Market statements
The directors' report says strong underlying demand for cutting tools was seen in aerospace and in smaller segments such as defense, while tungsten powder demand was solid because of global supply conditions (PDF p. 2). In the third quarter of 2026, management said supply constraints and high market activity continued to support demand for tungsten powder, and that the powder business more than doubled orders and revenues from a year earlier, helped by higher tungsten prices (Q3 report, p. 2, p. 3 and p. 8). Aerospace continued to benefit from substantial order backlogs among aerospace manufacturers (p. 3). That powder comment refers to the tungsten business Sandvik still owns. Osprey was inside the group through 30 September 2026 and left on 1 October 2026.

## Customers and suppliers
Named Osprey customers are not disclosed. The independence release says existing contracts, orders and technical contacts remain unchanged, and Mimir's Joakim Notö is quoted as intending to be a long term owner. Sandvik does not name tungsten raw material suppliers. It refers to APT and Argus scrap prices, which fell during the third quarter of 2026 but stayed well above the prior year (Q3 report, p. 3).
