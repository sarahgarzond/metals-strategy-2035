# Zirconium

Notes for chapter 4.7.1. Three chains are kept apart throughout: zircon sand (the mineral, gross weight), zirconium chemicals, and nuclear zirconium alloy tube. Chemical plant zirconium metal is a fourth, smaller metal use and is not treated as either chemicals or nuclear tube. No world market value is calculated, because the volume and price series do not describe the same product.

## Key findings

1. USGS publishes world mine production of zirconium mineral concentrates at 1,200 thousand metric tons gross weight in 2025 (estimated, rounded) and 1,300 thousand metric tons in 2024, in both the data release and the printed table [ZR_MINE_GROSS_2025_WORLD]. The same chapter's narrative says production decreased by 12% to an estimated 1 million tons gross weight in 2025 [ZR_MINE_NARRATIVE_2025]. Those two USGS statements do not match, and the table is the one used here.
2. The 2025 country estimates in that table are led by Australia at 400 thousand metric tons, South Africa at 270, and Mozambique at 160, all gross weight [ZR_MINE_GROSS_2025_AU]. Country lines do not add to the rounded world total. Australia's table reserves are 55,000 thousand metric tons of ZrO2, while a footnote puts JORC compliant or equivalent reserves at 21 million tons of ZrO2 [ZR_RESERVE_ZRO2_2025_AU].
3. Zircon sand, chemicals, and metal are different markets. USGS says the leading end use of zircon is ceramics, with foundry sand, refractories, and zirconium chemicals as the other primary uses, and it gives no percentage split [ZR_USGS_USES_2026]. U.S. trade in the metal is hundreds to about a thousand metric tons, while mine output is measured in hundreds of thousands of tons of concentrates.
4. There is no Pink Sheet zircon or zirconium price. The 2025 estimated premium grade price, CIF China, is 1,800 dollars per metric ton gross weight, and the U.S. import unit value from Australia, Senegal, and South Africa is 1,900 dollars per metric ton [ZR_PRICE_PREMIUM_CIF_2025]. Iluka's own received price for premium and standard zircon sand in the first half of 2026 was 1,514 dollars per tonne FOB [ZR_ILUKA_ZIRCON_H1_2026].
5. Eurostat does not describe PRODCOM 24453063 as zirconium. On 7 November 2026 the dataset labelled it "Articles of hafnium and germanium, n.e.s.", with sold production only through 2021 [ZR_PRODCOM_24453063_LABEL]. The zirconium codes with 2022 to 2024 cells are 24453049 (unwrought zirconium and powders) and 24453050 (other zirconium and articles). EU sold production of unwrought zirconium was 4,000 kg in 2024, with flag :R [ZR_PRODCOM_UNWROUGHT_EU_2024].
6. Nuclear alloy tube demand is not measured in zirconium tons in the sources opened here. The World Nuclear Association table of 6 November 2026 lists 443 operable reactors and 405,667 MWe net, plus 81 reactors under construction and 88,463 MWe gross [WNA_OPERABLE_2026]. In the IEA Net Zero Emissions by 2050 Scenario, installed nuclear capacity goes from 420 GW in 2024 to 710 GW in 2035 [WEO_NZE_NUCLEAR_GW]. That is a reactor outlook, not a tube forecast.
7. Tronox reports 274 million dollars of zircon sales revenue in 2025 and a capability to produce about 297,000 metric tons of zircon a year, which is capacity rather than output [ZR_TRONOX_ZIRCON_REV_2025]. ATI produces reactor grade zirconium and hafnium alloys at Millersburg, Oregon, and Huntsville, Alabama, and buys zirconium sand and sponge [ZR_ATI_ZR_SITES_2025]. Rio Tinto publishes a marketable zircon reserve share, not a 2025 zircon production tonnage.

The narrative on page 2 says global mine production decreased by 12% to an estimated 1 million tons gross weight in 2025. The table's move from 1,300 to 1,200 thousand metric tons is a rounded decline of 100 thousand metric tons, which is not a 12% decline and is not 1 million tons. The data file matches the table. This note uses 1,200 thousand metric tons for 2025 and records the narrative as a conflict, not as a second official total.

USGS says one company recovered zircon from surface mining in Florida and Georgia in 2025 as a coproduct of heavy mineral sands, and a second company processed existing tailings in California. It does not name them. Zirconium metal and hafnium metal were produced from chemical intermediates by one producer in Oregon and one in Utah. Zirconium chemicals were produced by the Oregon metal producer and by at least five other companies. No company tonnage is given.

Iluka's 18 February 2026 full year notice does not print a 2025 zircon tonnage on the pages read. It does report mineral sands revenue of 976 million and underlying mineral sands EBITDA of 300 million, a 31% margin, without labelling the currency as Australian or U.S. dollars [ZR_ILUKA_MINSANDS_REV_2025]. The June 2026 quarterly report gives 2026 zircon production guidance of about 180 kt, with a more even split between zircon sand and zircon in concentrate than before [ZR_ILUKA_ZIRCON_GUIDE_2026]. In the second quarter of 2026, Narngulu processed Jacinth Ambrosia heavy mineral concentrate into 47 kt of zircon, including zircon in concentrate, and group zircon, rutile, and synthetic rutile production was 58 kt against sales of 157 kt.

Rio Tinto's 2025 annual report publishes titanium dioxide slag and Richards Bay ore reserves. It does not publish a 2025 zircon production tonnage in the production tables. TODO(data): Rio Tinto 2025 zircon production tonnes were searched in the 2025 annual report and were not found.

Eurostat sold production is the metal stage, not the mine. For 24453049, "Unwrought zirconium and zirconium powders (excluding waste and scrap)", EU sold production was 6,450 kg in 2022 (flag :E, value 900,000 euro), 4,000 kg in 2023 (flag :R, value 800,000 euro), and 4,000 kg in 2024 (flag :R, value 1,400,000 euro). Quantity unit is kilograms. Germany accounts for the whole 6,450 kg EU quantity in 2022, and German quantity and value are confidential in 2023 and 2024. France reports zero sold production in each of those years. The 2021 query for this code returned no cells, which fits a code that the product list dates from 2022.

For 24453050, "Other zirconium and articles thereof", EU sold production is published as 1,200,000 kg in 2022 (160 million euro), 1,000,000 kg in 2023 (80 million euro), and 1,200,000 kg in 2024 (210 million euro), all with flag :R. These are round published figures, not a plant weighbridge. Germany's 2022 sold production of this code is 6,450 kg, the same figure as its unwrought line, and later German quantities are confidential. The EU article total is therefore not a German total. It is also not a pure nuclear tube series, because the code covers other zirconium articles as well.

PRODCOM 24453063, the code named in the project fetcher as zirconium, is labelled by DS-059358 as articles of hafnium and germanium. EU sold production in 2021 was 68,880 kg and 3,103,000 euro, flag :E, and Germany matches that EU total. France sold production is zero. The API returned no numeric cells for 2022, 2023, or 2024 for the EU, Germany, or France. Those kilograms are not used as zirconium production. Dataset updated 20 October 2026. Clean files: data/clean/prodcom_24453063.csv and data/clean/prodcom_zirconium_codes.csv.

## Trade

UN Comtrade was not pulled. TODO(data): world exports and imports for HS 8109 (zirconium unwrought, powders, and articles) and HS 2615 (zirconium ores and concentrates, zircon) are missing. No clean Comtrade file exists for either code. The flows below are U.S. statistics and EU PRODCOM trade indicators, not a world matrix. Detail is in research/materials/zirconium_trade.md.

U.S. import sources for 2021 to 2024, from the USGS chapter, are shares of U.S. imports and not world trade. Ores and concentrates: South Africa 48%, Australia 35%, Senegal 15%, other 2%. Zirconium compounds: China 41%, South Africa 31%, France 12%, Australia 10%, other 6%. Unwrought zirconium: China 55%, Germany 15%, Canada 12%, France 7%, other 11%. Wrought zirconium: France 72%, Germany 10%, Belgium 7%, China 4%, other 7% [ZR_US_WROUGHT_IMPORT_SHARE_FR]. USGS says Australia and South Africa were the leading global exporters of mineral concentrates and China was the leading importer, without tonnages.

U.S. zirconium metal trade shows the United States as a net exporter, which the narrative also states. In 2025 the estimates are 1,300 metric tons exported and 530 metric tons imported for unwrought zirconium, powder, and waste and scrap, and 1,000 metric tons exported and 380 metric tons imported for wrought zirconium [ZR_US_METAL_TRADE_2025]. Ore and concentrate trade is in ZrO2 content, calculated by USGS as 65% of gross weight, and ore exports exclude zircon in mixed concentrates. 2025 estimates are imports of 16,000 metric tons ZrO2 and exports of 10,000 metric tons ZrO2.

EU trade in 24453049 is much larger than EU sold production. EU imports of unwrought zirconium and powders were 559,660 kg in 2022, 512,350 kg in 2023, and 580,820 kg in 2024. EU exports were 176,226 kg, 136,170 kg, and 138,032 kg. France imported 240,876 kg, 242,752 kg, and 256,117 kg in those three years, with zero exports and zero sold production. That pattern fits a French user of unwrought metal, which is the form that goes into alloy tube, and it is not by itself proof of which company imported it. Member state trade and the EU aggregate are reported separately and are not added together here.

## Consumption

U.S. apparent consumption of zirconium ores and concentrates is defined as production plus imports minus exports, in ZrO2 content, and is withheld below 100,000 metric tons in every year from 2021 to 2025. Net import reliance for ores and concentrates, defined as imports minus exports, is estimated below 25% in 2025 [ZR_US_ORE_RELIANCE_2025]. That is a mineral sand balance for the United States, not a world zircon balance and not a nuclear tube balance.

World zircon consumption by country is not in the USGS data release. Tronox states, as its own belief, that China accounts for about 50% of world zircon demand, with weaker Chinese ceramics demand and more Chinese domestic sand [ZR_TRONOX_CHINA_DEMAND_2025]. That sentence is a company risk factor, not a measured trade share.

No source opened here gives tons of zirconium alloy per reactor, per reload, or per gigawatt. Reactor counts and nuclear generation are the demand context only. The World Nuclear Association, updated 6 November 2026, reports 2025 nuclear generation of 2,701.6 TWh, about 9% of world electricity, 443 operable reactors (connected to the grid) with 405,667 MWe net, and 81 reactors under construction with 88,463 MWe gross. China has 40 of those units under construction and 45,230 MWe gross. Planned reactors, defined as approvals, funding, or commitment in place and mostly expected within about 15 years, are 120 units and 109,283 MWe gross. The IAEA PRIS page read on 7 November 2026 separates 417 reactors in operation (379,611 MWe net, 31 countries), 21 in suspended operation (19,387 MWe, 2 countries), and 78 under construction (81,349 MWe net, 17 countries) [IAEA_PRIS_OPERATION_2026]. The two compilations are not the same object. Adding the IAEA in operation and suspended fleets gives 438 reactors, still five below the Association's operable count, and the Association's construction capacity is gross while PRIS capacity is net.

OECD STAN has no zirconium product row. data/clean/oecd_stan_P1.csv is sector output, not zircon or zirconium consumption. UNIDO INDSTAT was not available: data/raw/unido/ contains no download.

## Applications

Zircon sand is ZrSiO4, a coproduct of titanium mineral sands. USGS names ceramics as the leading end use and foundry sand, refractories, and zirconium chemicals as the other primary uses. It does not publish a percentage split in the 2026 chapter, so older share claims are not repeated. Substitutes named by USGS are chromite and olivine for some foundry uses, and dolomite and spinel for some high temperature refractories. Tronox describes the same sand uses in its Form 10-K, including ceramic glazes and foundry moulding sand for high value castings, and it also names zircon as the feedstock for zirconium metal and zirconium chemicals.

Zirconium chemicals are a separate chain. The USGS import line for zirconium compounds is blank (zero) from 2021 to 2024 and 1,300 metric tons in the 2025 estimate. The 2021 to 2024 import sources for compounds are led by China and South Africa. No world chemical production tonnage, and no split between zirconia, zirconium oxychloride, and other salts, was found in the sources opened here. TODO(data): world zirconium chemical production and the ceramic versus chemical share of zircon sand.

Nuclear alloy tube is a metal product, not a sand product. USGS says niobium, stainless steel, and tantalum can substitute in some nuclear uses, and that titanium and synthetic materials may substitute in some chemical processing plant uses. Those two sentences are the Survey's own split between nuclear metal and chemical equipment metal. Hafnium metal's leading use is superalloys, and boron or cadmium silver indium alloys can substitute for hafnium in control rods. Nuclear grade zirconium is the metal from which hafnium has been separated. The 2025 estimated price of unwrought hafnium is 3,800 dollars per kilogram, against 22 dollars per kilogram for zirconium sponge, ex works China [ZR_PRICE_HF_2025]. That price gap is the coproduct, not a zirconium price.

ATI's Form 10-K for the year ended 28 December 2025 says the company pioneered reactor grade zirconium and hafnium alloys for nuclear plants and still produces them, in plate, sheet, and strip, at Millersburg, Oregon, and Huntsville, Alabama. It also names naval nuclear products among defence uses. The filing does not give zirconium tons, a tube versus plate split, or a nuclear share of sales. A Framatome brochure describes M5 as a recrystallized zirconium niobium alloy for pressurised water reactor fuel rod cladding and assembly structures, made from zircon powder and zirconium sponge, with a claim of more than 7 million fuel rods and 96 reactors [ZR_FRAMATOME_M5]. The direct PDF download returned 403, so that claim is taken from the indexed brochure and the edition date is not confirmed (the file path is September 2025 and the document code contains 05 18). A 2024 methods paper used M5 tube from Framatome and Optimized ZIRLO tube from Westinghouse, cut from 17 by 17 pressurised water reactor cladding [ZR_CLADDING_TUBES_2024]. Westinghouse purchase tons are not published. TODO(data): kilograms of zirconium alloy tube per gigawatt and per reload.

Chemical equipment in zirconium metal is the use that sits next to the company's corrosion resistant portfolio. USGS names it only as an application where titanium and synthetic materials may substitute. No tonnage was found. VDM grades are an application benchmark in the draft and are not evidence of this company's sales.

## Market size

No aggregate value is stated for zircon sand, zirconium chemicals, or nuclear alloy tube.

Tronox's 274 million dollars is 2025 revenue from its own zircon sales, not the world market. The same filing reports 2.3 billion dollars of titanium dioxide revenue, which shows zircon is the coproduct inside a pigment business. Iluka's 976 million is mineral sands revenue, covering zircon, rutile, and synthetic rutile, and the notice does not label the currency. Multiplying the USGS 1,200 thousand metric tons by the 1,800 dollar premium price would mix a rounded gross weight total with one grade's CIF China price, and it would ignore the narrative conflict inside USGS. That product is not used.

EU sold production value for unwrought zirconium and powders is 1.4 million euro in 2024 on 4,000 kg, flag :R on both cells. The article code's 2024 value is 210 million euro on 1,200,000 kg, also flag :R. Because the quantities are round and flagged, they are not converted here into a euro per kilogram price or into an addressable market.

The bases differ: Iluka is its own FOB mix of premium and standard sand, Fastmarkets is premium grade CIF China, and the U.S. figure is a landed unit value for three origins. ## Capacity and supply

USGS reserves, thousand metric tons of ZrO2 content, for 2025 are Australia 55,000, South Africa 5,900, other countries 5,700, Indonesia 3,400, Senegal 2,600, Madagascar 2,100, Mozambique 1,500, the United States 500, China 500, and Sierra Leone 290. The world total is rounded to more than 70,000. Footnote 10 says Joint Ore Reserves Committee compliant or equivalent reserves for Australia were 21 million tons of ZrO2, so the 55,000 thousand metric ton cell is a broader figure than the JORC subset. U.S. zircon resources, a different concept from reserves, are about 14 million tons associated with titanium in heavy mineral sands.

Tronox says its current mining and beneficiation operations can produce about 297,000 metric tons of zircon a year, alongside 832,000 metric tons of titanium feedstock. That capability covers KwaZulu Natal (Fairbreeze), Namakwa, Cooljarloo and Chandala, Atlas and Broken Hill, and Wonnerup. In 2025 the Fairbreeze extension was commissioned and construction at Namakwa East was completed and was being commissioned. The filing does not say how much of the 297,000 metric tons was produced in 2025. It does say Chinese zircon production and exports are a competitive risk.

Iluka suspended Cataby and the SR2 synthetic rutile kiln, kept 2026 zircon guidance near 180 kt, and was commissioning Balranald, which it describes as rich in rutile and premium zircon. Eneabba is a rare earths refinery, budgeted at 1.7 to 1.8 billion in the same unlabelled dollars, with commissioning in 2027. It is not a zircon sand expansion.

Rio Tinto's ore reserves at 31 December 2025, annual report pages 290 and 291, show Richards Bay Minerals at 1,079 million metric tons of ore (proved 416 at 0.2% zircon, probable 663 at 0.4% zircon) and a total grade of 0.3% zircon. Rio's 74% share of marketable product is stated as 9.0 million metric tons of titanium dioxide feedstock and 2.2 million metric tons of zircon. QMM, where the marketable product footnote refers to zirsil, shows a Rio share of 0.2 million metric tons in the zircon column at an 85% interest. These are reserves after the report's own definitions, not annual capacity and not 2025 sales. A 2025 zircon production tonnage, and any restart date for mine life projects at Richards Bay, were not in the pages read.

ATI names the metal plants but not a furnace capacity in zirconium. USGS names one Oregon and one Utah metal producer and does not identify them. ATI's Oregon zirconium site is Millersburg, not the Albany titanium melt shop. The Utah producer in the USGS sentence is not identified here. ## Outlook to 2035

Zircon sand has no dated 2035 volume in the sources opened. The near term evidence is a softer sand market, not a growth path. USGS estimates a lower world concentrate total in 2025 than in 2024, with the narrative conflict noted above. Iluka describes subdued mineral sands demand, suspended capacity at Cataby and SR2, and pricing pressure from other producers. Tronox points to weaker Chinese ceramics and more Chinese sand. Both producers are still funding mine life (Balranald, Fairbreeze, Namakwa East) rather than a demand boom. A quantitative sand balance to 2035 is TODO(data).

Zirconium chemicals have no outlook to 2035 in these sources. They move with ceramics, refractories, and the small metal chain, and those drivers are not separately forecast here. TODO(data): chemical demand to 2035.

Nuclear alloy tube follows the reactor fleet, and only after an unmeasured conversion from gigawatts to tube tons. In the World Energy Outlook 2025, Table 7.1, the Net Zero Emissions by 2050 Scenario has installed nuclear capacity of 420 GW in 2024, 520 GW in 2030, and 710 GW in 2035. The text says installed nuclear capacity expands by 70% by 2035 in that scenario (data/raw/WorldEnergyOutlook2025.pdf). In the Stated Policies discussion, nuclear capacity increases by 3% a year on average to 2035. In the Current Policies Scenario, global nuclear capacity increases by three quarters from the current level by 2050, and China is described as close to half of capacity under construction and on track to be the largest nuclear operator around 2030. Those are three different scenarios. None of them is a zirconium tonnage. The Association's planned category runs out about 15 years, so it is not a 2035 delivery schedule either.

The practical split for the company is that zircon sand and chemicals are large, cyclical, and led by ceramics and by Australian and South African mines, while nuclear tube is a qualified metal product whose demand can rise with the reactor outlook and whose U.S. wrought imports already come mostly from France. The missing step before any 2035 metal forecast is the kilogram of alloy tube per gigawatt.

## Spotlight facts

1. In the second quarter of 2026 Iluka produced 47 kt of zircon, including concentrate, from Jacinth Ambrosia concentrate at Narngulu in Western Australia, and guided about 180 kt of zircon for the full year 2026, split between sand and concentrate, at a second quarter contracted sand price of 1,546 dollars per tonne.
2. In 2025 Tronox sold 274 million dollars of zircon sand from mines in South Africa (Fairbreeze and Namakwa) and Australia (Cooljarloo, Atlas, Wonnerup), and stated a capability of about 297,000 metric tons of zircon a year, against 2.3 billion dollars of titanium dioxide revenue.
3. In fiscal 2025 ATI produced reactor grade zirconium and hafnium alloys in plate, sheet, and strip at Millersburg, Oregon, and Huntsville, Alabama, using zirconium sand and sponge sourced in the United States and China, for nuclear plants and naval nuclear products.
4. Over 2021 to 2024, USGS reports that France supplied 72% of U.S. imports of wrought zirconium, with Germany at 10% and Belgium at 7%, which is the trade signature of the alloy tube and other wrought metal chain rather than of zircon sand.
5. On 6 November 2026 the World Nuclear Association listed 40 reactors under construction in China, 45,230 MWe gross, out of 81 reactors and 88,463 MWe gross worldwide, the demand backdrop for zirconium alloy cladding from producers such as Framatome (M5) and Westinghouse (Optimized ZIRLO tube, as supplied for a 2024 test).
