# Molybdenum

Mine molybdenum, technical oxide, ferromolybdenum, molybdenum metal and alloy mill products are different stages. A tonne of contained molybdenum at the mine is not a tonne of HASTELLOY C-276 plate, and the US molybdic oxide price is not the price of either. Every figure below names the stage, the year and the unit. Contained molybdenum is written as Mo. Gross product weight is named when that is what the source measures.

## Key findings

1. World mine output in 2025 is 260,000 metric tons of contained Mo in USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026, and 672.6 million pounds of new molybdenum in the International Molybdenum Association release of 1 April 2026 [MO_USGS_MINE_WORLD_2025, MO_IMOA_PROD_2025]. Converted at 0.45359237 kilograms per pound, the IMOA total is 305,100 tonnes. The gap is about 45,000 tonnes and is concentrated in China, where USGS reports 97,000 tonnes and IMOA reports 302.1 million pounds, about 137,000 tonnes [MO_USGS_MINE_CHINA_2025, MO_IMOA_PROD_CHINA_2025]. North America and Chile plus Peru agree within about 2,000 tonnes. The two world totals should both be shown. They should not be averaged.

2. The United States mined an estimated 40,000 tonnes of contained Mo in 2025, up 18 percent from 34,000 tonnes in 2024, and remained a net exporter [MO_USGS_MINE_US_2025]. Reported consumption of primary products was 18,000 tonnes, while apparent consumption was 34,000 tonnes [MO_USGS_REPORTED_US_2025, MO_USGS_APPARENT_US_2025]. Exports of ore and concentrates were 27,000 tonnes of contained Mo. The mine number, the roast and alloy consumption number, and the trade identity are three different things.

3. The usable annual price is the USGS average for US molybdic oxide, specified at 57 percent molybdenum content and taken from Argus Non-Ferrous Markets. The 2025 estimate is 51 dollars per kilogram, up 7 percent from 47.72 dollars per kilogram in 2024, after 54.32 dollars per kilogram in 2023 [MO_USGS_PRICE_OXIDE_2025, MO_USGS_PRICE_OXIDE_2024]. The World Bank Pink Sheet file has no molybdenum series. IMOA says the reference price is roasted molybdenite concentrate, called technical oxide, but the page opened shows a chart and no numeric table.

4. IMOA first use of new molybdenum in 2025 was 671.8 million pounds, which IMOA prints as 304,700 tonnes, almost level with mine production of 672.6 million pounds [MO_IMOA_USE_2025]. China used 337.8 million pounds, up 9 percent. Europe used 114.3 million pounds, down 7 percent, and was still the second largest user [MO_IMOA_USE_EUROPE_2025, MO_IMOA_USE_CHINA_2025]. End use including scrap was 423,000 tonnes, up 2 percent [MO_IMOA_ENDUSE_2025]. In 2024 the industry reused circa 129,000 tonnes of secondary molybdenum and the average scrap ratio in molybdenum containing materials was 29 percent [MO_IMOA_SCRAP_2024].

5. Molybdenum intensity per tonne of mill product spans two orders of magnitude. Outokumpu Supra 316L/4404 has a typical molybdenum content of 2.1 percent. Haynes HASTELLOY C-276 has a nominal molybdenum content of 16 percent. Alloy 625 sits near 9 percent on the Haynes nominal composition, the ATI typical analysis and the Alleima tube nominal, inside the Special Metals limiting range of 8.0 to 10.0 percent [MO_OUTOKUMPU_316L_MO, MO_HAYNES_C276_MO, MO_ATI_625_MO, MO_ALLEIMA_625_MO]. On those published percentages, one tonne of 316L contains about 21 kilograms of Mo, one tonne of C-276 about 160 kilograms, and one tonne of Haynes 625 about 90 kilograms. Those are arithmetic illustrations, not melt recipes.

6. Only China and the United States produce molybdenum both from primary molybdenum mines and from copper byproduct mines. Chile, Peru, Mexico and the other countries in the USGS table rely on byproduct copper production. A primary mine can respond to the molybdenum price. A byproduct tonne follows the copper mine plan. USGS says a Canadian company remains on schedule to restart an idled molybdenum mine in Idaho in the second half of 2027 and is ramping a processing plant in Pennsylvania. Centerra Gold describes Thompson Creek in Idaho and Langeloth in Pennsylvania as its molybdenum mine and metallurgical plant. The 2027 date was not on the Centerra page opened in this pass.

7. No source opened here gives a molybdenum mine or use figure for 2035. IMOA consultants forecast growth in every first use segment and do not print a rate or a terminal year. The only dated volume path is an IMOA estimate for China, more than 300,000 kilometres a year of stainless water pipe from 2026 to 2031, about 12 percent of it molybdenum containing grades, mainly Type 316L. That path is pipe length, not tonnes of Mo, and it stops in 2031.

## Production

USGS mine figures are contained molybdenum, in metric tons, and the 2025 column is estimated. World mine production was 256,000 tonnes in 2024 and 260,000 tonnes in 2025, which USGS describes as an increase of about 2 percent [MO_USGS_MINE_WORLD_2024, MO_USGS_MINE_WORLD_2025]. The file is `data/clean/usgs_mcs2026.csv`, Commodity Molybdenum, and the narrative is the February 2026 chapter at https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-molybdenum.pdf. The chapter header says the data are metric tons of molybdenum content unless otherwise specified.

USGS country mine production for 2025, contained Mo, estimated, is China 97,000 tonnes, Chile 42,000 tonnes, the United States 40,000 tonnes, Peru 39,000 tonnes and Mexico 17,000 tonnes. Those five are the 90 percent USGS names. The arithmetic on the rounded tonnes is 235,000 of 260,000, or 90 percent. Armenia 5,300, Kazakhstan 4,300, Mongolia 4,200, Iran 3,300, Canada 2,200, Uzbekistan 2,000, Russia 1,300, Australia 1,000, North Korea 800 and the Republic of Korea 500 make up the rest. The other countries line is zero. Significant revisions were made to 2024 production for Australia and Canada.

The same chapter separates how the metal is mined. In 2025, concentrate continued at two primary molybdenum operations in Colorado and at seven byproduct operations, four in Arizona and one each in Montana, Nevada and Utah. Only China and the United States produce from both primary molybdenum mines and byproduct copper mines. Every other country in the table relies on byproduct copper production. Rising consumption has led many copper producers to upgrade facilities so they can extract molybdenite from existing deposits. That byproduct route is the supply response outside China and the United States, and it moves with copper mine plans rather than with a standalone molybdenum investment decision.

IMOA production statistics are also new molybdenum from mines. They exclude recycled material and molybdenum recovered from spent catalysts. The 1 April 2026 release, https://www.imoa.info/news-events-blog/latest-news-details.php?lang=en&objectID=835, puts 2025 production at 672.6 million pounds, up 4 percent from a revised 648.3 million pounds in 2024. China produced 302.1 million pounds, up 1 percent from 297.7 million pounds. South America was broadly unchanged at 175.6 million pounds. North America rose 17 percent to 133.7 million pounds from 114.5 million pounds. Other regions were unchanged at 61.3 million pounds. The regional pounds sum to 672.7 million, against a published world total of 672.6 million, which is rounding.

The 2 April 2025 release, https://www.imoa.info/news-events-blog/latest-news-details.php?lang=en&objectID=789, had put 2024 production at 639.7 million pounds, up 2 percent from 625.4 million pounds in 2023, with China at 294.8 million pounds and other regions at 55.7 million pounds. The April 2026 release therefore revised 2024 world production up by 8.6 million pounds and revised the 2024 China and other region bases. For 2025 and for any 2024 world total, use the April 2026 release. Use the April 2025 release for the 2023 level of 625.4 million pounds, and do not mix the two 2024 vintages in one table without a footnote.

Reserves in the USGS table, as of the 2025 column, are 17,000 thousand metric tons of contained Mo for the world, of which China 7,800, the United States 3,500, Chile 2,600, Russia 1,100 and Peru 1,000 thousand metric tons [MO_USGS_RESERVES_WORLD_2025]. Dividing 17 million tonnes by the 2025 USGS mine rate of 260,000 tonnes gives about 65 years, and dividing by the converted IMOA mine rate of about 305,100 tonnes gives about 56 years. Both ratios are arithmetic on published stocks and one year of production. They are not a forecast, and they ignore that most non Chinese and non US output is a copper byproduct. Australia is a special case inside the same table. The reserves cell is 760 thousand metric tons, while footnote 7 says Joint Ore Reserves Committee compliant or equivalent reserves were 260,000 tons. The world total of 17,000 thousand metric tons uses the table, not the footnote. Identified resources are about 5.4 million tons in the United States and about 20 million tons in the rest of the world. USGS says those resources are adequate for the foreseeable future. The IMOA facts page still cites a 2008 USGS reserve base of 19 million tonnes. That sentence is stale and should not be used.

The data release flags molybdenum as not a US critical mineral for 2025. That flag is the column `Is critical mineral 2025` in `data/clean/usgs_mcs2026.csv`. It matches the trade position. The United States is a net exporter in every year from 2021 to 2025.

## Trade

No molybdenum trade file was saved. Bilateral exports and imports for HS 8102 and for ferromolybdenum are a `TODO(data)` item and are written out in `research/materials/molybdenum_trade.md`. What follows is the US stage split from USGS, which is not a substitute for UN Comtrade.

US 2025 trade, contained Mo, estimated, from the same MCS chapter: imports of ore and concentrates 17,000 tonnes, imports of primary products 9,700 tonnes, exports of ore and concentrates 27,000 tonnes, exports of primary products 6,500 tonnes. Total imports for consumption were about 27,000 tonnes, against 26,900 tonnes in 2024. Total exports rose about 1 percent. Net import reliance is recorded as E, which the chapter defines as net exporter, for 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025. The ore export footnote says molybdenum content was estimated from US Census Bureau unit values.

Import source shares for 2021 to 2024, which are shares and not tonnes, split by product. Ferromolybdenum came from Chile 74 percent, the Republic of Korea 21 percent, the United Kingdom 4 percent and other countries 1 percent [MO_USGS_FEMO_IMPORT_CL]. Molybdenum ore and concentrates came from Peru 69 percent, Mexico 15 percent, Chile 12 percent and Canada 4 percent. Total imports, all molybdenum forms combined, came from Chile 36 percent, Peru 35 percent, Mexico 8 percent, the Republic of Korea 7 percent and other countries 14 percent. A US alloy mill that buys ferromolybdenum is therefore looking at Chile and Korea. A US roaster that buys concentrate is looking at Peru, Mexico and Chile. Those are not the same supply chain.

IMOA regional use is a demand geography, not a customs flow. In 2025 the United States used 65.7 million pounds of new molybdenum, up 3 percent. Europe used 114.3 million pounds, down 7 percent. Japan used 42.6 million pounds, down 3 percent. The CIS used 18.3 million pounds, down 12 percent. Other regions used 93.1 million pounds, down 1 percent. China used 337.8 million pounds. The six regions sum to the published 671.8 million pounds. Europe is still the second largest using region, and it is the region whose first use fell. That is the trade relevant fact for a European mill while the Comtrade files are missing. It does not say which country shipped the units.

In February 2025 the Government of China imposed export controls on molybdenum powders. USGS states the control and says the United States and other countries sought alternative sources. The controlled form is powder, which sits in the molybdenum metal chapter of the tariff schedule, not in concentrates, technical oxide or ferromolybdenum. ## Consumption

US consumption has two USGS measures, both contained Mo. Reported consumption of primary products was 16,100 tonnes in 2021, 15,800 tonnes in 2022, an estimated 16,000 tonnes in 2023, an estimated 17,000 tonnes in 2024 and an estimated 18,000 tonnes in 2025. Apparent consumption, defined as production plus imports minus exports plus or minus adjustments for all industry stock changes, was 33,100, 31,500, 30,700, 28,500 and an estimated 34,000 tonnes over the same years. The 2025 apparent figure is up 18 percent on 2024, in the chapter's own words. Consumer material stocks were about 1,900 tonnes at the end of 2024 and about 2,000 tonnes at the end of 2025. A check of the 2025 identity, 40,000 plus 17,000 plus 9,700 minus 27,000 minus 6,500, equals 33,200 tonnes before stock adjustments, against the published apparent total of 34,000 tonnes. The residual is the stock adjustment USGS does not itemise. The identity is a check, not a third official series.

IMOA usage is first use of new molybdenum, supplied by Steel and Metals Market Research in Austria, and it excludes scrap. World first use was 630 million pounds in 2023 and 648.4 million pounds in 2024 on the April 2025 release, then a revised 653.6 million pounds for 2024 and 671.8 million pounds for 2025 on the April 2026 release. The market page https://www.imoa.info/molybdenum/molybdenum-market-information.php prints the 2025 first use total as 671.8 million pounds of Mo, equal to 304,700 tonnes of Mo. On the revised 2024 figures, new mine supply of 648.3 million pounds sat 5.3 million pounds below first use. In 2025, mine supply of 672.6 million pounds sat 0.8 million pounds above first use. Those gaps are new molybdenum only. They are the inventory swing IMOA describes, not a scrap estimate.

The annual review for 2025/2026, https://www.imoa.info/download_files/annualreview/IMOA_AnnualReview_2025_2026.pdf, page 5, cites an SMR update of May 2026. Global end use demand including molybdenum contained in scrap reached 423,000 tonnes in 2025, up 2 percent. Over the same period that update says mine production grew 5 percent and first use excluding scrap grew 4 percent, while end use grew 2 percent. The differences are attributed to inventory changes along the chain. The April statistical release says mine production grew 4 percent and usage grew 3 percent. The two IMOA products are a quarterly statistical release and a later consultant update. They are close, and they are not the same vintage. The 423,000 tonne end use total and the 304,700 tonne first use total differ by construction, because one includes scrap and inventory effects. The only published scrap mass opened here is the 2024 figure.

USGS says molybdenum recycled in new and old steel and other scrap may be as much as 30 percent of the apparent supply of molybdenum. There is no separate refinery for secondary molybdenum from alloys. The molybdenum stays in the alloy scrap and is reused. IMOA says that in 2024 the industry reused circa 129,000 tonnes of secondary molybdenum and that the average scrap ratio in molybdenum containing materials was 29 percent. The study sits in the 2024 SMR report and is members only. The 30 percent and the 29 percent are the same order of magnitude and they use different denominators, apparent supply versus the scrap ratio in molybdenum containing materials. Alleima reports that about 80 percent of the steel melted at Sandviken is recycled feedstock, with nickel, chromium and molybdenum bought outside the company. That 80 percent is a steel scrap share at one tube mill, not a world molybdenum scrap share.

IMOA first use of 65.7 million pounds in the United States converts to about 29,800 tonnes. USGS reported primary product consumption is 18,000 tonnes and apparent consumption is 34,000 tonnes. The IMOA use figure sits between them. The boundaries differ, so the three numbers should be labelled and not reconciled by averaging.

## Applications

IMOA splits the chain into three product families on https://www.imoa.info/molybdenum/molybdenum-products.php. Chemical products are pure molybdic oxide, molybdates and lubricant grade molybdenum disulfide, used in catalysts, polymer compounding, corrosion inhibitors and lubricants. Meltstock products are the alloy additions to iron, steel, nickel and titanium, and they are technical oxide, ferromolybdenum and molybdenum metal pellets. Molybdenum metal products are powder, molybdenum metal, molybdenum base alloy mill products, and parts made from them. Steel mills and nickel alloy mills buy the second family. Furnace, electronic and sputter users buy the third. The company sells alloy mill products in which molybdenum is one element, not oxide and not ferromolybdenum as a merchant product.

The facts page https://www.imoa.info/molybdenum-media-centre/facts-and-figures.php describes the route and does not date the shares. Concentrate from flotation contains 85 to 92 percent molybdenum disulfide. Roasting in air at 500 to 650 degrees Celsius produces roasted molybdenite concentrate, also called technical oxide. Some 40 to 50 percent of molybdenum is used in that form, mainly as an alloying element in steel. Of roasted concentrate production, 30 to 40 percent is converted to ferromolybdenum by a thermite reaction with iron oxide, ferrosilicon and aluminium, and the ingots are crushed. About 20 percent of roasted concentrate is processed into chemicals such as pure molybdic oxide and molybdates. Molybdenum metal powder is made by a two stage hydrogen reduction. Those shares are not one partition. The 40 to 50 percent is a share of molybdenum used as oxide. The 30 to 40 percent and the 20 percent are shares of roasted concentrate production. Adding them as if they were slices of one pie would double count.

On the same undated facts page, about 20 percent of new molybdenum from mined ore goes into molybdenum grade stainless steel. Engineering steels, tool and high speed steel, cast iron and superalloys together account for a further 60 percent. The remaining 20 percent goes into upgraded products, including lubricant grade disulfide, chemical compounds and molybdenum metal. The annual review, page 17, says about a third of all molybdenum is used to improve carbon steel, which is a subset of the engineering steel group rather than a rival percentage. An older IMOA brochure that puts stainless at 25 percent and other iron at 50 percent was not used, because the figures in it refer to the mid 2000s.

The annual review gives the 2025 end use split, including scrap, for the 423,000 tonne total. Chemical and petrochemical processing is 17 percent. Oil and gas, including refining, is 14 percent. Mechanical engineering is 11 percent. Automotive is 11 percent. Process industry excluding chemical and petrochemical is 9 percent. Other transportation is 9 percent. Building and construction is 9 percent. Power generation is 7 percent. Aerospace and defense is 5 percent. Other uses are 5 percent. Consumer goods, electronics and medical equipment together are 3 percent. The shares sum to 100. The review says aerospace, defense and power generation recorded particularly strong growth, without a rate. These are end use shares of molybdenum units, not shares of alloy mill product revenue.

Inside carbon and engineering steels the addition rate is small and the tonnes of steel are large. The annual review says cold rolled advanced high strength low alloy steels typically contain 0.1 to 0.2 percent molybdenum, for seat structures, storage racks and battery casings. A German vehicle maker is qualifying a press hardened steel with 0.15 percent molybdenum for mass production, and the Maserati Grecale already uses a molybdenum bearing press hardened grade. A completed project with Dillinger and the Nickel Institute found that 0.5 percent molybdenum gives hardenability in extra heavy plate. A collaboration with ArcelorMittal Industeel on plastic mould plate up to 250 millimetres thick found that 2.0 percent molybdenum outperforms 0.7 percent molybdenum on the target hardness. These are plate and sheet, not nickel alloy bar.

Stainless and nickel alloys are where the grades named for this note sit, and the molybdenum percentage is the point. Outokumpu describes Supra 316L/4404 (EN 1.4404, UNS S31603) as its most widely used molybdenum alloyed austenitic stainless steel, with a typical molybdenum content of 2.1 percent, alongside typical chromium of 17.2 percent and nickel of 10.1 percent, on https://www.outokumpu.com/en/products/product-ranges/supra. Supra 316/4401 is also listed at 2.1 percent molybdenum. Supra 316L/4435 (EN 1.4435) is listed at 2.6 percent molybdenum. Supra 316plus, a lower molybdenum concept, is listed at 0.7 percent. The page says these grades are available in many product forms. The linked range datasheet carries a file stamp of 17 November 2025. Outokumpu does not, on that page, give a shipped tonne figure for 316.

Haynes brochure H-2002H, copyright 2023, https://haynesintl.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/c-276.pdf, gives HASTELLOY C-276 (UNS N10276) a nominal molybdenum content of 16 percent, with nominal chromium 16 percent, iron 5 percent, tungsten 4 percent and nickel as the balance at 57 percent. The forms are plate, sheet, strip, billet, bar, wire, pipe, tube and covered electrodes. The named uses are chemical process reactors, heat exchangers and columns, plus sour oilfield service. Haynes and VDM are one Acerinox competitor. The brochure predates the November 2024 acquisition and remains the Haynes composition document.

Alloy 625 (UNS N06625) is the same grade under four publishers, with molybdenum clustered around 9 percent. Haynes brochure H-3073H, copyright 2020, https://haynesintl.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/625-brochure.pdf, gives a nominal molybdenum content of 9 percent, nominal chromium 21 percent and nominal niobium plus tantalum 3.7 percent, in aerospace, chemical processing and power applications, with long term service best kept to 593 degrees Celsius. ATI data sheet for sheet and plate, https://www.atimaterials.com/Products/Documents/datasheets/nickel-cobalt/nickel-based/ati_625_ludlum_version_tds_en_v1.pdf, page 2, lists a typical molybdenum content of 9.0 percent, typical chromium 22.0 percent and typical niobium plus tantalum 3.5 percent. The sheet says the alloy is melted by vacuum induction or AOD and is sold as plate, sheet, strip and long products. Alleima Sanicro 625 seamless tube, datasheet updated 9 May 2025, https://www.alleima.com/en/technical-center/material-datasheets/tube-and-pipe-seamless/sanicro-625/, gives a nominal molybdenum content of 8.7 percent and a nominal chromium content of 21.5 percent, in outside diameters of 6 to 42 millimetres. The Special Metals technical bulletin https://www.specialmetals.com/documents/technical-bulletins/inconel/inconel-alloy-625.pdf gives a limiting range of 8.0 to 10.0 percent molybdenum and a nickel minimum of 58.0 percent, and it says strength comes from molybdenum and niobium. The bulletin pages that were read do not carry a publication year. The four statements agree. Nominal and typical values near 9 percent sit inside an 8 to 10 percent purchase specification.

Two architectural and water facts show where 316L sheet and pipe actually go. The IMOA annual review, page 21, says the Suzhou Museum of Contemporary Art uses 900 tonnes of pattern finished Type 316L stainless sheet for its roof. The fabricator is not named. The same page says an upgrade of secondary water systems in Chinese high rise buildings is estimated to need more than 300,000 kilometres a year of stainless water pipe from 2026 to 2031, and that about 12 percent of that pipe is expected to be molybdenum containing grades, mainly Type 316L. Neither fact is a molybdenum tonne figure. At the Outokumpu typical of 2.1 percent, the 900 tonnes of sheet would contain about 19 tonnes of Mo. That conversion is arithmetic and the museum sheet may not be the Outokumpu analysis.

USGS says there is little substitution for molybdenum in steels and cast irons. Potential substitutes are boron, chromium, niobium and vanadium in alloy steels, tungsten in tool steels, and graphite, tantalum and tungsten in high temperature furnace parts. Outokumpu's own Supra 316plus, at 0.7 percent molybdenum against 2.1 percent in 316L, is a commercial illustration that some stainless applications can be redesigned with less molybdenum. C-276 at 16 percent molybdenum is not in that substitution set. The pitting resistance is the molybdenum.

## Market size

No source opened here publishes a transaction value for the world molybdenum market. Multiplying mine tonnes by the US oxide price would mix a mine stage with an oxide benchmark and would invent a market value. That calculation is not used.

The physical market that can be stated is the 2025 mass balance of new molybdenum. IMOA mine production was 672.6 million pounds and first use was 671.8 million pounds, printed by IMOA as 304,700 tonnes of first use. End use including scrap was 423,000 tonnes. USGS mine production was 260,000 tonnes of contained Mo. The value of alloy mill products that contain molybdenum is visible only inside company sales, and those sales are alloy sales, not molybdenum sales. Acerinox high performance alloys, which is VDM plus Haynes, recorded sales of EUR 1,683 million in 2025. Aperam Alloys and Specialties recorded sales of EUR 1,113 million and shipments of 61,000 tonnes at an average selling price of EUR 17,475 per tonne. ATI recorded sales of USD 4,587.4 million, of which nickel based and specialty alloys were 46 percent. None of those figures is a molybdenum market, and none should be added to an oxide price times mine output.

Eurostat PRODCOM was opened as context and does not contain a molybdenum product. `data/clean/prodcom_2019-2024.csv` is a header with no rows. `data/clean/prodcom_2023.csv` contains one product, 24452200 nickel plates, sheets, strip and foil, for EU27 in 2023. Sold production quantity is 71,394,665 kilograms and sold production value is EUR 1,201,546,766, both flagged estimated. That is nickel plate. It is evidence that the EU mill product statistic exists for a sister metal, and it is not a molybdenum production or trade figure. The portfolio list in `tools/data/fetch.py` has no molybdenum PRODCOM code. `TODO(data): Eurostat PRODCOM codes for ferromolybdenum, molybdenum oxides and unwrought molybdenum were not in the fetched file. The codes themselves were not confirmed from the Eurostat list in this pass.`

OECD STAN was opened as context for the industries that melt and fabricate molybdenum bearing steels and alloys. It is output and value added of whole sectors, in current prices and national currency, with the file's unit multiplier labelled as millions. It is not molybdenum demand. In `data/clean/oecd_stan_P1.csv`, German manufacture of basic metals (ISIC C24) has output of 139,869 million euro in 2023, and US manufacture of basic metals has output of 338,285 million dollars in 2024. German value added for C24 in `data/clean/oecd_stan_B1G.csv` is 27,778 million euro in 2023. The same files hold C20 chemicals, C25 fabricated metal products, C28 machinery, C29 motor vehicles and C30 other transport equipment for Germany, France, Italy, the United Kingdom, Japan, Korea and the United States, at various latest years from 2023 to 2025. Those sectors are where the IMOA end use shares for chemicals, automotive, mechanical engineering and other transport sit. They cannot be multiplied by a molybdenum intensity that this note does not have.

UNIDO INDSTAT was not available. `data/raw/unido` contains only a placeholder. Automated access is blocked. Global manufacturing output by ISIC is therefore missing, and the STAN figures above cover only the OECD reporters in the clean file.

## Outlook to 2035

There is no 2035 molybdenum balance in the documents opened. USGS says global consumption was expected to remain strong as countries invest in renewable energy infrastructure, and that identified resources are adequate for the foreseeable future. It gives no tonne path and no year 2035. The SMR update of May 2026, as quoted by IMOA, says six years of moderate growth have come from a diversified first use and end use, that aerospace, defense and power generation have been particularly strong, and that consultants continue to forecast use growth in all first use segments. No compound rate and no 2030 or 2035 level is printed. The detailed series are members only.

The dated pieces that do exist stop before 2035 or are not molybdenum tonnes. The Idaho primary mine restart is scheduled, in the USGS chapter, for the second half of 2027, with the Pennsylvania processing ramp already under way. The China stainless water pipe estimate runs from 2026 to 2031 and is in kilometres, with about 12 percent expected in molybdenum grades, mainly 316L. IMOA's bridge specifications for duplex stainless, the first two of six, were published in April 2026. Duplex is a molybdenum bearing stainless family, and the review does not give a molybdenum tonne consequence. A flue gas project with Shanghai University of Electric Power, Jinduicheng Molybdenum, Qingshan and the Nickel Institute will test molybdenum bearing stainless and nickel alloys and has not published results.

The supply structure that will still matter in 2035 is already visible. Chile, Peru and Mexico, which together are 98,000 tonnes of the USGS 2025 mine total, are byproduct copper producers. Their molybdenum comes if the copper mines run and if the molybdenite circuit is switched on. China and the United States can also run primary mines, which is the tranche that can be restarted when the oxide price is high, as the Idaho case illustrates. Scrap already returns on the order of 30 percent of supply and is not a separate refined market. Powder is the form China put under export control in February 2025, so a 2035 story about molybdenum metal mill products is not the same story as a 2035 story about 316L sheet or C-276 plate.
