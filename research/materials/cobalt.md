# Cobalt

Research note for chapter 4.2 and the cobalt rows of chapter 6. Notes only. `sections/` was not edited. `tools/data/fetch.py` was not run. Every figure below states the stage (mine, refined, intermediate or end use), whether it is contained cobalt or gross weight, the year and the unit. Team arithmetic is labelled as such.

## Key findings

1. World mine production in 2025 is an estimated 310,000 metric tons of contained cobalt, and the published world total is rounded [CO_USGS_MINE_WORLD_2025]. Congo (Kinshasa) is 230,000 metric tons and Indonesia is 44,000 metric tons in the same table [CO_USGS_MINE_DRC_2025, CO_USGS_MINE_IDN_2025]. The Mineral Commodity Summaries narrative says Congo (Kinshasa) accounted for an estimated 73 percent of the world total and Indonesia for 14 percent. The country lines are separately estimated and sum to 314,600 metric tons, 4,600 metric tons above the rounded world total, so the rounded world total is the figure to use for the global mine balance.
2. Refined production is a different stage from mine output. The Cobalt Institute, using Benchmark Mineral Intelligence, puts global refined cobalt at 240 kt contained in 2025, up 5 percent on 2024, with China at 78.6 percent of refined production [CO_CI_REFINED_2025, CO_CI_CHINA_REF_2025]. The same report puts mined supply at 270 kt, which is not the USGS mine total. USGS does not publish a world refined tonnage in the 2026 cobalt chapter. It only says China remained the leading refined producer and the leading consumer, mainly for lithium ion batteries.
3. The commercial split that matters for a high performance alloy producer is battery chemicals versus alloy grade metal, not a single cobalt price. In 2025, electric vehicle batteries took 113 kt and portable electronics 93 kt, while superalloys took 20 kt [CO_CI_EV_2025, CO_CI_PORTABLE_2025, CO_CI_SUPER_2025]. Those two battery segments sum to 206 kt, a team sum, or about three quarters of the 276 kt demand total. In the United States the mix is the reverse: an estimated 51 percent of 2025 consumption went to superalloys, mainly aircraft gas turbine engines, 25 percent to chemicals, 15 percent to other metallic uses and 9 percent to cemented carbides [CO_USGS_US_ENDUSE_2025].
4. Hydroxide and metal did not share one price path in 2025. Benchmark cobalt hydroxide, CIF Asia, rose from 5.90 USD per pound at the start of 2025 to 25.25 USD per pound at year end, a 328 percent increase [CO_CI_OH_PRICE_2025]. Benchmark cobalt metal, ex works Europe, rose from 10.75 USD per pound to 24.75 USD per pound, a 130 percent increase [CO_CI_METAL_PRICE_2025]. The USGS annual average London Metal Exchange cash price is a third series: 11.84 USD per pound in 2024 and an estimated 15 USD per pound in 2025 [CO_USGS_LME_2025]. The World Bank Pink Sheet has no cobalt price.
5. Availability in 2026 and 2027 is a quota, not a mine capacity number. In February 2025 Congo (Kinshasa) banned cobalt exports. In October the ban was replaced by quotas of 18,125 metric tons of contained cobalt for the rest of 2025 and up to 96,600 metric tons a year in 2026 and 2027, including 9,600 metric tons for strategic reserves [CO_USGS_QUOTA_2026]. The IEA policy record of ARECOMS Decision No. 004/2025 gives the same 96,600 tonne total, split as an 87,000 tonne base quota and a 9,600 tonne strategic quota. The International Energy Agency Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 says the projected cobalt supply gap widens from just over 15 percent to over 25 percent because of that quota [CO_IEA_CO_GAP_2026]. The Outlook page places this next to the 2035 cobalt supply chart. It does not print the gap in tonnes.
6. The two largest DRC miners cannot export anything like their mine output. CMOC produced 117,549 metric tons of cobalt in 2025, all from the DRC, and the Cobalt Institute states 117.5 kt for the same year [CO_CMOC_CO_2025, CO_CI_CMOC_2025]. Its reported 2026 export entitlement is 31,200 metric tons [CO_CMOC_QUOTA_2026]. Glencore own sourced cobalt was 36.1 kt in 2025, of which the Cobalt Institute assigns 33.5 kt to DRC hydroxide [CO_GLEN_CO_2025, CO_CI_GLEN_OH_2025]. Nornickel restarted an electrolytic cobalt shop rated at up to 3,000 metric tons a year of 99.9 percent metal, which is the alloy grade form and is small next to hydroxide [CO_NORNI_CO_CAP_2025].
7. EU sold production of unwrought cobalt is not in the files. PRODCOM code 24453030, the code mapped to cobalt in `tools/data/fetch.py`, is labelled in Eurostat DS-059358 as bismuth and cadmium articles, and the value arrays for EU27_2020 and Germany, 2019 to 2024, are empty [CO_PRODCOM_24453030]. UN Comtrade for HS 8105 and HS 2605 was not retrieved because the public preview quota was exceeded. There is no clean cobalt trade file.

Mine production and refined production are different products and cannot be added.

Values for 2025 are estimated. The rounded world total is 310,000 metric tons in 2025 and 302,000 metric tons in 2024 [CO_USGS_MINE_WORLD_2025].

The only US primary mine named is the Eagle Mine in Michigan, a nickel copper mine whose cobalt bearing nickel concentrate was exported to Canada or overseas for processing. US mine production was an estimated 300 metric tons in 2025, and US secondary production from purchased scrap was an estimated 2,000 metric tons [CO_USGS_US_SECONDARY_2025]. About five US companies produced cobalt chemicals. USGS does not name them in this chapter.

Identified world terrestrial resources are about 25 million tons of cobalt, mostly in sediment hosted copper deposits in Congo (Kinshasa) and Zambia, in nickel laterites in Australia, nearby island countries and Cuba, and in magmatic nickel copper sulphide deposits in Australia, Canada, Russia and the United States. US identified resources are about 1 million tons, mostly in Minnesota. An estimated 5 billion tons of cobalt is contained in sea floor nodules. That nodule figure is not a reserve and is not a 2035 supply source in any document read here.

The Cobalt Institute market report dated May 2026, file `data/raw/aerospace/cobalt_institute_cmr2025.pdf`, was prepared by Benchmark Mineral Intelligence. It measures mined supply at 270 kt contained in 2025, flat on a 2024 figure that Benchmark revised up to 270 kt [CO_CI_MINE_2025]. Congo (Kinshasa) held a 73 percent share, down from 77 percent in 2024. Indonesia produced 42.5 kt, up 29 percent, a 14 percent share, up from 11 percent [CO_CI_IDN_2025]. Mined supply outside the DRC and Indonesia was 38 kt, up 3 percent. One chart note says the mined figures include downstream yield losses, and the 2025 DRC bar is production, not exports. That scope difference is the main reason the 270 kt series sits below the USGS 310,000 metric ton estimate. Both series still put the DRC share at 73 percent and Indonesia at 14 percent.

Cobalt is mined mostly as a by product of copper in the DRC and of nickel in Indonesia. The report says cobalt hydroxide is the main DRC intermediate, with payabilities of 20 to 40 percent. In Indonesia, mixed hydroxide precipitate is the largest intermediate, and cobalt in matte is a second intermediate with up to 70 percent nickel content. Kisanfu (KFM) is estimated to have exceeded 80 kt of cobalt in hydroxide in 2025. CMOC, operating KFM and Tenke Fungurume, produced 117.5 kt and held a 39.5 percent global share [CO_CI_CMOC_2025]. Glencore DRC production was 33.5 kt of cobalt in hydroxide, an 11.3 percent share, down 4.6 percent, with Katanga down 15.8 percent and Mutanda up 34.2 percent [CO_CI_GLEN_OH_2025]. ERG Metalkol produced 19,000 metric tons, unchanged. The DRC was able to export approximately 41 kt in 2025, and Indonesian mine output of 42.5 kt exceeded those exports, so Indonesia was the largest effective supplier to the market even though it was not the largest miner [CO_CI_DRC_EXPORT_2025].

Refined production, meaning cobalt sulphate, tetroxide, metal and other salts, and not crude hydroxide, was 240 kt in the text and 241 kt on Figure 7 for 2025, against 228 kt on the 2024 bar of the same figure [CO_CI_REFINED_2025]. The 1 kt gap between the sentence and the figure is left as printed. China was 78.6 percent of refined production in 2025 (79.4 percent in 2024). The narrative rounds China to 79 percent. Finland was second at 6.6 percent, with no growth since 2024 and no new operation since 2021. Indonesia rose from 1.7 percent to 3.2 percent and passed Canada (3.0 percent). Madagascar was 1.9 percent, Japan 1.7 percent, Norway 1.3 percent and Australia 1.0 percent.

Product form inside that refined total, contained cobalt: cobalt sulphate 90 kt in 2025, down 17 percent, used mainly in nickel cobalt manganese battery cathodes [CO_CI_SULPHATE_2025]. Tricobalt tetroxide 73 kt, up 68 percent, used mainly in lithium cobalt oxide cathodes for portable electronics [CO_CI_TETROXIDE_2025]. Figure 7 shows a 2025 split of 38 percent sulphate, with the other two slices at 30 percent and 32 percent on a 241 kt bar. Sulphate at 90 kt is the 38 percent slice. Tetroxide at 73 kt is about 30 percent of 241 kt, so the remaining slice is metal, and the text says metal was the second largest refined product. A world metal tonnage is not printed as its own line. Outside China, metal production was 34 kt, up 8 percent, with Tsingshan starting cobalt metal in Indonesia in 2025 and PT Lygend ramping cut cathode. Metal's share of Chinese refined output was 23 percent in 2025, against 18 percent in 2023, after a 2 percent decline in Chinese metal output in 2025. Mixed hydroxide precipitate is described as better suited to sulphate, while hydroxide stocks were drawn toward tetroxide and metal when DRC hydroxide was scarce.

USGS confirms the stage split in one sentence only: China remained the leading refined producer. It gives no world refined tonnes, no hydroxide tonnes and no sulphate tonnes.

## Consumption

Two consumption concepts have to stay separate: US apparent and estimated consumption of contained cobalt, and world demand by application from the Cobalt Institute.

United States, metric tons of contained cobalt, from the USGS salient statistics. Estimated consumption includes reported data and USGS estimates: 7,830 in 2024 and 8,000 in 2025. Apparent consumption is defined as secondary production plus imports minus exports, with adjustments for industry stock changes of refined cobalt. Government stock changes dropped out of the definition from 2023. Apparent consumption was 7,960 in 2024 and an estimated 9,600 in 2025 [CO_USGS_US_APPARENT_2025]. The 1,600 metric ton gap in 2025 is a definition gap, not a range to average. Purchased scrap was 25 percent of estimated consumption in 2025, which matches 2,000 divided by 8,000. The estimated value of cobalt consumed in the United States in 2025 was 320 million USD [CO_USGS_US_VALUE_2025]. That is a US consumption value, not a world market size.

World demand in the Cobalt Institute report was 276 kt contained in 2025, up 13 percent, after Benchmark revised 2024 demand up to 245 kt. The increase was 32 kt [CO_CI_DEMAND_2025]. Electric vehicles were 113 kt, 39 percent of the total in the text (the demand chart prints 41 percent, which is 113 divided by 276). Portable electronics were 93 kt, up 15 kt. Superalloys were 20 kt, up 7.5 percent. Military uses across batteries, magnets and superalloys were an estimated 6.8 kt. Industrial applications as a group were 57 kt in 2025 against 54 kt in 2024, and within that bar superalloys were 35 percent and hard metals 16 percent [CO_CI_INDUSTRIAL_2025]. Catalysts, ceramics and colours, hard facing, tyres, soaps, paint driers, magnets and other uses fill the rest of the industrial bar. A separate world total for hard metals in tonnes is not printed.

Secondary supply met roughly one tenth of global demand in 2025, and that share is projected to almost double by 2040 [CO_CI_SECONDARY_2025]. This is a world demand share. It is not the US 25 percent scrap share, and the two should not be averaged. Battery recycling enters as black mass from cell scrap and end of life portable batteries. The report says traceability is still poor.

OECD STAN is sector output and value added, not cobalt consumption. Files `data/clean/oecd_stan_P1.csv` and `data/clean/oecd_stan_B1G.csv` are in national currency, unit multiplier millions, current prices. Germany stops at 2023. The United States output series used below is 2024. These industries buy alloys, chemicals and components. They do not report contained cobalt.

## Applications

World use is battery chemicals. US use, and the relevant use for this company, is alloy grade metal and cobalt chrome.

Battery chemicals. Sulphate feeds nickel cobalt manganese cathodes. Tetroxide feeds lithium cobalt oxide cathodes in portable electronics. Electric vehicle cobalt demand of 113 kt in 2025 came from gigafactories, with growth in mid nickel and high nickel cathodes, not from a return to high cobalt chemistries. The report says lithium iron phosphate dominates grid storage, so storage is a small cobalt use. By 2035 the same report forecasts electric vehicle cobalt demand of 220 kt, a compound rate of 6.5 percent (printed as GAGR), while portable electronics grow at 0.3 percent a year over the same span [CO_CI_EV_2035]. China is expected to account for 63 percent of cathode cobalt demand and 68 percent of cell cobalt demand in 2026. Gigafactory cobalt demand is expected to stay ahead of end use battery demand until 2034.

Superalloys. The Cobalt Institute says superalloys need alloy grade cobalt metal, a small part of refined supply, and a chain that is distinct from batteries. Demand was 20 kt in 2025, driven by aerospace turbines, with a further pull from industrial gas turbines linked to data centre power [CO_CI_SUPER_2025]. USGS puts the US share at 51 percent of domestic consumption in the same year, mainly aircraft gas turbine engines [CO_USGS_US_ENDUSE_2025]. The tonnage behind that US share is on the order of half of 8,000 metric tons of estimated consumption, about 4,000 metric tons, which is a team reading of the USGS percentage and is not a printed USGS line. It should not be compared with the 20 kt world superalloy figure without saying one is a US consumption share and the other is a world demand estimate. Commercial aircraft context in the Cobalt Institute report: Airbus delivered 793 aircraft in 2025, after 766 in 2024, and Boeing and Airbus together still look for more than 40,000 deliveries over 20 years. Combined deliveries in 2024 were just under 1,300 aircraft. Those are airframes, not cobalt tonnes.

Hard metals and chemicals. Cemented carbides were 9 percent of US consumption. World hard metals were 16 percent of the 57 kt industrial bar. Other US chemical uses were 25 percent. Other metallic uses were 15 percent. Substitutes listed by USGS include nickel base alloys or ceramics in jet engines, titanium base alloys in prosthetics, and iron phosphate chemistries that already hold a large share of batteries in China. Substitution in engines and implants costs performance or qualification time. Substitution in batteries is already happening and is why the IEA says cobalt demand growth to 2040 has moderated, partly offset by changes inside nickel cobalt manganese chemistries.

Medical cobalt chrome is an end use of alloy grade metal, not of hydroxide. Zimmer Biomet, Stryker and Smith & Nephew buy or machine implant stock. None of the filings read states a cobalt tonne. Smith & Nephew sells OXINIUM, an oxidized zirconium alloy described as having virtually no nickel, cobalt and chromium, as a substitute for cobalt chrome bearing surfaces. Detail is under company evidence.

## Prices

The Pink Sheet file `data/clean/worldbank_pinksheet_monthly.csv` runs from 1960M01 through 2025M12 and contains Aluminum, Copper, Nickel, Zinc, Tin, Lead, Iron ore, Brent crude and European natural gas. It does not contain cobalt. The fetcher note on WB_PINKSHEET already says cobalt is not in the Pink Sheet. There is therefore no World Bank monthly cobalt price to cite [CO_WB_PINK_NO_COBALT].

USGS annual averages, USD per pound, cobalt content basis. London Metal Exchange cash: 23.17 in 2021, 28.83 in 2022, 15.48 in 2023, 11.84 in 2024, and an estimated 15 in 2025. US spot cathode, which USGS defines as refined electrolytic metal and sources from S&P Global Platts Metals Week: 24.21, 30.78, 17.20, 16.77, and an estimated 21 in the same years [CO_USGS_LME_2025, CO_USGS_CATHODE_2025]. Cathode sat above the LME cash average in every year shown. Both series fell hard from 2022 to 2024 and only partly recovered in the 2025 annual average, because the average includes the weak months before the export ban.

Benchmark assessments in the Cobalt Institute report, USD per pound of contained cobalt, are path prices rather than annual averages. Hydroxide CIF Asia: 5.90 at the start of 2025, a soft print of 5.65 early in the year, then 25.25 at year end, up 328 percent [CO_CI_OH_PRICE_2025]. The executive summary says hydroxide was as low as 5.60 USD per pound by mid February and rose by over 300 percent. The 5.60 and 5.65 prints are the same episode inside one report. Metal ex works Europe: 10.75 at the start of 2025 to 24.75 at year end, up 130 percent, with the fourth quarter moving from 20.85 to above 25 in October and then settling in the low to mid 24s [CO_CI_METAL_PRICE_2025]. Sulphate rose 266 percent over the year. Start and end sulphate levels in USD per pound were not extracted as a clean pair. The report says hydroxide was briefly priced above metal when hydroxide was scarce. That inversion is the commercial point: a melter buying metal and a cathode plant buying hydroxide did not face the same market.

The price move is attributed to the export ban and then the quota, not to a demand shock. The report describes an apparent deficit created by restricting exports while mine production continued, with stocks building inside the DRC and stocks outside the DRC being drawn down. Government stockpiling, including Chinese purchases, was estimated to be lower in 2025 than in 2024, when low prices had been an opportunity to buy.

## Capacity and supply

Mine capacity and export capacity diverged in 2025.

DRC policy. Exports were suspended from 22 February 2025. ARECOMS Decision No. 004/2025 of 20 September 2025 extended the suspension through 15 October 2025 and set a quota system from 16 October. The IEA policy record, which matches the USGS narrative and the Cobalt Institute account, sets October 2025 at 3,625 tonnes, November at 7,250 tonnes and December at 7,250 tonnes (18,125 tonnes for the quarter). For 2026 the base quota is 87,000 tonnes, or 7,250 tonnes a month, plus a strategic quota of 9,600 tonnes at ARECOMS discretion, for a 96,600 tonne total. 2027 is the same total, subject to quarterly adjustment [CO_USGS_QUOTA_2026, CO_IEA_ARECOMS_2025]. Quotas follow historical exports from 2022 to 2024, with exceptions for Entreprise Générale du Cobalt and the Lubumbashi tailings company. Operators that exported under 100 tonnes in 2024, refiners without their own mine, and depleted mines are excluded. The strategic quota can absorb unused base quota. ARECOMS can buy excess stocks. This is contained cobalt, not gross hydroxide weight.

Company volumes sit under that cap. CMOC 2025 cobalt output was 117,549 metric tons in the company research note (news reports of the 2025 results) and 117.5 kt in the Cobalt Institute report. 2026 guidance is 100,000 to 120,000 metric tons of cobalt, unchanged while copper guidance rises [CO_CMOC_GUIDE_2026]. The reported 2026 export entitlement is 31,200 metric tons against the national quota. 31,200 divided by 117,549 is 27 percent, a team ratio. Subtracting 31,200 from 117,549 gives 86,349 metric tons of 2025 output above the 2026 entitlement, also team arithmetic. That difference is not a measured stockpile. The company note does not say whether the metal was stored as hydroxide, left in solution, or partly converted in the DRC. ## Outlook to 2035

Three outlook statements answer three different questions. They are not averaged.

Mined supply volume, Cobalt Institute / Benchmark. Supply is forecast to rise from 270 kt in 2025 to 550 kt in 2035 [CO_CI_SUPPLY_2035]. Indonesia's share of that supply reaches 32 percent by 2035 on the chart (the 2035 bar is printed as 550 kt). The DRC share on the same bar is 55 percent, and the text says the DRC stays above 50 percent through 2040. The chart note again includes downstream yield losses. This path assumes mine growth. It is not a statement that all of those tonnes can be exported under the present quota.

Demand by use, same report. Electric vehicle cobalt demand reaches 220 kt by 2035. Portable electronics barely grow (0.3 percent a year). Battery storage grows faster than portables (3.8 percent a year) but stays small because lithium iron phosphate dominates that use. North American cobalt demand, which had been forecast near 85 kt by 2030 before the 2024 US election, was cut to just shy of 45 kt after the removal of the clean vehicle tax credit and tighter rules on Chinese content. Superalloy tonnes in 2035 are not given. TODO(data): a 2035 superalloy and hard metal cobalt demand from the Cobalt Institute tables, if a later page prints them. The passages read stop at the 2025 level of 20 kt for superalloys.

Policy constrained balance, IEA. The Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 outlook page says cobalt is the exception to improved balances. The projected supply gap widens from just over 15 percent to over 25 percent because expected production falls after the DRC quota [CO_IEA_CO_GAP_2026]. The cobalt chart on that page is titled for 2035 and compares mine supply from existing and announced projects with primary requirements in the stated policies, current policies and high demand scenarios. Demand growth for cobalt to 2040 is described as moderated by lithium iron phosphate, partly offset by shifts inside nickel cobalt manganese cathodes. No 2040 cobalt tonnage is printed on the page. The same outlook ranks cobalt among the higher risk exposure materials for high technology, aerospace and defence, with supply concentration, limited substitutes and existing export restrictions.

The previous IEA book is more comfortable and should not be mixed in. World Energy Outlook 2025, `data/raw/WorldEnergyOutlook2025.pdf`, PDF page 247, records the February 2025 four month suspension and the October 2025 quota. PDF page 62 says the average share of the top three refining countries across key energy minerals rose from about 82 percent in 2020 to 86 percent in 2024, with nickel and cobalt called out. The chart axes on those pages run to 0.6 million tons for cobalt. That number is an axis maximum, not a data point, and it is not used. Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 is the later document and is the one that widens the cobalt gap. The direction of the revision is the finding: a 2025 view that projects could cover stated policies demand was overtaken by the quota.

No price forecast to 2035 is taken from these sources. The Cobalt Institute says the quota supports prices in the short term and that a stockpile inside the DRC can be released later if policy changes. That is a qualitative risk, not a price path.

## Spotlight facts

1. In 2025 CMOC produced 117,549 metric tons of contained cobalt at Tenke Fungurume and Kisanfu in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, sold mainly as hydroxide, and its reported export entitlement for 2026 is 31,200 metric tons under the ARECOMS quota, so most of the mined units are not free to leave the country on that entitlement (CMOC results as reported in the company note, Cobalt Institute 2025 market report, ARECOMS via the IEA policy record).
2. In February 2026 the US Geological Survey estimated that 51 percent of US cobalt consumption in 2025 went into superalloys for aircraft gas turbine engines, with the Eagle Mine in Michigan exporting cobalt bearing nickel concentrate for processing in Canada or overseas, while about five unnamed US firms made cobalt chemicals (USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026, cobalt chapter).
3. In 2025 Benchmark, for the Cobalt Institute, put world superalloy demand at 20 kt of alloy grade cobalt metal and electric vehicle demand at 113 kt of battery chemicals, and it recorded Airbus deliveries of 793 aircraft, which is the aerospace pull on that metal rather than on hydroxide (Cobalt Market Report 2025, May 2026).
4. In its fiscal 2025 Form 10-K, Zimmer Biomet stated that it buys cobalt chrome as a raw material commodity for implants made mainly in Warsaw, Indiana, on supply contracts of generally 12 to 24 months, and the filing names no metal producer (Zimmer Biomet Form 10-K for the year ended 31 December 2025, Item 7A, p. 40).
5. In 2025 Nornickel finished rebuilding an electrolytic cobalt shop at Norilsk rated at up to 3,000 metric tons a year of 99.9 percent metal, the alloy grade form a superalloy melter can charge, while the same year's DRC system was built around hydroxide export quotas rather than around this metal (Nornickel 2025 annual report).
