# Chromium

Chromium reaches alloy plants in four commercial forms that this note does not add together. Chromite ore is reported as gross weight, or in some reserve columns as chromic oxide (Cr2O3). Ferrochrome (ferrochromium) is a smelted alloy, and the only public price located here is in US dollars per pound of chromium content. Chromium metal is a separate traded product with its own gross weight price. Stainless flat products, ingots and wire are steel tonnes, not contained chromium. A tonne of ore is not a tonne of ferrochrome, and a tonne of stainless melt shop output is not a tonne of chromium.

## Key findings

1. World chromite mine output in the USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 is 49,600 thousand metric tons gross weight in 2024 and an estimated 51,000 thousand metric tons gross weight in 2025, with South Africa at 22,900 and an estimated 23,000 of those totals [CR_MINE_ORE_2024_WORLD, CR_MINE_ORE_2025_WORLD, CR_MINE_ORE_2025_ZAF]. The same chapter estimates that global chromite ore mine production rose by 3 percent in 2025. The United States has no mine production. These are ore tonnes, not contained chromium (data/clean/usgs_mcs2026.csv, Commodity Chromium, and the February 2026 chapter PDF).

2. The International Chromium Development Association public dashboard, under the heading "Chrome ore world production in 2025", shows a different ore level. The six regional bars are South Africa 25,195 kt (+3.5 percent), CIS and Middle East 4,875 kt, rest of Africa and the Americas 3,255 kt, India 3,083 kt, Europe including Albania and Türkiye 2,935 kt, and rest of Asia and Australasia 689 kt. A team sum of those bars is 40,032 kt. The summary box on the same page prints production as greater than 40 M and says the figure includes UG2, with sources USGS, BRGM, H. Pariser and ICDA [CR_ICDA_ORE_2025_ZAF]. Turkey alone is 9,000 thousand metric tons in the USGS 2025 estimate, which is already larger than the entire ICDA Europe bar that includes Türkiye. The two ore series are not interchangeable. This note uses the USGS country series when a gross weight mine total is required, and it does not convert one series into the other.

3. Ferrochrome smelting has moved to China while the ore has not. ICDA states that China became the largest ferrochrome producer in 2012, holds virtually no chromite resources, and relies on imported ore, mainly from South Africa. On the 2025 ferrochrome dashboard China is 9,907 kt (+5 percent) and South Africa and Zimbabwe together are 2,091 kt (down 48 percent) [CR_ICDA_FECR_2025_CHN, CR_ICDA_FECR_2025_ZAFZWE]. USGS independently calls China the leading ferrochromium producing country, the leading stainless producing country and the leading chromium consuming country. Team Stainless reported 2024 ferrochrome production of 18.51 Mt, up 15 percent, driven notably by China and Indonesia, and 2024 chrome production of 39.20 Mt, up 9 percent [CR_TEAMSS_FECR_2024, CR_TEAMSS_ORE_2024]. The 2024 note does not say whether those tonnes are gross alloy or contained chromium.

4. Stainless melt shop production, which is the demand pool for most ferrochrome, was 64,157 thousand metric tonnes in 2025, up 2.1 percent, of which China produced 40,868 thousand metric tonnes and the European Union 5,659 thousand metric tonnes (down 1.9 percent) [CR_SS_MELT_2025_WORLD, CR_SS_MELT_2025_CHN, CR_SS_MELT_2025_EU]. These are gross melt shop tonnes. They are not contained chromium and they are not cold rolled flat product sales. worldstainless released the figures on 27 February 2026. The 2024 column in that same table is 62,821 thousand metric tonnes.

5. The United States is a price and consumption observation point, not a mining country. Apparent consumption in 2025 is estimated at 480 thousand metric tons of chromium content, reported consumption at 360 thousand metric tons, and net import reliance at 79 percent of apparent consumption [CR_US_APPCONS_2025, CR_US_NIR_2025]. Recycled chromium in reported stainless scrap receipts was 21 percent of apparent consumption. The value of chromium material consumption, measured as net imports excluding stainless steel, is estimated at USD 720 million in 2025, down from USD 852 million in 2024. There is no World Bank Pink Sheet chromium price. The USGS prices, sourced to Argus Non Ferrous Markets, must stay on three lines: chromite ore was an estimated USD 290 per metric ton gross weight in 2025, ferrochromium an estimated USD 1.60 per pound of chromium content, and chromium metal an estimated USD 5.90 per pound gross weight [CR_PRICE_ORE_2025, CR_PRICE_FECR_2025, CR_PRICE_METAL_2025]. Ore and ferrochromium prices fell from 2024, while the metal price rose.

6. The only chromite mine in the European Union is Outokumpu's Kemi mine in Finland, which feeds the Tornio ferrochrome plant and the Tornio stainless melt shop. USGS puts Finland's 2025 chromite mine output at an estimated 1,900 thousand metric tons gross weight, with ore reserves of 63,000 thousand metric tons and Cr2O3 reserves of 16,000 thousand metric tons. Outokumpu's ferrochrome segment recorded sales of EUR 462 million and adjusted EBITDA of EUR 138 million in 2025, a third year of improvement, while group stainless deliveries were 1,751 kt [CR_OUTOKUMPU_FECR_SALES_2025]. Ferrochrome tonnes at Tornio are not in the company profile. VDM Metals and Haynes are one Acerinox group competitor, not two.

Kazakhstan's ore reserves are likely on a GKZ A+B+C1 basis, and USGS excluded C2. World resources are greater than 12 billion tons of shipping grade chromite, enough for centuries on the USGS wording, and about 95 percent of world chromium resources are in Kazakhstan and southern Africa. US resources are mostly in the Stillwater Complex in Montana. ICDA's public page says South Africa holds the largest resources and reserves and that Zimbabwe and Kazakhstan are also significant. Its summary boxes print resources greater than 12000 M, reserves greater than 470 M and production greater than 40 M for 2025, without defining M on the page, so those three boxes are not used as tonne totals [CR_USGS_RESOURCES].

ICDA separates metallurgical, chemical, refractory, foundry and UG2 chromite in its collection rules, and it says the vast majority of ore produced is metallurgical grade used to make ferrochrome. The public 2025 ore bars are not split by those grades. South African UG2 chromite is a by product of platinum group metal mining and, on ICDA's own description, has changed the structure of the ferrochrome industry. The USGS country series does not show a UG2 split either.

Ferrochrome production is not in the USGS world table. The ICDA heading "Ferrochrome world production in 2025" gives grade bars and regional bars in kilotonnes. The page does not state whether the kilotonnes are gross alloy or contained chromium. Grade bars: charge chrome (high carbon) 15,939 kt, down 9 percent, medium carbon 26 kt, up 10 percent, and low carbon 978 kt, up 13 percent. A team sum of the grade bars is 16,943 kt, so charge chrome is the bulk of the published grade split. Regional bars: China 9,907 kt (+5 percent), South Africa and Zimbabwe 2,091 kt (down 48 percent), CIS and Middle East 1,776 kt (down 10 percent), India 1,304 kt (down 8 percent), Americas and rest of Asia 1,257 kt (+27 percent), and Europe including Albania and Türkiye 609 kt (down 5 percent). A team sum of the regional bars is 16,944 kt. The 1 kt gap is left as rounding. Other producing countries named by ICDA, and not all shown as separate bars, include Albania, Brazil, Finland, Germany, Indonesia, Japan, Oman, Russia, Sweden, Turkey and Zimbabwe. Indonesia is named by Team Stainless as a 2024 growth driver and is not a separate 2025 bar.

## Applications

USGS states that chromium has no substitute in stainless steel, which is the leading end use, or in superalloys, which are the major strategic end use. Scrap can replace ferrochromium in some metallurgical uses. ICDA states that metallurgical ore is used mainly to produce ferrochrome and that the stainless industry is the major driver of the chromium industry. Those two statements agree on the direction of demand and neither gives a world percentage split for 2025. A numeric end use share such as a percent of chromium units going to stainless was not in the MCS 2026 chapter text that was read. TODO(data): a dated end use percentage for chromium units in stainless, alloy steel, superalloys and chemicals, searched in USGS MCS 2026 chromium chapter and the ICDA public dashboard.

Inside stainless, the product forms that matter for a high performance alloy comparison are cold rolled flat product, plate, precision strip and tube, not commodity melt shop slabs. Outokumpu's flat grades that overlap specialty corrosion service include Forta DX 2205, Forta SDX 2507, Ultra 254 SMO and Ultra 6XN, produced at Avesta and Degerfors. Alleima's tube grades include SAF 2507 super duplex and SAF 3007 hyper duplex for umbilicals, plus Sanicro 625 and Sanicro 925 where the competition is nickel alloy tube rather than commodity stainless. Aperam Alloys Imphy produces nickel alloys, including grades equivalent to Alloy 625, Alloy 718 and Alloy 825, where chromium is an alloying addition inside a nickel alloy, not a stainless flat product. Acerinox's high performance alloys division is VDM Metals and Haynes together. VDM's portfolio is an application benchmark only. It is not evidence of the client's own sales.

Chemical use is visible in the trade pattern rather than in a world tonnage. The United States imports chromite mainly from South Africa for a single chemical producer, and it imports chromium chemicals from Kazakhstan, China, Germany and India. Foundry sands and refractory ore are ICDA statistical categories. Their 2025 tonnes are not on the public dashboard.

## Capacity and supply

Reserve life is long on the USGS resource statement, and the binding questions are smelting location, power, and the split between ore exports and domestic ferrochrome. South Africa remains the leading ore producer in both USGS and ICDA. ICDA still calls South Africa the largest ferrochrome producer outside China, and the 2025 bar for South Africa and Zimbabwe is less than a quarter of the China bar (2,091 kt against 9,907 kt). USGS flags South African risks that can move supply even when reserves are large: shifting demand in China, deep level mining, labour costs, and an unreliable electricity supply. It also says investment and new mines may raise chromite output in Brazil, Kazakhstan and Zimbabwe, and that possible South African export controls or tariffs on chromite ore could affect ferrochrome production in China.

Installed stainless capacity in the company profiles is flat product or melt capacity, not ferrochrome furnace capacity.

Outokumpu reports melting capacity of 2,800 kt in Finland and Sweden, Tornio melting capacity of 1.2 million tonnes a year for standard austenitic and ferritic grades, Avesta melt shop 500 kt a year, hot rolling about 2,320 kt and cold rolling about 1,880 kt. Kemi and Tornio supply chromium internally. External chromium and nickel suppliers are not named. A hot rolling mill is described as a barrier that can require more than EUR 1 billion. The EVOLVE programme at Avesta is about 13 kt of additional flat high nickel alloy capacity, which is a specialty flat increment, not a commodity stainless expansion. FY2025 capital expenditure was EUR 145 million.

Aperam states stainless flat capacity of 2.5 million tonnes in Europe and Brazil and calls itself the only integrated stainless producer in South America. European melt and hot rolling sites named are Genk, Chatelet and Gueugnon. Imphy alloy output is about 36,000 tonnes a year. A new vacuum induction furnace complex at Imphy is part of a EUR 160 million package. Aperam Recycling supplies stainless and alloy scrap internally. Universal Stainless, acquired in January 2025, is a US specialty steel and nickel alloy producer and is not a chromite miner.

Acerinox group melting shop output was 1,866 kt in 2025. NAS has a USD 244 million project for 20 percent more stainless flat capacity. The high performance alloys division, VDM Metals plus Haynes, had sales of EUR 1,683 million and melt shop output of 83 kt in 2025. Counting VDM and Haynes as separate competitors would double count one Acerinox business.

Alleima's Sandviken works is an integrated melt to tube route using about 80 percent recycled steel, with chromium bought externally and suppliers not named. A fourth VAR furnace was operational in autumn 2025. Mill 68 is being reopened to raise nuclear tube capacity by about 60 percent by the end of 2026. That is tube capacity in advanced stainless and nickel alloys, not commodity flat capacity.

Tsingshan produces stainless steel, including PT Indonesia Tsingshan Stainless Steel, and is a stainless competitor. Group stainless tonnage and ferrochrome tonnage were not found. The profile's figures are nickel ore and nickel pig iron, which do not measure chromium. Baowu is the largest Chinese steel group and has stainless and special steel units. A primary 2025 stainless tonnage was not found. The Ma'anshan electric furnace figures in the profile (140,000 and 220,000 tonnes a year) are special steel capacity from a trade article with an unclear date, and they are not used here as chromium capacity. Nippon Yakin reports high nickel alloys and stainless as one business. Melt shop tonnes and page numbers remain TODO(data) because the integrated report was not opened.

US supply of primary chromium is imports plus scrap. Secondary production of 100 thousand metric tons of chromium content in 2025 is estimated from stainless scrap receipts. Net import reliance of 79 percent means the US market does not clear from domestic mine supply. Finland is the EU's domestic ore and ferrochrome source and also 5 percent of US ferrochromium imports over 2021 to 2024.

## Outlook to 2035

No source read for this note gives a chromium, ferrochrome or stainless demand number for 2035. The IEA World Energy Outlook 2025 mentions chromium in four places, none of them a demand path. Figure 1.23 (page 63) includes chromium among 20 energy related strategic minerals and says China is the dominant refiner for 19 of the 20, with an average share around 70 percent. The note says the values refer primarily to 2024. The text does not print chromium's own refiner share, so the 70 percent average is not used as a chromium statistic. Figure 5.15 (page 248) places chromium in a wider vulnerability set covering top producer, price volatility, trade restrictions and by product status. The text extraction does not yield chromium's score on those four flags. Section 8.5.2 (page 380) says Africa holds over 55 percent of cobalt and chromium reserves. That claim is not defined as ore or Cr2O3 and it is not reconciled here to the USGS table, where South Africa plus Zimbabwe is 490,000 of a world ore reserve greater than 1,200,000 thousand metric tons. The glossary (page 479) includes chromium in a typical critical minerals list. USGS also marks chromium as a critical mineral for 2025 in the data release. The file is data/raw/WorldEnergyOutlook2025.pdf.

What can be said about the path to 2035 without inventing a growth rate is structural. Ore reserves and resources, on the USGS wording, cover centuries, and the contested layer is conversion: China smelts imported ore, South African ferrochrome fell sharply in the ICDA 2025 bars, and stainless melt shop growth in 2025 was 2.1 percent with the European Union down and China still about 64 percent of world melt shop tonnes. USGS expects possible ore growth in Brazil, Kazakhstan and Zimbabwe and lists power, depth and Chinese demand as constraints on South Africa. High carbon charge chrome dominates the ICDA grade split, while low carbon ferrochrome and chromium metal are the smaller flows that sit closer to specialty alloys. Specialty flat and tube capacity additions in the company profiles (Outokumpu Avesta about 13 kt, Alleima nuclear tube, Acerinox NAS flat and the VDM and Haynes investments) are small next to Tornio, Aperam and Chinese melt shop tonnes, and they are the part of the chain where chromium is sold as a qualified alloy product rather than as ore or charge chrome.

## Company evidence

Outokumpu's annual report 2025 (pages 4, 6, 25 and 32 in the profile's citation) is the European integrated chain: Kemi chromite, Tornio ferrochrome, Tornio and Avesta stainless melt, and cold rolling in Germany and the Netherlands. Ferrochrome sales were EUR 462 million and adjusted EBITDA EUR 138 million in 2025. Group deliveries were 1,751 kt of stainless. Europe net sales were EUR 3,600 million with adjusted EBITDA of minus EUR 46 million. The Americas were EUR 1,676 million of sales and EUR 102 million of adjusted EBITDA. Management said demand in Europe and North America stayed weak and that European project business in energy, oil, gas and chemicals was on hold. The August 2026 investor presentation describes high nickel alloys, at 5.5 to 12 percent nickel, as a faster pocket than standard stainless, at 5.5 to 12 percent a year. That growth range is a company statement about nickel bearing flat products, not a chromium demand forecast. Named customer: Metso, for duplex equipment. External chromium suppliers are not named.

Aperam's half year report 2025 and the full year 2025 results separate commodity stainless from alloys. Stainless and Electrical Steel shipped 1,668 kt and sold EUR 3,823 million, with adjusted EBITDA of EUR 140 million. Alloys and Specialties shipped 61 kt at EUR 17,475 per tonne. The half year report said LNG carrier shipbuilding, aerospace, medical and electronics still had healthy demand, while oil and gas, petrochemicals, automotive and electrical components were weak. Scrap comes through Aperam Recycling. Imphy doubled production capacity in the first half of 2025 on LNG tanker demand, on the profile's reading of the company material. Stainless overcapacity of about 12 million tonnes globally is Aperam's description.

Alleima's annual report 2025 gives tube revenue of SEK 13,063 million, Kanthal SEK 3,996 million and strip SEK 1,571 million. Customer mix in 2025 was oil and gas 24 percent, industrial 17 percent, chemical and petrochemical 16 percent, industrial heating 10 percent, consumer 8 percent, medical 7 percent, transportation 6 percent, mining and construction 6 percent, nuclear 5 percent, and hydrogen and renewable energy 1 percent. About 80 percent of Sandviken feedstock is recycled steel. Chromium is purchased. Suppliers are not disclosed. Demand in medical rose, oil and gas and nuclear stayed high, and short cycle industrial and chemical demand fell.

Acerinox results for 2025 show stainless and high performance alloys as two divisions of one group. Stainless sales were EUR 4,119 million. High performance alloys, which is VDM Metals plus Haynes, were EUR 1,683 million of sales, EUR 135 million of EBITDA and 83 kt of melt shop output. VDM and Haynes are one competitor. Joint purchasing across Haynes, NAS and VDM is the raw material statement in the results, without supplier names. NAS stainless flat capacity is being raised by about 20 percent under a USD 244 million project. The 2025 commentary said oil and gas was sluggish, chemical processing was significantly weaker, automotive and electronics were stable, and aerospace was better than 2024.

Tsingshan's profile has no audited stainless or ferrochrome tonnage. It is a stainless producer in China and Indonesia and a nickel cost setter. Those nickel figures are out of scope for a chromium tonne balance. Baowu's profile likewise has no primary stainless chromium tonne. Fortune and a Shanghai government note support a group revenue rank, not a chromium share. Nippon Yakin's fiscal 2025 results (year to 31 March 2026) show net sales of JPY 150,866 million, down 12.3 percent, and a 6.8 percent drop in shipment volume, with high performance materials down 9.9 percent. The integrated report was not opened, so page numbers and melt capacity stay open. Its own ferronickel at Oheyama is a nickel integration story, not a chromite mine.

## Spotlight facts

1. Outokumpu in 2025 ran the only chromite mine in the European Union at Kemi in Finland and the Tornio ferrochrome plant that feeds Tornio's 1.2 million tonne stainless melt shop, with ferrochrome segment sales of EUR 462 million and adjusted EBITDA of EUR 138 million, while external chromium suppliers are unnamed (Outokumpu annual report 2025).

2. ICDA's 2025 ferrochrome bars put China at 9,907 kt, up 5 percent, and South Africa plus Zimbabwe at 2,091 kt, down 48 percent, for the ferrochrome product form, with China described as holding virtually no chromite and importing ore mainly from South Africa (ICDA market insights page, retrieved 7 November 2026).

3. Over 2021 to 2024 the United States bought 96 percent of its chromite ore from South Africa, 41 percent of its ferrochromium from South Africa and 24 percent from Kazakhstan, and 40 percent of its chromium metal from China, with the United Kingdom, Russia and France supplying most of the rest of the metal (USGS MCS 2026).

4. worldstainless on 27 February 2026 reported 2025 stainless melt shop production of 64,157 thousand metric tonnes of gross steel, with China at 40,868 thousand metric tonnes and the European Union at 5,659 thousand metric tonnes, which is the steel pool that draws metallurgical chromite through ferrochrome rather than a contained chromium total.

5. Alleima at Sandviken in 2025 melted advanced stainless and special alloys for tube using about 80 percent recycled steel and bought chromium from undisclosed external suppliers, while oil and gas was 24 percent of group customer sales (Alleima annual report 2025).
