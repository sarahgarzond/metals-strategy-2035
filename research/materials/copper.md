# Copper

Scope: high performance copper and copper alloy mill products (strip, sheet, foil and wire), for a fabricator that buys cathode, scrap and cast shapes. Mine output is context for price and availability. It is not a client capability. Figures below state the stage, whether the unit is contained copper or gross product weight, the year and the unit. No Comtrade extract for copper was available, and `tools/data/fetch.py` was not run.

## Key findings

1. The metal a European mill can buy is refined copper, not ore. World mine production was 22.955 million tonnes of copper in 2024 and a preliminary 23.180 million tonnes in 2025, while refined production was 27.392 million tonnes in 2024 and a preliminary 28.578 million tonnes in 2025 [CU_ICSG_MINE_2024, CU_ICSG_REFPROD_2025]. USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 rounds the same mine total to 23 million tonnes in both 2024 and the 2025 estimate, and rounds refinery production to 27.6 million tonnes in 2024 and 29 million tonnes in the 2025 estimate [CU_USGS_MINE_2024, CU_USGS_REF_2025]. The gap between mine and refinery output is secondary refined copper from scrap, 4.705 million tonnes in 2024 and a preliminary 5.348 million tonnes in 2025, equal to 17.2 percent and 18.7 percent of refined production [CU_ICSG_SECONDARY_2025].

2. Flat products are a small share of copper use. The International Wrought Copper Council, published in the ICSG World Copper Factbook 2025, splits 2024 global first use of copper and copper alloy semis into wire 63 percent, tube 12 percent, flat rolled plate, sheet and strip 11 percent, rods, bars and sections 9 percent, and foil 5 percent [CU_IWCC_FIRSTUSE_2024]. The factbook states that alloy semis are counted at an assumed 70 percent copper content. Semis plant capacity, which is gross weight and is capability rather than output, was 66.1 million tonnes in 2024, of which the same factbook assigns 59 percent to wire rod and 13 percent to flat rolled products [CU_ICSG_SEMIS_CAP_2024].

3. Producers already report the three stages separately. Aurubis produced 1,096,000 tonnes of copper cathode, 869,000 tonnes of continuous cast wire rod and 90,000 tonnes of flat rolled products plus specialty wire in fiscal year 2024/25 [CU_AURUBIS_CATH_FY2425, CU_AURUBIS_ROD_FY2425, CU_AURUBIS_FLAT_FY2425]. Mitsubishi Materials, in the year ended March 2025, produced 397 thousand tons of copper cathode and sold 117 thousand tons of wrought copper products [CU_MMC_CATH_FY2025, CU_MMC_WROUGHT_FY2025]. Wieland, which sells strip, sheet, tube, rod, wire and profiles rather than cathode, reported group revenue of EUR 6,619 million for the year ended 30 September 2025, with electronics and electrical engineering at 28 percent [CU_WIELAND_REV_FY2425].

4. The price that enters a mill's metal account is the refined copper quotation, and it jumped again after the summaries were closed. The average of World Bank Pink Sheet monthly copper prices was 9,142.14 US dollars per metric tonne in 2024 and 9,947.31 in 2025, and the December 2025 month was 11,785.25 [CU_WB_CU_2024, CU_WB_CU_2025, CU_WB_CU_2025M12]. ICSG reports LME period averages of 9,147 US dollars per tonne in 2024 and 9,945 in 2025, then 13,296 for January to August 2026 and 14,353 in August 2026 [CU_ICSG_LME_2025, CU_ICSG_LME_2026YTD]. The IEA states that prices surpassed 12,000 US dollars per tonne in December 2025 and exceeded 14,000 in May 2026, and that the annual smelter treatment and refining charge benchmark settled at 0 US dollars per tonne in January 2026 [CU_IEA_PRICE_2026, CU_IEA_TCRC_2026].

5. The supply gap in the official outlooks is a primary mine gap, not a 25 percent shortage of strip. In the Stated Policies Scenario, IEA copper demand increases by over 25 percent and by around 7 million tonnes by 2040, and primary copper supply faces a deficit of about 25 percent in 2035, narrowed from about 30 percent in the previous outlook [CU_IEA_DEMAND_2040, CU_IEA_GAP_2035]. The World Energy Outlook 2025 still carried that 30 percent shortfall by 2035 [CU_WEO_GAP_2035]. Nearer term, ICSG on 23 April 2026 projected a refined surplus of about 96,000 tonnes in 2026 and about 377,000 tonnes in 2027, after a shift away from the 150,000 tonne deficit it had forecast for 2026 in October 2025 [CU_ICSG_SURPLUS_2026]. Apparent refined use is still expected to grow, by 1.6 percent in 2026 and 2 percent in 2027, with the European Union and Japan described as subdued [CU_ICSG_USAGE_GROWTH_2026].

6. Data centre architecture changes conductivity copper in busbars, which is a wire rod and bar product, and the opened NVIDIA source does not quantify alloy strip. The NVIDIA technical blog of 20 May 2025 says a single 1 MW rack at 54 VDC requires up to 200 kg of copper busbar, so a 1 GW site could require up to 200,000 kg in rack busbars alone, and that row level 800 VDC busways reduce copper by 45 percent versus 415 VAC distribution [CU_NVIDIA_BUSBAR_2025]. Full scale 800 VDC production is timed to Kyber racks in 2027. Named partners include Infineon on the silicon side and Vertiv, Eaton and Schneider Electric on power systems. Tonnes of connector strip, leadframe or CuNiSi are not in that blog.

7. Trade rules already split cathode from semi finished copper, which is the relevant split for strip and sheet. IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 says the United States tariffed semi finished copper products and copper intensive derivative products in 2025, while refined cathode was excluded but under consideration, and that the prospect of a cathode tariff drew net imports of 1.5 million tonnes of cathode and a 0.5 million tonne build on COMEX [CU_IEA_US_CATHODE_2025]. USGS estimates US refined imports for consumption at 1,700 thousand metric tonnes in 2025, against 903 thousand in 2024, and year end refined stocks at 450 thousand metric tonnes against 123 thousand [CU_USGS_US_IMPORTS_2025]. HS 7403, 7409 and 7410 trade matrices are still missing (see `research/materials/copper_trade.md`).

## Production

USGS reports contained copper. World mine production in 2024 was 23.000 million tonnes rounded, led by Chile at 5.510 million tonnes, Congo (Kinshasa) at 2.990 million, Peru at 2.740 million, China at 1.840 million, the United States at 1.050 million, Russia at 1.020 million and Indonesia at 1.010 million [CU_USGS_MINE_2024]. Germany, Japan and the Republic of Korea have no mine production in that table. The 2025 mine estimates keep the world total at 23 million tonnes, with Congo (Kinshasa) at 3.200 million and Indonesia down to 710 thousand tonnes, which lines up with the IEA account of the Grasberg disruption (almost 4 percent of world mined copper in 2024, and 2025 output around half of 2024) [CU_IEA_GRASBERG_2025].

World refinery production is larger because it includes scrap. USGS gives 27.600 million tonnes in 2024 and an estimated 29.000 million tonnes in 2025, of which China is 12.400 million and an estimated 14.000 million [CU_USGS_REF_2024, CU_USGS_REF_2025]. That is about 45 percent of the 2024 world total. The ICSG factbook, using preliminary 2024 figures, says refinery production was 27.5 million tonnes, including 4.7 million tonnes of secondary refined production, and that China held 45 percent. Japan refined 1.570 million tonnes in 2024 with no domestic mine, and Germany refined 597 thousand tonnes, estimated at 610 thousand tonnes in 2025 [CU_USGS_REF_DE_2024]. Those two countries are fabricator locations, not mining locations.

United States recoverable mine production was 1.050 million tonnes in 2024 and an estimated 1.000 million tonnes in 2025, valued at an estimated 11 billion dollars in 2025 against 10.0 billion in 2024. Primary refinery output was 882 thousand tonnes in 2024 and an estimated 790 thousand tonnes in 2025, plus secondary refinery output of 39 thousand and an estimated 60 thousand tonnes. The chapter counts about 30 brass mills and 14 rod mills as the domestic consumers of refined copper and scrap. Reserves in the same edition are 980 million tonnes of contained copper. Chile holds 180 million tonnes, Australia 100 million, Peru 85 million, Congo (Kinshasa) and Russia 80 million each, Mexico 53 million, the United States 47 million and China 41 million. Germany and Japan are shown with no reserves. Australia's Joint Ore Reserves Committee compliant figure in the footnote is 27 million tonnes, against 100 million in the main column, so the two reserve definitions must not be added.

ICSG mine capacity was 27.979 million tonnes in 2024 and 28.807 million in the 2025 preliminary column, with utilisation 82.0 percent and 80.5 percent. Refinery capacity was 33.880 million tonnes and 35.646 million tonnes, with utilisation 80.9 percent and 80.2 percent. Those rates are capacity use, not a statement that mills are short of cathode.

Aurubis group cathode output of 1,096,000 tonnes in fiscal year 2024/25 (514,000 tonnes in Multimetal Recycling and 582,000 tonnes in Custom Smelting and Products) is a company total across Hamburg, Pirdop, Lünen, Olen and Beerse. It is not the USGS Germany refinery total, and it should not be added to country statistics.

## Trade

Country to country flows for HS 7403 (refined copper, unwrought), HS 7409 (plate, sheet and strip) and HS 7410 (foil) are not in `data/clean`. The trade note records that gap and the partial substitutes. What can be said now is the United States import pattern, the European sold production and cross border totals for two PRODCOM codes that are not the flat rolled codes, and the 2025 policy split between cathode and semi finished products.

USGS import sources for 2021 to 2024, copper content, put refined copper imports at Chile 68 percent, Canada 16 percent, Peru 7 percent, Mexico 6 percent and other 3 percent. Refined copper was 88 percent of unmanufactured copper imports. Ore and concentrate imports were more than 99 percent from Canada. US refined exports were only 72 thousand metric tonnes in 2024 and an estimated 110 thousand in 2025, against refined imports of 903 thousand and an estimated 1,700 thousand. Net import reliance, defined as refined imports minus refined exports plus or minus refined stock changes, was 45 percent of apparent consumption in 2024 and an estimated 57 percent in 2025.

The MCS tariff table dated 31 December 2025 lists refined copper and copper alloys, unwrought, at HS 7403.00.0000 with a 1 percent ad valorem normal trade relations rate, and wire rod of refined copper at HS 7408.11.0000 at 1 percent or 3 percent. That schedule is not the 2025 Section 232 action. The IEA account, cited above, is the one that separates a tariff on semi finished products from an exclusion, still under review, for cathode. For a strip mill, HS 7409 and HS 7410 sit on the semi finished side of that distinction. The tonnes moved under those headings are TODO(data).

Aurubis reported 27 percent of group sales in Germany and 37 percent in the rest of the European Union in fiscal year 2024/25, against 27 percent and 34 percent the year before. That is sales value across metals, not a copper strip trade balance.

## Consumption

ICSG apparent refined usage, which is use by semis plants and other first users and which does not treat direct melt scrap as refined use, was 25.857 million tonnes in 2022, 26.633 million in 2023, 27.416 million in 2024 and a preliminary 28.159 million in 2025 [CU_ICSG_USAGE_2024, CU_ICSG_USAGE_2025]. The refined balance was a 24,000 tonne deficit in 2024 and a preliminary 419,000 tonne surplus in 2025. The factbook rounds 2024 usage to 27.4 million tonnes and gives China 58 percent of global refined usage, and Asia 76 percent of refined usage in 2024 against 10 percent in 1960.

The IEA says global demand for refined copper reached almost 28 million tonnes in 2025. A chart on the same page, of the change in consumption by sector and region, is noted as including direct use scrap, so the 28 million tonne sentence and the chart note are not automatically the same definition. The number is close to the ICSG preliminary usage of 28.159 million tonnes, which is the figure used here for 2025 refined use.

United States reported consumption of refined copper was 1,580 thousand metric tonnes in both 2023 and 2024 and an estimated 1,700 thousand in 2025. Apparent consumption of primary refined copper plus copper from old scrap was 1,860 thousand metric tonnes in 2024 and an estimated 2,200 thousand in 2025. The jump is consistent with the stock build, not with a matching jump in mill shipments. Old post consumer scrap converted to refined metal, alloys and other forms was an estimated 160 thousand metric tonnes in 2025, and new manufacturing scrap was an estimated 760,000 tons in the chapter narrative. Brass and wire rod mills accounted for about 80 percent of copper recovered from scrap, and scrap contributed about 30 percent of US copper supply.

OECD STAN is the output of the industries that buy electrical and electronic components, not copper tonnes. In `data/clean/oecd_stan_P1.csv` and `data/clean/oecd_stan_B1G.csv`, values are current prices, national currency, unit multiplier millions. Germany 2023, the latest year in the file, shows C26 (computer, electronic and optical products) output of 117,895 million euro and value added of 60,252 million euro, and C27 (electrical equipment) output of 140,725 million euro and value added of 51,960 million euro [CU_STAN_DE_C26_2023, CU_STAN_DE_C27_2023]. United States C26 output was 424,283 million dollars in 2024 and C27 output was 194,208 million dollars. Japan C26 output was 26,044,800 million yen in 2024 and C27 output was 21,708,900 million yen. These pools are orders of magnitude larger than the copper strip that enters them. They are not an addressable market for the company.

## Applications

Global first use in 2024, from IWCC via the ICSG factbook, is the right cut for product form. Wire at 63 percent is the conductivity chain that starts at cathode and wire rod and ends in cable, winding wire and much of the building wire. Flat rolled plate, sheet and strip at 11 percent is the family that contains high performance alloy strip, together with ordinary copper sheet. Foil at 5 percent includes both rolled foil and electrodeposited foil, so electronics foil is inside that share and is not equal to it. Rods, bars and sections at 9 percent include both pure copper bar, of the kind used for busbars, and alloy rod. The factbook says the copper content of alloy semis is assumed at 70 percent, so the 11 percent and 9 percent are not gross weight and are not pure copper.

End use in the same IWCC table for 2024 is building construction 26 percent, consumer and general products plus cooling plus electronic 23 percent, infrastructure for power and telecom 17 percent, transport 13 percent, industrial equipment 12 percent and others 9 percent. The factbook text, citing the International Copper Association, says building construction was the largest end use market in 2024, followed by consumer and general products, cooling, and electronics. That ordering matches the IWCC sectors once cooling and electronics are kept inside the 23 percent group.

A different split appears in the ICA Copper Environmental Profile (file name dated March 2025). The page says nearly 28 million tonnes of copper are used annually, of which 70 percent is electrical and conductivity uses, and the chart marked ICA/IWCC 2021 shows power grid 44 percent, buildings 20 percent, electrical equipment 14 percent, transport 12 percent and other 10 percent. The 2021 chart and the undated "today" sentence are not used as the 2024 market split. They do show why a grid share and a building share move around: building electrical systems sit in infrastructure in one taxonomy and in buildings in another.

USGS quotes the Copper Development Association for United States use of copper and copper alloy products: building construction 42 percent, electrical and electronic products 23 percent, transportation equipment 18 percent, consumer and general products 10 percent, and industrial machinery and equipment 7 percent. The MCS paragraph does not give the year of that split. It is a national shipment pattern, not the IWCC world table.

The factbook, citing ICA, puts copper in a conventional car at 23 kg and in a battery electric car at 83 kg, against a prose line on the same page of about 22.5 kg in the average internal combustion engine. Those kilograms are windings, wire, busbars and charging equipment. They support wire rod more than alloy strip. Copper nickel alloys are mentioned for ship hulls, and brass, bronze and copper nickel for marine equipment. No tonne total for those alloy uses is given.

NVIDIA's 800 VDC note is an application fact for conductivity copper. Up to 200 kg of busbar in a 1 MW rack at 54 VDC, and a 45 percent copper reduction on row busways when moving from 415 VAC to 800 VDC, describe busbar and busway cross section. The blog separates that 45 percent claim from the rack busbar mass. It does not say that alloy strip falls by 45 percent. Connector and leadframe alloys could move with rack redesign, and that quantity is TODO(data).

## Market size

There is no single euro market for high performance copper alloy strip in the sources opened here, and none is invented.

The refined copper pool that mills draw on was 27.416 million tonnes of apparent refined use in 2024. Pricing that entire pool at the cathode quotation would describe all copper uses, not the strip a mill can sell, so no such value is entered as a market size.

IWCC's 11 percent flat rolled share and 5 percent foil share, applied to a copper content concept that already assumes 70 percent copper in alloy semis, are shares of first use rather than a priced market. They are the best public bound on how small plate, sheet, strip and foil are next to wire.

Company revenue shows the same ranking inside two integrated producers. Aurubis group revenue was 18,171 million euro in fiscal year 2024/25. The product chart in the annual report, with 2023/24 then 2024/25, reads wire rod at 6,102 then 6,284 million euro, copper cathodes at 4,035 then 4,143 million euro, shapes at 969 then 1,042 million euro, and strip, bars and profiles at 1,299 then 1,002 million euro. The strip, bar and profile decline matches the sale of the Buffalo site on 30 August 2024. Wire rod revenue is about six times strip, bar and profile revenue in 2024/25. Mitsubishi Materials Copper and Copper Alloy net sales were 433.6 billion yen in the year ended March 2025, against 410.2 billion the year before, with operating profit of 3.0 billion yen and ordinary profit of minus 1.0 billion yen. The ordinary loss is after a hedge suspension effect of minus 1.8 billion yen. Ordinary profit excluding that effect was 0.8 billion yen. The forecast ordinary profit for the year ending March 2026 is 2.6 billion yen, on a recovery in some semiconductor products and sales to existing customers. Wieland group revenue of 6,619 million euro, operating EBITDA of 707 million euro and capital expenditure of 323 million euro cover the whole semis range, including tube and refrigeration, not strip alone. Electronics and electrical engineering were 28 percent of revenue, refrigeration and air conditioning 21 percent, trade 16 percent, construction 13 percent, other 10 percent, automotive and transport 6 percent, and industrial engineering 6 percent.

Eurostat PRODCOM was queried directly because `data/clean/prodcom_2019-2024.csv` contains only a header and `data/clean/prodcom_2023.csv` contains only nickel plate. The live DS-059358 labels, retrieved 7 November 2026, do not match the names in `tools/data/fetch.py`. Code 24442200 is "Copper and copper alloy bars, rods, profiles and hollow profiles (excluding bars and rods obtained by casting or sintering, copper wire rod in coils)". Code 24442330 is "Copper wire, refined (transv. section > 6 mm), of copper alloy". EU27 sold production in 2024 was 902,534 tonnes and 4,251 million euro for 24442200, with the quantity flagged estimated, and 1,963,235 tonnes and 11,453 million euro for 24442330 [CU_PRODCOM_24442200_EU2024, CU_PRODCOM_24442330_EU2024]. Germany sold 187,350 tonnes and 868 million euro of 24442200, and 650,073 tonnes and 2,624 million euro of 24442330. The 2023 German quantity and value for 24442330 are confidential. These quantities are gross product kilograms as disseminated. Dividing value by quantity gives a euro per tonne below the refined copper price, which is not a credible selling price for wire or rod, so the cells are not used as market size. Plate, sheet and strip (PRODCOM 24442400) and foil (24442500) were not queried.

## Capacity and supply

Mine capacity is not tight on the ICSG utilisation rates above, but the project pipeline for new mines is the constraint in the IEA outlook. Average copper ore grades have fallen 40 percent since 1991, brownfield capital intensity has risen 65 percent since 2020, only 5 percent of deposits found in the last 35 years were found in the last decade, and lead times from discovery to production are around 17 years. Copper accounts for about 310 billion dollars of a little over 750 billion dollars of mining and refining capital that the Stated Policies Scenario requires from now to 2040. China accounts for just under 50 percent of global copper refining capacity. The factbook puts China's share of 2024 blister and anode output at 51 percent, which is the smelter stage, beside 45 percent of refinery output.

The IEA base case adds almost 650 thousand tonnes of mined supply in 2035 in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Zambia relative to the previous outlook, naming higher output from Chinese backed operations and the Kisanfu expansion operated by CMOC, plus Lumwana in Zambia. Peru adds almost 300 thousand tonnes in that comparison, Canada 200 thousand tonnes and, more conditionally, Russia almost 350 thousand tonnes. The Resolution project in the United States is excluded from both the base and the high production case because of legal proceedings. Over 15 percent of global primary copper is produced by leaching that uses sulphuric acid, including almost 1.5 million tonnes in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and 1.2 million tonnes in Chile, and China banned sulphuric acid exports from May 2026 to the end of the year. That is a cathode supply risk, concentrated in solvent extraction operations, not a strip mill outage.

Semis capacity of 66.1 million tonnes gross weight in 2024 is more than double refined usage, because capacity is not forecast production and because direct melt scrap sits on top of refined use. The factbook chart on the previous page labels wire rod at 61.7 percent of 66 million tonnes, while the sentence gives 59 percent wire rod, 13 percent flat rolled, 9 percent rod bar and shapes, 9 percent tubes and 10 percent other. Both are recorded. The sentence is the one used in the findings. Either reading says wire rod plants dominate capacity and flat rolling is a low teens share. Projected semis capacity growth in the factbook is 3.6 percent a year, to around 76 million tonnes by 2028. That projection is directory capacity, not a demand forecast, and it is not a forecast for alloy strip.

United States old and new scrap, and the 80 percent share recovered at brass and wire rod mills, is the domestic supply route into alloy mills and rod mills. It is not a world scrap balance. Direct melt scrap is drawn in the factbook beside refined usage and is not given a 2024 world total in the pages read here.

## Outlook to 2035

Use three layers, and do not collapse them into one deficit.

The ICSG statistical balance is the near term refined market. Preliminary 2025 was a 419,000 tonne surplus. The 23 April 2026 forecast is a surplus of about 96,000 tonnes in 2026 and about 377,000 tonnes in 2027. Mine production growth was revised to 1.6 percent in 2026 and 2.3 percent in 2027, with Grasberg and Kamoa still constrained. Refined production growth is 0.4 percent in 2026 and 3 percent in 2027. The same release states primary refined production growth of 2.3 percent and secondary refined production growth of 5.7 percent without pinning those two rates to a single year in the sentence. Usage growth is 1.6 percent in 2026, with China at about 1.9 percent and the rest of the world at 1.3 percent, then 2 percent in 2027. The release says the 2026 surplus replaces a 150,000 tonne deficit forecast in October 2025, because usage was lower than expected and secondary refined production was higher. Chinese apparent demand in this balance excludes unreported stocks.

The IEA 2035 gap is primary supply from existing and announced mines against primary requirements, after secondary supply and refining losses. It is about 25 percent under today's policies, and the text also says the deficit narrowed from 30 percent to over 25 percent. The World Energy Outlook 2025, pages 207 and 250, still states a potential 30 percent shortfall by 2035 and cites an earlier IEA piece (2025e). The 2026 outlook is the later number. Demand growth of around 7 million tonnes by 2040 is total copper demand in the Stated Policies Scenario. Page 114 does not say whether those tonnes are refined or mined. A chart note on page 110 says the copper figures there are refined demand excluding direct use scrap. Aluminium is named as the main substitute, at about 60 percent of copper's conductivity, and the copper to aluminium price ratio has been above 3.5 to 1 for much of the past five years without a major switch. Substitution that does happen is described in overhead lines, heat exchangers and some electric vehicle cabling, which again is conductor copper.

For alloy strip the implication is indirect. A primary gap supports a higher cathode price and more interest in scrap, which Aurubis and Wieland already run as a raw material strategy. It does not by itself create tonnes of CuNiSi or phosphor bronze. The demand growth the IEA lists for copper includes grids, vehicles, construction, industry, data centres, defence and electronics. Inside that list, the mill relevant pieces are connectors, leadframes, springs and thermal hardware, and those pieces are not given tonnes in the outlook. ICSG expects new semis capacity, including outside China, to keep supporting refined use, and it expects European Union and Japanese refined use to lag Asia.

## Spotlight facts

1. On 20 May 2025 NVIDIA published that a 1 MW rack at 54 VDC can require up to 200 kg of copper busbar, and that row level 800 VDC busways cut copper by 45 percent versus 415 VAC, with volume production aligned to Kyber racks in 2027, naming Infineon as a silicon partner and Vertiv, Eaton and Schneider Electric as power system partners.
2. Aurubis in fiscal year 2024/25 produced 869,000 tonnes of continuous cast copper wire rod at Hamburg, Olen, Emmerich and Avellino, against 90,000 tonnes of flat rolled products and specialty wire at Stolberg and Pori, after selling the Buffalo mill on 30 August 2024.
3. Wieland, for the year ended 30 September 2025, reported EUR 6,619 million of revenue, 28 percent of it in electronics and electrical engineering, from copper and copper alloy strip, sheet, tube, rod, wire and profiles across 89 sites, including the Schwermetall headcount.
4. Mitsubishi Materials in the year ended March 2025 sold 117 thousand tons of wrought copper products against 397 thousand tons of cathode production, and guided the Copper and Copper Alloy business toward a semiconductor related recovery in the year to March 2026 after an ordinary loss of 1.0 billion yen.
