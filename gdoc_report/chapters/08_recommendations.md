# 8. Strategic Recommendations

Allocate resources first to applications with demonstrated technical fit and a credible route to repeat orders. Aerospace qualification, chemical replacement equipment and precision electrical products form the proposed core. Preserve profitable oil-and-gas and automotive accounts while reducing dependence on declining engine-only applications.

Treat new energy products and powders as options that earn further funding through customer milestones. Market growth alone is insufficient: each proposal must show achievable qualification, contribution margin, production capacity and cash requirements.

## 8.1 Priority Products, Industries, Regions, and Customers

First priority: qualified nickel-alloy aerospace products and repair consumables, initially through European and North American engine supply chains. Second: corrosion-resistant plate and matching welding products for chemical equipment fabricators. Third: magnetic, expansion and resistance strip/wire sold to component manufacturers. This ranking reflects the report’s application analysis; it must be checked against internal profitability and capabilities.

Develop energy opportunities through stack and equipment makers with funded programmes. Retain sour-service gas applications where approvals and delivery performance support returns. In medical technology, pursue validated implant and instrument routes, subject to regulatory and customer requirements. Potential accounts are prospects, not confirmed customers; distinguish direct alloy buyers from the end users that specify materials.

## 8.2 Recommended Actions and Implementation Roadmap

Months 0–3: the commercial lead and operations team should map 2025 sales by grade, form, application, region, margin and approval status, then select a small account shortlist. Months 3–12: application engineers should run customer-backed trials and document specifications, expected annual quantities, qualification cost and purchase milestones. Track sample-to-approval conversion, repeat orders, contribution per bottleneck hour, on-time delivery and working-capital days.

Months 12–24: expand finishing, testing or inventory only where qualified demand demonstrates a capacity constraint and an acceptable return under the downside case. Management should approve investment gates using the company’s hurdle rate, not an invented project-wide target. Review the portfolio quarterly against funded project orders, aerospace production schedules, customer inventories and substitution. Use alloy-surcharge clauses where appropriate, diversify critical feedstocks and develop scrap-return arrangements.
