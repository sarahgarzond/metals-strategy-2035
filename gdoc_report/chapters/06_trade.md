# 6. Global Market Dynamics and Trade

Trade connects the metal supply base to the industrial locations where materials are processed and consumed. A country can export raw material while importing refined metal or finished alloys. Production leadership therefore does not establish access to the particular grades and forms required by a downstream buyer.

The analysis should compare imports and exports for an explicitly defined metal, product form, reporting year and geographic scope. Each trade figure must identify its classification, value or quantity basis and treatment of re-exports. Bilateral imports and partner-reported exports may differ because of timing, valuation, classification and reporting coverage. Such differences require explanation rather than averaging without a method.

The existing nickel product scope identifies HS 7505, 7506 and 7507 as covering bars and wire, flat products and tubes respectively. These categories do not isolate every specialty grade and do not cover the report’s entire steel-, cobalt- and zirconium-based scope. HS 8411 engine trade figures used in the aerospace analysis describe downstream equipment trade and are not a substitute for alloy trade.

No complete, harmonised bilateral dataset has been established in the existing report, so this chapter does not assert a global trade total or rank countries using unlike product categories. The practical output is a product-specific sourcing and sales assessment: identify material origins, processing locations, importing markets and the direct buyers capable of purchasing Münvest’s proposed delivery forms. Quantification remains conditional on verified trade extracts and documented code coverage.

In the Metals Atlas, import and export views should share the same selected metal and product classification. Flow thickness represents either volume or value, with that measure stated explicitly. Missing routes must not be interpreted as zero trade. An attractive trade route becomes commercially relevant only when specifications, approvals, logistics, delivery reliability and customer access are considered alongside its size.
